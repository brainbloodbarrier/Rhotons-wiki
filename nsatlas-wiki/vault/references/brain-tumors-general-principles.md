# Brain Tumors — General Principles

*Source: NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol | DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v4.ch01*

Craniotomy for brain tumors has evolved from a mortality-avoidance exercise into a morbidity-minimization discipline, driven by MRI navigation, cortical/subcortical mapping, and microsurgical instrumentation. The shift reframes the operative goal: **maximal safe resection**, not heroic total resection. Pathology-specific tenets live in the dedicated tumor chapters; this page captures the cross-cutting framework.

## Evaluation and Differential Diagnosis

A meticulous history and physical must precede imaging interpretation. **Non-neoplastic mass lesions** routinely masquerade as tumors and, when missed, expose patients to unnecessary craniotomy:

- **Infectious** — toxoplasmosis, cerebral abscess, histoplasmosis, nocardia
- **Vascular** — evolving infarction (gyral enhancement pattern, DWI positivity, T2 hyperintensity crossing the striatum)
- **Inflammatory** — sarcoidosis (can mimic [[medial-sphenoid-wing-meningioma|convexity or parafalcine meningioma]])
- **Demyelinating / autoimmune** — multiple sclerosis with tumefactive lesions (incomplete ring enhancement, disproportionately low mass effect for lesion size)

Tumors that are **not resection candidates** must also be recognized preoperatively:

- **Germinomas, CNS lymphoma, cerebral myeloma, leukemic masses** — nonresective diagnostic strategies (CSF markers, stereotactic biopsy) precede any incision
- **Multifocal disease** — multiplicity undermines the benefits of aggressive resection
- Lesions involving **eloquent cortex/fibers** or **vital neurovascular structures** with poorly defined boundaries

## Imaging Discipline

- **MRI** is the backbone — superior soft-tissue resolution defines size, location, boundaries, eloquent proximity, and enhancement pattern
- **CT** clarifies bone involvement and erosion pattern (skull base tumors, growth-rate inference)
- **CTA / catheter angiography** map vascular anatomy; **embolization** can be lifesaving for hypervascular tumors (glomus jugulare, hemangiopericytoma, solid hemangioblastoma) when intraoperative devascularization is not feasible
- When imaging is ambiguous and non-resectable entities are in the differential, **stereotactic biopsy** directs final management

## Preoperative Considerations

- **Age-stratified workup** — chest x-ray if >40 y, ECG if >60 y
- **Labs** — CBC with platelets, BMP, type & cross when transfusion is plausible; PT/PTT and coagulation panel for bleeding diathesis or anticoagulation
- **Systemic staging** — CT chest/abdomen/pelvis and bone scan for known/suspected metastatic disease; neuro-oncology consult
- **Futility threshold** — aggressive resection is usually not justified when systemic disease implies <6-month lifespan
- **Seizure prophylaxis** — [[preoperative-evaluation|levetiracetam]] 20 mg/kg IV load then 500–1000 mg q12h, particularly for temporal-lobe tumors or extensive cortical dissection
- **Steroids** — not routine; **dexamethasone** 10 mg IV load + 4 mg q6h for significant vasogenic edema

## Anesthetic Considerations

Four intraoperative priorities:

1. **Cerebral perfusion pressure**
2. **Brain relaxation**
3. **"Smooth" anesthesia** — avoid ICP spikes
4. **Efficient emergence** for immediate neurologic exam

Induction favors barbiturates to reduce CMRO₂ and ICP. After nondepolarizing relaxant, narcotics are safe without chest-rigidity ICP effect. Maintenance is typically **volatile (isoflurane)** + low-dose narcotic + relaxant; isoflurane reduces CMRO₂ but mildly raises ICP — balance accordingly. **Iso-osmolar fluids only**; **glucose-containing fluids are forbidden** (hyperglycemia worsens ischemic outcome). Dexamethasone 10 mg and cefazolin 1 g pre-induction. SCDs and Foley universal; [[lumbar-drain|lumbar drain]] in selected skull base or large supratentorial cases for brain relaxation.

## Patient Positioning

- [[skull-clamp-placement|Mayfield clamp]] pins placed **as far from the incision as possible** and **never over shunt catheters or burr holes** (shunt puncture, dural laceration at prior burr-hole sites)
- Previous craniotomy sites are left **unpinned** to prevent bone-flap sink; pin near the orbital rim has caused globe rupture — pick an alternate geometry
- Head rotation <45° in supine; lateral / park-bench for posterior exposures
- Generous padding for pressure points; firm strapping for obese patients or planned tilt
- Recheck all IV lines and the [[lumbar-drain|lumbar drain]] after final positioning, before skin prep
- **Minimal shaving**; mark incision after confirming tumor location with MRI navigation against landmarks — external auditory meatus, medial/lateral canthus, nasion

## Intraoperative Conduct

Approach-specific nuances belong in the Cranial Approaches volume (see [[pterional-craniotomy]], [[bifrontal-craniotomy]], [[retromastoid-craniotomy]], [[suboccipital-craniotomy]]). General rules:

- Near **dural venous sinuses**, place two burr holes over the sinus and save the cut closest to the sinus for last
- **Thorough irrigation** to evacuate bone dust before dural opening
- Posterior fossa dural opening leaves a **dural sleeve** for watertight closure
- After dural reflection, confirm the tumor's cortical surface extension by inspection ± image guidance ± intraoperative ultrasound

### Brain shift and navigation caveat

**Single-source navigation reliance is unsafe after dural opening** because CSF release induces brain shift. Complement electronic navigation with:

- Large cortical surface vessels
- Normal sulci / gyral pial boundaries at the tumor margin
- Preoperative imaging landmarks mapped to the exposed field

Cortical mapping protocols (language, sensorimotor) are covered in dedicated mapping chapters not ingested here.

### Resection philosophy

*En bloc* resection is preferred when boundaries or pseudocapsule are distinguishable from parenchyma and when the operative corridor admits it without retraction on normal structures. **Aggressive coagulation of surrounding normal gyri is avoided** — persistent bleeding usually signals residual tumor, not failed hemostasis, and mandates further inspection before reaching for more bipolar. Hemostatic materials are **not used to line the cavity** in Cohen-Gadol's practice: delayed scarring enhances on postoperative MRI (mimicking recurrence) and breeds false confidence in hemostasis. Thrombin-solution irrigation handles venous ooze. Watertight dural closure is mandatory for **posterior fossa** but not for routine **supratentorial** craniotomies.

## Postoperative Considerations

- **Immediate neurologic exam** in the OR on emergence; any unexpected deficit or difficult arousal triggers a CT scan
- Diet advances from ice chips as tolerated
- Hourly neuro checks overnight for high-risk patients; q2–4h otherwise
- Most patients leave ICU / IMC on POD1
- Antibiotics stop at 24 h unless otherwise indicated
- **Steroid taper** individualized; typically start weaning POD4–5 over 1–2 weeks
- **Anticonvulsant** discontinued ~1 week post-op if no seizures
- **Post-op MRI within 48 h** to quantify residual disease
- PT/OT for speech, motor, sensory deficits
- Typical discharge ~POD3

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- Always exhaust the **non-neoplastic differential** before committing to resection; toxoplasmosis, MS, sarcoidosis, and infarction are the classic misreads
- **Multifocality** and **eloquent involvement** change the calculus from resection to biopsy + adjuvant therapy
- **Brain shift** invalidates single-source navigation — anchor to anatomic landmarks
- **No hemostatic lining** in the resection cavity — it obscures follow-up imaging and masks inadequate hemostasis
- **Posterior fossa ≠ supratentorial** for dural closure — watertight vs approximated
