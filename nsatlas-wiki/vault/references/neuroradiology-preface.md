# Neuroradiology — Preface

*Source: NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol (volume edited by Aaron P. Kamer, MD, Indiana University)*

Medical imaging, **in all its forms, is the most powerful tool for noninvasive diagnosis and characterization of preoperative and postoperative neurosurgical disease**. Whether it is rapid CT assessment of intracranial hemorrhage or MRI delineation of tumor operability, the preoperative dataset often dictates the intraoperative decision.

## The Role of the Neurosurgeon

- Most patients reach neurosurgery **already imaged**
- The surgeon must read the studies **independently** to plan the operation — not merely read the radiologist's report
- **Consultation with an experienced neuroradiologist** remains essential for the complex and the ambiguous — this is not optional
- Working imaging literacy means:
  - Distinguishing intra-axial vs extra-axial lesions at a glance
  - Recognizing the critical CT pathologies that require emergent action (see [[imaging-sah-and-aneurysm]])
  - Knowing which MRI sequence answers which question (see [[neuroradiology-imaging-modalities]])
  - Knowing when to repeat stale imaging (> 3 months)
  - Integrating vascular, venous, and metabolic sequences before skull base or eloquent-cortex work

## Volume Scope

The NSAtlas Neuroradiology volume covers:
- **General principles** — modalities, reading strategy, intra-/extra-axial taxonomy
- **Disease-specific radiology** — tumor, vascular, trauma, infection, demyelination, congenital, and degenerative lesions
- **Inoperable mimics** — the lesions the neurosurgeon must confidently *not* operate on ([[progressive-multifocal-leukoencephalopathy]], [[dysplastic-cerebellar-gangliocytoma]], etc.)

## Why This Volume Matters for Operative Planning

- **Imaging is the operative plan.** Every dural opening, corridor, and retraction vector is chosen on the preop MRI before the patient enters the OR
- **The most preventable intraoperative injuries are the ones visible on preop imaging** — a vein of Labbé, a sphenoparietal sinus crossing the flap, a high-riding jugular bulb, a calcified ICA wall
- **Postoperative imaging closes the loop** — early postop MRI documents extent of resection before reactive enhancement obscures the field; DWI catches intraop-silent infarcts

## Pearls

- **Read the scans yourself, then read the report** — the reverse produces confirmation bias
- **Bring the whole DICOM, not just the report**, to tumor board and preop discussions
- **Imaging > 3 months is stale** — lesional change in that window changes plans
- **If the imaging doesn't answer your operative question, order the sequence that does** — fat-suppressed orbital T1, SWI for cavernoma, MRS for recurrence vs necrosis
- **Team up with neuroradiology for complex skull base, vascular, and pediatric cases** — the conversation catches findings neither side will alone
