# Cranial Base Surgery — General Principles

*Source: NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol*

Cranial base surgery is the discipline of **trading bone for brain retraction**. Lesions at the floor of the anterior, middle, and posterior fossae sit behind dense osseous barriers, surrounded by cranial nerves, named arteries, and venous sinuses that tolerate no force. The founding insight of modern skull base surgery — pioneered by Dolenc, Al-Mefty, Fukushima, Yasargil, and Sekhar — is that aggressive **extradural osteotomy** converts a long, retracted, narrow intradural dissection into a short, unretracted, wide one. The brain thanks you with preserved function; the cranial nerves thank you with preserved function; the patient thanks you with a shorter ICU stay.

## Foundational Tenets

1. **Bone removal ≫ brain retraction.** Every millimeter of skull base drilled is a millimeter of brain not compressed.
2. **The approach is the operation.** By the time intradural dissection begins, the battle is already won or lost.
3. **Cranial nerve preservation is measured in technique, not hope.** Know the fascicular course, the dural entry points, the branches at risk, and the monitoring that will warn you.
4. **Multidisciplinary from day one.** Otolaryngology, neurotology, ophthalmology, endocrinology, neuroradiology, and plastic/reconstructive surgery each own portions of the perioperative care. Skull base programs succeed or fail on team cohesion.
5. **Anatomy-first training.** Every skull base approach is learned in the cadaver lab before the OR, repeatedly.

## The Hierarchy of Approach Selection

Given a lesion, the skull base surgeon iterates through these questions in order:

1. **Can this be biopsied only, not resected?** (e.g., germinoma, lymphoma, some chordomas)
2. **Is an endoscopic endonasal corridor sufficient?** Midline clival, sellar/parasellar, and central skull base lesions often are best addressed through the nose — avoiding any craniotomy.
3. **Among transcranial options, which minimizes retraction?** See [[cranial-base-operative-corridor-selection]] for the anatomic algorithm.
4. **What combined approach is needed?** Some lesions span fossae; the operative plan sequences osteotomies to expose the whole tumor.
5. **What reconstructive plan closes the defect?** Plan closure before the first bone cut — vascularized flap, fat graft, titanium mesh, fascia lata, lumbar drain.

## Categorical Approaches

Each approach is a named osteotomy or corridor, matched to an anatomic compartment:

### Anterior skull base

- Subfrontal ([[bifrontal-craniotomy]]) — olfactory groove, cribriform, planum
- [[supraorbital-craniotomy]] — anterior fossa midline/paramedian lesions through the eyebrow
- Transbasal — extensive anterior fossa with nasal extension
- Endoscopic endonasal — sellar, parasellar, clival, olfactory-groove via nose

### Anterolateral / lateral skull base

- [[pterional-craniotomy]] — parasellar, anterior circulation aneurysms, intrasylvian
- Orbitozygomatic — superior / posteromedial parasellar; adds ~1 cm of vertical working distance
- Extended pterional with anterior clinoidectomy — paraclinoid aneurysms, clinoidal meningiomas
- Far lateral / transcondylar — foramen magnum and ventral craniocervical

### Middle and posterior fossa

- [[anterior-petrosectomy]] — upper petroclivus, Meckel's cave, ventrolateral upper pons (Kawase)
- Posterior petrosectomy (translabyrinthine, transcochlear) — petroclival with hearing sacrifice acceptable
- Combined petrosal — spans middle and posterior fossae
- [[retromastoid-craniotomy]] — cerebellopontine angle, lower petroclival

### Posterior and craniocervical

- [[suboccipital-craniotomy]] — midline posterior fossa, fourth ventricle
- Far lateral, extreme lateral — ventrolateral lower brainstem, CC junction
- [[paramedian-supracerebellar-craniotomy]] — pineal region, posterior incisura

## Core Preoperative Considerations

See [[cranial-base-preoperative-considerations]] for the full workup. The irreducible elements:

- **Thin-slice CT** for bone — pneumatization, fractures, hyperostosis, vascular canals
- **MRI with vascular sequences** — T1 pre/post, T2, FIESTA/CISS for cranial nerves, MRA, MRV
- **Catheter angiography** — for hypervascular tumors, sinus involvement, aneurysm coexistence
- **Endocrine panel** — any lesion touching the sellar or hypothalamic regions
- **Ophthalmology / neurotology** — visual fields, audiogram, vestibular testing as indicated
- **Anesthesia / IOM plan** — cranial nerve EMG, SSEP/MEP, BAERs per corridor
- **Lumbar drain availability** and team comfort with it — see [[lumbar-drain]]

## Intraoperative Philosophy

- **Wide extradural exposure first**, then conservative intradural dissection
- **Drill with continuous irrigation** — diamond burrs near nerves and vessels, fluted cutters on cortical bone, never a dry burr
- **Identify before removing** — cranial nerves, named vessels, and venous sinuses are found and labeled before any work is done around them
- **Tentorium and falx are ropes, not walls** — divide them when geometry demands it, respecting dural venous drainage
- **Sacrifice bone, not function** — if a wider drilling gives 20% more exposure and 80% less retraction, drill
- **Anticipate CSF leak** — every skull base opening plans its own closure; vascularized nasoseptal flaps, fat grafts, and multilayer repairs are standard, not afterthoughts
- See [[cranial-base-surgery-lessons-learned]] for the catalogue of case-based mistakes to avoid

## Closure and Reconstruction

- **Dural**: primary closure when possible; otherwise fascia lata / pericranium / synthetic dural substitute
- **Bone**: titanium mesh or cranial bone graft for defects that affect cosmesis or dural support
- **Soft tissue**: vascularized flap (temporalis, pericranial, nasoseptal) for irradiated fields or large defects
- **Lumbar drain**: 3–5 days postoperative for extensive dural work
- **Sinus drainage** (frontal, mastoid): cranialize or obliterate exposed air cells with fat/muscle/bone wax

## Postoperative Principles

- Endocrine surveillance for sellar/parasellar cases (sodium, cortisol, thyroid)
- Vision and cranial nerve exams documented hourly in first 24 hours
- Antibiotics through drain removal; vigilance for CSF rhinorrhea/otorrhea
- Early PT/OT; vestibular rehabilitation for CN VIII injury
- Outpatient follow-up with imaging at 3 months, then annually for benign disease

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Approach before resection.** A wrong corridor cannot be fixed with more skill.
- **Plan the closure first.** If you cannot see how to seal the defect, you should not open it.
- **The cranial nerve you cannot find is the one you will injure.** Dissect extradurally until each named nerve is identified before intradural work.
- **Petrous ICA**, **cavernous sinus venous plexus**, **labyrinth/cochlea**, and **optic canal** are the four most common preventable injuries — know them cold for every approach.
- **Hyperostosis in meningiomas** extends the osteotomy and can unintentionally propagate into the optic canal — see [[pterional-craniotomy|pterional craniotomy pearls]].
