# Imaging of Subarachnoid Hemorrhage and Cerebral Aneurysm

*Source: NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol (contributor: Aaron Kamer, MD et al.)*

## Overview

### Subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH)

- **Ruptured aneurysm** — 80% of nontraumatic SAH
- **Vascular malformations** — up to 5%
- **Perimesencephalic nonaneurysmal SAH** — distinct benign entity (venous origin hypothesized)
- **Traumatic SAH** — separate workflow

### Intracranial aneurysms

- **Prevalence 2–3%** of the general population
- **Saccular aneurysms** — far more common than dissections/pseudoaneurysms; arise at arterial branch points
- High-flow phenomena: feeders to dural AVF / AVM; intranidal AVM aneurysms
- **Infectious (mycotic), traumatic, neoplastic** aneurysms — <5%

## Presentation

- **Acute severe headache** ("worst headache of my life") = classical ruptured aneurysm
- **Sequelae**: acute hydrocephalus, vasospasm, cerebral edema, ischemic infarct, herniation, coma, death
- **Terson syndrome** — vitreous hemorrhage in up to 13%, associated with severe SAH

## Imaging Workup — The Decision Tree

```
Suspected SAH
 ├── NECT (sensitivity ~98% within 6h; drops after 24–72h)
 │    ├── Positive → CTA
 │    │    ├── Aneurysm found → DSA for characterization + treatment planning
 │    │    └── Negative → DSA (to rule out small or thrombosed aneurysm)
 │    └── Negative → LP if clinical suspicion remains
 │         ├── Xanthochromia / RBC → CTA/DSA
 │         └── Negative → consider alternative diagnosis
MRI / MRA — uncommonly used acutely; excellent for surveillance of unruptured / treated aneurysms
```

## Nonenhanced CT (NECT)

### Technique

- Axial 5-mm collimation
- Coronal/sagittal reformats available if acquired helically

### Findings

- **Hyperdense (50–75 HU)** sulcal/cisternal blood = SAH
- **Sensitivity > 95% in first 6h**, falls to ~75% at 24h, further declines after
- **SAH distribution points to aneurysm location**:

| SAH location | Likely aneurysm |
|--------------|-----------------|
| **Interhemispheric / anterior cisterns** | **AComA** |
| **Suprasellar cistern** | **IC-PComA, AComA** |
| **Sylvian fissure** | **MCA bifurcation** |
| **Prepontine, CPA, 4th ventricle** | **PICA, BA, VA** |
| **Perimesencephalic only** | **Nonaneurysmal perimesencephalic SAH** (DSA often negative) |

### Fisher CT Grading

| Grade | Finding |
|-------|---------|
| 1 | No SAH |
| 2 | Diffuse thin layer (<1 mm) |
| 3 | Localized clot or thick layer (>1 mm) |
| 4 | Intraventricular blood (± parenchymal) |

Fisher grade correlates with vasospasm risk — **Grade 3 highest**.

### Sequelae detectable on NECT

- **Intraventricular hemorrhage**
- **Hydrocephalus** — up to 90% at presentation; low threshold for [[external-ventricular-drain]]
- Cerebral edema, infarct, mass effect, herniation

### NECT limitations

- Low sensitivity for aneurysm itself (except large/giant with mass effect, or thrombosed aneurysm hyperdense to parenchyma)
- **Beam-hardening artifact** — skull base, posterior fossa, metal clips, coil mass

### NECT mimics (DDx)

- **Pseudo-SAH from diffuse cerebral edema** — hypodense parenchyma makes normal arteries look bright
- **AVM / dural AVF**
- **Perimesencephalic nonaneurysmal SAH** (benign, diagnosis of exclusion)
- **Intracranial arterial dissection**
- **Traumatic SAH**
- **Cavernous malformation** rupture
- **Vasculitis, RCVS**
- **Leptomeningeal metastasis / infection (meningitis)**
- **Mycotic aneurysm**
- **Recent intrathecal or intra-arterial contrast** (contrast extravasation post-thrombectomy)

## CT Angiography (CTA)

### Technique

- Thin collimation (1–2 mm)
- 4–5 mL/sec of high-iodine contrast (350–370 mg I/mL)
- Coronal/sagittal reformats, **10-mm MIPs**, volumetric reconstructions
- Often performed immediately after NECT

### Findings

- **Patent aneurysms enhance uniformly**
- **Specificity 100%, sensitivity 96–99.7%** for aneurysms **> 4 mm**
- Smaller aneurysms (< 3 mm), blister aneurysms, and aneurysms near bone/coil may be missed

### Neck, dome, daughter-sac, and branch anatomy

- CTA informs surgical clippability vs endovascular coilability
- **Dome-to-neck ratio**, **aspect ratio**, branch take-off proximity
- Wall irregularity, daughter sac — markers of recent / impending rupture

## CTA Sensitivity by Size

- **≥ 4 mm**: sensitivity **96–99.7%**, specificity ~100%.
- **≥ 2 mm**: sensitivity **90–95%**.
- **< 3 mm, blister aneurysms, aneurysms adjacent to bone/coil mass**: frequently missed — DSA required.
- **Dual-energy CT** improves skull-base/posterior-fossa aneurysm detection (HU thresholding removes bony interference).

## The Infundibulum Trap

- **Vessel infundibulum**: a **< 3-mm conical protrusion** at the origin of a branch vessel, with the branch arising directly from the apex.
- CTA / MRA cannot always distinguish it from a small aneurysm.
- **Classic location**: origin of the PComA from the distal ICA.
- **DSA (lateral projection)** clarifies — a visible branch vessel emerging from the apex supports infundibulum; a focal outpouching without branch emergence is an aneurysm until proven otherwise.

## MRI / MRA

- **Not first-line** for acute SAH (time, motion, patient instability)
- **FLAIR** can show SAH (hyperintensity in normally dark CSF) — **87% sensitivity** for SAH alone; **~100%** combined with SWI.
- **SWI/GRE** (88% sensitivity) demonstrates blood products and chronic **superficial siderosis** in unrecognized old SAH; "blooming artifact" exaggerates volume with progressive stages.
- **TOF-MRA** and contrast-enhanced MRA: surveillance of **unruptured and previously treated aneurysms** (avoid ionizing radiation).

### Blood-Product Evolution on MRI (the classic 5 stages)

| Stage | Time | Blood product | T1 | T2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hyperacute | < 24 h | Intracellular oxyHb | Isointense | Iso → hyper |
| Acute | 1–2 d | Intracellular deoxyHb | Iso → dark | Dark |
| Early subacute | 2–7 d | Intracellular metHb | Hyper | Dark |
| Late subacute | 7 d – 4 wk | Extracellular metHb | Hyper | Hyper |
| Chronic | > 4 wk | Hemosiderin | Dark | Dark |

Hemosiderin may persist indefinitely depending on BBB integrity. **Superficial siderosis** (hemosiderin cortical coating) from chronic/repeated SAH causes cortical atrophy and dysfunction.

### Large / Giant Aneurysm MRI Advantages

- Detects **mural thrombus** that CT cannot distinguish from flowing blood — crucial for partially thrombosed giant aneurysms that mimic hematoma.
- **Pulsation artifact** supports patency.
- **SPGR post-contrast T1** shows intra-aneurysmal enhancement clearly.
- MRI is **superior to CT and conventional angiography** for characterizing complex giant aneurysms.
- Assesses **mass-effect sequelae** (parenchymal edema on FLAIR).

### MRI Limitations

- Less cost-effective; less available than CT.
- Unsuitable for unstable, combative, or claustrophobic patients.
- **Contraindicated with ferromagnetic aneurysm clips** — modern clips are non-ferromagnetic, but verify pre-scan.

## Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA)

- **Gold standard** for aneurysm detection and characterization
- **3D rotational angiography** with C-arm — superior spatial and temporal resolution
- Defines:
  - Precise neck geometry
  - Daughter sacs, wall irregularity
  - Branch take-offs and inclusion
  - **Collateral flow** — AComA, PComA patency, cross-filling
- **Therapeutic access** — coiling, flow diversion, stent-assisted coiling, WEB
- **Vasospasm surveillance** post-SAH — angiography for treatment (intra-arterial vasodilators, balloon angioplasty)
- **Repeat DSA at 7–14 days** for initially negative studies with confirmed SAH (5–20% reveal occult aneurysm)

## Delayed Cerebral Ischemia Imaging

- **CT perfusion** — mismatch maps detect penumbra from vasospasm-induced hypoperfusion
- **Serial TCDs** — noninvasive vasospasm surveillance (MCA mean velocity > 200 cm/s or Lindegaard ratio > 6 concerning)
- **MRI DWI** — identifies completed infarcts

## Clinical Grading Systems (paired with imaging)

- **Hunt-Hess** (I–V) — clinical severity
- **WFNS** (I–V) — GCS-based
- **Modified Fisher** — adds IVH weighting to Fisher

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **NECT first, always** — within 6h is when its sensitivity peaks
- **Negative NECT + high suspicion → LP** (xanthochromia); then CTA/DSA if positive
- **SAH distribution fingerprints the aneurysm location** — read the cisterns before reading the vessels
- **Pseudo-SAH** in diffuse cerebral edema — don't mistake bright arteries on a dark brain for blood
- **Perimesencephalic nonaneurysmal SAH** is a diagnosis of exclusion — DSA still required
- **CTA misses <4 mm aneurysms and blister aneurysms** — DSA when clinical picture insists
- **Repeat DSA at 7–14 days** if initial study is negative and SAH is confirmed
- **Hydrocephalus is present in up to 90%** — don't let a clean GCS make you forget the EVD tray
- **Vasospasm peaks day 4–14** — CTP + TCD surveillance
- **MRA / contrast-MRA** for follow-up of treated or incidental unruptured aneurysms
- **Clip/coil artifact** obscures recurrence on CTA — DSA is the revalidation tool
