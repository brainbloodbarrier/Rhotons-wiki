# Skull Anatomy

*NSAtlas: Operative Anatomy of the Human Skull — A Virtual Reality
Expedition*

**Source:** Cohen-Gadol et al., developed from cadaveric osteologic
specimens, software-modelled radiographic reconstructions, and
anatomist review. Eight 3D models divide the cranium into regional
zones (see [[3d-anatomy]]).

## Overview

The cranium comprises eight bones forming the neurocranium — frontal,
paired parietal, paired temporal, sphenoid, ethmoid, occipital — plus
the facial skeleton. Four form the calvaria; four contribute to the
skull base (sphenoid, ethmoid, portions of frontal and temporal,
occipital). Every cranial approach is defined by its bony exposure; a
working mental model of the cranium's foramina, sutures, and sinus
grooves is the foundation of approach selection.

Two overview models from the series:

- **Model 1** — exterior skull osteology.
- **Model 2** — interior skull osteology (endocranial surface).

## Frontal Bone

Unpaired bone, developmentally two halves fused at the metopic
suture. Articulates with parietals, zygomatics, sphenoid, ethmoid,
lacrimals, maxillae, nasals. Three parts: **squamous, orbital,
nasal**.

- **Squamous portion** — frontal eminences, superciliary arches,
  glabella, supraorbital margins with supraorbital notch/foramen
  (supraorbital vessels and nerves).
- **Internal surface** — sagittal sulcus (for superior sagittal
  sinus), frontal crest (falx cerebri attachment).
- **Orbital portion** — paired orbital plates separated by
  ethmoidal notch (filled by cribriform plate); lacrimal fossa for
  lacrimal gland; contains **frontal sinuses** and frontonasal ducts
  — key consideration in [[bifrontal-craniotomy]] and subfrontal
  approaches.

## Ethmoid Bone

Unpaired, cube-shaped, articulates with 13 bones. Three parts:

- **Cribriform plate** — transmits olfactory nerves (CN I); crista
  galli as midline falx attachment; foramen cecum anteriorly;
  olfactory bulb grooves laterally.
- **Perpendicular plate** — midline vertical plate contributing to
  nasal septum.
- **Ethmoidal labyrinth** — ethmoid air cells; lamina papyracea
  forms medial orbital wall; superior and middle nasal conchae;
  uncinate process at maxillary sinus.

Relevant to endonasal approaches and anterior cranial fossa CSF
leak repair.

## Sphenoid Bone

Centre of the skull base. Articulates with temporal, parietal,
frontal, occipital, ethmoid, zygomatic, palatine, and vomer bones.

- **Body** — contains sella turcica; pneumatized sphenoid sinus
  provides the corridor for
  [[microscope-guided-endonasal-transsphenoidal-approach]].
- **Greater wings** — anterior portion of each middle fossa.
- **Lesser wings** — posterior portion of anterior cranial fossa;
  terminate in **anterior clinoid processes**.
- **Clinoid processes** — anterior (lesser wing tip), middle
  (anterior sellar border), posterior (dorsum sellae). Anterior
  clinoidectomy opens the optic-carotid window in paraclinoid
  aneurysm surgery.
- **Foramina of the greater wing:**
  - *Superior orbital fissure* — CN III, IV, V1 branches, CN VI,
    superior ophthalmic vein, sympathetic fibres.
  - *Foramen rotundum* — V2.
  - *Foramen ovale* — V3, accessory meningeal artery, lesser
    petrosal nerve.
  - *Foramen spinosum* — middle meningeal vessels.
- **Optic canals** — at junction of body and lesser wings; CN II
  and ophthalmic artery.
- **Pterygoid (Vidian's) canal** — inferomedial to foramen rotundum;
  transmits Vidian's nerve (greater + deep petrosal).

Critical for every [[pterional-craniotomy]] (sphenoid ridge rongeured),
middle-fossa approaches, and endonasal corridors.

## Temporal Bone

Five segments: squamous, mastoid, tympanic, styloid, petrous.
Articulates with zygomatic, sphenoid, parietal, occipital bones.

### External / lateral temporal bone

- **Squamous portion** — superior and inferior temporal lines for
  temporalis fascia and muscle attachment (see
  [[pterional-craniotomy]]).
- **Zygomatic process** — anterior/posterior roots, articular
  tubercle, glenoid fossa (TMJ), petrotympanic fissure (chorda
  tympani).
- **External auditory meatus** — tympanic portion.
- **Mastoid process** — attachment of SCM, digastric posterior belly,
  splenius and longissimus capitis; pneumatized air cells.
- **Styloid process and stylomastoid foramen** — CN VII exits here
  with the stylomastoid branch of the posterior auricular artery.

### Internal / petrous temporal bone

- **Carotid canal** — ICA and sympathetic plexus.
- **Tympanic and cochlear canaliculi** — CN IX tympanic branch;
  perilymphatic duct.
- **Sigmoid sulcus** — sigmoid sinus groove on endocranial surface.
- **Superior petrosal sulcus** — drains cavernous sinus to transverse
  sinus.
- **Arcuate eminence** — surface landmark for superior semicircular
  canal; critical for middle-fossa approach trajectory.
- **Tegmen tympani** — thin bone separating tympanic cavity from
  middle cranial fossa; often dehiscent (CSF leak risk).
- **Internal acoustic meatus (IAM)** — CN VII, CN VIII, labyrinthine
  artery.
- **Hiatus of facial canal** — greater petrosal nerve.
- **Eustachian tube (bony portion)** — nasopharyngeal communication.

Temporal bone relevance: middle fossa, subtemporal anterior
transpetrosal (Kawase), translabyrinthine, transcochlear, subtemporal
preauricular infratemporal, postauricular transtemporal, and
presigmoid (supra- and infra-tentorial) approaches. See
[[infratentorial-operative-anatomy]] for middle-ear content.

## Parietal Bone

Paired; articulates at sagittal, coronal, lambdoid, squamosal
sutures.

- **External** — parietal eminence, superior/inferior temporal
  lines.
- **Internal** — superior sagittal sulcus with foveolae granulares
  (Pacchionian granulations), middle meningeal artery groove.

[[parietal-craniotomy]] is centered here; [[parasagittal-craniotomy]]
respects the sagittal sinus at the midline suture.

## Occipital Bone

Posterior cranium + skull base. Three parts: squamous, basilar,
lateral (two condylar parts).

### Squamous part

- **External occipital protuberance (inion)** — nuchal ligament,
  trapezius attachment.
- **Superior, inferior, median nuchal lines.**
- **Internal occipital protuberance** — torcular Herophili
  (confluence of sinuses).
- **Vermian fossa** — posterior to foramen magnum.

### Basilar part (clivus)

Extends up from foramen magnum, articulates with dorsum sellae at
the sphenooccipital synchondrosis. Covers ventral brainstem.
Pharyngeal tubercle on inferior surface. Target of transclival
endonasal approaches for chordoma, chondrosarcoma, and ventral
brainstem cavernomas.

### Lateral parts

- **Occipital condyles** — articulate with C1 lateral masses.
- **Condyloid fossa / condyloid canal** — emissary vein.
- **Hypoglossal canal** — CN XII + meningeal branch of ascending
  pharyngeal artery; key landmark for far-lateral / transcondylar
  approach.
- **Jugular tubercle** — overlies hypoglossal canal; partial
  resection widens ventral access to lower CNs.
- **Jugular notch / foramen** — CN IX, X, XI, internal jugular vein,
  inferior petrosal sinus.

### Foramen magnum

Transmits medulla, CN XI (spinal part), vertebral arteries, anterior
and posterior spinal arteries, alar ligaments. Central to
[[suboccipital-craniotomy]] and craniocervical junction surgery.

## Sutures

| Suture | Between |
|---|---|
| Coronal | Frontal ↔ parietals |
| Sagittal | Parietals |
| Lambdoid | Parietals ↔ occipital |
| Squamosal | Temporal squama ↔ parietal |
| Metopic | Frontal halves (normally fused) |
| Sphenosquamosal | Sphenoid ↔ temporal squama |
| Sphenoparietal | Sphenoid ↔ parietal |
| Occipitomastoid | Occipital ↔ temporal mastoid |
| Parietomastoid | Parietal ↔ mastoid |
| Sphenofrontal / sphenoethmoidal | as named |

The **pterion** — sphenoparietal / sphenosquamosal / sphenofrontal
junction — is the classic target of [[pterional-craniotomy]].
**Asterion** (lambdoid / occipitomastoid / parietomastoid junction)
marks the transverse-sigmoid transition for
[[retromastoid-craniotomy]] trajectory.
