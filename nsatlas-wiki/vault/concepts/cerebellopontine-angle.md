# Cerebellopontine Angle

The cerebellopontine angle (CPA) is the triangular-pyramidal subarachnoid cistern formed at the junction of the cerebellum, pons, medulla, and petrous bone. It is arguably the most surgically active corner of the posterior fossa: every vestibular schwannoma, most CPA meningiomas, most epidermoid and dermoid cysts, and all cranial-nerve vascular decompressions for trigeminal neuralgia, hemifacial spasm, and glossopharyngeal neuralgia are worked in this cistern. Its neurovascular architecture — dictated by the caudal brainstem cranial nerves and the AICA loop — must be memorized.

## Boundaries

| Boundary | Structure |
|----------|-----------|
| Anteromedial | Lateral pons and upper medulla |
| Posterolateral | Anterior surface of cerebellar hemisphere and flocculus |
| Lateral / anterior | Posterior surface of petrous temporal bone (porus acusticus, jugular foramen) |
| Roof | Tentorium cerebelli |
| Floor | Posterior cranial fossa |

Superiorly the CPA opens into the **cerebellomesencephalic** and **ambient** cisterns around CN IV and V; inferiorly it continues into the **cerebellomedullary** (magna) cistern around the lower cranial nerves.

## Cranial Nerves

Encountered from rostral to caudal:

- **CN V (trigeminal)** — exits the pons at the pontine root entry zone, traveling anterosuperiorly to Meckel cave. Classic site of vascular compression by the superior cerebellar artery causing [[trigeminal-neuralgia-mvd]] indication.
- **CN VII / VIII complex** — emerges from the pontomedullary sulcus and travels laterally to the porus acusticus (IAC). CN VII is anterior and superior; CN VIII (cochlear + superior/inferior vestibular) posterior and inferior. This is the surgical theater for [[acoustic-neuroma]] resection.
- **CN IX, X, XI complex** — emerges from the postolivary sulcus and travels laterally to the jugular foramen; vascular compression (posterior inferior cerebellar artery) causes glossopharyngeal neuralgia.
- **CN VI (abducens)** — arises from the medullopontine sulcus and ascends toward the Dorello canal; occasionally encountered at the inferomedial CPA.

## Vessels

- **Anterior inferior cerebellar artery (AICA)** — the defining CPA vessel. Courses laterally from the basilar, loops around or between CN VII–VIII, then gives the labyrinthine (internal auditory) artery before supplying the flocculus and petrosal surface of the cerebellum. Three loop morphologies: premeatal, meatal (into the IAC), postmeatal.
- **Posterior inferior cerebellar artery (PICA)** — arises from the vertebral; wraps around the medulla at the lower cranial nerves.
- **Superior cerebellar artery (SCA)** — separated from CN V by the arachnoid of the cerebellomesencephalic cistern above; commonest offender in [[trigeminal-neuralgia-mvd]].
- **Petrosal vein complex (vein of Dandy)** — superomedial drainage; sacrifice risks venous cerebellar infarction.

## Surgical Approaches

- **[[retromastoid-craniotomy]]** (retrosigmoid) — the universal CPA approach; opens dura behind the sigmoid and below the transverse sinus.
- **Translabyrinthine approach** — sacrifices hearing to gain wide lateral exposure of the IAC and CPA (acoustic neuroma without serviceable hearing).
- **Middle fossa approach** — best for small intracanalicular [[acoustic-neuroma]] with preserved hearing.
- **Far-lateral** — extends caudally for lesions near the foramen magnum; complements CPA access for giant or inferiorly extending tumors.

## Common Pathology

- **[[acoustic-neuroma]]** (vestibular schwannoma) — the prototypical CPA tumor; fills the IAC and expands medially displacing CN VII.
- **CPA meningioma** — arises from posterior petrous dura, displaces rather than splays CN VII/VIII.
- **Epidermoid cyst** — pearly, conforms to spaces, encases neurovascular structures.
- **Arachnoid cyst** — thin-walled, displaces brainstem and nerves.
- **Neurovascular compression syndromes** — trigeminal neuralgia (SCA on V), hemifacial spasm (AICA/PICA on VII), glossopharyngeal neuralgia (PICA on IX).

## Key Points for Surgery

- CSF release from the cerebellomedullary cistern relaxes the cerebellum and minimizes fixed retraction.
- Intraoperative cranial nerve monitoring (VII EMG, BAER, lower cranial nerves) is mandatory for tumor resection.
- Preserve every bridging vein to the petrous dura — especially the vein of Dandy — or the cerebellum will swell.
- Mastoid air cells opened during drilling must be obliterated with bone wax and fat to prevent CSF rhinorrhea/otorrhea.
