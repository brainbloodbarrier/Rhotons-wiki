# Circle of Willis

The circle of Willis (CoW) is the anastomotic polygon of arteries at the base of the brain, named for Thomas Willis (1664). It connects the anterior (carotid) and posterior (vertebrobasilar) circulations via the posterior communicating arteries, and connects the two hemispheres via the anterior communicating artery. Although a "complete" circle exists textbook-style, anatomical variants are present in roughly half of the population. The circle provides hemodynamic collateralization in occlusive disease, and every one of its junctions is a classic locus for saccular aneurysm.

## Constituent Arteries

| Segment | Artery |
|---------|--------|
| Anterior (midline) | A1 segments of anterior cerebral arteries (×2) |
| Anterior bridge | Anterior communicating artery (AComA) |
| Anterolateral (paired) | Supraclinoid internal carotid arteries (×2) |
| Lateral-to-posterior bridge | Posterior communicating arteries (PComA, ×2) |
| Posterior (paired) | P1 segments of posterior cerebral arteries (×2) |
| Posterior (midline) | Basilar apex (tip of basilar artery) |

Perforators arising from the circle include the recurrent artery of Heubner (from A1/A2 junction), medial lenticulostriates (from A1), anterior choroidal (from supraclinoid ICA), premamillary (thalamoperforator) from PComA, and thalamoperforators from P1/basilar tip.

## Anatomical Variants

The "complete" CoW is present in only ~40–50% of brains:

- **Hypoplastic A1** — common; one A1 supplies both A2s via AComA; predisposes to AComA aneurysm.
- **Fetal PCA** — PCA predominantly supplied by PComA rather than basilar (~20–25%); ipsilateral PComA aneurysm risk and failure of posterior-to-anterior collateralization.
- **Absent or hypoplastic PComA** — reduces collateral flow after ICA occlusion.
- **Azygous ACA** — single A2 trunk; rare.
- **Infundibular dilation** at PComA origin — ≤3 mm funnel, not a true aneurysm but monitored.

## Aneurysm Distribution

| Site | Frequency |
|------|-----------|
| Anterior communicating (AComA) | ~30% |
| Posterior communicating (PComA) | ~25% |
| Middle cerebral bifurcation (M1/M2) | ~20% |
| Supraclinoid ICA (ophthalmic, clinoidal, carotid cave) | ~10% |
| Basilar apex / basilar–SCA / basilar–PCA | ~5% |
| Pericallosal / distal ACA | ~4% |
| Vertebral / PICA | ~3% |

Junctions (bifurcations and trifurcations) experience maximal hemodynamic shear and are the loci of saccular aneurysm formation. The surgical discipline of **microsurgical clipping** operates primarily on these loci, while endovascular coiling/flow-diversion has partly displaced open surgery for many CoW aneurysms.

## Collateral Physiology

- Two primary communicating pathways: **interhemispheric** (via AComA) and **anteroposterior** (via PComA).
- Secondary pathways: leptomeningeal collaterals (pial-pial), ophthalmic artery retrograde flow (ECA-to-ICA in ICA occlusion), persistent embryonic anastomoses.
- Balloon test occlusion (BTO) of the ICA assesses adequacy of CoW collateral before carotid sacrifice for skull-base tumor or giant aneurysm.

## Surgical Relevance

- **[[pterional-craniotomy]]** exposes the supraclinoid ICA, proximal MCA, and AComA region through the Sylvian fissure and carotid cistern.
- **[[orbitozygomatic-craniotomy]]** extends the pterional trajectory to basilar apex and contralateral CoW structures.
- **[[bifrontal-craniotomy]]** with interhemispheric dissection reaches AComA and distal ACA.
- **Subtemporal and transcavernous** approaches are classical routes to basilar apex aneurysms.

## Common Pitfalls

- Mistaking **infundibular dilation** for a small aneurysm — overtreatment risk.
- Missing a **fetal PCA** pattern — sacrifice of PComA may produce thalamic infarct.
- Sacrificing the **recurrent artery of Heubner** during AComA surgery — contralateral caudate/capsular infarct, hemiparesis.
- Sacrificing **thalamoperforators** during basilar apex clipping — devastating midbrain/thalamic infarct.
