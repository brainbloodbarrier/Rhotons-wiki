# Infratentorial Operative Anatomy

*NSAtlas Operative Neuroanatomy — Infratentorial Compartment*

**Source:** Cohen-Gadol, cross-referenced with Jackler Atlas of
Neurotology and Rhoton microsurgical anatomy.

## Overview

The infratentorial compartment contains the cerebellum, brainstem
(midbrain, pons, medulla), lower ten cranial nerves (III-XII), and
the vertebrobasilar arterial system with its three neurovascular
complexes. Enclosed by the petrous and occipital bones and
covered by the tentorium cerebelli, it is the most tightly packed
operative region of the skull — a "bony cage filled with eloquent
tissue" where millimetre-scale dissection matters.

The NSAtlas 3D infratentorial model emphasizes the temporal bone
and middle-ear relationships because most posterior fossa skull base
approaches traverse or drill through this bone. See [[3d-anatomy]] for
interaction instructions.

## Bony Framework

- **Petrous temporal bone** — pyramidal bone with apex at the
  dorsum sellae and base at the mastoid. Houses the otic capsule
  (cochlea, vestibule, semicircular canals), internal acoustic
  meatus (IAM), and facial nerve canal.
- **Occipital bone** — clivus (to dorsum sellae), foramen magnum,
  condyles, and squamous portion. Clival dura covers the anterior
  brainstem.
- **Mastoid portion of temporal bone** — pneumatized; key for
  [[retromastoid-craniotomy]] and translabyrinthine exposures.
- **Sigmoid and transverse sinuses** — dural sinuses defining the
  superior and posterolateral limits of retrosigmoid exposure.

See [[skull-anatomy]] for comprehensive osseous description.

## Middle Ear / Facial Nerve Labyrinth

The NSAtlas model highlights:

- **Horizontal (lateral) semicircular canal** — key landmark for
  facial nerve depth in mastoidectomy.
- **Posterior semicircular canal** — posterior limit of otic capsule.
- **Labyrinthine segment of CN VII** — shortest, narrowest portion;
  runs anteriorly from IAM fundus to geniculate ganglion.
- **Geniculate ganglion** — sensory ganglion of CN VII; gives rise
  to the greater superficial petrosal nerve (GSPN).
- **Tympanic (second) segment of CN VII** — above the oval window and
  stapes; turns at the second genu.
- **Mastoid (third, descending) segment of CN VII** — vertical course
  from second genu to stylomastoid foramen.
- **Stylomastoid foramen** — exit of CN VII extracranially.
- **Chorda tympani** — branch of mastoid CN VII conveying taste to
  anterior two-thirds of tongue and parasympathetic to submandibular
  gland; crosses the middle ear between malleus handle and incus
  long process.
- **Tensor tympani muscle, stapes, cochleariform process, round
  window, oval window, tympanic plexus, Eustachian tube** — middle
  ear content relevant to translabyrinthine, transcochlear, and
  middle fossa approaches.

## Cerebellum

Three surfaces (tentorial, suboccipital, petrosal) — relevant to the
classification of cerebellar veins and bridging veins (see
[[cerebrovascular-anatomy]]).

Three lobes:

- **Anterior lobe** — rostral to primary fissure.
- **Posterior lobe** — between primary and posterolateral fissures.
- **Flocculonodular lobe** — vestibular connections; floccular loop
  of AICA.

Deep cerebellar nuclei (medial → lateral): fastigial, globose,
emboliform, dentate. The dentate nucleus is a landmark for
[[telovelar-approach]]-based fourth ventricle tumour surgery.

## Brainstem

- **Midbrain** — cerebral peduncles, tectum (superior/inferior
  colliculi), substantia nigra, red nucleus. Houses nuclei of CN III,
  IV.
- **Pons** — basis pontis, pontine tegmentum. Nuclei of CN V, VI, VII,
  VIII.
- **Medulla** — pyramids, olives. Nuclei of CN IX, X, XI, XII.
- **Fourth ventricle** — rhomboid fossa on the floor; facial colliculus
  and hypoglossal trigone are surface markers over CN VII and CN XII
  nuclei.
- **Safe entry zones** — supracollicular, infracollicular, lateral
  mesencephalic sulcus, peritrigeminal, suprafacial, infrafacial,
  olivary; mapped to minimize deficit in intrinsic brainstem lesions.

## Cranial Nerves (Posterior Fossa)

| CN | Exit/entry | Key relations |
|---|---|---|
| III (oculomotor) | Interpeduncular fossa → cavernous sinus | Between SCA and PCA at emergence |
| IV (trochlear) | Dorsal midbrain below inferior colliculi | Only CN to emerge dorsally; thinnest |
| V (trigeminal) | Lateral pons → Meckel's cave | AICA often loops near root; compression → trigeminal neuralgia |
| VI (abducens) | Pontomedullary junction → Dorello's canal → cavernous sinus | Long intracranial course; vulnerable |
| VII–VIII | Pontomedullary junction → IAM | Enter together; CPA neurovascular bundle |
| IX-X-XI | Postolivary sulcus → jugular foramen | Glomus jugulare tumours distort this complex |
| XII | Preolivary sulcus → hypoglossal canal | Inferior to PICA origin |

## Neurovascular Complexes

Cohen-Gadol and Rhoton's three-tier organisation (see
[[cerebrovascular-anatomy]]):

- **Upper complex** — SCA, superior cerebellar peduncle, CN III/IV,
  mesencephalon. Tentorial surface of cerebellum.
- **Middle complex** — AICA, middle cerebellar peduncle, CN V/VII/VIII,
  pons. Petrosal surface.
- **Lower complex** — PICA, inferior cerebellar peduncle, CN IX/X/XI/XII,
  medulla. Suboccipital surface.

This triad organises approach selection: upper complex via
supracerebellar infratentorial ([[midline-supracerebellar-craniotomy]],
[[paramedian-supracerebellar-craniotomy]]); middle complex via
[[retromastoid-craniotomy]]; lower complex via far-lateral or
[[suboccipital-craniotomy]] with or without [[telovelar-approach]].

## Cisterns of the Posterior Fossa

- **Cerebellopontine (CP) angle cistern** — CN V-XI territory.
- **Cerebellomedullary (magna) cistern** — behind medulla.
- **Premedullary / pontine cistern** — anterior to brainstem.
- **Quadrigeminal cistern** — tentorial incisura, vein of Galen.
- **Ambient / crural cisterns** — lateral midbrain, P2 PCA.

## Surgical Approaches

- **[[retromastoid-craniotomy]]** (retrosigmoid) — workhorse for
  CP angle (vestibular schwannoma, trigeminal microvascular
  decompression, hemifacial spasm).
- **[[suboccipital-craniotomy]]** midline — vermian and fourth
  ventricle access.
- **[[telovelar-approach]]** — natural cerebellomedullary fissure
  corridor to floor of fourth ventricle.
- **[[midline-supracerebellar-craniotomy]]** / **[[paramedian-supracerebellar-craniotomy]]** —
  pineal region, dorsal midbrain.
- **Far-lateral craniotomy** — foramen magnum, vertebral artery, PICA
  origin, anterior brainstem lesions.
- **Translabyrinthine / transcochlear** (neurotology) — petrous apex,
  clivus.

## Operative Pearls

- **Know facial nerve depth relative to horizontal SCC** — the
  tympanic CN VII lies immediately medial and slightly superior; a
  drill slip into the canal = permanent facial weakness.
- **Preserve the superior petrosal vein (Dandy's vein)** whenever
  possible — sacrifice causes variable venous infarction of
  cerebellum/brainstem.
- **AICA loops into the IAM** in ~40% of cases; expect it in the
  superior-anterior quadrant of the meatus during vestibular
  schwannoma surgery.
- **PICA's caudal loop** is the landmark for the tonsillomedullary
  and telovelotonsillar segments; hugs the tonsil and is vulnerable
  to retraction injury.
