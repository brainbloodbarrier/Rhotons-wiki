# Cerebrovascular Anatomy

*NSAtlas Operative Neuroanatomy 3D Series*
*Operative Neurosurgery* 2018;15(6):613-623

**Source:** Cohen-Gadol et al., distilled with Rhoton microsurgical
anatomy cross-reference.

## Overview

Cerebrovascular anatomy divides into two functional arterial systems
(anterior/carotid and posterior/vertebrobasilar) and a venous tree
(superficial, deep, dural sinuses). The two arterial systems
anastomose at the circle of Willis via the posterior communicating
arteries; the two venous systems converge on the dural venous
sinuses and drain principally via the internal jugular veins.

The NSAtlas 3D series renders this as twelve interactive models
(Models 1-12) covering arterial-alone, venous-alone, combined, deep
brain relationships, endonasal perspective, and pathology (callosal
AVM). See [[3d-anatomy]] for the interaction paradigm.

## Anterior (Carotid) Circulation

The anterior circulation is the distal distribution of the internal
carotid artery (ICA). It contains three principal territories: the
ICA itself, the middle cerebral artery (MCA), and the anterior
cerebral artery (ACA).

### ICA Segments (Bouthillier classification — 7 segments, C1-C7)

| Segment | Name | Key relations |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Cervical | Neck — no branches |
| C2 | Petrous | Within petrous temporal bone; horizontal genu |
| C3 | Lacerum | Across foramen lacerum cartilage |
| C4 | Cavernous | Through [[cavernous-sinus-anatomy\|cavernous sinus]]; branches: meningohypophyseal trunk, inferolateral trunk |
| C5 | Clinoid | Between proximal and distal dural rings |
| C6 | Ophthalmic | Origin of ophthalmic artery and superior hypophyseal artery |
| C7 | Communicating | Gives PCoA, anterior choroidal, then bifurcates into ACA/MCA |

### Endonasal ICA Segmentation (Labib et al., 2014)

Alternative classification for endoscopic endonasal surgery:
parapharyngeal, petrous, paraclival, parasellar, paraclinoid, and
intradural segments. Maps the ICA to bony landmarks visible via the
[[microscope-guided-endonasal-transsphenoidal-approach]].

### ACA Segments (A1-A5)

- **A1 (Precommunicating)** — ICA bifurcation to AComA.
- **A2 (Postcommunicating/Infracallosal)** — AComA to rostrum of
  corpus callosum; gives orbitofrontal and frontopolar arteries.
- **A3 (Precallosal)** — curves around genu of corpus callosum.
- **A4 (Supracallosal)** — over the body of corpus callosum.
- **A5 (Postcallosal)** — posterior to the splenium; terminal cortical
  branches include paracentral, superior/inferior internal parietal.

Named branches: pericallosal, callosomarginal, orbitofrontal,
frontopolar; perforators include the recurrent artery of Heubner and
medial lenticulostriate arteries.

### MCA Segments (M1-M4)

- **M1 (Sphenoidal/Horizontal)** — from bifurcation to genu; gives
  lateral lenticulostriate perforators supplying basal ganglia.
- **M2 (Insular)** — within the sylvian fissure over the insula.
- **M3 (Opercular)** — turns laterally over the opercular surface.
- **M4 (Cortical)** — terminal cortical branches (anterior, middle,
  posterior temporal; central; posterior parietal; angular).

## Posterior (Vertebrobasilar) Circulation

Vertebral → basilar → bilateral PCAs, feeding midbrain, pons, medulla,
cerebellum, and occipital/mesial temporal cortex. Three posterior
fossa neurovascular complexes organize the vascular-neural
relationships:

| Complex | Artery | Cerebellar peduncle | Cranial nerves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Upper** | SCA | Superior | CN III, IV |
| **Middle** | AICA | Middle | CN V, VII, VIII |
| **Lower** | PICA | Inferior | CN IX, X, XI, XII |

### PCA (P1-P4)

- **P1 (Peduncular/Precommunicating)** — basilar tip to PCoA junction;
  thalamoperforating arteries.
- **P2 (Ambient)** — around cerebral peduncle through crural and
  ambient cisterns; medial and lateral posterior choroidal arteries.
- **P3 (Quadrigeminal)** — behind midbrain.
- **P4 (Cortical)** — anterior/posterior temporal, parietooccipital,
  calcarine branches.

### SCA, AICA, PICA (segments)

- **SCA (s1-s4):** anterior pontomesencephalic, lateral
  pontomesencephalic, cerebellomesencephalic, cortical.
- **AICA (a1-a4):** anterior pontine, lateral pontine, flocculopeduncular,
  cortical. Loop into internal acoustic meatus is an AICA landmark.
- **PICA (p1-p5):** anterior medullary, lateral medullary,
  tonsillomedullary, telovelotonsillar, cortical. The caudal loop
  near the tonsil is a reference for [[telovelar-approach]] planning.

## Venous System

### Superficial Cerebral Veins (Rhoton's four groups)

Bridging veins drain cortex into the dural sinuses grouped by
territory:

1. **Superior sagittal group** — to the superior sagittal sinus.
2. **Sphenoidal group** — to the sphenoparietal sinus and cavernous
   sinus. The **superficial middle cerebral vein** (superficial Sylvian)
   is the principal vessel.
3. **Tentorial group** — to tentorial, straight, and transverse sinuses.
4. **Falcine group** — to the inferior sagittal sinus and straight
   sinus.

Named anastomotic veins: **Superior Anastomotic Vein of Trolard**
(sylvian ↔ superior sagittal) and **Inferior Anastomotic Vein of
Labbé** (sylvian ↔ transverse sinus). Both are critical to preserve —
loss of Labbé can cause disabling temporal venous infarction, a key
consideration in [[temporal-subtemporal-craniotomy]].

### Deep Cerebral Veins

- **Basal vein of Rosenthal** — drains basal forebrain and
  mesial temporal structures; courses around the midbrain to the
  vein of Galen.
- **Internal cerebral veins** — paired, in the velum interpositum;
  receive thalamostriate, anterior septal, and choroidal tributaries
  at the **venous angle** (foramen of Monro).
- **Great cerebral vein of Galen** — confluence of internal cerebral
  and basal veins; drains to the straight sinus.

### Dural Venous Sinuses

Superior sagittal, inferior sagittal, straight, transverse, sigmoid,
cavernous ([[cavernous-sinus-anatomy]]), superior and inferior petrosal,
sphenoparietal, sphenobasal, sphenopetrosal, occipital. Confluence
(torcular Herophili) at the internal occipital protuberance. Outflow
to bilateral internal jugular veins.

### Posterior Fossa Veins (Rhoton's groups)

Three surfaces (tentorial, suboccipital, petrosal) drain via three
bridging groups:

- **Galenic group** → vein of Galen.
- **Petrosal group** → superior/inferior petrosal sinuses.
- **Tentorial group** → tentorial sinuses → transverse/straight.

Key posterior fossa veins: median anterior medullary, transverse
pontine, superior petrosal vein (of Dandy — classic CPA landmark),
vein of the cerebellopontine fissure, vein of the
cerebellomesencephalic fissure.

## Perforator Territories

- **Lenticulostriate (medial & lateral)** — basal ganglia, internal
  capsule; arise from M1 and A1.
- **Anterior choroidal artery** — optic tract, posterior limb of
  internal capsule, cerebral peduncle, lateral geniculate. Small
  vessel with disproportionate infarct footprint.
- **Thalamoperforators** — from P1 and P2 to thalamus and midbrain.
- **Recurrent artery of Heubner** — from A1/A2 junction to caudate
  head and anterior limb of internal capsule.

## Surgical Relevance

- **Aneurysms** — AComA, PComA, MCA bifurcation, basilar tip, PICA are
  the high-frequency locations. [[pterional-craniotomy]] is the
  classic approach for anterior circulation; basilar tip may require
  orbitozygomatic or [[temporal-subtemporal-craniotomy]].
- **AVMs** — Spetzler-Martin grading based on size, eloquence,
  venous drainage. Model 12 in the NSAtlas series illustrates a
  callosal/periatrial AVM with choroidal feeders.
- **Bypass planning** — STA, OA, and ECA-based grafts depend on
  correct identification of recipient branches along the
  cerebrovascular tree.
- **Venous preservation** — veins of Labbé, Trolard, and the superior
  petrosal vein dictate approach choice and retraction strategy.
