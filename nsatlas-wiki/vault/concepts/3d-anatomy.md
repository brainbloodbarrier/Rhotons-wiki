# 3D Operative Neuroanatomy

*Virtual Operative Neurosurgical Anatomy: Introduction of a New Interactive Paradigm*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol | Series: *Operative Neurosurgery*, 2018+

## Overview

The Neurosurgical Atlas 3D anatomy series is a collection of annotated,
interactive virtual specimens built from detailed cadaveric osteologic
analysis, radiographic reconstructions, and computerized sculpting. The
stated goal is to replace or augment the conversion of 2D schematics
into mental 3D representations — a cognitive burden that has defined
anatomy education since the earliest cerebral illustrations of the
Middle Ages.

Five years of modelling work by the Atlas team produced a library of
virtual specimens covering:

- Cranial osteology ([[skull-anatomy]])
- Cerebrovascular tree, arterial and venous ([[cerebrovascular-anatomy]])
- Parasellar and [[cavernous-sinus-anatomy|cavernous sinus]] contents
- [[supratentorial-operative-anatomy|Supratentorial operative anatomy]]
- [[infratentorial-operative-anatomy|Infratentorial operative anatomy]]
- A virtual neurosurgical operating room simulation

## Historical Context

2D schematic renditions of the ventricles and gyri date to the Middle
Ages. Modern 3D modelling in neurosurgery emerged in the late 1980s
alongside computer graphics and the Visible Human Project (1999). The
pattern since then has been domain-by-domain virtualization:

| Domain | Key early work |
|---|---|
| Temporal lobectomy planning | de Ribaupierre & Wilson, 2012 |
| Aneurysm clipping simulation | Kimura et al., 2009; Shono et al., 2017 |
| Transpetrous approach | Bernardo et al., 2003 |
| Temporal bone dissection | Kockro & Hwang, 2009; Wiet et al., 2002 |
| Cerebrovascular atlas | Nowinski et al., 2011 (7T MRI) |
| Posterior fossa planning | Anil et al., 2007 |

The NSAtlas series positions itself as the first comprehensive
repository spanning all cranial bones plus the major neurovascular
tree rather than a single anatomic region.

## Interaction Paradigm

Each 3D model ships with a standardized control scheme:

- **Rotate camera:** left-click drag / one-finger drag
- **Pan:** right-click drag / two-finger drag / SHIFT + left-click
- **Zoom:** mouse wheel / pinch / double-click to focus, double-click
  background to zoom out
- **Annotations:** toggle via "Select an annotation" → "Show
  annotations" to expose structure labels
- **Visibility layering:** toggle dura, bone, and other structures on
  or off to isolate the region of interest

The full-screen function is recommended for optimal visualization,
especially when inspecting microanatomy (e.g., inner ear, perforators)
that requires aggressive zoom.

## Educational Rationale

Cadaveric dissection remains the gold standard but is cost-, time-,
and specimen-prohibitive. 2D illustrations require trainees to
mentally reconstruct 3D relationships — the exact skill that breaks
down intraoperatively under cognitive load. The 3D virtual
specimens target that gap directly:

1. Unlimited repeat access (no specimen degradation).
2. Arbitrary viewpoints impossible in cadaveric work.
3. Selective transparency (e.g., bony windows over soft tissue).
4. Annotation toggling for recall-testing workflows.
5. Basis for patient-specific 3D-printed physical models
   (craniosynostosis reconstruction, vertebral fixation, aneurysm
   configuration).

## Virtual Operating Room

A separate model (Model 2 in the source series) reconstructs the
neurosurgical OR — table, microscope, surgeon positions, and
equipment — for setup education. Positioning, draping, and
instrument layout are otherwise difficult to teach outside the OR
itself. See [[cranial-approaches-general-principles]] for positioning
fundamentals referenced by the model.

## Relationship to Rhoton Microsurgical Atlas

The NSAtlas 3D series complements rather than replaces the Rhoton
microsurgical anatomy corpus. Rhoton's cadaveric dissection
photographs remain the reference standard for surface landmarks and
fine microsurgical relationships; the NSAtlas 3D models provide
spatial context, layered transparency, and interactive manipulation
that static images cannot. Many NSAtlas figures explicitly credit
Rhoton cadaveric specimens as their anatomical source.

## Use Within This Wiki

Pages in the `concepts/` directory that derive from the 3D series
include inline references to the source models. When a page says
"see Model N", the reader can find the interactive version at
nsatlas.com under the corresponding chapter. The wiki itself stores
only distilled text and descriptions of the models — the interactive
assets live at the source.
