# Cavernous Sinus Anatomy

*Operative Neuroanatomy: Cavernous Sinus*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol (3D Model series) | Cross-reference:
Rhoton Microsurgical Anatomy

## Overview

The cavernous sinus is a paired, irregular venous compartment on
either side of the sella turcica, extending from the superior orbital
fissure anteriorly to the petrous apex and Meckel's cave posteriorly.
It is the most complex venous compartment of the skull base: a
plexus rather than a single lumen, traversed by the intracavernous
(C4) segment of the internal carotid artery and four to five cranial
nerves. Every major ventral skull base operation — pterional,
orbitozygomatic, endonasal transsphenoidal, middle-fossa — must
respect its contents.

## Boundaries

| Wall | Key structures |
|---|---|
| **Lateral** | CN III, IV, V1, V2 running between dural leaves; temporal lobe abuts externally |
| **Medial** | Sella turcica, pituitary gland, sphenoid body |
| **Superior (roof)** | Dural reflection forming oculomotor (Paulus) and carotid triangles; anterior and posterior clinoid processes |
| **Inferior (floor)** | Greater wing of sphenoid, foramen ovale, foramen rotundum |
| **Anterior** | Superior orbital fissure — exit of CN III, IV, V1, VI, superior ophthalmic vein |
| **Posterior** | Petrous apex, dorsum sellae, Meckel's cave |

## Neural Contents

Within the lateral wall (from superior to inferior):

1. **Oculomotor nerve (CN III)** — entering via the oculomotor triangle
   in the roof, running forward to the superior orbital fissure.
2. **Trochlear nerve (CN IV)** — thinnest cranial nerve; enters roof
   lateral to CN III and crosses over CN III anteriorly.
3. **Ophthalmic division of trigeminal (CN V1)** — lies below CN IV in
   the lateral wall, carries sympathetic/parasympathetic fibres to
   orbit.
4. **Maxillary division (CN V2)** — transient occupant of the
   inferolateral cavernous space before exiting via foramen rotundum.

Within the venous lumen itself, free in the sinus rather than between
dural leaves:

5. **Abducens nerve (CN VI)** — crosses over the carotid artery laterally
   and runs toward the superior orbital fissure; uniquely intrasinusoidal.

## Vascular Contents

- **Cavernous (C4) segment of the ICA.** Enters posteroinferiorly
  through the foramen lacerum region, ascends, turns forward as the
  horizontal cavernous segment, then turns upward as the anterior
  vertical segment to exit through the proximal dural ring as the
  clinoid (C5) segment. Two major branches:
  - *Meningohypophyseal trunk* — tentorial (of Bernasconi-Cassinari),
    dorsal meningeal, and inferior hypophyseal branches.
  - *Inferolateral trunk (ILT)* — supplies CN III, IV, VI and the
    Gasserian ganglion.
- **Basilar venous plexus** — interconnects both cavernous sinuses
  across the midline via the anterior and posterior intercavernous
  sinuses.
- **Superior ophthalmic vein** — major anterior tributary from orbit.
- **Superior and inferior petrosal sinuses** — posterior drainage to
  the sigmoid sinus and jugular bulb respectively.
- **Sphenoparietal sinus** — anterior drainage along the lesser
  sphenoid wing.

## Surgical Triangles

Classic neurosurgical "triangles" of Parkinson, Dolenc, and Hakuba
divide the cavernous sinus roof and lateral wall into safe corridors
defined by adjacent cranial nerves and dural edges. The key ones:

- **Anteromedial (Dolenc) triangle** — between CN III and CN IV;
  entry for intra-cavernous aneurysm surgery.
- **Parkinson's triangle** — between CN IV and CN V1; classic access
  for carotid-cavernous fistulas.
- **Anterolateral (Mullan) triangle** — between CN V1 and V2;
  access for ILT lesions.
- **Glasscock's and Kawase's triangles** — inferolateral and posterior
  extensions into the middle fossa floor and petrous apex.

## Surgical Relevance

The cavernous sinus is a target region for:

- **Meningiomas** — cavernous, petroclival, sphenocavernous; often
  managed with subtotal resection plus radiosurgery due to CN
  morbidity risk.
- **Intracavernous ICA aneurysms** — rarely ruptured but compress
  CN III-VI; endovascular flow diversion is first-line.
- **Trigeminal schwannomas** — may occupy middle-fossa and posterior
  fossa components via Meckel's cave.
- **Carotid-cavernous fistulas (CCF)** — direct (post-traumatic) and
  indirect (dural) arteriovenous shunts presenting with
  pulsatile exophthalmos, chemosis, and bruit.
- **Pituitary macroadenomas with parasellar extension** — approached
  via [[microscope-guided-endonasal-transsphenoidal-approach]];
  lateral extension into the cavernous sinus is a Knosp-grade
  correlate of incomplete resection.

## Approaches

- **[[pterional-craniotomy]]** with [[cranial-approaches-general-principles|extended pterional]]
  modifications — workhorse for parasellar access, optionally with
  anterior clinoidectomy to open the optic-carotid and carotid-oculomotor
  triangles.
- **Orbitozygomatic craniotomy** — adds inferior working angle for
  tumours extending into middle fossa floor.
- **[[microscope-guided-endonasal-transsphenoidal-approach]]** —
  medial access to the intracavernous ICA, Knosp 3-4 adenomas.
- **Middle fossa / anterior petrosectomy (Kawase)** — posterior
  cavernous sinus and petroclival extension.

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Do not assume a single lumen.** The cavernous sinus is a
  venous plexus; dissection produces venous bleeding at multiple
  levels controlled with Surgicel or Floseal, not suture.
- **CN VI is the most vulnerable passenger** — intrasinusoidal,
  not protected between dural leaves. Lost first in medial
  intracavernous dissection.
- **The meningohypophyseal trunk** is a landmark for the bend of the
  cavernous ICA and a source of incidental tumour blush on
  angiography.
- **Knosp grade** on preoperative MRI predicts endonasal resectability
  of parasellar pituitary disease. Grade 4 (ICA encasement) is the
  usual reason for subtotal endonasal resection.
