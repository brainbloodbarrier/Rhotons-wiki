# Supratentorial Operative Anatomy

*NSAtlas Operative Neuroanatomy — Supratentorial Compartment*

**Source:** Cohen-Gadol interactive 3D model series (skull base
foramina focus), cross-referenced with Rhoton microsurgical anatomy.

## Overview

The supratentorial compartment contains the cerebral hemispheres, the
diencephalon (thalamus, hypothalamus, subthalamic structures), and the
pineal region down to the tentorial notch. It is bounded by the
calvaria superiorly and laterally, the anterior and middle cranial
fossae inferiorly, and the tentorium cerebelli posteroinferiorly.
Nearly every workhorse cranial approach — [[pterional-craniotomy]],
[[bifrontal-craniotomy]], [[interhemispheric-craniotomy]],
[[temporal-subtemporal-craniotomy]],
[[parasagittal-craniotomy]] — operates within this compartment.

The NSAtlas 3D model for supratentorial operative anatomy
emphasizes **skull base foramina**: the bony gateways through which
cranial nerves, arteries, and veins enter and leave the supratentorial
compartment.

## Anterior Cranial Fossa

Floor bounded by frontal bone (orbital plates), ethmoid (cribriform
plate + crista galli), and lesser wing of sphenoid. Contains:

- **Olfactory grooves and cribriform plate** — CN I transmission;
  floor of olfactory bulb. Meningioma hotspot.
- **Crista galli** — anterior falx attachment.
- **Planum sphenoidale** — smooth sphenoid body superior surface.
- **Foramen cecum** — variable emissary vein to superior sagittal
  sinus.
- **Lesser sphenoid wings** — terminate in anterior clinoid
  processes; optic canals at medial end.

Approaches: [[supraorbital-craniotomy]], [[bifrontal-craniotomy]],
extended [[pterional-craniotomy]], endonasal transcribriform.

## Middle Cranial Fossa

Paired; bounded by greater wings of sphenoid (anteriorly), squamous
and petrous temporal bone (laterally, posteriorly), and sphenoid
body (medially). Contains:

- **Sella turcica** — pituitary gland, dorsum sellae, clinoid
  processes. See [[cavernous-sinus-anatomy]] for parasellar contents.
- **Temporal lobe** — over middle fossa floor.
- **Key foramina** (greater wing of sphenoid):
  - *Optic canal* — CN II, ophthalmic artery.
  - *Superior orbital fissure* — CN III, IV, V1, VI, superior
    ophthalmic vein.
  - *Foramen rotundum* — V2.
  - *Foramen ovale* — V3, accessory meningeal artery.
  - *Foramen spinosum* — middle meningeal vessels.
  - *Foramen lacerum* — cartilaginous, overlies ICA petrous-cavernous
    transition.
- **Meckel's cave** — trigeminal ganglion dural pouch at petrous apex.

Approaches: [[pterional-craniotomy]],
[[temporal-subtemporal-craniotomy]], middle fossa,
orbitozygomatic, Kawase anterior petrosectomy.

## Sellar / Parasellar Region

Hub of anterior skull base surgery. Boundaries and contents:

- **Floor:** sphenoid sinus (corridor for
  [[microscope-guided-endonasal-transsphenoidal-approach]]).
- **Roof:** diaphragma sellae with pituitary stalk aperture.
- **Lateral walls:** medial walls of cavernous sinuses.
- **Anterior:** tuberculum sellae, chiasmatic sulcus, optic chiasm.
- **Posterior:** dorsum sellae with posterior clinoid processes.
- **Contents:** pituitary gland, intercavernous venous connections,
  superior hypophyseal arteries, superior/inferior intercavernous
  sinuses.

Knosp grading of cavernous sinus invasion (see
[[cavernous-sinus-anatomy]]) predicts endonasal resection
completeness for macroadenomas.

## Sylvian Fissure and Insula

- **Sylvian fissure** — lateral cerebral fissure separating frontal
  and parietal lobes from temporal lobe. Superficial and deep
  (Sylvian cistern) compartments. Opened in
  [[pterional-craniotomy]] and trans-sylvian approaches to
  insula, basal ganglia, and thalamus.
- **Insula** — buried cortex; central sulcus of insula separates
  anterior (short) and posterior (long) gyri. Covered by opercula
  (frontal, parietal, temporal).
- **M1–M2 MCA** (see [[cerebrovascular-anatomy]]) traverse the
  fissure with lenticulostriate perforators arising from M1 —
  do not sacrifice during aneurysm or insular tumour surgery.

## Superficial Venous Relationships

- **Superior sagittal sinus (SSS)** — midline, within falx; draining
  parasagittal bridging veins. Respected in
  [[parasagittal-craniotomy]] and [[interhemispheric-craniotomy]].
  Injury in anterior third may be tolerated; posterior third usually
  not.
- **Vein of Trolard (superior anastomotic)** — sylvian ↔ SSS.
- **Vein of Labbé (inferior anastomotic)** — sylvian ↔ transverse
  sinus; critical in [[temporal-subtemporal-craniotomy]].
- **Superficial middle cerebral vein** — over sylvian fissure.
- **Sphenoparietal sinus** — along lesser sphenoid wing.

## Deep Structures Accessible From Above

- **Corpus callosum** — rostrum, genu, body, splenium.
  [[interhemispheric-craniotomy]] and
  [[contralateral-interhemispheric-transfalcine-transprecuneus-approach]]
  approach callosal and thalamic/pulvinar targets via falcine or
  transcallosal routes.
- **Lateral ventricles** — frontal horn, body, atrium, occipital and
  temporal horns. Foramen of Monro → third ventricle.
- **Third ventricle** — thalami lateral walls, hypothalamus floor,
  pineal recess posteriorly.
- **Thalamus / basal ganglia** — trans-sylvian-transinsular or
  transcallosal corridors.
- **Pineal region** — accessed supratentorially via occipital
  transtentorial ([[occipital-craniotomy]]) or infratentorially via
  supracerebellar ([[midline-supracerebellar-craniotomy]]).

## Cranial Nerves in the Supratentorial Compartment

- **CN I** — olfactory bulb/tract along olfactory groove.
- **CN II** — optic nerves, chiasm, tracts. Chiasmatic
  sulcus/prefixed vs postfixed chiasm variations are critical for
  pituitary surgery corridor choice.
- **CN III** — between PCA and SCA at midbrain, through cavernous
  sinus, to superior orbital fissure.
- **CN IV** — emerges dorsally from midbrain; longest
  intracranial course; reaches cavernous sinus.
- **CN V** — ganglion in Meckel's cave (at junction of middle/
  posterior fossa); V1/V2/V3 branches through superior orbital
  fissure, foramen rotundum, foramen ovale.
- **CN VI** — through Dorello's canal to cavernous sinus (see
  [[cavernous-sinus-anatomy]]).

## Arterial Relationships

Anterior circulation (see [[cerebrovascular-anatomy]] for full
segmental detail):

- **ICA** — cervical → petrous → cavernous → clinoid → ophthalmic →
  communicating → bifurcation into ACA/MCA.
- **ACA (A1-A5)** — crosses above chiasm, through interhemispheric
  fissure.
- **MCA (M1-M4)** — in sylvian fissure; M1 lenticulostriate
  perforators to basal ganglia.
- **PCoA** — connecting ICA → P1 PCA; anterior choroidal artery
  origin distal to PCoA.
- **Anterior choroidal artery** — supplies optic tract, posterior limb
  internal capsule, choroid plexus of temporal horn; small calibre,
  large functional footprint.

Posterior circulation contributes via PCAs (supratentorial extension
through tentorial incisura) — P2-P4 courses around midbrain to
occipital lobe. Tentorial incisura is the supra-infra-tentorial
transition zone.

## Operative Pearls

- **Map the chiasm before opening the sella.** A prefixed chiasm
  limits the suprasellar working angle from above; postfixed chiasm
  makes endonasal access harder.
- **Preserve lenticulostriate perforators** on the M1 superior
  surface during aneurysm dissection — their infarcts are
  catastrophic.
- **Bridging veins dictate retraction.** Sacrificing a vein of
  Trolard, Labbé, or a large parasagittal bridging vein causes
  venous infarction more often than corresponding arterial
  ischaemia.
- **Sylvian fissure opening technique** — superficial arachnoid from
  frontal side first, then deep dissection along M2-M1 backward to
  bifurcation. Pragmatic rule: "follow the artery, don't cut the
  vein."
