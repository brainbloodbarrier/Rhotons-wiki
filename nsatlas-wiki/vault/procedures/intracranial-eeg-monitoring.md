# Intracranial EEG Monitoring

*Epilepsy Surgery — Intracranial Monitoring Study*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Indication

Used when noninvasive workup (video-EEG, MRI, PET, SPECT, MEG, fMRI) fails to localize the seizure onset zone (SOZ) or when resection planning requires delineation from eloquent cortex.

## Modality Selection

| Feature | Subdural grids/strips | Stereo-EEG (SEEG) |
|---|---|---|
| Sampling | Cortical surface | Deep + cortical |
| Deep structures | Limited | Excellent (mesial temporal, insula, cingulate, SMA) |
| Bilateral | Requires bilateral craniotomies | Single or bilateral small burr holes |
| Morbidity | Higher (hematoma, infection) | Lower |
| Functional mapping | Robust cortical stimulation | Limited to electrode contacts |
| Resection guidance | 2D/3D map | Volumetric |

Current trend: **SEEG for most cases**; grids for large hemispheric mapping or functional cortex adjacency.

## Preoperative Planning

- Multidisciplinary epilepsy conference formulates the **implantation hypothesis** — specific network targets per electrode
- 3D MRI, angiography (avoid vessels on trajectories), coregistration
- For SEEG: plan 10–16 electrodes on a stereotactic frame or robotic guidance
- For grids: plan craniotomy large enough to expose all hypothesized SOZ and functional regions
- Antiepileptics often tapered peri-implant to induce seizures

## SEEG Implantation

1. Frame or frameless robotic registration (Mayfield; see [[skull-clamp-placement]])
2. Plan trajectories — avoid vessels, sulci, ventricles
3. Small skin incision + twist-drill 2.1 mm burr hole per electrode
4. Insert anchor bolt; pass electrode to planned depth
5. Confirm with intraop fluoro or postop CT
6. Connect to externalized cable leads; transfer to epilepsy monitoring unit

## Subdural Grid / Strip Implantation

### Positioning and Craniotomy

- Mayfield; plan craniotomy to expose the hypothesized region (typically frontotemporal or parietotemporal)
- See [[burr-holes-and-bone-flaps]], [[scalp-incisions]], [[dural-opening-and-closure]]

### Dural Opening

- Wide cruciate or U-shaped opening
- Preserve bridging veins

### Electrode Placement

- 1×4, 1×6, 1×8 strips through narrow dural openings along gyral long axis
- 4×4, 4×8, 8×8 grids laid flat against cortex
- Secure to dura with sutures to prevent migration
- Tunnel leads through separate stab incisions away from main wound
- Secure leads to skin to prevent dislodgement

### Closure

- Water-tight dural closure around tunneled leads
- Bone flap replaced loosely (no fixation in some centers — facilitates removal)
- Layered scalp closure

## Extraoperative Monitoring

- Transfer to EMU for 5–14 days
- Capture spontaneous seizures — localize SOZ
- Extraoperative functional mapping: cortical stimulation for motor, language, sensory
- Generate resection map integrating SOZ and functional boundaries

## Explant / Resection Stage

- Return to OR after sufficient data captured
- Remove grids/strips or SEEG electrodes
- For grids: typically combined with definitive resection in same anesthesia
- For SEEG: often staged — explant first, plan definitive resection at second admission
- Wound inspection; watertight dural closure; standard bone flap replacement

## Complications

- Hemorrhage (subdural, intraparenchymal) — 1–5% grids, <1% SEEG
- Infection — 2–8% grids, <2% SEEG
- CSF leak
- Electrode migration
- Post-explant re-implantation rare; multidisciplinary reassessment first

## Postoperative Considerations

- Antibiotics throughout monitoring period
- Close neuro checks for hemorrhage
- Scalp tension management — leads pull, cause breakdown
- Explicit discussion with family: monitoring is the tool, not the cure — definitive resection follows

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- Implantation without a clear hypothesis = expensive noise
- SEEG is replacing grids for most modern epilepsy programs
- Plan trajectories meticulously on vascular imaging
- Tunnel leads away from main incision to reduce infection
- Secure to skin + dura — migration destroys the study
- Stimulate for functional mapping extraoperatively at bedside for awake kids/adults
- Seize the opportunity — taper AEDs to provoke typical seizures
- Combine explant + resection in grids; stage for SEEG
