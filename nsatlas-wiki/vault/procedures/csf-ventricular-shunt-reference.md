# CSF Ventricular Shunt Reference (Procedures Catalog)

*CSF Diversion Procedures — Shunt Valve Reference*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

This page captures operative-side valve selection and re-programming considerations. For the general pressure-physics primer and X-ray identification tables, see [[ventricular-shunt-reference]].

## Valve Selection Logic

| Clinical scenario | Preferred class |
|---|---|
| Standard adult VP | Programmable differential-pressure ± gravitational |
| Pediatric VP | Low-pressure programmable (e.g., Strata, Certas Plus) |
| Ambulatory adult, concern for overdrainage | Programmable + anti-siphon / gravitational unit |
| VPl shunt | **Mandatory** gravitational or anti-siphon (pleural negative pressure) |
| NPH | Programmable, aggressive downward titration in follow-up |
| VA shunt | Fixed / programmable; avoid valves that require supine orientation |

## Aesculap

### proGAV 2.0

- Dual-unit: programmable differential-pressure + integrated gravitational component
- Programming: 0–20 cm H₂O in 1 cm steps
- MR-safe to 3 T with manufacturer tool; requires post-MRI verification

### proSA Gravitational Unit

- Add-on gravitational (position-dependent) resistor
- Combines with any differential-pressure valve to counter overdrainage when upright
- Essential partner for VPl systems

## Codman

### Certas Plus Valve

- Programmable; 8 pressure settings (plus virtual off)
- Non-invasive magnetic programmer
- MR-safe; post-MRI verification recommended
- Integrated Bactiseal (antibiotic-impregnated silicone) variant

## Medtronic

### PS Medical CSF-Flow Control Valve

- Fixed differential-pressure
- Small, low-profile; used when programming not required

### Delta Valve

- Fixed differential + integrated anti-siphon (ASD)
- Durable, predictable for straightforward adult VP

### Strata II Valve

- Programmable (5 settings)
- Magnetic programmer
- Requires post-MRI verification

### Strata NSC Valve

- "Non-Siphon Control" + programmable
- Combines programmability with anti-siphon — preferred when posture-related overdrainage suspected

### Strata MR Valve

- Updated version, MR-conditional to 3 T (reduced inadvertent reprogramming risk vs Strata II)

## Sophysa

### Polaris Valve

- Programmable (5 settings)
- Magnetic-lock mechanism — resists reprogramming from external MRI fields
- Post-MRI verification still recommended

## Programming Workflow

1. Confirm valve brand and model (shunt series X-ray if unknown)
2. Assemble manufacturer-specific programmer tool
3. Set patient supine, head of bed flat
4. Position programmer over the valve; capture current setting
5. Adjust to target setting in 1–2 cm H₂O increments based on clinical response
6. **Verify** with repeat read-back
7. Document setting pre- and post-any MRI exposure

## MR Safety Quick Reference

- **All programmable valves require post-MRI verification** — assume drift
- Magnetic-lock (Polaris) reduces but does not eliminate drift
- Document pre-scan setting in chart
- Reprogrammer must be available in radiology suite for immediate rescue
- Emergency fallback: if setting drifts and patient symptomatic, reprogram immediately; consider CT to exclude overdrainage hematoma

## Anti-Siphon and Gravitational Units

| Device class | Mechanism | When required |
|---|---|---|
| Anti-siphon device (ASD) | Flow-resistance element closes at high transmural ΔP | Upright postural overdrainage |
| Gravitational valve | Ball-weight gradient adds resistance upright | Tall ambulatory adults; VPl shunts |
| Programmable + gravitational combo (proGAV, Strata NSC) | Both mechanisms | Adults with recurrent overdrainage |

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- VPl shunt with a plain differential-pressure valve → slit ventricle syndrome; use gravitational
- Programmable valves drift in strong magnetic fields — verify after every MRI
- Know the valve you are connecting; re-programmers are brand-specific
- In kids, bake in room for growth — avoid valves that require orientation shifts
- Document valve serial / lot in operative note — simplifies recall management
