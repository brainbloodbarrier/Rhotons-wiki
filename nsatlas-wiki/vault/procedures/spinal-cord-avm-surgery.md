# Spinal Cord AVM Surgery

*Spinal Cord Surgery — Spinal Cord AVM*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Classification (Spetzler-Detwiler)

| Type | Description | Treatability |
|---|---|---|
| I | Dural AVF | See [[spinal-av-fistula-disconnection]] |
| II | **Glomus intramedullary AVM** — compact nidus | Surgical (sometimes staged embo) |
| III | **Juvenile / metameric** — diffuse, large, cord + paraspinal | Poor; palliative / partial embo |
| IV | **Perimedullary AVF** — pial surface fistula | Surgical; sub-classified IVa/b/c by size |
| V | Conus AVM | Multimodal (cauda equina involved) |

## Presentation

- **Acute**: subarachnoid or intramedullary hemorrhage (SAH symptoms + cord deficit)
- **Chronic**: progressive myelopathy from venous hypertension or steal
- Pediatric patients present with acute hemorrhage more often than adults

## Preoperative Evaluation

- **MRI** — cord signal, flow voids, hemosiderin from prior bleed
- **Spinal angiography** — defines feeders, nidus, venous drainage
- Functional baseline (ASIA, Aminoff-Logue)
- Decide: surgical, endovascular, combined, radiosurgical, conservative

## Treatment Strategy by Type

### Type II (Glomus Intramedullary)

- Pre-op embolization of easily accessed feeders to reduce intraoperative bleeding
- Surgical resection: posterior midline laminectomy at level
- Midline myelotomy (see [[operative-spinal-cord-anatomy]])
- Identify nidus; circumferential dissection in gliotic plane
- Devascularize systematically — feeders first, draining vein last
- Complete resection curative; subtotal = continued risk

### Type III (Juvenile / Metameric)

- Usually **inoperable** — diffuse, no plane
- Palliative embolization of high-risk feeders
- Symptom control, not cure
- Counsel families honestly

### Type IV (Perimedullary AVF)

- **IVa** (small pial feeder): microsurgical clipping/disconnection at the surface
- **IVb** (medium): embolization ± surgical disconnection
- **IVc** (giant, multiple feeders): combined approach, often staged
- Unlike Type I, the shunt is **intradural at the cord surface**

### Type V (Conus)

- Multimodal; cauda equina involvement limits aggressive resection
- Preserve root function

## Positioning and Exposure

- Prone, neutral head in Mayfield for cervical levels
- Laminectomy at level + one above/below
- Level marked intraop with fluoro + ICG confirmation

## Microsurgical Resection Principles

- **IONM required**: MEPs, SSEPs, D-wave
- Intraoperative **ICG angiography** multiple times — map the shunt, confirm devascularization
- Open pia sharply along planned corridor
- **Feeder-first** strategy — occlude arterial supply before approaching draining vein
- **Never sacrifice draining vein first** — catastrophic engorgement and hemorrhage
- Bipolar at lowest effective setting; irrigation continuous
- Temporary clips for test-occlusion before permanent sacrifice

## Intraoperative Hemorrhage Management

- Large-bore suctions available
- Cottonoid tamponade; packing; systemic BP support
- Have blood products available before skin incision

## Closure and Postoperative Care

- Watertight dural closure
- Flat bedrest 24 h
- Postoperative angiography at 1–3 months to confirm obliteration
- Neurologic baseline usually worsens transiently then recovers toward preop
- MRI annually

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Angio-define the lesion** before any skin incision
- **Pre-op embo** reduces intraoperative morbidity for Type II / IVc
- **Feeders first, vein last** — non-negotiable
- ICG angiography throughout — real-time shunt map
- Type III is rarely operable — set expectations
- Acute hemorrhagic presentation = higher re-bleed risk → treat
- IONM mandatory; stop on sustained MEP / D-wave drop
- Adamkiewicz at thoracolumbar junction — never sacrifice feeding radiculomedullary artery
