# Spinal AV Fistula Disconnection

*Spinal Cord Surgery — Spinal Arteriovenous Fistula Disconnection*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Clinical Context

Spinal dural arteriovenous fistulas (sDAVF / Type I spinal vascular malformations) are the most common spinal vascular lesion in adults. Pathophysiology: abnormal direct shunt from a **radicular artery** to a **radicular vein** at the dural nerve root sleeve → arterialization of **coronal venous plexus** on the cord surface → chronic venous hypertension → progressive myelopathy (**Foix-Alajouanine syndrome**).

Patient profile: middle-aged man, progressive leg weakness, sensory changes, bowel/bladder, worsened by exercise or upright posture.

## Classification (Spetzler)

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| I | Dural AVF (most common, 70%) — extradural arterial feed, intradural venous drainage |
| II | Glomus intramedullary AVM — compact nidus within cord |
| III | Juvenile / metameric AVM — diffuse, large, poor outcomes |
| IV | Perimedullary AVF — pial fistula on cord surface |

This chapter focuses on **Type I** disconnection.

## Preoperative Evaluation

- **MRI**: T2 cord hyperintensity, dilated perimedullary flow voids
- **Spinal angiography**: gold standard — identifies feeding radicular artery, fistula point at nerve root sleeve, draining intradural vein
- **Rule out Adamkiewicz** at or near the level — critical
- Neurologic baseline (Aminoff-Logue scale for gait/micturition)
- Consent: endovascular alternative (selective glue/onyx embolization of the fistula)

## Endovascular vs Surgical

- Endovascular: durable if the entire shunt reached with embolic cast; recanalization 15–30%
- **Surgical disconnection**: >95% cure; low morbidity if technique clean
- Combined endovascular first-pass with surgical rescue for persistent shunt

## Positioning

- Prone on Wilson frame or chest rolls
- Neutral head
- Level marked with fluoro before draping

## Exposure

- Midline incision over the fistula level (±1 above/below)
- Subperiosteal dissection
- Hemilaminectomy or laminectomy at involved level
- Partial medial facetectomy if fistula lateral at root sleeve

## Dural Opening

- Longitudinal midline durotomy
- Identify the draining intradural vein — **arterialized, red, tortuous** on cord dorsum
- Trace back to the dural root sleeve at the affected level
- Confirm with intraoperative indocyanine green (**ICG**) angiography — fistula fills early, cord veins washout shifted

## Disconnection

1. Identify the intradural draining vein at the root sleeve entry
2. **Temporary clip** — confirm that the fistula washout changes (ICG repeat or color change)
3. **Bipolar coagulate** the vein at its dural entry
4. **Divide** the vein sharply
5. Repeat ICG angiography — confirm **no residual shunt**

Single-point disconnection cures the fistula. Resection of the dural nidus is NOT required.

## Neuromonitoring

- MEPs, SSEPs — track during exposure and disconnection
- Any drop → stop, reassess, warm saline
- D-wave for direct motor pathway monitoring

## Closure

- Watertight dural closure
- Laminar replacement as laminoplasty when possible
- Layered closure

See [[dural-opening-and-closure]], [[suturing-and-closure]].

## Postoperative Considerations

- Flat 24 h
- Neurologic checks Q1h × 24 h
- Steroids taper
- **Postoperative spinal angiography** at 1–3 months confirms disconnection
- MRI — edema resolves over months
- Gait training, bladder rehab

## Outcomes

- Gait: ~60% improve, ~30% stabilize, ~10% worsen (usually pre-op severely affected)
- Bladder: less responsive to disconnection — counsel patients realistically
- Recurrence: rare after confirmed surgical disconnection

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Angio confirms the level** — do not rely on MRI alone
- **Single-point disconnection** at dural nerve root sleeve is curative
- **ICG angiography pre- and post-clip** — real-time cure confirmation
- Preserve Adamkiewicz — never sacrifice a feeding radiculomedullary artery
- Endovascular first in select anatomy; surgery for tough anatomy or failed embo
- Operate before the myelopathy becomes fixed — gait preservation is time-dependent
- **Bladder recovery lags motor** — manage expectations
