# Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy (ETV)

*CSF Diversion Procedures — Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v12.6*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol / Fulkerson / Ahmed

## Indication and Patient Selection

ETV creates a passage between the third ventricle and the prepontine cistern for treatment of **obstructive hydrocephalus**. It is an alternative to shunt placement and avoids shunt-related infection (5–8%) and early mechanical failure (~50% within 2 years in children).

### ETV Success Score (ETVSS)

| Points | Age | Etiology | Previous Shunt |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | <1 mo | Postinfection | Yes |
| 10 | 1 to <6 mo | — | No |
| 20 | — | Myelomeningocele, IVH, nontectal brain tumor | — |
| 30 | 6 mo to <1 y | Aqueductal stenosis, tectal tumor, other | — |
| 40 | 1 to <10 y | — | — |
| 50 | ≥10 y | — | — |

Score (age + etiology + previous shunt) approximates the probability of being shunt-free 6 months after ETV.

## Advantages vs Shunt

- Infection risk ~1% (vs 5–8% for shunts)
- Durable: after 1 shunt-free year, long-term failure is uncommon — survival curve flattens
- No implanted hardware, no overdrainage, no revision cascade

## Disadvantages and Complications

- Overall complication rate ~8.5%; permanent morbidity ~3%
- Hemorrhage severe enough to abort ~4%; stroke/death <1% in experienced hands
- Specific injuries: diabetes insipidus, memory loss (fornix), hemiparesis, hypothalamic dysfunction, seizures, CSF leak
- Most feared: **injury to basilar artery, its perforators, or the internal cerebral vein**

## Preoperative Considerations

- Analyze ventricular anatomy: **massa intermedia size** (enlarged in spina bifida blocks trajectory), tumor distortion
- Measure **dorsum sellae–basilar space** — a basilar hugging bone is a relative contraindication
- Plan trajectory from scalp entry through [[foramen-of-monro|foramen of Monro]] to third ventricle floor
- For pineal-region masses requiring biopsy: consider two burr holes (separate trajectories) or flexible endoscope; **perform ETV before biopsy** to avoid blood obscuring the view

## Positioning and Setup

- Supine, head elevated ~15°, neck slightly flexed, neutral rotation
- Head at the surgeon's elbow level for ergonomics
- Head 180° from anesthesia; navigation and endoscope monitors aligned with surgeon sight line
- Foley and arterial line (BP/fluid lability during floor manipulation)
- Neuronavigation registration before incision

See [[patient-positioning]] and [[operating-room-setup-and-workflow]] for general principles.

## Incision and Burr Hole

- Entry point lines up **scalp → foramen of Monro → third ventricle floor**
- Anterior to the coronal suture; right side preferred absent a contrary reason
- **Linear incision** allows anterior/posterior extension; **curved incision** preserves future shunt placement
- Single burr hole with wide dural cruciate opening; undercut inner table with Kerrison/curette if skull thick — allows endoscope angulation
- Coagulate dura and pia; open pia with 11-blade; small corticotomy

See [[burr-holes-and-bone-flaps]] for drill and dural-separation technique.

## Ventricular Cannulation

- **Peel-away sheath + navigation stylet** preferred (minimizes endoscope trauma, preserves access if bleeding obscures vision)
- Advance under navigation until **tactile "pop"** — **do not advance further**
- Position sheath just beyond ependyma, secure to scalp with staples
- Remove stylet, insert rigid endoscope
- White-balance and verify orientation on a suture pack before insertion
- Use **balanced irrigation solution** — tap water / saline imbalance causes hypothalamic injury

## Endoscope Handling

- **Both hands always.** Nondominant hand on scalp, endoscope shaft between thumb and index; dominant hand on camera in **pistol grip**
- Short, smooth, controlled movements — monocular vision defeats depth perception if rushed
- Endoscope **behind** the instrument tip at all times
- Remove scope fully from head when handing off between surgeons

## Intraventricular Anatomy

Identify the **correct (right) ventricle** before advancing — do not assume.

- **Choroid plexus** — most conspicuous landmark
- **Thalamostriate vein** — lateral to choroid; confirms laterality
- **Septal vein** — medial; joins thalamostriate at venous angle = **foramen of Monro**
- **Fornix** anteriorly, **anterior thalamus** posteriorly bound the foramen

Advance through the foramen. In the third ventricle:

- **Lamina terminalis** anterior/rostral
- **Optic chiasm** — horizontal white/yellow band
- **Infundibular recess** — red/brown midline dimple → pituitary stalk (always midline, always visible = key landmark)
- **Tuber cinereum** — thinnest part of floor, target for perforation
- **Mammillary bodies** just posterior
- **Premamillary recess** — downward bulging floor in severe hydrocephalus

## Floor Perforation (Stoma Creation)

1. **Perforation point**: midline, just anterior to mammillary bodies, through tuber cinereum
2. **Tool**: blunt EVD stylet (fits working channel; blunt tip protects perforators) or endoscopic grasping forceps
3. **Enlargement**: Fogarty balloon inflation, or opened grasping forceps withdrawn without closing (grabbing a perforator is disastrous)
4. **Two layers** must be breached: **tuber cinereum** + **membrane of Liliequist** — failing to cross Liliequist is a common cause of procedure failure
5. Confirmation: **unobstructed view of basilar artery** in prepontine cistern; floor pulsates and flaps freely
6. Stoma as large as feasible without encroaching on mammillary bodies, infundibular recess, or lateral walls
7. **Minimize irrigation** — over-irrigation causes hypothalamic electrolyte injury; minor floor bleeding stops spontaneously

## Hemostasis and Closure

- Inspect fornix for injury during withdrawal
- Small venous bleeding → irrigate until clear; avoid blind cautery
- Severe bleeding: pass EVD off-stylet through peel-away sheath, **abort procedure**
- Gelfoam over cortical defect; burr hole cover if visible under hair line

See [[intraoperative-hemostasis]].

## Postoperative Considerations

- Monitor for **seizures, BP lability, bradycardia, hyperthermia, diabetes insipidus**
- EVD only if significant intraop bleeding — routine ICP monitoring not required in primary obstructive hydrocephalus
- Antibiotics ×24 h; some use dexamethasone (prevent stoma closure from inflammation); some ×24–48 h antiseizure
- Discharge typical at 1–2 days without complications

### Imaging of Stoma Patency

- **Sagittal T2 / CISS / FIESTA MRI** — flow artifact (dark plume) crossing floor confirms patency
- Dynamic CINE flow sequences quantify CSF movement
- Ventricles shrink less than with shunt; any shrinkage on follow-up is a positive sign

See [[postoperative-care]].

## Controversies

- ETV in **normal-pressure hydrocephalus** — poor results
- ETV in **idiopathic intracranial hypertension** — technically hard, outcomes variable (small ventricles)
- **ETV + choroid plexus cauterization (CPC)** in infants — works in developing world, not reproduced in US (HCRN multicenter study: CPC adds nothing over ETVSS prediction)

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- Monocular endoscope vision → move slowly; small steps build depth perception
- Never assume laterality — confirm thalamostriate vein
- Excessive foramen manipulation injures the fornix — **permanent memory loss**
- **Keep the instrument tip always visible**; advance scope behind instrument in small steps
- Both tuber cinereum AND Liliequist must be breached — **basilar artery visualization = proof**
- Gentle irrigation first; cautery only on directly seen sources
- Unfavorable anatomy: small ventricles, large massa intermedia, tight dorsum-sellae–basilar space
- Leave EVD at the end if hemorrhage or if hydrocephalus physiology is uncertain
