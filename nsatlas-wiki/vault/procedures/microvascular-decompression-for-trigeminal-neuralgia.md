# Microvascular Decompression for Trigeminal Neuralgia — Operative Technique

This page documents the step-by-step surgical technique of MVD for classical trigeminal neuralgia (TN). The underlying pathology, epidemiology, and long-term outcome data live in [[trigeminal-neuralgia-mvd]]. For the generic craniotomy corridor see [[retromastoid-craniotomy]].

## Preoperative Planning

### Imaging

- **High-resolution 3D T2** (CISS/FIESTA) — identifies vascular conflict at root entry zone (REZ)
- **MRA/CTA** — vessel of conflict: **superior cerebellar artery (SCA) ~75%**, anterior inferior cerebellar artery (AICA) ~10%, venous compression (superior petrosal vein / vein of Dandy tributaries) ~10–15%, tandem SCA+AICA in a minority
- **MRI with contrast** — excludes tumor, MS plaque, AVM as secondary TN causes

### Patient Selection

| Favorable | Unfavorable |
|-----------|-------------|
| Classical TN (V2 or V3 distribution) | Atypical continuous pain |
| Failed or intolerant to medication | MS-related TN (venous etiology, poorer MVD outcome) |
| Good cardiopulmonary reserve | Very elderly with comorbidity → consider percutaneous |
| Imaging confirms vascular conflict | No conflict on CISS → radiosurgery / rhizotomy |

## Positioning

- **Lateral (park-bench)** position with operative side up, or **supine with head rotated 90°**
- Ipsilateral shoulder taped down to open cervicomastoid angle
- Head rigidly fixed; neck slightly flexed
- Check for free jugular venous return (no obstruction to cerebellar venous drainage)

## Incision and Craniotomy

- **Curvilinear retromastoid incision** 1–2 cm medial to mastoid tip, 2/3 above and 1/3 below the digastric groove
- Expose asterion, digastric groove, and mastoid tip
- **Single burr hole inferolateral to the asterion**
- Small craniotomy (~2–3 cm) exposing the **junction of the transverse and sigmoid sinuses** at the superolateral corner
- **Mastoid air cells** identified and waxed; failure to seal = postoperative pseudomeningocele and CSF leak

## Dural Opening

- **Curvilinear dural flap based on the sinuses** in a "T" configuration
- Tackups to keep dura elevated off the cerebellum
- Slow CSF drainage from the cerebellomedullary cistern (allows cerebellar relaxation) — avoid brisk CSF loss

## Microsurgical Dissection

### Supralateral Cerebellar Corridor

- Cerebellum falls away from the tentorium gravity-assisted in lateral position
- Identify the **superior petrosal vein (Dandy's vein)** complex — **preserve** large tributaries if possible; judicious coagulation and division of small branches is often necessary
- **Follow the tentorium medially** to the porus trigeminus
- Open the **cisternal arachnoid of the superior CPA** sharply — sharp dissection prevents nerve traction and surrounding perforator injury

### REZ Inspection

- Identify CN V at the porus trigeminus and follow to the REZ on the lateral pons
- Inspect the **entire circumference** of the nerve — superior, inferior, medial, lateral
- Identify the offending vessel:
  - **SCA loop** — most common; usually superomedial
  - **AICA loop** — inferolateral
  - **Vein (petrosal or pontine)** — requires coagulation and division when truly compressive (MS-associated or mixed cases)

## Decompression

### Arterial Conflict

- Mobilize the offending artery away from the nerve
- Interpose **shredded Teflon felt** (or Ivalon, muscle) as a pledget between artery and nerve
- Pledget must be stable, not bulky; use enough to prevent recontact but not so much as to itself compress
- Avoid wrapping the artery circumferentially (risk of thrombosis)

### Venous Conflict

- If a dominant vein compresses the nerve, coagulate and divide
- If multiple small veins, coagulate only those in clear contact with REZ
- Venous MVD has lower durability than arterial — counsel patient accordingly

## Closure

- Copious irrigation to ensure no intradural blood
- **Watertight dural closure** with graft (pericranial or dural substitute) if dura is retracted
- **Bone flap replaced** with mini-plates; bone defect filled with hydroxyapatite cement or autologous bone dust to reduce postoperative headache
- Waxed mastoid air cells re-sealed with fibrin glue
- Layered muscle and fascia closure

## Complications

| Complication | Rate | Mechanism / Management |
|--------------|------|------------------------|
| CSF leak / pseudomeningocele | 2–4% | Mastoid air cells; watertight closure |
| Hearing loss | 1–3% | AICA/IAC loop traction; intraoperative BAEP monitoring |
| Facial numbness | 3–5% | Nerve manipulation |
| Anesthesia dolorosa | <1% | Overly aggressive nerve handling |
| Cerebellar injury | <1% | Retraction or venous sacrifice |
| Death | <0.2% | Brainstem infarct, hemorrhage |
| Recurrence | 10–20% at 10 y | Pledget migration, new vessel contact, MS progression |

## Intraoperative Monitoring

- **Brainstem auditory evoked potentials (BAEP)** — wave V latency shift > 1 ms or amplitude drop > 50% is a warning
- **CN VII EMG** and lateral spread response — important if hemifacial spasm is concurrent
- Warn anesthesia to minimize hypotension (brainstem perforator vulnerability)

## Alternatives

- **Percutaneous rhizotomy** (glycerol, balloon, RF) — for poor surgical candidates; higher recurrence, sensory loss
- **Stereotactic radiosurgery** (Gamma Knife) — non-invasive, delayed onset (~2 months), ~60–70% pain relief at 3 years
- **Internal neurolysis ("nerve combing")** — considered when no vascular conflict at surgery
