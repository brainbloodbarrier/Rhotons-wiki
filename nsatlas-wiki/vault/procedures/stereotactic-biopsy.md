# Stereotactic Biopsy

Stereotactic biopsy is the default tissue-diagnosis operation for intracranial lesions that are **not surgical resection candidates**: deep basal-ganglia/thalamic/brainstem tumors, multifocal disease, diffuse infiltrating gliomas spanning eloquent cortex, suspected CNS lymphoma, and immunocompromised patients with an imaging differential that includes infection (toxoplasmosis) or demyelination. A diagnostic yield of 95%+ with a well-chosen trajectory makes biopsy the workhorse of the tumor service alongside [[brain-tumors-general-principles|open resection]].

## Indications

| Clinical scenario | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Deep lesion (basal ganglia, thalamus, brainstem) | Open resection prohibitive |
| Multifocal / periventricular pattern suggesting lymphoma | Avoid steroids pre-biopsy (obscure diagnosis) |
| Diffuse infiltrating glioma without resectable core | Tissue for molecular diagnosis (IDH, MGMT, 1p/19q) |
| Immunocompromised patient, differential includes infection | Cultures + histology |
| Suspected PML, vasculitis, or demyelinating disease | Unique to biopsy; stop immunomodulation decisions |
| Eloquent-area enhancing lesion where upfront oncologic plan depends on grade | Tailor radiation/chemo |

Conversely, if the lesion is accessible and resection confers either oncologic or mass-effect benefit, open craniotomy is preferred; see [[brain-tumors-general-principles]].

## Technology arms

### Frame-based stereotaxy
- **Rigid external reference frame** (Leksell G; Cosman-Roberts-Wells) pinned to the skull under local anesthesia.
- Imaging (MRI ± CT) acquired with the frame in place; target coordinates computed relative to frame fiducials.
- Sub-millimeter mechanical accuracy (typically <1 mm); the reference for high-precision deep targets and functional work (DBS electrode placement, Gamma Knife).
- Trade-off: pain of frame fitting, transport logistics, longer set-up.

### Frameless / image-guided stereotaxy
- **Skin fiducials** or **bone-screw fiducials** acquired on pre-op MRI; intraoperative registration to a tracked probe (optical or electromagnetic).
- Accuracy typically 1.5–2.5 mm at cortical targets, degrading at deeper targets due to registration error propagation.
- Advantages: preferred for convexity targets, pediatric cases, and when frame intolerance (anxiety, restlessness) is anticipated.
- Robotic platforms (ROSA, Neuromate) increasingly bridge frame-based accuracy with frameless ergonomics.

### Intraoperative-MRI biopsy
- Real-time MRI confirmation of needle position — available at high-cost, high-workflow centers.
- Particularly valued for deep pediatric targets.

## Trajectory planning principles

- Avoid crossing sulcal pial vessels, ventricles (if possible), eloquent cortex, and internal capsule.
- Single pass if possible; multiple samples along a single tract acceptable.
- Trans-sulcal rather than trans-gyral when geometry allows.
- Maintain a "safe corridor" of ≥10 mm from major vessels on the planned trajectory.
- Target the **enhancing rim** (not necrotic center) for high-grade gliomas; target the **peri-enhancement T2/FLAIR** zone when suspicion of lymphoma or demyelination — both yield higher diagnostic return.
- [[preoperative-evaluation]] includes routine MRI with contrast, optional MR perfusion / spectroscopy, and anticoagulant review.

## Operative steps

1. Local anesthesia ± conscious sedation (frame-based) or general anesthesia (frameless, pediatric, posterior-fossa).
2. Fiducial placement + imaging; co-register to planning software.
3. Small linear scalp incision (2–3 cm) at the pre-planned entry; twist-drill or mini burr hole (5–14 mm depending on platform).
4. Dural opening with bipolar coagulation; avoid pial vessels at entry.
5. Biopsy needle (side-cutting or cup forceps, 1.8–2.5 mm outer diameter) advanced to target along the computed trajectory.
6. **Three to five specimens** obtained at and around target; intraoperative smear / frozen section confirms diagnostic yield before needle withdrawal.
7. Hemostasis check (observe for 2–3 min for venous ooze); wound closure.

## Complications

| Complication | Incidence | Notes |
|---|-----------|-------|
| Symptomatic hemorrhage | 1–4% | Higher in high-grade gliomas (fragile tumor vessels) and basal ganglia targets |
| Asymptomatic hemorrhage on post-op CT | 20–30% | Usually inconsequential |
| Non-diagnostic specimen | <5% | Repeat biopsy or open resection |
| Seizure | 1–2% | Prophylactic antiepileptic in selected patients |
| Infection | <1% | Standard perioperative antibiotic |
| Trajectory-related deficit | <1% | Careful planning minimizes |

A sudden neurologic change post-biopsy mandates emergency CT; large symptomatic hematoma may require [[decompressive-craniectomy]] or evacuation.

## Post-biopsy management

- Short observation in neuro-ICU or step-down unit; most patients discharged at 24 h.
- Avoid anticoagulation for 48 h.
- Pathology result typically 3–7 days (permanent + molecular panel).
- Multidisciplinary tumor board discussion defines next steps — observation, radiation/chemotherapy, or delayed resection.
