# Ventriculo-Atrial Shunt

*CSF Diversion Procedures — Ventriculo-Atrial Shunt*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Indications

- Hydrocephalus when peritoneum is unavailable or has failed (prior multiple abdominal surgeries, peritonitis, severe adhesions, malabsorption)
- Second-line after VP shunt failure — physiologic distal site (CSF normally drains to venous system)
- Similar infection and revision rates to VP in experienced hands

See [[ventriculo-peritoneal-shunt]] for shared proximal technique and [[csf-ventricular-shunt-reference]] for valve selection.

## Preoperative Considerations

- **Echocardiogram** to exclude right-heart dysfunction, tricuspid regurgitation, and patent foramen ovale
- Fluoroscopy available intraoperatively — mandatory for tip verification
- Informed consent covers VA-specific complications: thrombosis, bacterial endocarditis, cardiac arrhythmia, shunt nephritis (immune-complex glomerulonephritis), pulmonary embolism, SVC syndrome
- In children: distal catheter must be re-lengthened with growth (fixed distal length, unlike VP)

## Positioning

- Supine, head turned opposite shunt side, shoulder bump
- Neck prepped from jaw to clavicle, continuous with cranial and upper chest fields
- Fluoroscopy C-arm planned for lateral and AP cardiac views

## Proximal Catheter

Identical to VP shunt — frontal Kocher's point or occipito-parietal Frazier's point. See [[external-ventricular-drain-placement]] for Kocher's landmarks.

## Distal Catheter — Atrial Access

### Cutdown Approach (open)

- Transverse incision overlying the **facial vein** (common facial vein drains into internal jugular vein)
- Isolate facial vein with vessel loops; proximal and distal ties
- Venotomy; introduce distal catheter; advance under fluoroscopic guidance
- Ligate facial vein around catheter (no retrograde flow risk)

### Percutaneous Seldinger

- Internal jugular vein puncture (ultrasound guidance)
- Peel-away sheath over wire; pass catheter through sheath; peel
- Faster; avoids neck dissection scar; preferred in many centers

## Tip Position — Fluoroscopic Landmarks

- **Target**: cavoatrial junction / high right atrium
- **Landmark**: vertebral level **T6–T7** on AP fluoroscopy
- Too deep (mid-atrium or ventricle): arrhythmia, tricuspid injury
- Too shallow (SVC): thrombosis risk, catheter migration
- Lateral view confirms catheter is in SVC/atrium, not aberrant vessel

## Connection and Flush

- Connect valve between proximal and distal catheters in subgaleal pocket
- Flush with Ringer's lactate (no bacitracin → airlock risk)
- Intrathecal vancomycin 10 mg + gentamicin 4 mg via valve tap; occlude distal during injection
- See [[ventriculo-peritoneal-shunt]] for shared assembly protocol

## Postoperative Considerations

- Immediate postop chest X-ray to confirm tip at cavoatrial junction
- Baseline ECG; monitor for new arrhythmia
- Watch for:
  - **Shunt nephritis** — proteinuria, hematuria, hypocomplementemia; months-to-years after placement; indication for shunt removal
  - **Bacterial endocarditis** — persistent bacteremia; tough to clear without shunt removal
  - **Atrial thrombosis / pulmonary embolism**
  - **Catheter tip withdrawal with growth** in children — annual chest X-ray surveillance

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- Do **not** leave tip in SVC — thrombosis; advance to cavoatrial junction (T6–T7)
- Do **not** push into right ventricle — arrhythmia and tricuspid injury
- Echo before surgery; ECG monitoring intraoperatively
- Facial vein ligation after cutdown avoids retrograde venous bleeding
- Children need catheter lengthening — monitor chest X-ray serially
- Consider VA over VPl when peritoneum fails but pleura contraindicated
- Prior endocarditis or known right-heart shunt: choose VP or [[ventriculo-gallbladder-shunt]] instead
