# Ventriculo-Peritoneal Shunt

*CSF Diversion Procedures — Ventriculo-Peritoneal Shunt*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Indications

- Primary and secondary **hydrocephalus** (adult and pediatric)
- **Normal-pressure hydrocephalus** after shunt trial
- **Idiopathic intracranial hypertension / pseudotumor cerebri**
- Pediatric macrocephaly with developmental regression
- Alternative for non-ETV candidates (see [[endoscopic-third-ventriculostomy]] and ETVSS)

## Baseline Risks

- Clinically relevant intracranial hemorrhage: ~1%
- Abdominal visceral injury: ~1%
- Shunt infection: 5–8%
- Pediatric revision within 2 years: ~50%

## Distal Site Selection

| Site | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Peritoneum** (default) | Safe access, excellent absorption, catheter slack for growth in children | Occasional adhesions from prior surgery |
| **Right atrium** (VA) | Physiologic (venous) absorption; comparable infection rate | Thrombosis, endocarditis, arrhythmia, nephritis; fixed distal length |
| **Pleura** (VPl) | Salvage option | Higher failure; inspiratory suck → overdrainage / slit ventricle; avoid in <7 y |
| **Gallbladder** (VGB) | Works when other sites fail | Infrequent; GI complications |
| Other (transverse sinus, bone marrow) | Described | Rarely used |

See [[ventriculo-atrial-shunt]], [[ventriculo-pleural-shunt]], [[ventriculo-gallbladder-shunt]].

## Valve Selection

Surgeon must know multiple valves. Programmable valves allow postoperative adjustment. Anti-siphon and gravitational units reduce overdrainage in VPl / ambulatory patients. Consult [[csf-ventricular-shunt-reference]] for the Aesculap (proGAV 2.0, proSA), Codman (Certas Plus), Medtronic (PS Medical, Delta, Strata II/NSC/MR), and Sophysa (Polaris) valve catalogs with MR-compatibility and X-ray identification tables.

## Positioning

- Supine, head turned 45–60° opposite the proximal catheter side (more for occipito-parietal)
- Shoulder bump — mastoid process and clavicle on **same horizontal plane** (safer tunneling)
- Entire shunt path prepped in a single field; skip incisions planned
- Double-gloving (reduces shunt infection)
- Preoperative antibiotics

## Proximal Catheter

Two standard entries:

### Frontal (Kocher's point)

- 1–2 cm anterior to the coronal suture, 2–3 cm lateral to midline
- Target: **foramen of Monro** in front of choroid plexus
- Infants: anterior fontanelle corner is an acceptable entry
- Requires a retroauricular skip incision for valve connection

See [[external-ventricular-drain-placement]] for identical cranial landmarks and perpendicular-drill discipline.

### Occipito-parietal (Frazier's point)

- 3 cm lateral to midline, 6 cm superior to inion
- Alternate estimate: halfway between external acoustic meatus and inion → halfway between that point and vertex → burr hole in the flat area just superior/posterior

Neuronavigation preferred, especially in small or slit ventricles. Frontal catheter depth capped at ~7 cm in adults.

## Valve Pocket and Dura

- Pocket between periosteum and galea in loose areolar plane
- **In infants, galea is the only layer that reliably holds a deep suture** — keep it attached to skin
- Cruciate durotomy; coagulate dural and pial edges; open pia sharply

## Peritoneal Access Methods

### Minilaparotomy (most common)

- Supraumbilical midline (avascular linea alba) or lateral subcostal (over liver — low bowel-injury risk)
- Layered dissection: skin → fat → Scarpa's fascia → anterior rectus sheath
- Split rectus fibers **vertically** with a hemostat
- Grasp posterior rectus sheath with two hemostats; sweep muscle aside; open sheath + transversalis + peritoneum in one cut under direct vision — bowel falls away under tension toward surgeon
- Confirm entry: blunt instrument passes without resistance; smooth abdominal undersurface palpable
- **Never leave catheter in the preperitoneal space** — coils, fails fast

### Trocar

- Supra-umbilical skin incision; trocar to anterior rectus sheath, aim for ipsilateral iliac crest under anterior skin traction; two distinct "pops" (anterior + posterior sheath)

### Laparoscopic

- Preferred for obese patients or multiply re-operated abdomens — general surgery collaboration

If multiple prior abdominal operations → **consult general surgery**. Fluoroscopy confirms free distal catheter distribution; a tightly coiled catheter is unacceptable.

## Subcutaneous Tunneling

- Generally head → abdomen (either direction acceptable)
- Greatest resistance at the **neck/nuchal fascia**
- Once past nuchal fascia, keep passer **superficial — above clavicle** to avoid **pneumothorax**
- Do not cross midline at neck until passer is palpable over clavicle (esophageal injury)
- Avoid the breast bud in developing girls
- In infants, notify anesthesia — tunneling transiently restricts ventilation; be quick

## Assembly and Flush

- Attach valve to distal catheter; flush with **Ringer's lactate** (bacitracin fluid risks airlock bubbles); cover equipment with bacitracin-soaked sponges between steps
- Place proximal catheter last; connect to the system rapidly to prevent CSF egress and catheter dislodgement
- Confirm distal flow; pull valve into pocket; place distal catheter in peritoneum

## Intrathecal Antibiotic Tap

- 25-gauge butterfly into valve reservoir after assembly
- **Vancomycin 10 mg + gentamicin 4 mg** intrathecal (adult/pediatric same dose)
- Occlude distal catheter during injection to force drug into ventricle
- Confirm proximal flow simultaneously

## Postoperative Considerations

- Postop CT / shunt series for proximal tip and distal tip position
- Watch for overdrainage (postural headache, subdural hygroma), slit ventricle syndrome
- Reprogram valve after MRI if programmable (adjust to prescribed setting)
- Counsel on infection warning signs; follow shunt series if symptomatic

See [[postoperative-care]].

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Double-glove and limit OR traffic** — infection is the dominant long-term enemy
- Always flush with Ringer's, never bacitracin fluid — airlock
- Max frontal catheter depth 7 cm in adults — deeper risks temporal horn / cistern tips
- Keep tunneler **above the clavicle** at the neck — pneumothorax and esophageal injury are career-defining complications
- Verify peritoneal entry before passing catheter — preperitoneal placement fails quickly
- In infants keep galea attached to skin; only layer that holds a deep stitch
- Consult general surgery for multiply operated abdomens
- ETV first in appropriate candidates (see ETVSS on [[endoscopic-third-ventriculostomy]])
