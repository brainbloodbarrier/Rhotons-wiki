# Lacerated Dural Venous Sinus Repair

*Emergency Neurosurgery and Trauma — Repair of a Lacerated Dural Venous Sinus*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## The Scenario

Laceration of a **superior sagittal sinus (SSS)**, **transverse sinus**, **sigmoid sinus**, or **confluence of sinuses (torcular)** during craniotomy, craniectomy, or burr hole — especially at parasagittal, bifrontal, retromastoid, and suboccipital approaches. Also from traumatic depressed skull fracture over a sinus.

Catastrophic risks: **massive hemorrhage**, **air embolism** (especially seated patient), **sinus thrombosis**.

## Prevention First

- **Master the venous anatomy on preoperative imaging** — MRV/CTV for sinus dominance, patency, venous lakes, and location of major cortical bridging veins.
- **Stay ≥ 1.5 cm from midline** on craniotomies whenever anatomy permits.
- **Separate the sinus wall from the inner skull generously** before using the drill footplate — anticipate thick skull, repeat operations, elderly patients with adherent venous dura.
- When dural adherence forbids a footplate pass: use a **B1 bit with no footplate** — mill the outer cortical + cancellous bone to create a trough, then gently fracture the inner cortex under direct vision to elevate the flap.
- **Last bony cut goes over the sinus** — so if the sinus tears, the flap can be lifted immediately rather than fighting hemorrhage through a half-attached flap. If bleeding occurs, **unroof the sinus rather than blind-packing** the lumen.
- **Incise the dura away from the sinus**, then work toward it. Venous lakes extend beyond the visible sinus margin; the paramedian dural incision is the classic site of inadvertent tears.
- **Aggressive retraction near a sinus can occlude it** — intraoperative cerebral swelling or spontaneous contusion should prompt immediate check of adjacent sinus patency. **Microdoppler** confirms flow under retraction.
- Occipital sinus (and other non-vital small sinuses) encountered during Y-shaped posterior-fossa durotomy can be **ligated with silk or Weck clips** without consequence.

## Immediate Response (Seconds)

1. **Direct pressure** with large cottonoid + gloved finger
2. **Notify anesthesia** — expect hypotension, large-bore IV access, blood products, watch end-tidal CO₂ for **air embolism** (precipitous drop = classic sign)
3. Lower head / Trendelenburg-reverse if seated; flood field with saline
4. Call for additional surgical hands and blood products
5. Do NOT panic-cauterize — will only enlarge the defect
6. **Do NOT pack hemostatic agents into the sinus lumen** — Gelfoam/Surgicel inside the lumen occludes the sinus, causing swelling and venous infarction. Agents go *over* the tear, not *into* the sinus.
7. **Obtain proximal and distal control** when feasible (long aneurysm clips or vascular bulldogs). If bone overhangs both ends and control is not possible, **temporary cotton-ball tamponade** of both lumen ends is the bridge.
8. **Bipolar coagulation is contraindicated** for partial-thickness tears — electrocautery shrinks the dural edges and enlarges the defect. Tamponade, suture, or patch — never bipolar.
9. **Work intermittently** — pause to let anesthesia catch up on volume; tolerating some controlled ooze beats inducing exsanguination by forcing uninterrupted repair.
10. **Head-up 15°** reduces venous pressure but **increases air embolism risk** — never elevate to stop anterograde bleeding (creates retrograde air siphoning). Keep head neutral or mildly elevated with active air-embolism surveillance.

## Definitive Repair — Technique Ladder

### Small Tear (linear <5 mm)

- Gelfoam + Surgicel strip over the tear
- Cottonoid pressure for 5–10 minutes
- Reassess; if dry, cover with Floseal or Gelfoam-thrombin
- No suture needed

### Medium Tear (5–15 mm, linear or curved)

- Apply hemostatic matrix (Gelfoam, Surgicel, Floseal)
- If ongoing bleeding: **pericranial or muscle patch** sutured to dural edges
- 6-0 Prolene simple interrupted or running across the tear with a patch graft
- Take **partial-thickness dural bites** on either side of the sinus

### Large Laceration or Segmental Loss

- **Autologous vein or fascial graft** patch (saphenous vein, fascia lata, temporalis fascia)
- 6-0 or 7-0 Prolene vascular suture technique
- If reconstruction impossible, consider:
  - **SSS anterior third (in front of coronal suture)**: can be ligated with minimal risk
  - **SSS middle third**: ligation causes venous infarct — avoid
  - **SSS posterior third**: ligation is usually fatal — do NOT ligate
  - **Non-dominant transverse sinus**: can ligate if contralateral transverse and both sigmoids are patent (confirm on imaging if time permits)
  - **Torcular**: do NOT ligate

See [[intraoperative-hemostasis]] for adjunctive agents.

### Swing Dural Flap (Roof Reconstruction)

The workhorse for superior-wall SSS tears not amenable to primary closure:

1. Control the bleeding with a long cotton patty / Surgicel fibrillar sheet over the tear.
2. Remove additional bone to **unroof the injured segment** and define the edges.
3. Incise a **parasagittal dural flap longer than the laceration** ("swing flap"), preserving parasagittal bridging veins.
4. Rotate the flap over the defect; **suture to the contralateral paramedian dura** (or to falx / tentorium if the laceration is more internal).
5. Remove the cotton sheet before the flap is fully closed; restore flow.
6. Confirm patency with **intraoperative fluorescent angiography** (ICG) after reconstruction.

### Vascular Shunt Rescue

If cerebral swelling appears during repair (functional occlusion from manipulation + clot):

- Place **vascular shunt tubing** inside the sinus lumen to restore flow while reconstruction proceeds around it.
- If flow is not restored after swing-flap closure, expect luminal thrombus — pass the suction tip under the flap, evacuate the clot, and **irrigate the lumen with heparinized saline**.

### Torcular Injury

Torcular laceration is the most dangerous variant — ligation is not survivable. Technique ladder (Figures from the chapter):

- **Small tear** → Gelfoam sponge + finger pressure; suture patch if needed.
- **Medium tear** → pericranial or fascial graft sutured over the defect.
- **Large tear with clot extending into lateral sinuses** → autologous **muscle graft** packed over the defect, supported by the overlying scalp/galea in a layered repair.
- Do NOT sacrifice the torcular; the straight sinus, both transverse sinuses, and both occipital lobes drain through it.

### Intraoperative Flow Confirmation

- **ICG / fluorescein angiography** after any reconstruction.
- **Microdoppler** if ICG unavailable.
- **Post-op CTV/MRV** at 24–48 h.

## Preoperative Planning for Known-Risk Cases

- Review MR venography / CTV for sinus dominance, patency, collaterals
- Consent the patient for sinus injury as a specific risk
- Plan craniotomy so the **last bony cut crosses the sinus**, allowing rapid flap elevation if injury occurs
- Have vascular sutures, hemostatic matrices, cottonoids, blood products staged in the room
- For seated posterior fossa cases, strict air embolism precautions (precordial Doppler, central line for air aspiration)

## Positioning and Exposure Adjuncts

- **Head elevation 15–20°** reduces venous pressure and bleeding
- Avoid jugular vein compression from excessive neck rotation
- In seated position, maintain absolute vigilance for air embolism

## Postoperative Considerations

- **Surveillance imaging**: MRV or CTV at 24–48 h to document sinus patency vs thrombosis
- If sinus thrombosed: anticoagulate if no bleeding risk remains
- Watch for:
  - Venous infarct — parasagittal hemorrhagic infarct after middle/posterior SSS loss
  - Elevated ICP from impaired venous outflow
  - Pseudotumor-like syndrome if dominant transverse sinus lost
- Serial neuro exams, ICP monitoring if sagittal sinus thrombosed

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Never ligate the middle/posterior SSS or torcular** — venous infarction or death
- Surgicel + Gelfoam sandwich + cottonoid pressure resolves most small tears
- Vein / fascial patch for larger tears — take partial-thickness bites on sinus wall
- Head elevation 15–20° during repair reduces venous pressure
- Brief anesthesia early — they need to prep for blood loss and air embolism
- For retromastoid / suboccipital craniotomy, know sigmoid sinus course before drilling
- Plan the last bony cut across sinus so flap can be lifted rapidly on injury
- Document patency post-op; anticoagulate if thrombosis and bleeding risk acceptable
