# Open Skull Fracture Management

*Emergency Neurosurgery and Trauma — Open (Compound) Skull Fracture*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Definition and Classification

Open skull fracture = skull fracture with overlying scalp laceration exposing bone or dura to the environment. Subtypes:

- **Simple open** — linear fracture + scalp laceration, no displacement
- **Depressed open** — bone fragment displaced inward by more than one skull thickness
- **Comminuted** — multiple fragments
- **Frontal sinus involvement** — anterior wall, posterior wall, or through-and-through (nasofrontal duct)
- **Venous sinus overlay** — fracture over SSS, torcular, transverse, or sigmoid

## Indications for OR

| Finding | Decision |
|---|---|
| Depression > one skull thickness | Elevate |
| Clinically infected wound | Debride + delay closure |
| Dural laceration / CSF leak | Explore + dural repair |
| Pneumocephalus with persistent leak | Explore |
| Grossly contaminated, contused bone | Debride; consider discard |
| Significant cosmetic deformity | Elevate |
| Associated mass lesion (EDH, SDH, contusion) | Combined craniotomy |
| Overlying venous sinus with depression | Individualize — see [[lacerated-dural-venous-sinus-repair]] |

Non-operative candidates: closed depressed fractures without neurologic deficit, dural integrity, minimal cosmetic issue.

## Preoperative Considerations

- **Tetanus prophylaxis** status
- **Broad-spectrum antibiotics** on arrival (cover skin + environmental flora — cefazolin ± coverage for contaminated wounds)
- Imaging: non-contrast head CT with bone windows; CTA if venous sinus involvement suspected
- Reverse anticoagulation; platelet transfusion for antiplatelets
- Type and cross

## Operative Technique

### Positioning

- Supine, head turned as needed; skull-clamp if extended craniotomy anticipated
- For frontal fractures, bicoronal or direct approach

### Incision

- **Incorporate the scalp laceration** into a planned skin incision
- Extend the wound to allow for adequate craniotomy exposure around the fracture
- Debride scalp edges **conservatively** — preserve vascularity for closure

### Bone Management

- Remove free, contaminated, and devitalized bone fragments
- Elevate depressed segments with a periosteal elevator through an adjacent burr hole (see [[burr-holes-and-bone-flaps]])
- **Bone flap replacement**:
  - **Clean contamination, viable bone, < 6 h injury** → replace after copious irrigation
  - **Gross contamination, devitalized bone, > 6 h injury, frank infection** → discard; plan cranioplasty later
  - Frontal sinus involvement with through-and-through → cranialization protocol

### Dural Inspection and Repair

- Open dura if violation suspected
- Irrigate copiously; debride contaminated dura
- Primary repair if possible; otherwise **duraplasty** with pericranium or synthetic graft (see [[dural-opening-and-closure]])
- Evacuate underlying hematoma, debride contused cortex only if necrotic

### Frontal Sinus Involvement

- **Anterior wall only**: reduce and reconstruct; preserve drainage through nasofrontal duct
- **Posterior wall with dural tear**: cranialize sinus (remove posterior wall, strip mucosa, plug nasofrontal duct with muscle or fat, cover with pericranial flap)
- Preserve pericranial flap vascularized on supraorbital pedicle for reconstruction

### Closure

- Copious irrigation (pulsed lavage if available)
- Intraoperative antibiotics per contamination level
- Layered closure: dural → bone → temporalis/galea → skin
- Drain if significant dead space
- Consider delayed primary closure if grossly contaminated

## Postoperative Considerations

- Continue IV antibiotics 5–7 days (longer for gross contamination or CSF involvement)
- Serial wound checks; low threshold for re-exploration
- CT at 24–48 h to document reduction and exclude new collection
- Seizure prophylaxis × 7 days (TBI guidelines)
- Follow-up CSF leak if frontal sinus involved; monitor for meningitis

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Incorporate the laceration** into your incision — fresh cut through adjacent scalp compromises healing
- Elevate through a **separate adjacent burr hole**, not directly through the depressed bone
- **Discard grossly contaminated bone** — do not save a flap destined to infect; stage with later cranioplasty
- Frontal sinus with posterior-wall violation → cranialize with pericranial flap
- Venous sinus overlay → have blood products and vascular sutures ready; see [[lacerated-dural-venous-sinus-repair]]
- Tetanus + broad-spectrum antibiotics at presentation, not after OR
- Dural violation = meningitis risk; repair watertight
