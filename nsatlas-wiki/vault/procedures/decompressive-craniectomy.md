# Decompressive Craniectomy

*Emergency Neurosurgery and Trauma — Decompressive Craniectomy*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Indications

### Traumatic Brain Injury

- Refractory ICP elevation despite tier-2 medical therapy (osmotic therapy, CSF drainage, sedation)
- RESCUEicp — decompressive craniectomy for TBI with refractory ICP >25 mmHg: reduces mortality with increased survival in vegetative / severely disabled states
- DECRA — early bifrontotemporoparietal DC for moderate ICP: did not improve outcome; reserve for true refractory cases

### Malignant MCA Infarction

- HAMLET / DESTINY / DECIMAL pooled data: hemicraniectomy within 48 h in patients <60 y reduces mortality and improves functional outcome
- Age >60: DESTINY II shows mortality reduction but with higher mRS — individualize

### Other

- Cerebellar stroke with brainstem compression → suboccipital DC
- Severe venous sinus thrombosis with mass effect
- Spontaneous ICH with herniation syndrome (controversial)

## Preoperative Considerations

- Reverse anticoagulation; platelet transfusion for antiplatelets if emergent
- Baseline Hgb, coagulation, type and cross
- Family discussion — significant long-term disability, cranioplasty expected
- ICP monitor / EVD placement (see [[external-ventricular-drain-placement]]) before or concurrent with DC

## Hemicraniectomy Technique (Unilateral)

### Positioning

- Supine, head turned contralaterally, shoulder roll
- Mayfield three-point fixation (see [[skull-clamp-placement]])
- Field extended from midline to below zygoma

### Incision

- **Reverse question mark** (Kempe incision): starts at zygomatic arch, curves posteriorly behind the ear, up and over the parietal bone, forward to the midline at hairline, ending 1 cm from midline to preserve superficial temporal artery for future cranioplasty vascularization
- Scalp flap reflected anteriorly on temporalis muscle
- Temporalis cut along posterior limb, reflected inferiorly

### Bone Flap

- **Flap must be ≥12 cm AP dimension** (smaller flaps = inadequate decompression, "corking" at edges)
- Extends from frontal above supraorbital ridge, back to occipitoparietal bone, laterally to floor of middle fossa (critical — remove temporal squama down to floor for uncus decompression)
- Rongeur the sphenoid wing and temporal squama down to the floor of the middle cranial fossa
- Preserve bone flap in sterile subgaleal pocket in abdomen or in -80°C bone bank

### Dural Opening

- Cruciate or stellate durotomy
- Release swollen brain gradually — avoid sudden transdural herniation
- Inspect for underlying hematoma, contusion requiring evacuation

### Duraplasty

- **Expansile duraplasty** mandatory — autograft (pericranium) or synthetic (DuraGen, collagen matrix)
- Loose closure; do not close the dura tight — defeats the decompression

### Closure

- Temporalis loosely approximated to galea
- Scalp closed in layers, watertight
- No bone replacement — reserved for cranioplasty in 6 weeks to 3 months

See [[dural-opening-and-closure]], [[burr-holes-and-bone-flaps]], [[intraoperative-hemostasis]].

## Bifrontal Decompressive Craniectomy

- Bicoronal incision
- Bone flap: bilateral frontal, extending posteriorly to coronal suture, anteriorly to supraorbital ridges, inferiorly to frontozygomatic suture
- Crossing midline → **ligate superior sagittal sinus** anteriorly only (sinus thrombosis below the coronal suture region — risk of venous infarct — this is controversial; some preserve anterior third)
- Dural opening with sagittal sinus preserved if possible
- Bifrontal DC is less effective in TBI (DECRA negative trial) — hemicraniectomy generally preferred

## Suboccipital Decompression

- Midline incision from inion to C2
- Bone removal from occipital protuberance to foramen magnum
- C1 laminectomy if cerebellar herniation
- Cruciate dural opening; duraplasty

## Postoperative Considerations

- ICP monitoring continues post-DC in TBI
- Head-of-bed elevation, sedation, osmotic therapy continued until swelling subsides
- Scan at 6–24 h for new hematoma, contusion progression, hydrocephalus
- Skin flap sags inward as brain reabsorbs edema — sunken flap syndrome ("syndrome of the trephined") a late concern
- Plan cranioplasty at 6 weeks–3 months based on clinical recovery

See [[postoperative-care]].

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Flap must be ≥12 cm** — inadequate size corks the swelling brain into the edges
- Extend inferiorly to floor of middle fossa — uncal herniation is the killer
- Expansile duraplasty is non-negotiable
- Preserve STA for future cranioplasty vascularization
- Kempe incision is the workhorse — wide exposure, good healing
- Label and store bone flap meticulously; lose it and the patient needs custom implant
- EVD placement before or during DC for ICP feedback
- Consent explicitly covers severe disability — DC reduces death but many survivors are severely disabled
