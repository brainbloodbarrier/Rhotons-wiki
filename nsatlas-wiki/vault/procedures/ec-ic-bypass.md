# EC–IC Bypass

Extracranial–intracranial (EC–IC) bypass is the microsurgical anastomosis of an extracranial donor artery to an intracranial recipient artery to restore or augment cerebral perfusion. The prototypical operation is the **superficial temporal artery to middle cerebral artery (STA–MCA) bypass**, introduced by Yaşargil in 1967. Although the 1985 EC–IC Bypass Trial failed to show benefit in atherosclerotic disease and caused a decades-long decline in volume, selective modern indications — particularly moyamoya disease and giant-aneurysm flow-replacement — keep the operation in the neurosurgical repertoire.

## Indications

| Indication | Typical Technique |
|------------|-------------------|
| Moyamoya disease (adult) | Direct STA–MCA + indirect EDAS or EMS |
| Moyamoya disease (pediatric) | Pial synangiosis, EDAMS, encephaloduroarteriosynangiosis — small donor caliber |
| Giant ICA aneurysm / carotid sacrifice | Flow-replacement STA–MCA (low flow) or radial/saphenous interposition graft (medium/high flow) |
| Giant MCA aneurysm | IC–IC reconstruction or [[lateral-sphenoid-wing-meningioma]] carotid sacrifice + bypass |
| Skull-base tumor with ICA encasement | Bypass + parent-vessel sacrifice |
| Failed medical therapy symptomatic carotid / MCA occlusion with hemodynamic failure | Carefully selected; COSS trial was negative overall |
| Vertebrobasilar insufficiency | Rarely — occipital–PICA bypass or other variants |

## Donor Options

- **Superficial temporal artery (STA) frontal or parietal branch** — pedicled, ~15–30 mL/min; standard low-flow donor.
- **Occipital artery** — pedicled; for posterior circulation bypass to PICA, AICA, or vertebral.
- **Radial artery graft** — free graft interposition; medium flow (~40–80 mL/min); requires Allen test.
- **Saphenous vein graft** — free graft interposition; high flow (~100–200 mL/min); for carotid replacement.

## STA–MCA Bypass Technique (Outline)

### Positioning and Incision

- **Supine, head rotated** to the contralateral side; Mayfield pinion.
- **Doppler-mapped STA course** on the scalp; zig-zag or linear incision centered over the chosen branch (frontal if targeting anterior MCA, parietal if targeting posterior).
- Careful dissection of the STA preserving the adventitia; harvest a length of at least 8–10 cm with a distal "fish-mouth" cut for anastomosis.

### Craniotomy

- Small **temporal craniotomy** (~3–5 cm) centered over the chosen Sylvian cortical branch.
- Sylvian fissure exposure is unnecessary — the target is an **M3 or M4 cortical branch** on the convexity.
- Dura opened; cortical arteries inspected; recipient ≥1 mm diameter selected.

### Anastomosis

- Recipient temporarily clipped proximally and distally; longitudinal arteriotomy 2–3× the donor caliber.
- End-to-side anastomosis with 9-0 or 10-0 nylon, interrupted or running; begin with heel and toe stitches, then sides.
- Typical total clamp time: 20–35 minutes; **mild hypothermia and burst suppression** are options for neuroprotection.
- Heparin reversal with protamine optional; many centers do not reverse.

### Confirmation of Patency

- Intraoperative ICG angiography shows anterograde filling from STA into MCA branch.
- Micro-Doppler or Charbel flow probe quantifies bypass flow; >20 mL/min predicts durable patency.
- Postoperative CT angiogram or DSA at 24–72 h.

## Indirect Revascularization (Moyamoya)

- **EDAS** — encephaloduroarteriosynangiosis: STA laid on cortex under dural flap.
- **EMS** — encephalomyosynangiosis: temporalis muscle laid on cortex.
- **EDAMS, pial synangiosis** — variants combining donors.
- Relies on neovascularization over weeks to months; useful adjunct in children.

## Complications

- **Hyperperfusion syndrome** — headache, seizures, ICH; manage with strict BP control post-op.
- **Stroke** — ischemic during clamp time; rate ~3–5%.
- **Bypass occlusion** — early (technical) vs late (progression of disease).
- **Wound issues** — scalp necrosis along STA dissection, infection.
- **CSF leak, subdural hygroma, seizures**.

## Key Decision Points

- **Flow demand**: estimated by BTO with SPECT/xenon CT; <20 mL/min deficit → STA; 20–60 → radial; >60 → saphenous.
- **Recipient vessel**: M2/M3 for flow-replacement to cover most of MCA territory; M4 for augmentation in moyamoya.
- **Prep**: dual antiplatelet often started preoperatively; continued postop for patency.
