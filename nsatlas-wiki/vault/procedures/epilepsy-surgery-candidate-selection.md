# Epilepsy Surgery Candidate Selection

*Epilepsy Surgery — Selection of Surgical Candidates*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol. Contributors: Samuel B. Tomlinson, BA; Vivek P. Buch, MD; Benjamin C. Kennedy, MD.

## Epidemiology and Burden

- Medical AED therapy is first-line, but **30–40% of patients have seizures not controlled by AEDs** (Kwan & Brodie 2000)
- DRE is associated with impaired quality of life and higher rates of **depression, anxiety, suicidality, and long-term mortality** (Kwon 2014; Sillanpää 2010)
- Patients with DRE account for **~80% of all US epilepsy-related costs** (Begley 2000; Cramer 2014)
- **Epilepsy surgery is the most effective treatment for DRE** with Class I evidence: 40–80% of surgical patients enjoy sustained seizure relief vs <10% on continued pharmacotherapy (Wiebe 2001; Engel 2012; Dwivedi 2017; Liu 2018)
- **Surgical underutilization** — only a fraction of potential candidates receive surgical consultation; lower referral thresholds are advocated (Engel 2003; Steinbrener 2019)

## Drug-Resistant Epilepsy (DRE)

ILAE definition (Kwan 2010): failure of adequate trials of **two** tolerated, appropriately chosen and used antiepileptic drug schedules to achieve sustained seizure freedom. Once DRE is established, the chance of any further AED achieving remission drops below 5%.

**Early referral** (within 2 years of DRE diagnosis) prevents preventable psychosocial, cognitive, and mortality morbidity (SUDEP risk).

### Ryvlin–Rheims 3-Criteria Framework

Three conditions must be met before considering surgical evaluation (Ryvlin & Rheims 2008):

1. The patient (or caregiver for children / cognitively impaired) **understands the objective of presurgical evaluation and accepts the possibility of surgery**
2. The patient has **disabling seizures despite appropriate medical therapy**
3. Available imaging and electro-clinical data are **consistent with a surgically-remediable epileptic syndrome**

### Rule Out Pseudo-Resistance

Before labeling DRE, exclude:

- **Misdiagnosis of epilepsy** (psychogenic non-epileptic events, syncope)
- **Inappropriate AED selection** for the seizure type
- **Inadequate dosage** or lack of dose escalation
- **Drug interactions** reducing effective levels
- **Non-adherence** to the regimen

Poor AED tolerance from side effects is **not** formal drug-resistance but can independently justify surgical consideration.

## Surgically-Remediable Epilepsy Syndromes

Clinically stereotyped patterns that are AED-resistant and surgery-responsive:

- **Mesial temporal sclerosis (MTS)** — prototypical; most common focal epilepsy substrate in adults; surgical cure rate 70–85% (Englot 2014; Spencer 2008)
- **Hemispheric disorders**: Rasmussen's encephalitis, hemimegalencephaly, Sturge-Weber, hemispheric encephalomalacia → [[peri-insular-hemispherotomy]]
- **Lesional epilepsy**: tumor-related, vascular malformations, malformations of cortical development (MCD / focal cortical dysplasia), tuberous sclerosis
- **Non-focal** syndromes (Lennox-Gastaut, generalized epilepsies) — not resective candidates; consider **corpus callosotomy, VNS, or DBS**

## Core Evaluation Hierarchy

| Tier | Test | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clinical seizure semiology | Localizing signs (aura, motor pattern) |
| 1 | **Long-term video-EEG** | Capture seizures, ictal + interictal patterns |
| 1 | **High-resolution epilepsy MRI** | Structural lesion (MTS, FCD, tumor, cavernoma) |
| 1 | Neuropsychology | Functional deficit localization, baseline, postop prediction |
| 2 | Ictal **SPECT** (subtraction SISCOM) | Blood flow at seizure onset |
| 2 | **FDG-PET** interictal | Hypometabolism in SOZ |
| 2 | **MEG** | Magnetic source imaging |
| 2 | **fMRI** | Language/motor lateralization |
| 2 | **Wada test** | Hemispheric language/memory lateralization |
| 3 | **Intracranial EEG** (grids, SEEG) | Final localization when Tier-1/2 discordant — see [[intracranial-eeg-monitoring]] |

## Referral Destination — Level 4 Epilepsy Center

The **National Association of Epilepsy Centers (NAEC)** accredits epilepsy care across four levels. **Level 4 Centers** offer the full range of complex resective procedures and intracranial monitoring techniques and are the appropriate destination for surgical candidates. A directory is maintained online.

## Core Questions of the Presurgical Evaluation

Every test exists to answer one (or both) of:

1. **Where is the epileptogenic zone?** — "the minimum amount of cortex that must be resected, inactivated, or completely disconnected to produce seizure freedom" (Lüders 2006)
2. **What deficits would be incurred by resection or ablation of this zone?**

## Concordance Principle

Surgery is offered when multiple modalities **concord** on a single seizure onset zone. Discordance escalates to invasive monitoring or disqualifies. The ideal surgical candidate shows a consistent and stereotyped electrographic ictal onset pattern that concords with semiology, interictal abnormalities, and (when present) anatomic lesion.

### EEG Nuances

- **Interictal scalp EEG** (10-10 or 10-20 montage, 30–60 min): detects interictal spikes, focal slowing, hemispheric asymmetry. Limitations: **skull distortion** of signal, muscle artifact, and poor coverage of **insular, midline sagittal, and inferior cortical foci** (Britton 2015)
- **Long-term video-EEG** (>24 h with synchronized video): provocative maneuvers include **AED withdrawal, sleep deprivation, exercise, hyperventilation, photic stimulation** to capture representative seizures
- **Significant delay between clinical onset and electrographic onset** suggests propagation from an unmeasured source
- **Generalized ictal onset on scalp EEG does not automatically disqualify** — invasive monitoring may reveal a focal origin

### MRI Protocol Essentials

Dedicated epilepsy protocols (Moosa 2015; Miserocchi 2017):

- **3 Tesla** magnet, high soft-tissue contrast, thin slices
- **Volumetric T1** (MP-RAGE or FSPGR for signal-to-noise)
- Axial and sagittal **T2**
- Axial **DWI**
- Coronal and 3D **FLAIR**
- Coronal **T2 gradient echo (GRE / SWI)**

**Non-lesional MRI lowers the likelihood of seizure-free outcome** and influences candidacy (Tonini 2004; Kuzniecky 1993; Chapman 2005) — push to Tier-2/3 evaluation.

## Syndrome-Based Pathways

### Mesial Temporal Lobe Epilepsy (MTLE) with hippocampal sclerosis

- Most favorable surgical syndrome
- Anterior temporal lobectomy / selective amygdalohippocampectomy
- Seizure freedom 60–70% at 2 years
- Class I evidence (Wiebe 2001 trial)

### Lesional extratemporal

- Focal cortical dysplasia (FCD), tumor (DNET, ganglioglioma), cavernoma
- Tailored lesionectomy + epileptogenic cortex
- Functional mapping essential

### Non-lesional focal epilepsy

- Higher reliance on PET, MEG, SEEG
- SEEG hypothesis-driven implantation

### Catastrophic hemispheric epilepsy

- Rasmussen's, hemimegalencephaly, perinatal infarct, Sturge-Weber
- See [[peri-insular-hemispherotomy]]

### Hypothalamic hamartoma / gelastic

- See [[hypothalamic-hamartoma-surgery]]

### Palliative options (when resection not feasible)

- Corpus callosotomy for drop attacks (atonic, Lennox-Gastaut)
- Vagus nerve stimulator (VNS)
- Responsive neurostimulation (RNS)
- Deep brain stimulation of anterior nucleus of thalamus (ANT-DBS)

## Pediatric Considerations

- Earlier surgery = greater cognitive recovery
- Infantile spasms with lateralized lesion → early hemispherotomy
- Neuropsychology essential to counsel on projected trajectory with/without surgery
- Plasticity favors left-hemisphere surgery under age 6–8 y for language

## Counseling and Expectations

- Seizure freedom is the primary goal
- Realistic: 50–70% seizure-free, 20–30% improved, 10–20% unchanged
- Deficits: memory (dominant temporal), hemianopia (occipital), aphasia (dominant perisylvian)
- Continue AEDs postoperatively ≥ 2 years before any taper

## Ancillary Modality Notes

### TMS for Functional Mapping

Transcranial magnetic stimulation can **discriminate "essential" from "non-essential"** task-related cortex by the presence or absence of functional arrest during stimulation — an advantage over fMRI, which cannot make this distinction. Motor-evoked-potential threshold **asymmetry** (higher on one side) can also support ipsilateral seizure onset.

### fMRI vs Wada

- **AAN 2017 guideline** (Szaflarski): fMRI may be used in place of the Wada test for lateralization of language and memory in TLE
- fMRI typically measures **higher right-sided language representation** than Wada in left TLE
- Degree of right-sided fMRI language representation predicts **extent of language recovery after left ATL** — a relationship less apparent with Wada

### MEG

Measures magnetic fields from synchronized neural currents. Superior spatial resolution and less tissue distortion than EEG; useful for **3D dipole modeling** and focal-dipole identification when scalp interictal spikes are diffuse.

## Multidisciplinary Conference — Three Possible Outcomes

After non-invasive evaluation, the team (neurology, neurosurgery, radiology, neuropsychology, nursing, social work) selects one of three paths (Feldman 2019):

1. **Not a surgical candidate** — multifocal or generalized epilepsy; consider VNS or ketogenic diet + optimized medical management
2. **Surgical candidate, proceed directly to resection/ablation** — concordant non-invasive findings define a well-circumscribed epileptogenic focus safely excisable without functional compromise
3. **Surgical candidate requiring invasive evaluation** — **~30–40%** of patients require iEEG (Kovac 2017) because of discordance, uncertainty, non-lesional MRI, or need to define resection margins. Options: **SEEG** (stereoelectroencephalography) or **subdural grid / strip electrodes**. See [[intracranial-eeg-monitoring]]

Document the conference recommendation, implantation hypothesis (if iEEG), and consent conversation.

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- Refer after **2 failed AEDs** — don't wait 10 years
- Concordant Tier-1/2 = operable without iEEG (classic MTLE-HS)
- Discordant evaluation = iEEG or disqualify
- Pediatric epilepsy is an emergency for brain development, not just quality of life
- Palliative options exist when resection is not safe
- Track outcomes with Engel / ILAE class systematically
