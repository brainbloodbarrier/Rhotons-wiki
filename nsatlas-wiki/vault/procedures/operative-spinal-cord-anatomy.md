# Operative Spinal Cord Anatomy

*Spinal Cord Surgery — Operative Spinal Cord Anatomy*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Orientation

The spinal cord is organized in a stereotyped longitudinal and cross-sectional pattern that the surgeon must visualize before entering it. Errors in midline identification or safe-entry-zone choice produce permanent deficits.

## Dorsal Surface

- **Dorsal median sulcus** — true midline; invisible shallow groove; septum posterior separates posterior columns
- **Dorsal intermediate sulcus** — between gracilis (medial) and cuneatus (lateral) in cervical cord; a safe entry corridor
- **Dorsal lateral sulcus** = dorsal root entry zone (DREZ) — site of posterior root filaments

Surgical entry corridors on the dorsal surface (posterior midline myelotomy vs DREZ vs posterior lateral) are chosen based on lesion depth and lateralization.

## Ventral Surface

- **Ventral median fissure** deeper than dorsal median sulcus; contains the anterior spinal artery (ASA) with sulcal branches
- **Ventral rootlets** emerge laterally from the anterolateral sulcus

## Cross-Section at Cervical Level

| Region | Contents |
|---|---|
| Dorsal columns | Fasciculi gracilis (T6 below) and cuneatus (T6 above) — proprioception, vibration |
| Dorsal horn | Sensory gray matter, substantia gelatinosa |
| Lateral corticospinal tract | Motor, laminated (cervical medial, sacral lateral) |
| Spinothalamic tract | Pain/temperature (decussated 1–2 segments below entry) |
| Ventral horn | Motor neurons |
| Central canal | CSF conduit |

## Safe Entry Zones (SEZ)

| Zone | Location | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Posterior median sulcus / myelotomy | True midline | Intramedullary ependymoma, central cavity |
| Dorsal root entry zone (DREZ) | Lateral to dorsal horn | DREZ lesioning for pain |
| Dorsal intermediate sulcus | Between gracilis/cuneatus (cervical) | Lateral tumor / cavernoma |
| Postero-lateral sulcus | Adjacent to DREZ | Lateral intramedullary tumor |

## Vascular Supply

### Anterior Spinal Artery (ASA)

- Formed from two branches of vertebral arteries at foramen magnum
- Runs in ventral median fissure entire length
- Supplies anterior 2/3 of cord
- Reinforced by radiculomedullary arteries, including **Adamkiewicz artery** (T9–T12 typically; left >right)
- Injury → anterior cord syndrome (motor, pain/temp loss; preserved proprioception)

### Posterior Spinal Arteries (PSAs)

- Paired, on dorsolateral cord
- Supply posterior 1/3 (dorsal columns + dorsal horn)
- Plexiform anastomoses — more tolerant of single-vessel injury

### Venous Drainage

- Anterior and posterior median veins
- Radicular veins drain to epidural plexus
- AVMs and dural AV fistulas exploit this anatomy — see [[spinal-cord-avm-surgery]], [[spinal-av-fistula-disconnection]]

## Functional Localization (Sparing)

- **Brown-Sequard** — hemi-cord injury; ipsilateral motor + proprioception, contralateral pain/temp
- **Central cord** — worse arms than legs (medial corticospinal)
- **Anterior cord** (ASA) — motor loss, pain/temp loss, proprioception spared
- **Posterior cord** (PSA, rare) — dorsal column loss

## Identifying Midline Intraoperatively

- Follow dorsal median sulcus visually with microscope at high mag
- Cross-reference with dorsal root entry zones on both sides (symmetry)
- Vein patterns on dorsal surface often cross midline — note before dissection
- Stimulation / D-wave mapping confirms corticospinal tract location

## Intraoperative Neuromonitoring (IONM)

- **MEPs** (motor evoked potentials) — corticospinal tract
- **SSEPs** (somatosensory evoked potentials) — dorsal columns
- **D-wave** — direct corticospinal wave; threshold drops predict permanent deficit
- **EMG** — free-running for nerve root irritation

## Myelotomy Technique

1. Midline identification under high mag
2. Open pia sharply with 11-blade or arachnoid knife
3. Extend with micro-scissors; never force
4. Separate dorsal columns with tiny cottonoids
5. Suction minimal; irrigate copiously

See [[intramedullary-spinal-cord-tumor-resection]].

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Midline mistakes are permanent** — take time to confirm; use both DREZs as mirrors
- **Adamkiewicz** entry level varies; preserve radicular feeders at thoracolumbar junction
- **IONM mandatory** for intramedullary surgery
- **DREZ is safe** for pain procedures, not for entering tumor
- **ASA injury = catastrophic** — avoid ventral surface manipulation in the midline
- PSAs are paired — one can usually be sacrificed if necessary
- Drape veins with cottonoid, don't cauterize casually
