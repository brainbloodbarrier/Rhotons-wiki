# Extramedullary Spinal Cord Tumor Resection

*Spinal Cord Surgery — Extramedullary Spinal Cord Tumor*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Tumor Types (IDEM — Intradural Extramedullary)

| Histology | Features |
|---|---|
| **Schwannoma** | Most common (30–45%); nerve root origin; well-circumscribed; eccentric |
| **Meningioma** | 25–40%; thoracic > cervical; F:M 4:1; dural attachment; often calcified |
| **Neurofibroma** | NF1 association; dumbbell extension common |
| **Myxopapillary ependymoma** | Filum terminale / cauda equina |
| **Dermoid / epidermoid** | Congenital; often midline; cauda |
| **Drop metastasis** | Any level; primary CNS malignancy; multiple lesions |

## Preoperative Considerations

- Whole-spine MRI with contrast — lesion level, cord displacement, dumbbell component
- CT — bone detail if aggressive appearance, calcification, vertebral erosion
- Neuro exam — deficit localization
- Steroids if significant cord edema
- Type and cross
- Discuss fusion for multilevel laminectomy or extensive facetectomy

## Approach Selection

| Tumor location | Approach |
|---|---|
| Posterior / posterolateral | Posterior midline laminectomy |
| Lateral | Hemilaminectomy ± partial facetectomy |
| Ventral (rare) | Lateral (costotransversectomy) or anterolateral depending on level |
| Dumbbell with foraminal extension | Posterior + transforaminal; consider staged anterior if large paraspinal |

## Positioning

- Prone on Wilson frame or chest rolls
- Neutral head in Mayfield if cervical level
- Arms tucked or on armboards depending on level
- Pad prominences; abdomen free to reduce epidural venous pressure

## Incision and Exposure

- Midline incision centered on lesion ± one level above/below
- Subperiosteal muscle dissection; avoid violating facet capsule unless decompressing
- Laminectomy or laminoplasty; pediatric prefer laminoplasty (future deformity)

See [[scalp-incisions]] (analogous skin handling) and general soft-tissue discipline.

## Dural Opening

- Longitudinal midline durotomy over the tumor
- Tack sutures to paraspinal muscle — expose cord + tumor
- Open arachnoid separately under microscope
- Avoid disturbing underlying cord

## Tumor Resection

### Schwannoma

1. Identify parent nerve root (usually sensory)
2. Open tumor capsule; internal debulking with ultrasonic aspirator or scissors
3. Circumferential dissection off adjacent nerve roots, preserving motor roots when feasible
4. Sacrifice the sensory parent root (usually asymptomatic given redundant innervation)

### Meningioma

1. Identify and coagulate the **dural attachment** first (Simpson-like principle for spinal)
2. Internal debulking
3. Develop an arachnoid plane on the cord side — never push against cord
4. Remove capsule from cord surface carefully
5. **Dural management**: resect involved dura (Simpson I equivalent) and duraplasty, or coagulate base (Simpson II) if dense adhesion to arteries

### Neurofibroma (NF1)

- Plexiform involvement — subtotal resection often acceptable to preserve function
- Intraoperative neuromonitoring essential

### Myxopapillary Ependymoma

- En-bloc resection preferred (capsular rupture → dissemination)
- Preserve filum proximally if possible

## Closure

- **Watertight dural closure** — running 5-0 or 6-0 Prolene; Valsalva test
- Consider dural sealant for high-tension closures
- Bone flap replacement as laminoplasty when possible
- Layered fascia, muscle, subcutaneous, skin

See [[dural-opening-and-closure]], [[suturing-and-closure]].

## Intraoperative Neuromonitoring

- MEPs, SSEPs, free-running EMG (rootlets)
- Drop in MEPs > 50% → pause, reassess, consider irrigation, warm saline, BP elevation

## Stability Considerations

- Multilevel laminectomy (especially thoracolumbar junction / cervical) may require **fusion**
- Cervical: >2-level laminectomy → kyphosis risk → fusion
- Thoracic: stable due to rib cage; rarely needs fusion
- Lumbar: consider fusion with bilateral facet sacrifice

## Postoperative Considerations

- Flat bedrest 24 h if extensive durotomy (CSF leak prevention)
- Neuro checks Q1h × 24 h
- Steroids taper
- Early mobilization as able
- MRI at 3 months + annually for meningioma (recurrence), ependymoma (drop mets)

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Microscope + IONM** are non-negotiable
- Devascularize meningioma at the dural base first
- Debulk internally before capsular dissection
- For schwannoma, identify parent root early; sacrifice sensory root if needed
- Watertight dural closure — CSF leak leads to wound complications and meningitis
- Consider fusion upfront for cervical multilevel laminectomy
- Preserve ASA — never dissect ventral midline in lower thoracic without knowing Adamkiewicz level
