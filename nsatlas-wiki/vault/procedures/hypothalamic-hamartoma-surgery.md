# Hypothalamic Hamartoma Surgery

*Epilepsy Surgery — Hypothalamic Hamartoma*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Clinical Context

Hypothalamic hamartomas (HH) are non-neoplastic, heterotopic collections of neurons and glia on the floor of the third ventricle / tuber cinereum / mammillary region. Clinical hallmarks:

- **Gelastic seizures** (pathognomonic mirthless laughter)
- Secondary generalization, drop attacks
- Precocious puberty
- Cognitive / behavioral decline (epileptic encephalopathy)

Medical therapy usually fails — surgery is definitive.

## Delalande Classification

| Type | Attachment | Preferred approach |
|---|---|---|
| I | Horizontal, below floor of 3rd ventricle, pedunculated | Pterional / orbitozygomatic |
| II | Vertical, intraventricular, attached to floor | Transcallosal-interforniceal or endoscopic |
| III | Combined horizontal + vertical | Staged or combined |
| IV | Giant lesion spanning multiple compartments | Multimodal |

## Preoperative Considerations

- High-resolution MRI (thin-slice coronal + sagittal through sella)
- Endocrine panel — baseline hypothalamic-pituitary axis
- Neuropsychology baseline — memory, executive
- Video-EEG confirms gelastic semiology
- Precocious puberty management ongoing

## Approach Selection

### Transcallosal-Interforniceal (Delalande II)

- Bicoronal incision, right parasagittal craniotomy
- Interhemispheric approach (see [[interhemispheric-craniotomy]])
- Split corpus callosum between anterior and middle third
- **Open between the fornices** (interforniceal corridor) — extreme caution for fornix integrity (memory)
- Disconnect or resect the intraventricular portion of the HH

### Endoscopic Disconnection (Delalande II)

- Frontal burr hole (Kocher's point — see [[external-ventricular-drain-placement]])
- Rigid endoscope through lateral ventricle, foramen of Monro
- Coagulate and incise the HH at its attachment to the third ventricle floor
- Less invasive; similar seizure outcomes to open for well-selected Type II

### Pterional / Orbitozygomatic (Delalande I)

- See [[pterional-craniotomy]] and OZ variants
- Open Sylvian fissure — access infrachiasmatic and retrochiasmatic cisterns
- Disconnect the horizontal HH at the tuber cinereum / mammillary attachment
- Preserve perforators to the hypothalamus and basilar apex

### Stereotactic Laser Ablation (SLA / LITT)

- MRI-guided laser fiber placement under stereotactic guidance
- Real-time thermography
- Outpatient / short-stay
- Best for Delalande I–II small lesions; emerging standard

## Operative Principles

- **Disconnection > total resection** — separating the HH from eloquent hypothalamus often achieves seizure freedom with less endocrine / memory morbidity
- Preserve mammillary bodies (memory)
- Preserve hypothalamic perforators
- Use neuronavigation and intraop MRI when available
- Do not chase residual tissue buried in hypothalamic gray matter

## Postoperative Considerations

- **Diabetes insipidus** surveillance (hourly urine output, serum Na) 72 h
- **SIADH** second-week risk
- **Hyperphagia / weight gain** — hypothalamic injury
- Thermoregulatory disturbance
- Memory evaluation (forniceal approach)
- Continue antiepileptics; weaning per epilepsy team over months
- Repeat MRI at 3 months + EEG for seizure status

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- Classify before choosing approach — Delalande guides everything
- Type II = transcallosal or endoscopic; Type I = pterional or OZ
- Disconnect, don't over-resect
- Fornix injury = memory disaster — use strict interforniceal corridor
- Watch perforators around mammillary bodies
- LITT is changing the field — consider when available
- Endocrine and temperature surveillance for at least 72 h
- Gelastic seizure cessation is the primary metric; secondary generalization often resolves separately
