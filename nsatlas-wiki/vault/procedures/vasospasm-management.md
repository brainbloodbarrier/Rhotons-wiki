# Vasospasm Management

Cerebral vasospasm following aneurysmal [[subarachnoid-hemorrhage]] is a delayed, temporally predictable narrowing of the large intracranial arteries that, if unrecognized and untreated, produces **delayed cerebral ischemia (DCI)** and permanent neurologic injury. It is the leading cause of preventable morbidity in SAH survivors and therefore the central task of the neurosurgical ICU from day 3 through day 14 post-ictus.

## Pathophysiology

Vasospasm results from a multifactorial response to subarachnoid blood-breakdown products, not purely mechanical contact. Current understanding emphasizes:

- **Oxyhemoglobin and free heme** → arterial smooth-muscle contraction and endothelin-1 release.
- **Endothelial NO-synthase downregulation** → reduced vasodilator tone.
- **Cortical spreading depolarizations** → microvascular dysfunction independent of large-vessel caliber.
- **Inflammation and microthrombosis** — increasingly recognized as codrivers of DCI even in the absence of angiographic spasm.

The Fisher CT grade strongly predicts vasospasm risk (Fisher 3 > Fisher 1–2), making it a useful early stratification tool (see [[subarachnoid-hemorrhage]] for the grading scale).

## Temporal pattern

| Day post-ictus | Dominant concern |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Rebleeding — aneurysm securement window |
| 4–7 | Onset of angiographic vasospasm |
| 7–10 | Peak clinical DCI |
| 10–14 | Declining risk |
| >14 | Rare; evaluate alternate etiology |

## Monitoring

- **Daily neurologic exam** — any decline in GCS or new focal deficit in the DCI window is vasospasm until proven otherwise.
- **Transcranial Doppler (TCD)** — bedside, non-invasive; MCA velocity >200 cm/s or Lindegaard ratio >6 strongly suggests significant vasospasm.
- **CT perfusion** — demonstrates regional hypoperfusion; the modern standard when TCD disagrees with clinical exam.
- **DSA** — gold standard for confirmation and for concurrent endovascular treatment (see [[imaging-sah-and-aneurysm]]).
- **Multimodal neuromonitoring** (brain tissue oxygen, microdialysis, continuous EEG) — in high-risk / comatose patients where clinical exam is unreliable.

## Tiered management

### Tier 1 — prevention (every patient, day 0)
- **Oral nimodipine** 60 mg PO/NG q4h × 21 days — only drug with level-I evidence for improved neurologic outcome in SAH; mechanism favors DCI prevention rather than angiographic spasm reversal.
- **Euvolemia** — avoid the historical "prophylactic hypervolemia" strategy (associated with pulmonary edema without outcome benefit).
- **Avoid hypotension** — systolic BP kept above baseline, especially after aneurysm securement.
- **Statin therapy and magnesium** — investigational, not standard of care.

### Tier 2 — symptomatic DCI (confirmed clinical or TCD/CTP)
- **Induced hypertension** — norepinephrine to target systolic 180–220 mmHg (or 20% above baseline) until deficit resolves.
- **Optimize euvolemia** with isotonic crystalloid.
- **Hemodilution is not recommended** — the classical "triple-H" has been superseded by "single-H" induced hypertension.
- Maintain normothermia, normoglycemia, normoxia.

### Tier 3 — refractory vasospasm
- **Intra-arterial vasodilator infusion** — verapamil, nicardipine, or milrinone via microcatheter into affected vessel. Effective but transient (4–12 h); often repeated.
- **Balloon angioplasty** — for proximal (ICA, M1, A1, basilar) vasospasm refractory to pharmacotherapy; durable effect but risk of arterial rupture limits use to large vessels.
- **[[ec-ic-bypass]] rescue** is a historical consideration for severe focal ischemia but is rarely used in the acute vasospasm setting.

## Adjunctive neurocritical care

- **External ventricular drainage** ([[external-ventricular-drain]]) for acute hydrocephalus — a shared complication of SAH.
- **Seizure prophylaxis** in the acute phase (short-course levetiracetam is typical).
- **Hypertonic therapy** if cerebral edema or raised ICP supervenes.
- **Decompressive craniectomy** ([[decompressive-craniectomy]]) — rarely needed, reserved for malignant DCI with mass effect.

## Outcome

- Unrecognized DCI: 30–70% severe disability / death.
- With protocolized monitoring and tiered treatment: DCI-attributable infarction falls to 10–15%.
- Nimodipine alone reduces poor outcome by an absolute 5–10%.
