# Spinal Cord Cavernous Malformation Resection

*Spinal Cord Surgery — Intramedullary Spinal Cord Cavernous Malformation*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Pathology

Cavernous malformations (cavernomas, cavernous angiomas) are **angiographically occult** vascular lesions composed of thin-walled sinusoidal vessels without intervening neural tissue. Intramedullary location: 3–5% of all CNS cavernomas. Associated developmental venous anomaly (DVA) in 20–30%.

## Presentation

- **Stuttering myelopathy** — stepwise worsening from recurrent microbleeds
- **Acute neurologic deterioration** — larger hemorrhage
- Back pain, radicular symptoms
- Rarely incidental on MRI

## Hemorrhage Rates

- Annual: 2–5% for spinal intramedullary cavernomas (higher than cerebral hemispheric)
- Rebleed risk elevated in first year after hemorrhage (~15–20%)

## Imaging

- **MRI with T2* or SWI** — "popcorn" appearance, hemosiderin halo
- Cord signal changes beyond the lesion from edema/gliosis
- **DVA identification** — preserve during resection
- Angiography normal (angiographically occult)

## Indications for Surgery

| Scenario | Decision |
|---|---|
| Symptomatic, superficial (touches pia) | **Operate** |
| Symptomatic, deep, in eloquent cord | Individualize; often observe |
| Recurrent hemorrhage | Operate if accessible |
| Asymptomatic, incidental, deep | Observe |
| Asymptomatic, superficial | Consider observation vs surgery per discussion |

## Preoperative Considerations

- Baseline exam (ASIA, modified McCormick scale)
- IONM setup plan (MEPs, SSEPs, D-wave)
- Counsel: transient postop worsening common; recovery weeks to months
- Family aware of DVA preservation rationale — sacrificing DVA can cause venous infarct

## Positioning

- Prone, neutral head in Mayfield for cervical / high thoracic
- Wilson frame or chest rolls
- Abdomen free to reduce epidural venous pressure

## Exposure

- Laminectomy / laminoplasty one level above and below
- Midline durotomy
- Arachnoid opened sharply
- **Identify the lesion's cord-surface signature** — hemosiderin staining, discoloration, swelling, vein pattern

## Entry Corridor

Choose by anatomic proximity:

1. **Posterior median sulcus / midline myelotomy** — for dorsal, central lesions
2. **Dorsal root entry zone (DREZ)** — for lateral lesions abutting DREZ
3. **Posterolateral sulcus** — for dorsolateral intermediate
4. **Dorsal intermediate sulcus** — cervical, between gracilis/cuneatus

Never enter the ventral midline (ASA). See [[operative-spinal-cord-anatomy]].

## Resection Technique

1. Open pia with 11-blade along planned corridor
2. Extend with micro-scissors under high magnification
3. Enter gliotic plane around the cavernoma — **hemosiderin-stained cord is adhesive**; respect it
4. **Coagulate feeders circumferentially** at low power
5. **En-bloc removal** preferred — piecemeal risks residual lesion
6. **Preserve the DVA** — mark it, do not coagulate
7. Irrigate gently; achieve hemostasis with microcrystalline oxidized cellulose (Surgicel) over bed

## Intraoperative Neuromonitoring

- Continuous MEPs, SSEPs, D-wave
- MEP drop >50% or D-wave drop >50% → pause, warm saline, MAP augmentation
- Free-running EMG if near nerve roots

## Closure

- Watertight dural closure 5-0 or 6-0 Prolene
- Laminoplasty in pediatrics
- Layered closure

## Postoperative Considerations

- Flat 24 h
- Neuro checks Q1h × 24 h
- Early MRI at 48 h to confirm complete resection (hemosiderin residue may mimic lesion — radiology experience required)
- Rehab program
- Long-term MRI surveillance for recurrence or new lesion (familial cavernous malformation syndrome)

## Outcomes

- Most improve or stabilize after resection
- Transient worsening common in first 6 weeks; recovery continues over months
- Complete resection essentially eliminates rebleed risk at that site
- Risk stratification worse for recurrent hemorrhages before surgery

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Operate when superficial and symptomatic**
- **Preserve associated DVA** — sacrificing it can cause venous infarction
- Choose the entry corridor by lesion proximity; never enter ventral midline
- **En bloc > piecemeal**
- Hemosiderin margin is adhesive and fragile — dissect patiently
- IONM thresholds absolute — do not push through
- Counsel on transient worsening; the long-term arc usually beats pre-op baseline
- Familial cases (CCM1/2/3) = multiple lesions, screen relatives
