# Ventriculo-Gallbladder Shunt

*CSF Diversion Procedures — Ventriculo-Gallbladder Shunt*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Indications

- **Last-resort** distal terminus when peritoneum, atrium, and pleura are exhausted or contraindicated
- Specifically useful in patients with:
  - Severe peritoneal adhesions / pseudocysts / malabsorption
  - Right-heart contraindications to VA shunt
  - Pleural insufficiency or recurrent hydrothorax with VPl
- Not applicable in:
  - Prior cholecystectomy
  - Active cholelithiasis / chronic cholecystitis
  - Biliary dyskinesia

## Preoperative Planning

- **Right upper quadrant ultrasound / CT** — confirm gallbladder presence, exclude stones and wall thickening
- General surgery consult — joint procedure typical
- Cover infection risk discussion — bile does not inactivate CSF antibiotic prophylaxis as peritoneum does

## Surgical Technique

### Positioning

- Supine, 15° left tilt to shift bowel away from right upper quadrant
- Shoulder bump aligns mastoid–clavicle for tunneling
- Cranial and abdominal fields draped as a continuous column

### Proximal Catheter

Same as VP — Kocher or Frazier point (see [[ventriculo-peritoneal-shunt]], [[external-ventricular-drain-placement]]).

### Gallbladder Access — Open Mini-Cholecystotomy

1. Right subcostal or upper midline mini-laparotomy by general surgeon
2. Identify gallbladder fundus through the peritoneum
3. Place two stay sutures on gallbladder fundus
4. Aspirate bile sample for culture
5. Small fundus incision between stay sutures
6. Insert distal catheter ~5 cm into gallbladder lumen
7. Purse-string suture around catheter entry — watertight seal
8. Second layer: imbricate gallbladder serosa over suture line

### Laparoscopic Variant

- 3–4 port standard cholecystectomy setup
- Grasp fundus, secure with endoloop after catheter insertion
- Preferred by general surgeons comfortable with advanced laparoscopy

## Tunneling, Assembly, Flush

- Head → abdomen tunneling (above clavicle at neck)
- Valve + Ringer's flush
- Intrathecal vancomycin 10 mg + gentamicin 4 mg via valve tap
- See [[ventriculo-peritoneal-shunt]] for shared assembly protocol

## Postoperative Considerations

- Immediate abdominal imaging (ultrasound or CT) to confirm catheter intra-gallbladder
- NPO overnight, advance diet as tolerated
- Monitor for:
  - **Cholecystitis** — RUQ pain, fever, leukocytosis → urgent shunt externalization + cholecystectomy
  - **Biliary leak** — bilious drainage, peritonitis
  - **Catheter migration** out of gallbladder — reflux of bile into catheter / obstruction
  - **Shunt obstruction** from biliary sludge — protein precipitation on catheter tip

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- Rule out gallstone disease first — stones on a shunted gallbladder means disaster
- Insist on general-surgery partnership for fundus access and layered closure
- Watertight purse-string + serosal imbrication — bile leakage causes peritonitis
- Counsel: any RUQ pain → immediate evaluation; this is not a tolerable symptom
- If obstruction recurs from sludge, consider conversion back to a higher-flow site
- Long-term data limited; reserve for salvage
