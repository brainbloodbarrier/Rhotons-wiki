# Intramedullary Spinal Cord Tumor Resection

*Spinal Cord Surgery — Intramedullary Spinal Cord Tumor*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Tumor Types

| Histology | Frequency | Plane |
|---|---|---|
| **Ependymoma** | Most common adult (60%) | Well-defined plane — gross total resection (GTR) feasible |
| **Astrocytoma** (pilocytic → diffuse) | Most common pediatric | Infiltrative — subtotal often; morbidity rises with aggressive resection |
| **Hemangioblastoma** | 3–8%; VHL association | Well-circumscribed; vascular; en-bloc |
| **Other**: ganglioglioma, metastasis, lipoma | Rare | Depends on biology |

## Preoperative Considerations

- Whole-spine MRI with contrast — lesion extent, associated syrinx, hydrocephalus (cervicomedullary extension)
- Neuro exam + functional baseline
- Steroids if significant edema
- Consider functional rehab consultation preop
- Counsel: outcome tracks preoperative function — **operate before the patient is paraplegic**

## Approach and Positioning

- Prone, neutral head in Mayfield for cervical / high thoracic
- Posterior midline laminectomy (or laminoplasty for children) one level above and below lesion
- IONM electrodes (MEPs, SSEPs, free-running EMG, D-wave) placed before positioning

## Dural Opening

- Longitudinal midline durotomy
- Arachnoid opened sharply
- Exposed cord inspected for widening, discoloration, dilated veins
- Cord surface photographed for orientation

## Midline Identification

- Follow dorsal median sulcus at high microscope magnification
- Reference both DREZ lines for symmetry
- Map dorsal veins crossing midline before entry
- See [[operative-spinal-cord-anatomy]]

## Myelotomy

- Midline pia opened sharply with arachnoid knife
- Extend with micro-scissors along the length of the tumor
- Separate dorsal columns with tiny cottonoids
- Do not retract — dynamic separation with suction and irrigation

## Tumor Resection by Histology

### Ependymoma

- Clear **glial-tumor plane** often present
- Devascularize at rostral and caudal poles
- Circumferential dissection; avoid ASA on ventral surface
- Goal: **gross total resection** — recurrence rare; long-term control excellent

### Astrocytoma

- **No clear plane** for diffuse/infiltrative lesions
- Pilocytic astrocytomas may shell out
- Use **internal debulking** with ultrasonic aspirator
- Stop if MEPs drop >50% even with recovery — neurologic preservation > resection completeness
- Biopsy + decompression often the best outcome for diffuse lesions

### Hemangioblastoma

- **Never enter the tumor** — highly vascular, catastrophic bleeding
- Identify **feeding artery** and draining veins
- Devascularize circumferentially, starting at the feeder
- Remove **en bloc** after complete devascularization
- NF2 / VHL screen — plan for multiple lesions

## Intraoperative Neuromonitoring Thresholds

- **MEPs**: sustained loss > 50% amplitude or threshold rise > 100 V → pause
- **D-wave**: drop > 50% predicts permanent deficit → stop regardless of MEPs
- **SSEPs**: less sensitive for motor pathway but track dorsal columns
- Pause maneuvers: irrigation with warm saline, MAP augmentation, release of any retraction

## Syrinx Management

- Associated syrinx often collapses after tumor resection
- Do not shunt primarily — remove the obstruction (tumor) and observe
- Persistent symptomatic syrinx after tumor resection → syringo-subarachnoid shunt

## Closure

- **Watertight dural closure** 5-0 or 6-0 Prolene; Valsalva test
- Dural sealant adjunct in large durotomies
- Laminoplasty preferred in pediatrics
- Consider fusion for extensive multilevel resection in cervical / thoracolumbar junction

## Postoperative Considerations

- Flat 24 h to reduce CSF leak risk
- Neuro checks Q1h × 24 h
- Steroids taper
- Early rehab once cleared
- MRI at 3 months + annually for ependymoma (recurrence), astrocytoma (progression), hemangioblastoma (new lesions)

## Outcomes

- Ependymoma GTR → 5-year recurrence-free > 90%
- Astrocytoma: function preservation is the metric
- Hemangioblastoma: definitive with complete en-bloc resection
- Deterioration postop common transiently; usually recovers to preop baseline if resection principles followed

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Operate before paraplegia** — preop function predicts postop function
- Midline myelotomy requires strict dorsal median sulcus identification
- **Never enter hemangioblastoma** — devascularize first
- Ependymoma has a plane — pursue GTR
- Astrocytoma without a plane → subtotal; preserve function
- **Stop on MEP / D-wave drop** — do not push through
- Warm saline irrigation is your friend for transient monitoring change
- Watertight dural closure — CSF leak = return trip
