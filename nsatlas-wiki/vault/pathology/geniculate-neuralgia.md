# Geniculate Neuralgia

*Source: NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol (contributor: Aqueel Pabaney)*

Also called **nervus intermedius neuralgia** or **primary otalgia**. Extremely rare (<150 reported cases). Paroxysmal ear-deep lancinating pain, triggered by sensory/mechanical stimulation of the posterior wall of the external auditory canal.

## Anatomy

The **nervus intermedius (Wrisberg nerve)** runs between the motor facial and superior vestibular nerves. It carries:

- **Parasympathetic** fibers (superior salivary nucleus) → lacrimal, nasopalatine, sublingual, submandibular glands via geniculate ganglion and superior petrosal nerve
- **Special sensory** (gustatory, via chorda tympani) → anterior 2/3 tongue, floor of mouth, palate
- **Somatic sensory** (dorsal trigeminal tract) → concha, posterior EAC, tympanic membrane

Four segments: cisternal, meatal, labyrinthal, extracranial (~2 cm each). **In the CPA it lies between CN VII and CN VIII (superior vestibular)**; within the IAC it joins the motor facial root.

## Diagnosis — Strictly Clinical

- **Acute paroxysmal stabbing pain centered in the ear** — auditory canal, pinna, retroauricular region, soft palate; may radiate to temporal region or mandibular angle
- **Cutaneous/mechanical triggers** mandatory for surgical consideration — water in shower, air draft on posterior EAC
- Associated with lacrimation, gustatory, salivatory disturbance
- **Rule out**: otitis, malignancy, dental disease, TMJ, Eagle syndrome, CPA mass, [[trigeminal-neuralgia-mvd|trigeminal neuralgia]], [[glossopharyngeal-neuralgia]]
- **Fine-cut MRI CPA** identifies vascular loop on CN VII/VIII
- **BAERs, pure tone audiometry, vestibular testing** support diagnosis

## Indications

- **Medical therapy first**: carbamazepine, gabapentin, lamotrigine; combinations for refractory cases; regional nerve blocks as alternative
- **Surgery only for refractory, confirmed, trigger-positive cases**
- **Postop intractable vertigo from CN VIII manipulation is a real risk** — Cohen-Gadol is highly selective

## Surgical Procedure — Retromastoid Craniotomy + Nervus Intermedius Transection + MVD

Cohen-Gadol prefers **intracisternal transection of the nervus intermedius** (alternative: MVD at the root entry zone).

### Preop

- Comprehensive ENT workup complete
- **BAERs** intraop — mandatory
- Pain must be neuralgic, not neuropathic; cutaneous triggers confirmed

### Approach

- **[[retromastoid-craniotomy]]** — same as MVD for trigeminal neuralgia
- **Supralateral cerebellar corridor** to CN VII/VIII (the infralateral/infrafloccular corridor is used for hemifacial spasm and glossopharyngeal neuralgia)
- **C-shaped dural opening** exposes CN V-X

### Intradural

- **No fixed retractors**; dynamic suction retraction parallel to CN V and CN IX — never parallel to CN VII/VIII (auditory transmission)
- **Rubber-dam glove strip** over cottonoid to glide the cerebellum
- Generous arachnoid opening minimizes hearing loss

### The operation itself — three-step exploration

Cohen-Gadol's **operative philosophy**: explore all three relevant CN complexes because the neuralgias overlap.

1. **CN V root entry zone** — check for arterial loop (SCA most common). If no arterial offender, consider a vein causing nerve discoloration
2. **CN VII/VIII complex** — under magnification, carefully mobilize VII or VIII with a fine dissector; find the **nervus intermedius** between and along the inferior aspect of VII/VIII (1–4 fine strands) with a ball-tipped dissector. **Track the cochlear nerve with BAERs.** Divide the nervus intermedius with microscissors, keeping it off VII/VIII with a fine dissector during cut
3. **CN IX-X root exit zones** — inspect for offending loop (PICA); mobilize with shredded Teflon if present. Atypical glossopharyngeal neuralgia may present as geniculate neuralgia

### Closure

- Dura approximated (not necessarily watertight — low CSF leak rate in Cohen-Gadol's experience)
- **"Wax in, wax out"** mastoid air cells
- Bone flap replaced or methyl methacrylate cranioplasty

## Postoperative Considerations

- ICU overnight, regular ward 1–2 days
- Monitor hemodynamics, neurologic exam, wound
- **Expected side effects**: decreased lacrimation, salivation, taste (from transection)
- **Vertigo** possible, may need rehab
- Most appropriately selected patients achieve pain relief

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Geniculate neuralgia is rare — most intractable ear pain isn't it.** Experience in patient selection is paramount
- **Cutaneous trigger** in the posterior EAC is mandatory for surgical candidacy
- **Explore CN V-X** during every case — syndromes overlap and missed arterial offender on CN IX-X means recurrence
- **Retraction parallel to CN VIII = disabling vertigo.** Retract only along CN V and CN IX axes
- **BAERs during cochlear manipulation** — the nerve is unforgiving
- **Vestibular nerves should never be "aggressively manipulated"** to expose the nervus intermedius
