# Lateral Sphenoid Wing Meningioma

Cushing originally classified sphenoid-wing meningiomas by ridge location: **medial (clinoidal)**, **middle**, and **lateral (pterional)**. The lateral variant is the most resectable of the three — a globoid, convexity-like tumor arising from the lateral third of the greater sphenoid wing. Its operative profile contrasts sharply with [[medial-sphenoid-wing-meningioma]]: no clinoidal involvement, no cavernous sinus invasion, and rare neurovascular encasement.

## Epidemiology

- ~10–15% of all sphenoid-wing meningiomas (the most common subtype)
- Peak incidence 50–60 years; female-to-male ratio ~2:1 to 3:1
- Typically WHO grade 1; hyperostosing (en-plaque) variant more often at the sphenoid ridge and occasionally involves the lateral third
- Radiation-induced meningiomas and NF2-associated lesions can occur here

## Clinical Presentation

| Mechanism | Symptom |
|-----------|---------|
| Temporal lobe / inferior frontal mass effect | Focal seizure, cognitive change, aphasia (dominant side) |
| Dural irritation | Chronic headache |
| Extra-axial mass + edema | Personality / behavioral changes |
| Bony involvement (en-plaque subtype) | Proptosis (if orbital extension) |
| Incidental | Found on imaging for unrelated reason — increasingly common |

Unlike [[medial-sphenoid-wing-meningioma]], cranial nerve palsies (CN II, III, IV, V1, VI) are uncommon because the tumor is lateral to the superior orbital fissure and optic canal.

## Imaging

- **MRI with contrast**: avidly enhancing extra-axial mass on the sphenoid ridge, dural tail along the lateral ridge
- **CT**: possible hyperostosis of the adjacent greater wing (minority); en-plaque variants show "blistering" of bone
- **MRA/CTA**: MCA branches are typically **displaced** but **not encased** (distinguishing feature from medial variant)
- **MR venography**: preoperative venous-phase imaging confirms patency of the sphenoparietal sinus and sylvian veins

## Differential Diagnosis

- Dural metastasis (breast, prostate)
- Hemangiopericytoma / solitary fibrous tumor
- Langerhans cell histiocytosis (in younger patients)
- Lymphoma (dural)
- Plasmacytoma

## Surgical Management

### Goals

- **Simpson grade I–II resection** (tumor + dural attachment ± abnormal bone) is the standard for WHO grade 1
- Lateral sphenoid-wing meningiomas are highly resectable — gross total resection (GTR) is achievable in **>90%**
- Adjuvant radiotherapy reserved for atypical (WHO 2), anaplastic (WHO 3), recurrent, or subtotal-resection cases

### Approach: [[pterional-craniotomy]]

- Standard pterional exposure; the OZ extension ([[orbitozygomatic-craniotomy]]) is usually **not** required
- Craniotomy hugs the sphenoid ridge; ridge drilling flush to dura devascularizes the tumor from meningeal branches
- Dural opening curvilinear around the tumor base; core the tumor before peripheral dissection
- Circumferential dissection from adjacent frontal and temporal cortex with preservation of the pial plane
- Sylvian veins and superficial middle cerebral vein must be preserved — the tumor typically displaces but does not invade them

### Adjunct Technique

- **Preoperative embolization** of middle meningeal artery feeders (see [[cerebrovascular-anatomy]]) can reduce intraoperative blood loss for large or en-plaque lesions
- **Intraoperative neuromonitoring** (SSEP, motor MEP) is optional for lateral-variant tumors (low morbidity)

## Outcomes

| Endpoint | Lateral variant | Medial variant (for contrast) |
|----------|-----------------|-------------------------------|
| GTR rate | >90% | 40–70% |
| Recurrence at 10 y (WHO 1, GTR) | ~5–10% | 10–20% |
| Major neurologic morbidity | <5% | 15–30% |
| Cranial nerve deficit | Rare | Common |
| Mortality | <1% | 1–3% |

## Complications

- Postoperative seizure (reduced by meticulous hemostasis and perioperative AED)
- Transient aphasia (dominant side, retraction of inferior frontal or superior temporal gyri)
- Frontotemporal branch (CN VII) palsy (cosmetic)
- Superficial siderosis from residual hemorrhage (rare)
- Postoperative CSF leak (rare if no frontal-sinus breach)
