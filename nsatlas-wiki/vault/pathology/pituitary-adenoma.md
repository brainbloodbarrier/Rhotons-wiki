# Pituitary Adenoma

Pituitary adenomas (recently redesignated **pituitary neuroendocrine tumors / PitNETs** by WHO 2022) are the most common sellar pathology and the prototypical skull-base endocrine neoplasm. Management pivots on three dimensions: **hormonal activity**, **anatomic extent**, and **visual consequence**.

## Taxonomy

### By size
- **Microadenoma** — <10 mm, intrasellar.
- **Macroadenoma** — ≥10 mm, may expand into suprasellar cistern or cavernous sinus.
- **Giant adenoma** — ≥40 mm; often subfrontal, intraventricular, or clival extension.

### By secretion (clinical + immunohistochemistry)
| Subtype | Syndrome | First-line therapy |
|---|---|---|
| **Prolactinoma** (lactotroph) | Amenorrhea-galactorrhea in women; hypogonadism in men | Cabergoline / bromocriptine |
| **Somatotroph** (GH) | Acromegaly / gigantism | Surgery → somatostatin analogues / pegvisomant |
| **Corticotroph** (ACTH) | Cushing disease | Surgery → mitotane / ketoconazole / SRS |
| **Thyrotroph** (TSH) | Central hyperthyroidism | Surgery + somatostatin analogues |
| **Gonadotroph / null-cell** | Typically "non-functioning" (silent FSH/LH) | Surgery for mass effect |

### By cavernous sinus invasion — Knosp grade
Based on coronal MRI at the level of the cavernous ICA:

- **0**: tumor does not cross intracarotid medial tangent.
- **1**: crosses medial tangent but not intercarotid line.
- **2**: crosses intercarotid line but not lateral tangent.
- **3A/3B**: crosses lateral tangent, superior or inferior compartment.
- **4**: encases ICA fully.

Knosp 3–4 predicts residual disease after surgery.

## Clinical presentation

- **Mass effect**: bitemporal hemianopsia (optic chiasm compression — start with superior-temporal quadrantanopia), headache, CN III/IV/V1/V2/VI deficits from cavernous sinus involvement, hydrocephalus with giant tumors compressing 3rd ventricle.
- **Hormonal excess**: acromegaly, Cushing, hyperprolactinemia syndromes, rare TSH-oma / gigantism.
- **Hypopituitarism**: fatigue, hypogonadism, adrenal insufficiency, central hypothyroidism, growth failure in children.
- **Pituitary apoplexy**: thunderclap headache, ophthalmoplegia, visual loss, acute adrenal crisis; neurosurgical emergency requiring stress steroids and urgent decompression when vision deteriorates.

## Workup

- **MRI sella with/without contrast** — thin-cut coronal dynamic imaging; identifies microadenoma, suprasellar / cavernous extension, diaphragma integrity, optic apparatus displacement.
- **Full anterior-pituitary hormone panel**: prolactin (beware hook effect in giant prolactinomas — dilute), morning cortisol / ACTH / 24-h urinary free cortisol / dexamethasone suppression, IGF-1, free T4/TSH, FSH/LH, testosterone/estradiol.
- **Formal visual fields and OCT** for any lesion in contact with chiasm.
- **Inferior petrosal sinus sampling** for equivocal Cushing disease.

## Treatment

### Prolactinoma
**Medical first**. Dopamine agonists (cabergoline) shrink most prolactinomas and normalize prolactin. Surgery reserved for intolerance, resistance, apoplexy, or CSF-leak risk from medical shrinkage of large invasive tumors.

### All other subtypes
**Surgery first** — [[microscope-guided-endonasal-transsphenoidal-approach]] is the workhorse. Endoscopic technique (binostril, expanded when needed) affords panoramic view; microscopic approach remains valid. Operative principles:

- Preoperative stress-dose hydrocortisone for any suspected hypocortisolism.
- Navigation with MRI fusion; intraoperative Doppler for cavernous carotids.
- Wide sellar floor exposure; inferior-to-superior extracapsular dissection when possible.
- Reconstruct with fat / fascia / nasoseptal flap for high-flow CSF leak (Kassam grade 2–3); lumbar drain adjunct ([[lumbar-drain]]) in large defects.
- For lesions with substantial supra- or parasellar extension, expanded endonasal corridors or combined transcranial / transsphenoidal approaches may be needed. A select few giant adenomas are approached via [[pterional-craniotomy]] or [[bifrontal-craniotomy]] for subfrontal control.

### Radiation and radiosurgery
Adjuvant stereotactic radiosurgery for residual / recurrent functioning adenomas and for [[lateral-sphenoid-wing-meningioma]]-like invasive residuals in the cavernous sinus. Tumor-control rates >90% at 5 years; hormonal remission 30–60%. Hypopituitarism is the principal late toxicity.

## Postoperative management

See [[postoperative-care]]. Specific pituitary concerns:

- **Diabetes insipidus** — urine output, Na, osmolality q4–6h first 48 h; triphasic response classic.
- **SIADH** — POD 5–10 hyponatremia is common; fluid restriction.
- **Secondary adrenal insufficiency** — stress steroids, taper guided by morning cortisol.
- **CSF leak** — saline-over-nose test, lumbar drain, return to OR if persistent.
- **Meningitis** — fever + nasal drainage demands urgent evaluation.
