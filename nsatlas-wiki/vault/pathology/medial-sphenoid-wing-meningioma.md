# Medial Sphenoid Wing Meningioma

*Source: NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol*

Sphenoid wing meningiomas account for **~15–20% of all meningiomas**; half arise from the medial third. Medial sphenoid wing / clinoidal meningiomas are a **heterogeneous group** originating from the anterior clinoid and medial lesser sphenoid wing. Surgical challenge is dominated by the dense neurovascular neighborhood: ICA, optic apparatus, oculomotor nerve, cavernous sinus. See [[cranial-base-surgery-general-principles]] and [[cranial-base-surgery-lessons-learned]] for the philosophical scaffolding.

The **Simpson scale** remains the best predictor of recurrence:

| Simpson | Resection | 10-yr recurrence |
|---------|-----------|------------------|
| I | Complete, with dura and bone removed | 9% |
| II | Complete, dural attachment coagulated | 19% |
| III | Complete, no dural coagulation | 29% |
| IV | Subtotal | 40% |

## Al-Mefty Classification (Anterior Clinoid Meningiomas)

**Group 1 — arise from the short 1–2 mm ICA segment between the upper and lower dural rings (no arachnoid covering).**
- ICA engulfment, grows distally to bifurcation and proximal MCA
- **No intervening arachnoid → densely adherent to adventitia**
- Optic apparatus *has* an arachnoid plane (dissectable)
- Cavernous sinus invasion common
- Lowest surgical cure rate — **plan subtotal + radiosurgery**

**Group 2 — superior/lateral anterior clinoid dura.**
- ICA engulfment *with* carotid-cistern arachnoid plane preserved
- Arachnoid plane around optic nerve/chiasm
- **Most amenable to aggressive safe resection**

**Group 3 — optic foramen origin, extends into optic canal.**
- Symptomatic early → smaller at diagnosis
- Arachnoid plane around ICA, but **none** between tumor and optic apparatus
- Visual decline risk is real; cure less common

**Middle sphenoid wing** tumors grow very large before presentation (temporal lobe mass effect, visual disturbance if medial extension). Small lesions without medial extension are treated as [[convexity-meningioma]].

## Diagnosis

- **Headaches + visual disturbance** dominate: blurred vision, field cut, optic atrophy (optic apparatus compression), diplopia (CN III)
- **Cavernous sinus / SOF invasion** → additional cranial neuropathies
- **Large tumors** → seizures, hemiparesis, cognitive/personality change, dysphasia
- **Hyperostotic (*en plaque*, spheno-orbital)** variants → proptosis, orbital pain — covered separately in [[lateral-sphenoid-wing-meningioma]]

## Evaluation

- **Thin-cut MR** with orbital fat suppression — orbital involvement
- **T2 sequences** usually sufficient for vascular relationships; MRA/CTA only when uncertain
- **CT bone windows** — hyperostosis mapping for Simpson-I planning and custom implant design
- **Catheter angiography + BTO** — only when ICA sacrifice is contemplated. Cohen-Gadol advocates **subtotal + radiosurgery over ICA sacrifice**.
- **Preop embolization** — rarely needed; early extradural sphenoid wing + clinoid resection + dural coagulation devascularizes the tumor sufficiently
- **Neuro-ophthalmology + endocrinology** workup for all symptomatic parasellar tumors

## Indications

- **Surgery is the mainstay.**
- **SRS** — small asymptomatic tumors without mass effect; limited by optic-apparatus radiation tolerance
- **Observation** — small incidental lesions
- See [[cranial-base-operative-corridor-selection]] for corridor decision

## Operative Anatomy

Critical structures in the resection field:
- Anterior clinoid process (ACP) + optic strut
- **ICA**: clinoidal segment between upper and lower dural rings; ophthalmic artery branches just distal; **superior hypophyseal perforators**
- **CN II** — emerges through optic canal; **falciform ligament** compresses swollen nerves
- **CN III** — at risk during clinoidectomy and medial tumor dissection
- **Cavernous sinus** — tumor invasion should be **left behind** (morbidity vs. benefit mismatch)
- **MCA + lenticulostriate perforators** — at the superior pole of the tumor

Meningiomas preferentially **enter the optic canal medial to the nerve** (available potential space).

## Operative Technique — Extended Pterional with Extradural Clinoidectomy

>90% of medial SW meningiomas are managed through an **extended [[pterional-craniotomy]] with extradural clinoidectomy**. Orbitozygomatic is added for significant suprasellar or intraorbital extension.

### Preoperative / induction

- **Prophylactic AED** — loading dose at surgery, 7 days postop, taper if seizure-free
- **Lumbar drain after induction** — critical for early extradural clinoidectomy and gradual brain relaxation
- For **giant tumors with edema**: gradual CSF drainage *after* dural opening to avoid transtentorial herniation

### Extradural phase

1. Extended pterional craniotomy with aggressive drilling of orbital roof and lateral sphenoid ridge
2. **Extradural clinoidectomy** — hyperostotic clinoid is drilled with **continuous irrigation**; optic nerve skeletonized and protected; intraoperative CT navigation for distorted foramen/canal anatomy
3. **Extradural optic nerve decompression** — drill the roof of the optic canal *first* so the nerve is released before any traction is applied
4. **Devascularize** — cauterize tumor's dural base along sphenoid wing and clinoid

### Intradural phase

5. Crescent dural opening; slow CSF egress via lumbar drain
6. **Anterior Sylvian fissure split** — expose distal MCA branches and proximal ICA; these are the landmarks that outline the presumed route of the ICA embedded in tumor
7. **Debulk centrally** with ultrasonic aspirator, respecting the capsule
8. **Identify the distal ICA by following from both ends** — distal MCA branches + proximal ICA at skull base. Gentle bilateral traction reveals the vessel; transect tumor along the arterial path
9. **Protect lenticulostriate perforators** with papaverine-soaked gelfoam pledgets
10. **Optic canal unroofing** (intradural, if not fully addressed extradurally): incise falciform ligament; remove intracanalicular tumor via the medial space *only after* decompression
11. **Tailored intradural clinoidectomy** when needed — hollow out residual clinoid with diamond burr

### Strategic restraint

- **Cavernous sinus extension: leave it behind.** Radiosurgery handles residual.
- **ICA-adherent Group 1 tumor: subtotal.** Adventitial stripping = stroke.
- **Optic nerve**: ring of discoloration at the falciform ligament signals chronic compression — decompress before manipulation

## Closure

- Watertight dura (pericranium or fascia lata as needed)
- **Frontal sinus** — curette mucosa, occlude ostia with temporalis pledgets if entered
- Custom titanium mesh for hyperostotic bone defects
- Lumbar drain 3–5 days for extensive dural work

## Postoperative Care

- **Hourly vision checks** × 24 h
- **Endocrine surveillance** — sodium, cortisol, thyroid
- CT at 24 h: pneumocephalus, hematoma, brain swelling
- **Delayed hydrocephalus** possible — low threshold for [[ventriculo-peritoneal-shunt]]
- MRI at 3 months, then annually

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Arachnoid plane determines resectability.** Group 2 > Group 3 > Group 1.
- **Extradural clinoidectomy + optic canal unroofing before intradural work** — optic nerve is decompressed before anyone touches the tumor.
- **Continuous irrigation during clinoid/optic drilling** — the optic nerve does not tolerate heat.
- **Preserve perforators religiously.** Lenticulostriates and superior hypophyseals are the silent killers of this operation.
- **Subtotal is a legitimate endpoint.** A neurologically intact patient with residual tumor treated by radiosurgery beats a cured patient with a stroke.
- **Never enter the cavernous sinus** for medial SW meningioma.
- **Route the ICA through the tumor using its known endpoints** (proximal carotid + distal MCA) rather than hunting blindly.
