# Hydrocephalus

Hydrocephalus is the clinical consequence of **imbalance between CSF production and absorption**, producing ventriculomegaly that is *active* (causing symptoms or raised ICP) rather than merely *ex-vacuo* (compensatory for tissue loss). It is among the oldest and most common neurosurgical conditions — and the one with the widest palette of operative solutions, from bedside drainage to endoscopic fenestration to implanted shunt systems.

## CSF dynamics — quick review

- Production: ~500 mL/day, chiefly by **choroid plexus** in the lateral ventricles and 4th ventricle.
- Flow: lateral ventricles → **foramen of Monro** ([[foramen-of-monro]]) → 3rd ventricle → **cerebral aqueduct** → 4th ventricle → **foramina of Luschka/Magendie** → subarachnoid space.
- Absorption: **arachnoid granulations** along the dural venous sinuses (chiefly superior sagittal sinus); a small fraction via olfactory nerve lymphatics and spinal meninges.
- Steady-state total CSF volume ~150 mL; full turnover ~3 times per day.

Any bottleneck — mass, blood, inflammation, congenital atresia — produces hydrocephalus.

## Taxonomy

### Obstructive (non-communicating)
Blockage **within** the ventricular system upstream of the arachnoid granulations.

| Site of obstruction | Examples |
|---|---|
| Foramen of Monro | Colloid cyst, septum pellucidum lesions |
| Aqueduct of Sylvius | Congenital aqueductal stenosis, pineal-region tumors ([[pineal-region-tumors]]), tectal gliomas |
| 4th ventricle / outlets | Posterior fossa tumor, Chiari malformation, Dandy-Walker malformation |

### Communicating (non-obstructive)
CSF moves freely through the ventricles; failure at the **absorption** step.

| Etiology | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Post-SAH (see [[subarachnoid-hemorrhage]]) | Arachnoid scarring / plugging |
| Post-meningitic | Inflammatory adhesions |
| Carcinomatous meningitis | Granulation infiltration |
| Normal-pressure hydrocephalus | Idiopathic — older adults |
| Intraventricular hemorrhage | Combined obstructive (clot) + absorptive injury |

### Normal-Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH)
Adams-Hakim triad: **gait apraxia + urinary incontinence + cognitive decline** in an older adult with ventriculomegaly out of proportion to cortical atrophy and normal mean CSF pressure. High-volume lumbar tap or 72-h lumbar drain predicts shunt response.

## Clinical presentation

- **Infants**: rapid head circumference increase, bulging fontanelle, "sunset sign" (upward-gaze palsy), scalp vein distension.
- **Children / adults**: headache (worse in morning, supine), nausea/vomiting, diplopia (CN VI palsy), papilledema, lethargy.
- **Acute decompensation**: Cushing triad (hypertension, bradycardia, irregular respiration), pupillary changes, coma — precipitates emergent CSF diversion.
- **Chronic NPH**: gait-first triad with slow progression over months to years.

## Diagnosis

- **Non-contrast CT / MRI**: ventriculomegaly with Evans ratio >0.3; transependymal flow on FLAIR; rounded third ventricle.
- **High-resolution sagittal MRI** (CISS / FIESTA): identifies aqueductal webs, patency of prepontine cistern — critical for ETV candidacy.
- **Phase-contrast MRI**: aqueductal flow assessment.
- **ICP monitoring**: confirmatory in ambiguous cases.
- **Lumbar infusion test / high-volume tap / trial lumbar drainage**: NPH shunt-response prediction.

Differentiating obstructive from communicating at the imaging step directs therapy choice.

## Treatment

### Temporary diversion
- **[[external-ventricular-drain]]**: bedside frontal horn cannulation via Kocher's point; the workhorse for acute hydrocephalus (post-SAH, post-ICH, post-operative, infection).
- **Lumbar drain**: for communicating hydrocephalus only; contraindicated if any obstructive component (risk of upward herniation).

### Definitive diversion — endoscopic
- **[[endoscopic-third-ventriculostomy]]**: fenestration of the 3rd ventricle floor into the prepontine cistern — the modern first-line operation for non-communicating hydrocephalus with patent prepontine anatomy. See [[principles-of-intraventricular-surgery]] for general intraventricular operative principles.
- ETV success score (Kulkarni): age + etiology + prior shunt history predicts success. Aqueductal stenosis in adults >10 years: ~70% success.
- Complications: basilar injury, memory/hypothalamic injury (CN injury via floor manipulation), CSF leak.

### Definitive diversion — shunts
- **Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) shunt**: most common; peritoneal cavity absorption. See [[csf-ventricular-shunt-reference]] and [[ventricular-shunt-reference]].
- **Ventriculoatrial (VA)**: alternative when peritoneal cavity compromised; risk of shunt nephritis.
- **Ventriculopleural (VPl)**: third-line; risk of pleural effusion.
- **Lumboperitoneal**: used for pseudotumor cerebri or post-SAH communicating HCP.
- **Programmable vs fixed-pressure valves**: programmable allows post-operative adjustment for over- or under-drainage; fixed cheaper and more reliable.

### Tumor-related hydrocephalus
Resection of the obstructing lesion (posterior fossa tumor, pineal tumor, colloid cyst) is the definitive treatment when feasible. Pre-operative ETV is commonly performed in pediatric posterior fossa tumor patients to avoid emergency shunting and remove the need for post-operative shunt dependence in ~50%.

## Complications of CSF diversion

| Complication | Mechanism / management |
|---|---|
| Shunt infection | 5–15% lifetime; re-externalize, IV + intrathecal antibiotics, replacement |
| Shunt obstruction | Proximal (choroid plexus occlusion) > distal; revision |
| Over-drainage | Slit ventricle syndrome, subdural hematoma; programmable valve adjustment |
| Under-drainage | Persistent symptoms; valve adjustment or revision |
| Catheter migration | Plain X-ray series; surgical revision |
| Abdominal pseudocyst | Peritoneal reaction; revision to alternative distal site |

## Decompressive craniectomy caveat
Severe raised ICP from an acute hydrocephalic crisis is primarily treated with CSF diversion rather than [[decompressive-craniectomy]]; the latter is reserved for parenchymal swelling refractory to ICP-lowering measures.
