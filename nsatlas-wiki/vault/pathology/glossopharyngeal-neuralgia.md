# Glossopharyngeal Neuralgia

*Source: NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol*

Glossopharyngeal neuralgia (GN) is **100× rarer than trigeminal neuralgia**, presents in the 5th decade, usually on the **left**, bilateral in 2%. Paroxysmal lancinating pain in territories of CN IX + auricular/pharyngeal branches of CN X: **external ear canal, base of tongue, tonsillar pillar, posterior pharynx, angle of jaw**. Triggered by swallowing, speaking, eating, cold air, tongue/jaw movement.

**Vagoglossopharyngeal neuralgia (~10%)** — paroxysms with **bradycardia, hypotension, syncope, asystole, seizures**. CN IX impulses stimulate CN X dorsal nucleus.

## Etiology and History

- Most **idiopathic** — possibly severe CN IX/X demyelination
- Secondary: CPA/skull-base tumor, elongated styloid (**Eagle syndrome**), calcified stylohyoid ligament, parapharyngeal abscess, trauma, dental extraction, MS, Paget's, Sjogren's
- Pope 1889 first suggested vascular compression; **Dandy** confirmed; **Jannetta 1977** popularized MVD (aberrant vertebral / PICA loops at CN IX-X REZ)

## Diagnosis — Strictly Clinical

- Stabbing or electric-shock in throat, tongue base, angle of jaw, ear
- **Two subtypes**: tympanic (ear) and oropharyngeal
- **Topical pharyngeal anesthesia** relieving pain supports diagnosis
- **Overlap** with trigeminal neuralgia (jaw angle), [[geniculate-neuralgia]] (ear only), superior laryngeal neuralgia — careful history is decisive
- **High-definition MRI CPA** — look for vascular loop at lower CN REZ, styloid length, tumors; **absence of visible loop does not disqualify surgery**
- **Neurologic exam**: CN V–X sensory, uvular deviation, gag/cough reflex, voice quality
- ENT consultation to exclude occult neoplasm

## Indications

- **Medical first**: carbamazepine, gabapentin, phenytoin, oxcarbazepine, pregabalin + antidepressant adjuncts
- GN is **more refractory to medication than TN**; most patients come to surgery
- **MVD — ~90% long-term pain-free** in experienced hands

## Preoperative Considerations

- **BAERs** + lower CN IOM
- Volatile anesthetics < 1% MAC to preserve monitoring
- **Vagal hemodynamic instability risk** — prophylactic oropharyngeal anesthetization + atropine at intubation
- **Cardiac pacing pad** on for patients with preoperative syncope

## Surgical Approach — Infrafloccular Retromastoid MVD

Cohen-Gadol's preferred: **CN IX transection + CN X decompression** via **retromastoid infrafloccular** corridor.

### Positioning and exposure

- [[retromastoid-craniotomy]], park-bench position
- **Modified reverse-U incision**, apex 1 cm below TS/SS junction
- Burr hole at SS edge, 1 cm below TS; craniotomy 1–1.5 × quarter size; avoid craniotomy in elderly (adherent venous dura) — use craniectomy
- Dural opening parallel to SS and posterior fossa floor; **transverse sinus NOT exposed**; leaves cerebellum draped to avoid desiccation
- **Curvilinear scalp incision** mobilizes myocutaneous flap inferiorly, reducing working distance

### Intradural — corridor anatomy

- **Supramedial cerebellar corridor is NOT used** — that path retracts parallel to CN VIII (hearing loss)
- **Infrafloccular (infralateral cerebellar) corridor** — retraction parallel to CN IX
- **Rubber-dam glove** over cottonoid protects cerebellum during dynamic suction retraction
- Identify **petrous bone / posterior fossa floor junction (P)** and advance cottonoid over rubber dam toward lower CNs
- **No fixed retractors** — dynamic suction only; generous arachnoid opening minimizes hearing loss

### Jugular foramen anatomy (Rhoton)

- **Glossopharyngeal meatus** and **vagal meatus** separated by **dural septum**
- Vertebral artery, PICA loops are the classic offenders at CN IX-X REZ
- Choroid plexus + **rhomboid lip** adjacent to CN IX

### MVD maneuvers

1. Sharp arachnoid dissection over lower CNs; identify CN IX at brainstem REZ
2. Inspect for **PICA loop** or vertebral artery offender
3. **Transect CN IX** cleanly at jugular foramen side of REZ — eliminates the neuralgia
4. **Decompress CN X** — mobilize offending vessel along entire length with multiple pieces of **shredded Teflon**
5. **Do NOT rhizotomize CN X** — swallowing/hoarseness unacceptable (abandoned historically)
6. Check for tortuous vertebral artery — may require creative mobilization with Teflon "slings"

### Closure

- Non-watertight dural approximation acceptable in Cohen-Gadol's practice
- **Mastoid air cell wax** — "wax in, wax out"
- Bone flap replace or methyl methacrylate cranioplasty

## Postoperative

- ICU overnight for hemodynamic monitoring (vagal effects may persist)
- Swallowing assessment before PO intake
- Watch for dysphagia, hoarseness, aspiration
- Pain relief usually immediate

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Medical therapy usually fails** — low threshold for MVD when diagnosis is certain
- **Vagal cardiac events** intraop and postop — have atropine drawn and pacing ready
- **Infrafloccular corridor, NOT supramedial** — retraction parallel to CN IX preserves hearing
- **CN IX rhizotomy is the key step** — CN X is only decompressed, never cut
- **Absence of visible vascular loop on MRI does not disqualify** exploration
- **Atypical GN may present with ear pain only** → consider nervus intermedius involvement (see [[geniculate-neuralgia]])
