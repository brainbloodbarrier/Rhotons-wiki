# Medulloblastoma

Medulloblastoma is the most common malignant brain tumor of childhood, accounting for ~ 20 % of pediatric primary CNS tumors. Once treated with a single rigid protocol regardless of biology, it is now the textbook example of molecular-tailored neuro-oncology: four genomically defined subgroups carry distinct presentations, surgical considerations, adjuvant strategies, and outcomes. The neurosurgical contribution — **maximal safe resection without cerebellar-mutism-syndrome morbidity** — remains the single most actionable predictor of survival across subgroups, but every other dimension of care has been rewritten over the past 15 years.

## Epidemiology

- ~ 5–10 cases per million children annually; rare in adults.
- Pediatric peak age 4–8 years; an adult peak in the third decade is increasingly recognized (often SHH subgroup with adult-specific biology).
- Male predominance modest (1.5–2 : 1) overall.
- Hereditary syndromes: **Gorlin (PTCH1, SHH subgroup)**, **Li-Fraumeni (TP53, often SHH-TP53-mutant — most aggressive)**, **Turcot (APC, WNT)**, **Fanconi anemia**.

## Pathology

- WHO grade 4 embryonal neoplasm.
- Histologic patterns (less prognostic than molecular subgroup): classic, desmoplastic / nodular, large cell / anaplastic, MBEN (medulloblastoma with extensive nodularity).
- Origin: cerebellar **granule neuron precursors** for SHH subgroup; cerebellar **rhombic lip / lower rhombic-lip progenitors** for Group 3 and 4; **Wnt-pathway-active hindbrain progenitors** for WNT.

## Molecular Subgroups (Taylor 2012; WHO 2021)

| Subgroup | Demographics | Anatomic location | Key drivers | Prognosis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WNT** | Children 6–12 y, adolescents | Midline / cerebellopontine angle / brainstem-margin | CTNNB1 (β-catenin), monosomy 6 | **Excellent** — > 90 % 5-yr survival |
| **SHH** | Infants and adults > children; bimodal | Lateral cerebellar hemisphere | PTCH1, SUFU, SMO; TP53 in adults (poor) | Variable: SHH-TP53-WT good, SHH-TP53-MT poor |
| **Group 3** | Infants and young children | Midline / fourth ventricle | MYC amplification (in subset), photoreceptor signature | **Worst** — high metastasis, poor survival |
| **Group 4** | Children most common subgroup | Midline | Chromosome 17 isodicentric, MYCN, KDM6A | Intermediate |

The 2021 WHO classification incorporates molecular subgrouping into the diagnostic name (e.g., "medulloblastoma, WNT-activated"). This drives:
- Eligibility for reduced-intensity protocols (WNT and select SHH).
- Targeted therapy (SHH-pathway inhibitors).
- Surveillance and survivorship counseling.

## Anatomic Distribution

- **Midline cerebellar / fourth-ventricular** (most common): Group 3, Group 4.
- **Lateral cerebellar hemisphere**: SHH (especially in infants and adults).
- **Cerebellopontine-angle / brainstem margin**: WNT (a clue to molecular subgroup before pathology).

## Clinical Presentation

### Pediatric (typical)
- **Morning headache, vomiting, papilledema** — obstructive [[hydrocephalus]] from fourth-ventricular obstruction is the dominant early picture.
- **Truncal ataxia, gait disturbance** — vermian / midline involvement.
- **Limb dysmetria** — hemispheric involvement.
- **Cranial nerve deficits** — when tumor extends laterally toward the [[cerebellopontine-angle]] or brainstem.
- **Skew deviation, sixth-nerve palsy** from raised ICP.
- **Failure to thrive** in infants; macrocephaly if open fontanelles.

### Disseminated disease
- **Leptomeningeal metastasis** in ~ 30 % at diagnosis (drop metastases to spinal cord, to cerebral cisterns).
- Symptoms: back pain, radicular signs, communicating hydrocephalus, cranial nerve palsies.
- All patients require whole-neuraxis MRI plus CSF cytology before adjuvant therapy planning.

## Imaging

### MRI (modality of choice)
- **T1**: iso- to hypointense to brain.
- **T2**: variable; midline tumors often relatively hypointense compared to cerebellar parenchyma (high cellularity).
- **DWI**: **restricted diffusion** in the high-cellular tumor — distinguishes from low-grade gliomas with facilitated diffusion.
- **Post-gadolinium**: heterogeneous enhancement, often robust.
- **Cysts and necrosis** common; calcifications less common than in [[ependymoma]].
- **Drop metastases**: sugar-coating of the spinal subarachnoid space, nodular leptomeningeal enhancement.

### Whole-neuraxis MRI
- **Mandatory at diagnosis**: post-gadolinium thin-slice spine MRI to rule out leptomeningeal seeding.
- Imaging precedes lumbar puncture; obstructed flow can produce false-negative LP.

### CSF cytology
- Performed after the obstructive hydrocephalus is treated and after recovery from surgery; not pre-operatively in symptomatic obstruction.

## Differential Diagnosis (pediatric posterior fossa)

| Tumor | Features |
|---|---|
| **Pilocytic astrocytoma** | Cyst with mural nodule, hemispheric, indolent, ~ 6–10 yr |
| **[[ependymoma]]** | Plastic foraminal extension into cisterns; calcifications common; "toothpaste" through Luschka / Magendie |
| **Atypical teratoid / rhabdoid tumor (ATRT)** | Infants < 3 y; very aggressive; INI1 / SMARCB1 loss |
| **Brainstem glioma** | Intra-axial brainstem; characteristic location distinguishes |
| **Ependymal cyst** / **dermoid** / **arachnoid cyst** | Non-enhancing; differential for cystic posterior-fossa lesions |

The combination of midline location, restricted diffusion, robust enhancement, and a young child is highly suggestive of medulloblastoma but molecular confirmation is mandatory.

## Risk Stratification (Modern Pediatric)

Combined clinical + molecular framework:

- **Average risk**: gross-total or near-total resection (≤ 1.5 cm² residual), no metastases (M0), age > 3 yr, favorable molecular subgroup.
- **High risk**: > 1.5 cm² residual, M+ disease, age < 3 yr, large-cell / anaplastic histology, unfavorable molecular subgroup (MYC / MYCN amplification, SHH-TP53-mutant).

## Management

### Surgery — cornerstone
- **Maximal safe resection** drives prognosis across subgroups.
- Approach: [[suboccipital-craniotomy]] with telovelar exposure of the fourth ventricle (preferred over trans-vermian to reduce cerebellar mutism syndrome).
- **Residual tumor at the brainstem** — accept a thin remnant rather than risk catastrophic injury; modern molecular-tailored adjuvant therapy and the survival data both support this trade.
- **Cerebellar mutism syndrome** is the principal post-operative morbidity (~ 20–25 % of pediatric posterior-fossa surgery): transient mutism with emotional lability and ataxia, attributed to bilateral injury of the proximal dentato-rubro-thalamic tract; recovery over weeks-months but often with persistent cognitive and language sequelae.
- **CSF management**: pre-operative [[external-ventricular-drain-placement]] for symptomatic [[hydrocephalus]] is common; permanent VP shunting required in 20–30 % post-resection.

### Adjuvant therapy

#### Average-risk children (> 3 y)
- **Craniospinal irradiation (CSI)** 23.4 Gy (reduced from historical 36 Gy) + posterior-fossa boost to 54 Gy.
- **Concurrent vincristine** during radiation.
- **Maintenance multi-agent chemotherapy** (cisplatin + cyclophosphamide / lomustine + vincristine) × 6–8 cycles.
- 5-year survival ~ 75–85 %.

#### High-risk children
- **Higher-dose CSI** 36–39.6 Gy.
- More intensive chemotherapy regimens.
- 5-year survival ~ 50–70 %.

#### Infants (< 3 y) — radiation deferral
- **High-dose chemotherapy with autologous stem-cell rescue** (multi-cycle); CSI deferred or replaced with focal radiation when feasible.
- **Outcomes** improving with intensive chemotherapy alone in selected molecular subgroups (especially SHH-MBEN); CSI delay reduces neurocognitive cost.

#### Adults
- Generally average-risk protocols are adapted (CSI 23.4–30.6 Gy + boost + chemotherapy); SHH adult-specific disease has its own trajectory with vismodegib trials.

### Targeted and emerging therapy

- **SHH-pathway inhibitors**: vismodegib, sonidegib for SHH-pathway-driven disease — particularly relevant in adult SHH medulloblastoma. Bone toxicity in pediatric patients (premature physeal closure) limits use.
- **MYC-amplification-targeted strategies** for Group 3 are in clinical trial.
- **Reduced-intensity CSI** for biologically favorable WNT-subgroup tumors is the current frontier — preserving survival while sparing late effects.

### Long-term sequelae

- **Cognitive decline** from CSI is age-, dose-, and volume-dependent; the principal motivator for proton therapy and reduced-dose protocols in modern practice.
- **Endocrinopathies**: growth-hormone deficiency, hypothyroidism, gonadal dysfunction.
- **Hearing loss** from cisplatin chemotherapy + radiation.
- **Secondary malignancies** (radiation-induced) in the long-term survivor population.
- **Psychosocial** — survivorship clinics with neuropsychology, audiology, endocrinology, and oncology integration.

## Surveillance

- Brain + spine MRI every 3 months for 2 years post-treatment, then progressively less frequent through 10 years.
- Recurrence is most often local (posterior fossa) plus or minus disseminated; salvage with re-irradiation, intensified chemotherapy, and selective re-resection has limited but real role.

## Surgical Pearls

1. **Telovelar over trans-vermian.** Modern standard for fourth-ventricular tumors; reduces cerebellar mutism syndrome relative to splitting the inferior vermis.
2. **Trade brainstem residual for safety.** A thin residual on the floor of the fourth ventricle is accepted; chasing the last bit of tumor is the classic catastrophic error.
3. **Send molecular tissue immediately.** WNT vs SHH vs Group 3 vs Group 4 changes adjuvant therapy and, increasingly, surgical aggressiveness in borderline cases.
4. **Image whole neuraxis pre-operatively when feasible.** Drop metastases change the protocol; identifying them at diagnosis is far better than discovering at post-resection imaging.
5. **Plan for hydrocephalus.** EVD pre-operatively for symptomatic obstruction; counsel for ~ 20–30 % permanent shunt requirement.
6. **Counsel realistically about cerebellar mutism syndrome.** The 20–25 % rate, the transient-but-incomplete recovery, and the cognitive sequelae must be discussed pre-operatively — families understandably interpret post-operative mutism as a surgical disaster, and prepared expectations matter.
7. **Recognize medulloblastoma as a chronic survivorship disease.** Cure rates in average-risk are high; the cost is decades of cognitive, endocrine, audiologic, and psychosocial follow-up. Hand off to a comprehensive survivorship program.
8. **Distinguish from [[ependymoma]] surgically.** Both midline pediatric posterior-fossa tumors but molecular and behavioral differences inform extent-of-resection decisions and adjuvant planning.
