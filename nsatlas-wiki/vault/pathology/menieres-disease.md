# Meniere's Disease

*Source: NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol*

Characterized by the classic triad: **episodic vertigo (>20 min), fluctuating low-frequency sensorineural hearing loss, tinnitus**, plus **aural fullness**. Pathophysiology = **endolymphatic hydrops** — distension of endolymphatic spaces; K-rich endolymph leaking into K-poor perilymph depolarizes vestibular nerve endings.

## AAO-HNS Diagnostic Categories

- **Certain** — histopathologic confirmation of hydrops
- **Definitive** — ≥2 spontaneous vertiginous episodes ≥20 min + audiometric hearing loss (≥1 occasion) + tinnitus/fullness + other causes excluded
- **Probable / Possible** — partial fulfillment

## Differential

- **BPPV** (no hearing loss)
- **Vestibular neuritis**, labyrinthitis
- **Acoustic neuroma**, endolymphatic sac tumor — exclude with MRI
- **MS, TIA**
- **Migraine-associated vertigo**
- **Cogan's syndrome** — ocular + vasculitic signs
- **Neurosyphilis, Lyme disease** — VDRL/FTA-ABS
- **Oto-renal syndrome** — urine for proteinuria/hematuria

## Evaluation

- **Pure tone audiometry** — classic "fluctuating low-frequency SNHL"
- **Speech discrimination, tympanometry, ENG** (after 2-week vestibular suppressant holiday)
- **ECoG** — SP:AP ratio > 35% during episode supports diagnosis
- **VEMP, CHAMPs, delayed 3D-FLAIR MRI** post-intratympanic gadolinium — emerging
- **MRI** to exclude CPA pathology, MS, ischemia

## Treatment Ladder

1. **Lifestyle**: salt restriction, caffeine/alcohol/nicotine/MSG avoidance, stress management
2. **Medical**: diuretics, betahistine, vestibular suppressants (meclizine, promethazine) for acute attacks
3. **Intratympanic gentamicin** — ~75% complete / ~93% substantial vertigo control (chemical ablation)
4. **Nonablative surgery**: endolymphatic sac shunting/decompression, sacculotomy
5. **Ablative surgery**: labyrinthectomy (sacrifices hearing), **vestibular neurotomy** (preserves hearing)

**~10% of patients are refractory** and become surgical candidates.

## Vestibular Neurotomy — Cohen-Gadol's Operation

**~95% vertigo relief** in appropriately selected patients. Low SNHL risk. The most effective hearing-sparing operation for refractory Meniere's.

### Historical approaches

- House (1961) — **middle cranial fossa** — abandoned (deafness, facial weakness)
- Silverstein (1986) — **retrosigmoid/retrolabyrinthine**
- **Retromastoid retrosigmoid** is Cohen-Gadol's current preference

### Preoperative considerations

- Audiogram + MRI before surgery
- **BAERs** routinely monitored — guides retraction adjustment during CN VII/VIII manipulation
- **Facial nerve monitoring not routine** (Cohen-Gadol has abandoned it as experience grew)

### Operative Anatomy

- **CN VII anterior** to superior vestibular nerve within the IAC
- **Cochlear nerve along inferior aspect** of CN VIII
- **Vestibular component is grayish**; cochlear component is **reddish**
- **Arteriole often delineates the demarcation line** between them
- AICA and PICA loop relationships with CN VII/VIII must be understood
- **Superior and inferior vestibular nerves** can be dissected separately from the cochlear nerve

### Approach

- [[retromastoid-craniotomy]] — identical to MVD for trigeminal neuralgia
- **Supralateral cerebellar corridor** (as for [[trigeminal-neuralgia-mvd]]), not infrafloccular
- Retraction vectors **parallel to CN V and CN IX**, **never parallel to CN VII/VIII**
- Rubber-dam glove + dynamic suction retraction; no fixed retractors
- Generous arachnoid opening over cranial nerves

### Intradural technique

1. Advance cottonoid over rubber dam to petrous-tentorial junction
2. Expose CN VII/VIII complex under BAER guidance
3. **Periodic irrigation** during dissection — microscope heat damages CN VIII
4. **Papaverine-soaked gelfoam** on inferior CN VIII for prophylactic vasospasm relief
5. Develop the cleavage plane between **vestibular (gray) and cochlear (reddish)** components — the arteriole landmark is the key
6. **Section the vestibular nerve** with microscissors — both superior and inferior divisions
7. **Spare the cochlear nerve** — monitored continuously with BAERs

### BAER change protocol

If BAERs change at any time intraop:
1. **Stop dissection, release all retraction**, irrigate
2. Wait several minutes for recovery; **increase BP** if needed
3. **Further open arachnoid** over CN VII/VIII to relieve traction
4. **Papaverine sponge** on the nerves
5. Only then reapply dynamic retraction

### Closure

- Non-watertight dural approximation
- "Wax in, wax out" mastoid air cells
- Bone flap or methacrylate cranioplasty

## Postoperative Considerations

- ICU overnight; monitor for hearing loss, facial weakness, CSF leak
- **Vestibular rehabilitation** is essential — central compensation of unilateral vestibular loss takes weeks
- Many patients have mild dysequilibrium for months; frank vertigo is essentially abolished

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Patient selection is everything.** Atypical symptoms predict surgical failure.
- **Diagnose by hearing pattern** (fluctuating low-frequency SNHL) — not by vertigo alone
- **Vestibular neurotomy preserves hearing**; labyrinthectomy does not
- **Supralateral cerebellar corridor** — as for trigeminal MVD; infrafloccular is for HFS/GN
- **The gray-reddish demarcation with its arteriole** is the cochlear-sparing landmark
- **BAER-driven retraction management** — back off immediately on any change
- **Papaverine-soaked gelfoam on CN VIII** mitigates vasospasm from traction + microscope heat
- **Contralateral hearing loss** is a relative contraindication
- **Intratympanic gentamicin** is a legitimate non-surgical option that often makes neurotomy unnecessary
