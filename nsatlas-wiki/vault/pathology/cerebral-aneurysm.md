# Cerebral Aneurysm

A cerebral aneurysm is a **focal, pathological dilatation** of an intracranial artery. The overwhelming majority are acquired saccular lesions arising at arterial bifurcations of the circle of Willis, where hemodynamic shear stress and a congenitally attenuated media converge. Understanding morphology, natural history, and treatment ladder is core competency for every neurosurgeon.

## Taxonomy

| Type | Mechanism | Typical site |
|---|---|---|
| **Saccular (berry)** | Degeneration of media + internal elastic lamina at bifurcation | ACom, PCom, MCA bifurcation, basilar tip, PICA origin |
| **Fusiform** | Circumferential dilatation, atherosclerotic or dolichoectatic | Vertebrobasilar trunk, M1 |
| **Dissecting** | Intimal tear with intramural hematoma | Vertebral V4, supraclinoid ICA |
| **Mycotic (infectious)** | Septic embolus seeding vasa vasorum | Distal MCA branches |
| **Traumatic / iatrogenic** | Direct arterial injury | Pericallosal at falx edge, petrous ICA |
| **Flow-related / oncotic** | High-flow AVM feeder or tumor invasion | Lenticulostriates, choroidal arteries |
| **Blister** | Thin-walled dorsal ICA | Supraclinoid ICA dorsal wall |

## Epidemiology and risk factors

- Prevalence 2–3% in unselected adults; female predominance after age 50.
- **Modifiable risks**: smoking, hypertension, heavy alcohol, sympathomimetics.
- **Non-modifiable / familial**: ADPKD, fibromuscular dysplasia, Ehlers-Danlos IV, first-degree relative with aneurysm or SAH, coarctation of the aorta.

## Natural history and rupture risk

PHASES score combines **P**opulation, **H**ypertension, **A**ge, **S**ize, **E**arlier SAH, **S**ite to estimate 5-year rupture risk. Key drivers:

- **Size** — risk inflects above 7 mm; >25 mm (giant) carries multi-percent annual risk.
- **Site** — posterior circulation (basilar tip, PCom) > anterior circulation (ACom) > MCA / cavernous ICA.
- **Morphology** — irregular lobulation, daughter sac, aspect ratio >1.6, size ratio >3.
- **Growth on serial imaging** — independent rupture predictor; mandates treatment in most centers.

## Presentation

- **Rupture** — thunderclap headache, meningismus, focal deficit, loss of consciousness → see [[subarachnoid-hemorrhage]].
- **Mass effect** — CN III palsy from PCom aneurysm is a classic emergency warning sign; CN VI palsy from cavernous ICA; brainstem compression from giant basilar.
- **Sentinel headache** — minor leak days before major rupture in ~10–40% of cases.
- **Incidental** — found on imaging performed for headache, trauma, unrelated symptoms.

## Imaging

- **CTA** — first-line, detects aneurysms ≥3 mm.
- **DSA** — gold standard for anatomic detail, parent vessel geometry, collaterals, balloon test occlusion.
- **MRA** — reasonable for screening in high-risk families; avoids radiation and contrast.
- **Vessel-wall MRI** — enhancement of the aneurysm wall correlates with instability.

## Treatment decisions

Management paradigm:

1. **Observe** small, stable, low-PHASES anterior-circulation aneurysms in older patients.
2. **Secure** all ruptured aneurysms within 24–72 h (vasospasm window).
3. **Secure** unruptured aneurysms when rupture risk > procedural risk.

### Microsurgical clipping
Preferred for MCA bifurcation, wide-neck aneurysms, those requiring hematoma evacuation, young patients with durable life expectancy. Standard operative corridors: [[pterional-craniotomy]] (most anterior-circulation), [[orbitozygomatic-craniotomy]] (basilar apex, complex skull-base), [[retromastoid-craniotomy]] (PICA, vertebral), [[bifrontal-craniotomy]] (high ACom). [[Subarachnoid-dissection]] of the Sylvian fissure exposes carotid, M1, ACA. [[Lacerated-dural-venous-sinus-repair]] techniques inform management of intraoperative rupture.

### Endovascular therapy
Primary coiling, balloon-assisted, stent-assisted, or flow diversion. Favored in posterior circulation, elderly, poor-grade SAH, lesions surgically inaccessible. ISAT long-term data show durable survival benefit in coilable anterior-circulation aneurysms, tempered by modestly higher retreatment rates.

### Bypass and deconstruction
Complex giant, fusiform, blister, and mycotic aneurysms may require parent-vessel sacrifice with [[ec-ic-bypass]] revascularization (STA-MCA, radial artery interposition, high-flow saphenous vein).

## Postoperative care

See [[postoperative-care]] and [[vasospasm-management]]. Ruptured aneurysms require ICU monitoring for vasospasm (POD 3–14), hydrocephalus (may need [[external-ventricular-drain]] or eventual [[ventriculo-peritoneal-shunt]]), hyponatremia, and delayed cerebral ischemia.
