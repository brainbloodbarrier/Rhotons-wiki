# Pineal Region Tumors

Pineal region tumors are a heterogeneous group of neoplasms arising from the pineal gland itself, surrounding glial tissue, meninges, or ectopic germ cell rests. They account for <1% of adult intracranial tumors but up to 3–8% in pediatric series, with a strong male predominance in germ cell tumors. Their anatomical location — immediately posterior to the third ventricle and superior to the tectal plate — produces a characteristic clinical syndrome of obstructive hydrocephalus and dorsal midbrain (Parinaud) signs. Workup always includes serum and CSF tumor markers, MRI with contrast, and a thoughtful decision about biopsy versus upfront resection.

## Histologic Categories

| Group | Subtypes | Notes |
|-------|----------|-------|
| Germ cell tumors | Germinoma, teratoma, embryonal carcinoma, yolk sac tumor, choriocarcinoma, mixed GCT | Most common pediatric; radiosensitive (germinoma); HCG/AFP markers |
| Pineal parenchymal tumors | Pineocytoma (Gr I), PPTID (intermediate), pineoblastoma (Gr IV) | Arise from pinealocytes; wide biology |
| Glial tumors | Pilocytic astrocytoma, tectal glioma, GBM, ependymoma | Often adjacent tectum or third ventricle |
| Meningioma | Falcotentorial, velum interpositum | Dural origin |
| Others | Epidermoid, dermoid, vascular (cavernoma, AVM), papillary tumor of pineal region (PTPR) | PTPR rare but distinctive |

## Clinical Presentation

- **Obstructive hydrocephalus** from aqueductal compression — headache, vomiting, papilledema, gait disturbance.
- **Parinaud syndrome** (dorsal midbrain syndrome) — upgaze paralysis, light-near dissociation, convergence-retraction nystagmus, lid retraction (Collier sign).
- **Precocious puberty** — β-HCG-secreting choriocarcinoma or germinoma.
- **Ataxia, tremor** — cerebellar compression in large tumors.
- **Pineal apoplexy** — sudden hemorrhage; rare.

## Imaging

- **MRI with and without contrast** is the study of choice.
- **Germinoma** — isointense, avidly enhancing, often associated with synchronous suprasellar lesion (bifocal tumor).
- **Teratoma** — heterogeneous with fat, calcium, cysts.
- **Pineocytoma** — well-circumscribed, uniform enhancement.
- **Pineoblastoma** — aggressive, heterogeneous, leptomeningeal dissemination — screen whole neuraxis.
- **Meningioma** — dural tail along tentorium/falx.
- **Calcification >1 cm in children <6 y** is abnormal and suggests tumor.

## Tumor Markers (Serum + CSF)

| Marker | Positive in |
|--------|-------------|
| β-HCG | Choriocarcinoma, some germinomas, embryonal CA |
| AFP | Yolk sac tumor, embryonal CA, immature teratoma |
| PLAP | Germinoma |

Marker-positive germ cell tumors can be treated without biopsy; marker-negative masses require tissue diagnosis.

## Management Algorithm

1. **Establish tissue diagnosis** — endoscopic biopsy (often combined with [[endoscopic-third-ventriculostomy]]) or stereotactic biopsy; occasionally open resection serves as first-line.
2. **Treat hydrocephalus** — ETV is preferred over ventriculoperitoneal shunt because it avoids peritoneal seeding of CSF-disseminated tumors and does not require hardware.
3. **Definitive treatment** — depends on histology:
   - **Germinoma** → radiotherapy (often with chemo); cure rates >90%.
   - **Non-germinomatous germ cell tumor (NGGCT)** → chemotherapy + radiation; resection of residual teratomatous elements (growing teratoma syndrome).
   - **Pineocytoma** → gross total resection curative.
   - **PPTID** → resection + adjuvant therapy.
   - **Pineoblastoma** → maximal safe resection + craniospinal radiation + chemotherapy.
   - **Meningioma / benign** → resection.

## Surgical Approaches

- **Infratentorial supracerebellar (Krause)** — [[paramedian-supracerebellar-craniotomy]] or [[midline-supracerebellar-craniotomy]]; ideal for lesions below the vein of Galen.
- **Occipital transtentorial (Poppen)** — for lesions above the vein of Galen or extending supratentorially; requires tentorial incision.
- **Interhemispheric transcallosal transvelum-interpositum** — for lesions filling the posterior third ventricle and extending anteriorly.
- **Endoscopic biopsy ± ETV** — frequently the first operative step; establishes diagnosis and treats hydrocephalus in one setting.

## Complications

- Deep venous injury (vein of Galen, internal cerebral veins, basal veins of Rosenthal) → bithalamic infarction.
- Tectal injury → worsened Parinaud syndrome, extraocular palsy.
- Occipital retraction → homonymous hemianopsia (Poppen approach).
- Cerebellar retraction, air embolism (sitting position).
- CSF leak, meningitis, subdural hygroma.
