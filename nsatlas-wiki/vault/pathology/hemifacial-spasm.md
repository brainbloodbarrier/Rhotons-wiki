# Hemifacial Spasm

*Source: NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol*

**Hemifacial spasm (HFS)** is unilateral involuntary clonic/tonic twitching of facial muscles innervated by CN VII. Starts periorbital (orbicularis oculi), spreads to the rest of the hemiface and platysma. Symptoms **persist during sleep**. "Ticking" sound on the affected side = stapedius contraction.

- Prevalence **~11/100,000**
- 2× more common in **women**
- Onset in the **4th–5th decade**
- **~2% bilateral**
- Average **8-year delay** to definitive treatment
- Not life-threatening — but psychosocial burden and binocular vision compromise from prolonged orbicularis tonic spasm are real

## Pathophysiology

- **Neurovascular conflict at CN VII root exit zone** (REZ) — the **PICA, AICA, or vertebral artery (VA)** loops are typical offenders
- The REZ is uniquely vulnerable: covered only by **arachnoid** (no epineurium), **no connective tissue septa between fascicles**, central-to-peripheral myelin transition
- **Peripheral hypothesis**: ephaptic excitation at REZ
- **Central hypothesis**: facial motor nucleus hyperexcitability
- Likely combined; **vascular compression is contributory, not invariably primary**

## Differential / Mimickers

- **Blepharospasm** — bilateral, symmetric eyelid contractions
- **Facial nerve tic** — complex, coordinated, switches sides
- **Postparalytic synkinesia** (post Bell's / post acoustic surgery)
- **Oromandibular dystonia**, focal seizures, hemimasticatory spasm
- **Red flag — hearing loss** → rule out CPA structural lesion (schwannoma, meningioma, epidermoid, arachnoid cyst)
- Brainstem glioma, MS, brainstem stroke can mimic

## Diagnosis — Clinical

- Careful history: onset, progression, Botox history, Bell's palsy history
- **MRI posterior fossa** to exclude CPA lesion; high-res T2 may show vascular loop at CN VII REZ
- **Absence of visible loop does not disqualify exploration** if diagnosis is clinically certain
- MRI also screens for **tortuous VA** that may not be safely mobilized — patient counseled on lower cure rate

## Medical Therapy

- Carbamazepine, clonazepam, baclofen, gabapentin — **disappointing**
- **Botulinum toxin** — symptomatic only, 3–6 month cycles, recurs at end of cycle; may partially damage motor terminals → residual post-MVD weakness

## Indications

- Severe HFS > **1–2 years** (some remit spontaneously)
- **Contralateral hearing loss is a contraindication** — HFS MVD has a <5% functional hearing-loss risk, unacceptable on an only-hearing ear
- Cosmetic disfigurement and failed Botox are the usual drivers
- **Operator experience** is as important as patient selection
- **80–90% durable spasm relief** in experienced hands

## Preoperative Considerations

- **BAERs** — mandatory (unlike MVD for TN, where Cohen-Gadol omits BAERs); **latency of Peak V** is the best cochlear injury indicator
- **Lateral Spread Reflex (LSR)** — electrical stim of zygomatic/temporal CN VII branch → response in mentalis. **LSR disappearance intraop** confirms adequate decompression. **Persistent LSR ≠ failure** if a convincing vascular offender was addressed
- Volatile anesthetic < 1% MAC
- Prone / park-bench / lateral — **never prone** (Cohen-Gadol)

## Surgical Approach — Infrafloccular Retromastoid MVD

Corridor identical to [[glossopharyngeal-neuralgia|MVD for glossopharyngeal neuralgia]] — **infralateral / infrafloccular**, not supramedial.

### Steps

1. [[retromastoid-craniotomy]]; reverse-U incision; single burr hole at TS/SS junction; small craniotomy (quarter-sized)
2. Dural opening parallel to SS and posterior fossa floor — **no transverse sinus exposure**
3. Rubber-dam glove + cottonoid; dynamic suction retraction **parallel to CN IX**, not parallel to CN VIII
4. Sharp arachnoid dissection over lower CNs; climb proximally along CN VII to the **REZ at the pontomedullary junction**
5. Identify offender — PICA is most common; AICA and VA also possible
6. **Mobilize the vessel with multiple pieces of shredded Teflon along its entire length** — not just at the point of contact
7. Confirm **LSR disappearance** (informative, not mandatory)
8. Inspect CN V and CN IX-X while there (overlap with other neuralgias)

### Special case — tortuous VA

- "Sling" technique: Teflon pledgets tethered to petrous dura to lift the VA off the REZ
- Discussed preop with patient; lower cure rate

### Closure

- Non-watertight dural approximation
- **"Wax in, wax out"** mastoid
- Bone flap or methacrylate cranioplasty

## Postoperative

- ICU overnight, regular ward 1–2 days
- **Delayed spasm resolution** — spasms may persist for weeks before remission; patient counseled
- Monitor for facial weakness, hearing loss, CSF leak

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **BAERs + LSR** — the two electrophysiologic tools that define the HFS operation
- **Hearing loss is the feared complication** (<5%); **contralateral deafness is a contraindication**
- **Decompress along entire length** of offending loop — not just a point Teflon pledget
- **Delayed resolution is common** — do not reoperate early
- **Rule out CPA tumor** if any hearing loss on exam — MRI before MVD always
- **Tortuous VA** warrants sling technique and realistic counseling
