# Convexity Meningioma

A convexity meningioma is defined by a dural base on the **lateral convexity of the cerebral hemisphere** — excluding the superior sagittal sinus region (parasagittal), falx (falcine), and any skull-base attachment. Topographically these are the most straightforward meningiomas to resect; they are the teaching archetype for the principles of dural-based tumor surgery.

## Epidemiology

- ~15–20% of all intracranial meningiomas
- Female-to-male ratio ~2:1
- Peak incidence 50–70 years
- Majority WHO grade 1; atypical (WHO 2) and anaplastic (WHO 3) variants over-represented in recurrences

## Topographic Subclassification (Cushing)

| Subtype | Location |
|---------|----------|
| Precentral | Anterior to motor strip — frontal convexity |
| Central | At or adjacent to motor cortex — eloquent |
| Postcentral | Parietal convexity |
| Paramedian | Edge of superior frontal sulcus — near but not at SSS |
| Temporal | Temporal convexity |
| Occipital | Occipital convexity |

Eloquent-cortex subtypes (central, dominant-side temporal) warrant additional workup (see below).

## Clinical Presentation

- **Seizure** is the most common presenting symptom (~50%)
- Headache, hemiparesis, cognitive change, or focal deficit depending on site
- Increasingly, **incidental** discovery on imaging for unrelated complaints
- Peritumoral edema is typical — a minority have extensive edema disproportionate to size, sometimes a hallmark of secretory or atypical subtypes

## Imaging

- **MRI with contrast:** homogeneous avidly-enhancing dural-based mass with a tapered "dural tail"; T2 signal variable; CSF cleft on heavily T2-weighted sequences separating tumor from cortex
- **CT:** isodense-to-hyperdense pre-contrast; ~10–15% show calcifications; may show localized hyperostosis of adjacent inner table
- **MRA / MRV:** defines arterial feeders (typically middle meningeal branches; see [[cerebrovascular-anatomy]]) and excludes critical venous structures (rolandic vein, vein of Labbé, vein of Trolard)
- **Functional MRI / DTI:** for lesions near eloquent cortex (central convexity, dominant Broca/Wernicke adjacency)

## Differential Diagnosis

- Dural metastasis (breast, prostate)
- Hemangiopericytoma / solitary fibrous tumor — aggressive, less well-defined dural base
- Lymphoma (dural)
- Hemangioblastoma (rare convexity location)
- Langerhans cell histiocytosis

## Surgical Management

### Goals

- **Simpson grade I** resection (tumor + dural base + involved bone) is the curative standard
- Convexity tumors allow grade I in **>95%** of cases — no major neurovascular obstacle typically
- Residual hyperostotic bone is drilled / excised and replaced with cranioplasty

### Planning

- Tumor-centered craniotomy with 1–2 cm dural margin
- **Image-guided navigation** for small tumors and for precise dural-base mapping in eloquent cortex
- **Preoperative embolization** considered for large, highly vascular tumors (>5 cm) supplied by MMA

### Approach Choice

- Standard tumor-centered craniotomy is sufficient for most
- **[[parasagittal-craniotomy]]** is used when the tumor abuts the sagittal sinus region (technically a parasagittal rather than pure convexity tumor)
- **[[parietal-craniotomy]]**, **[[occipital-craniotomy]]**, or temporal craniotomy by location
- Eloquent-cortex cases may require awake craniotomy with cortical mapping

### Operative Principles

1. Dura opened circumferentially around the tumor, leaving a cuff
2. **Early devascularization** by coagulating the dural base before debulking
3. Internal debulking with ultrasonic aspirator
4. Circumferential dissection preserving the pial-arachnoid plane — the CSF cleft is the anatomic safety layer
5. **Dural base excision** (Simpson I) with primary dural closure or pericranial/dural-substitute graft

## Outcomes

| Endpoint | Convexity variant | Parasagittal (contrast) |
|----------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| Simpson I resection | >95% | 60–80% |
| Recurrence at 10 y (WHO 1, Simpson I) | ~3–5% | 10–15% |
| Major neurologic morbidity | <5% | 10–20% |
| Mortality | <1% | 1–2% |

## Complications

- **Seizure** (perioperative; lower after complete resection)
- **Hemorrhage in tumor bed** — vigilant intraoperative hemostasis; early postoperative imaging if deterioration
- **Focal deficit** (motor, sensory, language) for eloquent-cortex cases
- **Cortical venous infarction** — mitigated by preoperative MRV and careful venous preservation
- **Wound complications and subcutaneous CSF collections**

## Adjuvant Therapy

- **WHO 1, Simpson I**: observation
- **WHO 1, Simpson III–V (subtotal)**: observation or stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) for residual
- **WHO 2 (atypical)**: adjuvant fractionated RT increasingly recommended even after GTR
- **WHO 3 (anaplastic)**: adjuvant RT standard; systemic therapy on trial
