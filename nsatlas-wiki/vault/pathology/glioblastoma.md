# Glioblastoma

Glioblastoma (GBM) is the **most aggressive and most common** malignant primary brain tumor in adults. Under the 2021 WHO CNS5 classification, the diagnosis requires an **IDH-wildtype** diffuse astrocytoma showing either microvascular proliferation, necrosis, TERT promoter mutation, EGFR amplification, or +7/−10 chromosomal signature. IDH-mutant grade-4 astrocytomas are now classified separately and carry a better prognosis.

## Epidemiology and risk

- Peak incidence: sixth to seventh decade (median ~65 years); slight male predominance.
- Accounts for ~15% of all primary brain tumors and ~60% of adult astrocytomas.
- Few modifiable risk factors — prior therapeutic ionizing radiation is the only established environmental cause. Genetic syndromes (Li-Fraumeni, Lynch, Turcot, NF1) are rare but informative.

## Molecular diagnosis

- **IDH-wildtype** (by definition in CNS5).
- **MGMT promoter methylation** — predicts temozolomide response; tested on every case.
- **TERT promoter mutation**, **EGFR amplification**, **+7/−10** — diagnostic in the absence of necrosis / microvascular proliferation.
- **ATRX retained**; **p53** wildtype in most primary GBM (differs from secondary / IDH-mutant lineage).

## Imaging

On MRI GBM is a **heterogeneous, ring-enhancing, necrotic** mass with vasogenic edema that respects white matter tracts. DWI shows restricted diffusion in hypercellular rim; MR spectroscopy shows elevated choline/NAA ratio. Tumor cells extend well beyond the contrast-enhancing rim — **T2/FLAIR volume** reflects the true infiltrative territory and should inform the resection plan when eloquence permits.

## Surgical principles

Extent of resection is the most powerful modifiable prognostic variable. Targets for newly diagnosed GBM:

- **>95% contrast volume** — survival advantage (Sanai 2011, Lacroix 2001).
- **Supratotal (FLAIR) resection** — further survival gain when eloquence allows.
- Residual <5 cm³ considered gross-total equivalent.

Techniques:

- **Awake mapping** for dominant-hemisphere or motor-strip tumors.
- **5-ALA fluorescence** doubles rate of complete resection (ALA-Glioma study).
- **Intraoperative MRI / ultrasound** reduces residual disease.
- **Tractography + DTI** for corticospinal tract, arcuate fasciculus, optic radiations.
- [[burr-holes-and-bone-flaps]], [[dural-opening-and-closure]], [[intraoperative-hemostasis]] — standard operative building blocks.
- For unresectable or multifocal deep-seated lesions: [[stereotactic-biopsy]] for tissue diagnosis.

Approach selection follows lobe of origin — [[pterional-craniotomy]] for frontotemporal/insular, [[parasagittal-craniotomy]] for frontal convexity, [[parietal-craniotomy]] for parietal convexity, [[temporal-subtemporal-craniotomy]] for deep mesial-temporal lesions.

## Adjuvant therapy — Stupp protocol

- **Concurrent phase**: 60 Gy in 30 fractions + daily temozolomide 75 mg/m² for 6 weeks.
- **Adjuvant phase**: six cycles of temozolomide 150–200 mg/m² days 1–5 of a 28-day cycle.
- **Tumor-treating fields (TTFields / Optune)** added to adjuvant phase — phase III survival benefit (EF-14 trial).
- MGMT-methylated tumors respond best; MGMT-unmethylated elderly patients may be candidates for hypofractionated radiation ± temozolomide or clinical trials.

## Recurrence and salvage

~80% of recurrences lie within 2 cm of the resection cavity. Salvage ladder — re-resection when feasible, [[radiation-necrosis]] must be distinguished from true progression (MR perfusion, amino-acid PET), bevacizumab for symptomatic edema, lomustine, re-irradiation, clinical trials. Leptomeningeal spread and distant parenchymal metastasis are terminal events.

## Prognosis

- Median overall survival: 15–18 months with full trimodal therapy.
- 2-year survival: ~25%; 5-year: ~5%.
- Favorable modifiers: younger age, high KPS, MGMT methylation, gross-total resection, no residual enhancement.
