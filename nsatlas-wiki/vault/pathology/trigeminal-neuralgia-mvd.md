# Trigeminal Neuralgia — Microvascular Decompression

*Source: NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol*

**"One of the worst pains that can affect a human being."** MVD for classic TN is one of the most satisfying operations in neurosurgery — when the patient is correctly selected.

- Annual incidence: **5.7/100,000 women, 2.5/100,000 men**
- Peak age **50–60**, rising with age
- ~**10%** refractory to medication → surgical candidates
- **~80% pain-free at 10–20 years** after MVD, 10% recurrence

## Pathophysiology

- **Neurovascular conflict at CN V REZ** — demyelination → ephaptic transmission (peripheral hypothesis)
- **Central hypothesis**: trigeminal nucleus hyperactivity contributes
- Offending vessels:
  - **SCA — 75%** (most common)
  - **AICA — 10%**
  - **Vein — 68% contributing, 12% sole offender**
- **Fiber lamination** in the root predicts symptom distribution:
  - **Medial compression** → V2
  - **Lateral/caudal compression** → V3
  - **Cranial compression** → V1 (rare)

## Diagnosis — Clinical

**Classic pain character**: unilateral, episodic, severe, stabbing, shock-like, lancinating. Triggered by **tactile pressure, chewing, brushing, air breeze, shaving**. "Neuralgic" — not "neuropathic" (burning + numbness).

- **Remissions shorten, attacks lengthen** over time
- 3 to 70 attacks/day; refractory period common; **nocturnal pain in ~1/3**
- Isolated V1 neuralgia is unusual
- **Positive response to carbamazepine/gabapentin supports the diagnosis** and predicts favorable MVD outcome
- **Ask about pain character at inception** — medication changes it over time

### Red flags (secondary TN — rule out MS, tumor, perineural spread)

- Significant sensory deficit
- Deafness / ear symptoms
- Refractory to carbamazepine
- Skin/oral lesions in trigeminal distribution
- **Isolated V1 TN**
- **Bilateral TN**
- **Age < 40** — question the diagnosis; investigate for MS

### Differential

- Dental/TMJ/eye disease
- Tolosa-Hunt, trigeminal autonomic cephalgias (cluster, paroxysmal hemicrania)
- Migraine / tension-type headache
- CPA tumor (meningioma, schwannoma, **epidermoid**)
- MS, brainstem ischemia/demyelination
- **Tic convulsif** — TN + [[hemifacial-spasm]] coexist

## Imaging

- **MRI mandatory** — excludes meningioma, acoustic neuroma, epidermoid, MS
- **High-res T2W** often shows vascular loop on CN V
- **Absence of loop does not disqualify MVD** — Cohen-Gadol routinely finds arterial offenders intraoperatively in MRI-negative cases

## Medical Therapy

1. **Carbamazepine** — 100% initial relief in 70%; CNS side effects, drug interactions
2. **Oxcarbazepine** — similar efficacy, better tolerability, fewer interactions
3. **Baclofen, lamotrigine** — for carbamazepine allergy

## Indications for MVD

- **Refractory to medical therapy ≥ 1 year** (acute TN not an indication)
- **< 70 years, no prohibitive comorbidity**
- Typical neuralgic pain character
- **Atypical/neuropathic pain is NOT an MVD indication** — may worsen after surgery

### Algorithm for poor MVD candidates

- **Age > 70 or prohibitive comorbidity** → percutaneous procedure
  - **Balloon compression rhizotomy** — Cohen-Gadol's preferred (easy, immediate)
  - Radiofrequency lesioning
  - Glycerol rhizolysis (if prior success)
- **High anesthesia risk or MS + no cognitive deficit** → RF ablation
- **High risk + cognitive deficit** → **stereotactic radiosurgery**
- Percutaneous procedures have **~50% recurrence at 3–5 years** — far worse than MVD

## Preoperative Considerations

- **BAERs NOT routinely monitored** (contrast: used for HFS, Meniere's, and glossopharyngeal cases)
- Volatile anesthesia < 1% MAC
- Park-bench / lateral position — never prone

## Operative Technique — Retromastoid MVD

### Approach

- [[retromastoid-craniotomy]] via **modified reverse-U incision**
- Burr hole at TS/SS junction; small quarter-sized craniotomy/craniectomy
- Dural opening parallel to SS and posterior fossa floor

### Corridor — supralateral cerebellar

- **Supralateral cerebellar corridor** (retraction parallel to CN V and CN IX) — **opposite** of the infrafloccular corridor used for [[hemifacial-spasm]] / [[glossopharyngeal-neuralgia]]
- Rubber-dam glove + cottonoid; dynamic suction retraction; no fixed retractors
- Identify petrous-tentorial junction; advance cottonoid toward CN V REZ
- Generous arachnoid opening — **superior petrosal vein** often obstructs the view and may be divided carefully if necessary

### Intradural — the decompression

1. Expose CN V from Meckel's cave dural entry to brainstem REZ
2. **Inspect the entire circumference of the nerve** — inferomedial (SCA) and superior (SCA/AICA) are the classic offender locations
3. If no arterial loop is found, **large discoloring veins** are the offender — coagulate and divide if safe
4. **Mobilize the offending vessel away from the REZ along its entire length** with multiple pieces of **shredded Teflon**
5. Avoid direct Teflon on the REZ — the pledget goes **between vessel and brainstem**, not between vessel and nerve when that would create ephaptic contact
6. Inspect for additional offenders (SCA + vein coexistence is common)

### Rhizotomy

- **Only if no arterial offender is found** and pain is severe; partial sensory rhizotomy (inferomedial 2/3) preserves V1 corneal sensation

### Closure

- Dura approximated (non-watertight acceptable)
- "Wax in, wax out" mastoid
- Bone flap or methacrylate cranioplasty

## Postoperative

- ICU overnight; regular ward 1–2 days
- **Immediate pain relief** in most cases
- Watch for: hearing loss (rare in TN MVD), facial numbness, CSF leak, cerebellar infarct from SCA manipulation, aseptic meningitis

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Character of pain at inception** drives diagnosis — medications mask the original picture
- **< 40 yo, bilateral, isolated V1, deafness, refractory to carbamazepine** → search for secondary cause (MS, tumor)
- **Absent MRI loop ≠ surgical disqualification**
- **SCA is the usual offender** (75%); mobilize along **entire length**, not just the contact point
- **Veins matter** — 12% are sole offenders; don't dismiss them
- **Balloon compression** is Cohen-Gadol's preferred percutaneous option for non-MVD candidates
- **Atypical/neuropathic pain** is a contraindication — often worse after surgery
- **Tic convulsif** — look for coexisting HFS; one operation can address both if corridors align
- **Partial sensory rhizotomy** only when no arterial offender is found — preserves V1 corneal sensation
