# Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH)

Spontaneous subarachnoid hemorrhage is the neurosurgical prototype of a time-critical cerebrovascular emergency: ~85% of cases arise from rupture of a saccular intracranial aneurysm at the circle of Willis (see [[circle-of-willis]]), with the remainder attributable to perimesencephalic non-aneurysmal SAH, AVMs, dural AVFs, and rarer etiologies. Management rests on three phases — diagnosis, aneurysm securement, and delayed-complication surveillance — and engages the full span of the neuro-ICU and operating room.

## Clinical presentation

- **"Thunderclap headache"** — the cardinal symptom; often "worst headache of my life," maximal within seconds.
- Brief loss of consciousness (~50%), meningismus, nausea/vomiting, photophobia.
- Focal deficit when the bleed dissects into parenchyma or compresses a cranial nerve (e.g. CN III palsy from PCoA aneurysm).
- Sudden cardiac arrhythmia or neurogenic pulmonary edema can dominate the early course.

### Warning-leak ("sentinel headache")
10–50% of patients recall a thunderclap headache in the 2–8 weeks prior — a minor leak. Missed recognition of the sentinel event is a classic diagnostic pitfall.

## Grading

| Grade (Hunt-Hess) | Clinical state |
|-----|----------------|
| I | Asymptomatic or minimal headache |
| II | Severe headache, meningismus, no deficit except CN palsy |
| III | Drowsiness, confusion, mild focal deficit |
| IV | Stupor, hemiparesis, early decerebration |
| V | Deep coma, decerebrate posturing, moribund |

| Grade (WFNS) | GCS | Motor deficit |
|-----|-----|---------------|
| I | 15 | Absent |
| II | 13–14 | Absent |
| III | 13–14 | Present |
| IV | 7–12 | Present/absent |
| V | 3–6 | Present/absent |

| Fisher | Appearance on CT |
|-----|------------------|
| 1 | No blood detected |
| 2 | Diffuse thin layer (<1 mm) |
| 3 | Localized thick clot (>1 mm) — highest vasospasm risk |
| 4 | Intraventricular or intraparenchymal extension |

## Diagnosis

- **Non-contrast CT** — ≥95% sensitive within 6 h; sensitivity drops after 24 h as blood redistributes.
- **Lumbar puncture** — xanthochromia by spectrophotometry at 12 h confirms subarachnoid blood in CT-negative cases.
- **CTA / DSA** — aneurysm localization and morphologic planning. DSA is the gold standard; 3D rotational DSA is the modern default for operative/endovascular planning (see [[imaging-sah-and-aneurysm]]).
- **MRI FLAIR / GRE** — useful subacutely when CT has normalized.

## Early complications

### Rebleeding
- Peak risk in the first 24 h (~4% in first day, ~1%/day thereafter for 14 days).
- Rebleeding doubles mortality — hence the imperative for ultra-early aneurysm securement.

### Hydrocephalus
- Acute obstructive hydrocephalus from intraventricular blood → [[external-ventricular-drain]] placement is a common bedside procedure.
- Delayed communicating hydrocephalus (weeks-months) → ventriculoperitoneal shunt in 10–20% survivors.

### Seizures
- Occur in ~20%; prophylactic short-course antiepileptic therapy is commonly practiced.

## Aneurysm securement

The binary modality decision — **microsurgical clipping** vs **endovascular coiling** — is driven by morphology, location, patient age, and clinical grade. The ISAT trial (2002) and updates favor coiling for good-grade posterior-circulation and small anterior-circulation aneurysms; complex MCA bifurcation, giant aneurysms, and associated hematomas typically favor surgery.

- Clipping: standard supra-orbital or [[pterional-craniotomy]] approach for anterior circulation; far-lateral or retromastoid for posterior circulation.
- Coiling: transfemoral DSA with platinum coils, flow diversion, or balloon-/stent-assist for wide-neck lesions.

## Delayed complications

### Vasospasm / Delayed Cerebral Ischemia (DCI)
- **Angiographic spasm** detectable days 3–14 post-ictus in up to 70% of cases; ~30% develop clinical DCI.
- Pathophysiology: blood-breakdown product effect on arterial smooth muscle, endothelial dysfunction, cortical spreading depolarizations.
- Prevention / treatment: oral nimodipine (60 mg q4h × 21 days) is the only level-I evidence-based intervention; euvolemia; induced hypertension for symptomatic DCI; intra-arterial verapamil / balloon angioplasty for refractory cases.

### Hyponatremia
- Cerebral salt wasting or SIADH — both common; volume status distinguishes.

### Cardiac dysfunction
- Neurogenic stress cardiomyopathy ("Takotsubo") with reversible LV dysfunction; troponin elevation is routine.

## Prognosis

- Overall 30-day mortality ~30–35% (including pre-hospital deaths).
- Among survivors, ~25% have significant cognitive or functional disability at 1 year.
- Good-grade (Hunt-Hess I-II) survivors with secured aneurysm: 70–80% independent at 1 year.
