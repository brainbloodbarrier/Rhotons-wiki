# Cerebral Arteriovenous Malformation

A cerebral AVM is a **congenital vascular anomaly** consisting of a network of abnormal vessels — the **nidus** — interposing between arterial feeders and draining veins, with no intervening capillary bed. The resulting high-flow shunt produces chronic hemodynamic stress, a lifetime hemorrhage risk, and a characteristic neurologic footprint.

## Epidemiology and genetics

- Prevalence ~10 per 100,000; most present between ages 20 and 40.
- Sporadic in the vast majority; **hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia (HHT / Osler-Weber-Rendu)** is the principal syndromic association (ENG, ACVRL1, SMAD4 mutations).
- Multiple AVMs in a single patient should prompt HHT workup.

## Presentation

| Mode | Frequency | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Hemorrhage (ICH, IVH, SAH) | ~50% | Rupture of nidal vessel or feeding artery aneurysm |
| Seizure | 20–30% | Cortical gliosis, hemosiderin staining |
| Headache | 10–15% | Non-specific; migraine-like with occipital AVM |
| Progressive neurologic deficit | ~5% | Vascular steal from adjacent parenchyma |
| Incidental | increasing | MRI for unrelated symptoms |

## Rupture risk

Baseline annual rupture risk **2–4%**. Independent predictors of increased risk (Stapf, Hernesniemi):

- **Prior hemorrhage** (risk ~6–10% per year in first year).
- **Deep (basal ganglia / brainstem / cerebellum) location**.
- **Deep venous drainage** (internal cerebral / basal vein of Rosenthal / vein of Galen).
- **Associated feeding-artery or intranidal aneurysm**.
- **Single draining vein** or venous stenosis / ectasia.
- **Smaller size** paradoxically — small AVMs present with hemorrhage more often.

## Imaging

- **CT/MRI** — identifies hemorrhage, nidus location, flow voids on T2.
- **MRA / SWI / 4D-MRA** — non-invasive delineation; useful for follow-up.
- **DSA** — gold standard: angioarchitecture, feeders, transit time, venous anatomy, associated aneurysms, eloquent proximity.
- **Functional MRI / tractography** — adjunctive for peri-eloquent lesions.

## Spetzler-Martin grading

Sum of three variables; total 1–5 (plus grade 6 = inoperable):

| Variable | Points |
|---|---|
| **Size**: <3 cm / 3–6 cm / >6 cm | 1 / 2 / 3 |
| **Eloquence of adjacent cortex** (sensorimotor, language, visual, hypothalamus/thalamus, internal capsule, brainstem, cerebellar peduncles, deep cerebellar nuclei) | 0 / 1 |
| **Deep venous drainage** | 0 / 1 |

Supplemented by the **Lawton-Young** score (age, unruptured status, diffuse nidus) for refined risk stratification.

## Treatment

The **ARUBA** trial raised the bar for treating unruptured AVMs: conservative management had lower stroke/death at 33 months than any-intervention. Interpretation is contentious (short follow-up, heterogeneous interventions), but it focused practice on selection — low-grade ruptured AVMs and those with high-risk features remain surgical candidates.

### Microsurgical resection
Gold standard for grades I–II and most grade III AVMs in non-eloquent cortex; provides immediate obliteration. Operative principles:

- Wide craniotomy tailored to lesion: [[pterional-craniotomy]] (sylvian / insular), [[parasagittal-craniotomy]] or [[bifrontal-craniotomy]] (medial frontal), [[parietal-craniotomy]] (parietal convexity), [[retromastoid-craniotomy]] (cerebellar), [[suboccipital-craniotomy]] (posterior fossa midline).
- Circumferential dissection — identify and interrupt feeders **before** the draining vein.
- Bipolar coagulation in the deep white matter where fragile perforators feed; be prepared for Spetzler's *normal-perfusion-pressure breakthrough* after obliteration.
- [[Lacerated-dural-venous-sinus-repair]] preparation for sinus-adjacent lesions.
- Meticulous [[intraoperative-hemostasis]] under permissive hypotension.

### Stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS)
Effective for nidi ≤3 cm, especially deep eloquent locations. Obliteration rates 60–85% at 3 years; latency period retains hemorrhage risk. Multi-stage and hypofractionated options extend SRS to larger lesions.

### Endovascular embolization
Adjunctive in most centers — targets high-flow fistulous components, intranidal aneurysms, or downsizes a large nidus before SRS / surgery. Onyx and n-BCA are standard liquid embolics. Curative embolization remains uncommon.

## Postoperative care

Postoperative management emphasizes **blood-pressure control** (prevents breakthrough hemorrhage and edema), delayed completion angiogram at 24–72 h, seizure prophylaxis, and follow-up DSA at 6 months and 1 year. See [[postoperative-care]].
