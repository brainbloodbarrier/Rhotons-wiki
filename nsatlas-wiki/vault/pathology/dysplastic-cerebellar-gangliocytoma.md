# Dysplastic Cerebellar Gangliocytoma (Lhermitte-Duclos Disease)

*Source: NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol (contributor: Rachel Seltman, MD)*

Also called **Lhermitte-Duclos disease (LDD)** — the neurologic manifestation of **Cowden syndrome (CS)** / **multiple hamartoma and neoplasia syndrome (MHAM)**. The combination **CS + LDD = MHAM + LDD = COLD (Cowden-Lhermitte-Duclos) syndrome**, a neurocutaneous / phakomatosis.

## Pathology

- **Benign cerebellar mass** with **thickened, irregular folia**
- **Pathogenesis uncertain** — hamartomatous, neoplastic, or congenital
- **WHO grade 1**, no malignant potential
- **MHAM genetics**: autosomal dominant **PTEN mutation**
  - Hamartomas of skin, GI, GU, mucosa, eye, CNS
  - Increased risk of mucocutaneous tumors, **thyroid adenomas**, **fibrocystic breast disease**, GI polyps, and malignancy
- **Microscopic triad**: loss of Purkinje cells, abnormal ganglion cells, hypertrophic granule cell layer

## Clinical Features

- **Any age**; most common 20–40 years
- **No sex predilection**
- **Presentation**:
  - **Increased ICP** — headache, nausea, vomiting
  - **Cerebellar signs** — ataxia, dysmetria, gait instability
  - **± extracranial MHAM/CS stigmata** — skin, thyroid, breast, GI

## Imaging — The "Tigroid" Cerebellum

### General

- **"Corduroy," striated, or tigroid"** appearance of the cerebellar hemisphere from thickened irregular folia — pathognomonic
- Usually **unilateral hemisphere** ± vermis
- **Midline crossing with hemispheric dominance** is typical
- Variable size; mass effect, tonsillar herniation, hydrocephalus if large
- **Stable or slow growth**

### CT

- Iso- to hyperdense with thickened tigroid folia
- Variable contrast enhancement

### MRI (workhorse)

- **T1WI**: hypo- to isointense
- **T2WI**: **hyperintense with hypo- to isointense folia** (the stripes) + hypointense vascular flow voids
- **FLAIR**: hyperintense infiltrative pattern with mild edema/mass effect *relative to size*
- **DWI**: hyperintense (T2 shinethrough, hypercellularity, axonal density)
- **T1+C**: variable enhancement — **conspicuous enhancing vessels within the lesion** are typical
- **MRS**: **decreased NAA, Cho, mI** (unlike malignant tumors which raise Cho)
- **MR perfusion**: increased **rCBV / rCBF** may be present

### Imaging recommendations

- **MRI without and with IV contrast + DWI + MRS**
- **Screen for MHAM/CS** if LDD is found (and vice versa) — PTEN carriers have lifetime tumor surveillance needs

## Differential

- **Ischemic infarct** — but LDD has no restricted diffusion in a vascular territory and has the tigroid folia
- **Cerebellitis** — acute, symmetrical, clinical context differs
- **Medulloblastoma / ependymoma** — enhance avidly, efface folia rather than accentuate them
- **Ganglioglioma** of cerebellum — rare, solid enhancing nodule

## Management

- **Surgical debulking** for symptomatic mass effect
- **CSF diversion** ([[endoscopic-third-ventriculostomy]] or [[ventriculo-peritoneal-shunt]]) for hydrocephalus
- **Observation** for small asymptomatic lesions
- **Whole-body cancer surveillance** per PTEN carrier guidelines (thyroid, breast, endometrial, colon, renal, melanoma)
- Posterior fossa approach typically **suboccipital or telovelar** — see [[suboccipital-craniotomy]], [[telovelar-approach]]

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Tigroid/corduroy cerebellum + midline crossing + internal enhancing vessels** = LDD until proven otherwise
- **Find the Cowden** — cutaneous trichilemmomas, oral papillomas, acral keratoses, thyroid/breast lesions
- **PTEN genetic testing** has family and patient cancer-surveillance consequences
- **Moderate rCBV** on perfusion doesn't mean malignant — LDD can perfuse
- **Surgery is palliative**, not oncologic — resect enough to decompress, spare the cerebellar function that remains
- **Follow with MRI** — slow growth or stability is the rule; rapid change = reconsider diagnosis
