# Cranial Approaches — General Principles

*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.ch01*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol | **Pages:** 12 | **Figures:** 11

## Philosophy of Approach Selection

Six guiding principles for selecting the operative corridor:

1. **The ideal approach protects normal cerebrovascular structures** and allows efficient, thorough handling of the lesion. *The technical complexity of the approach is secondary in importance.*
2. **Avoid routine use of limited approaches.** Instead, *tailor* the approach to the underlying target.
3. The most important priorities for corridor selection:
   - a. Strict protection of normal anatomy
   - b. Maximal and efficient exposure of the lesion without operative blind spots
4. **"The operation is not about *how to get there*, rather it is about *what to do when you are there*."** Minimize approach complexity; choose the most efficient and unobstructed route. The surgeon spends most time protecting normal anatomy while dealing with pathology.
5. Normal structures, pathology, and the surgeon's experience affect the trajectory of the approach.
6. Extensive [[anterior-petrosectomy|skull base osteotomies]] such as petrosal approaches have important but limited use. Careful patient selection and judicious use are strongly advised.

## Two-Step Approach Algorithm

After determining the **bony approach**, the next step is selection of the **intradural trajectory/route** to expose the lesion:

> The dissection via the intradural trajectory to reach the lesion must minimize extraneous brain injuries and transgression through proper planning of the operative approach and patient positioning. **Gravity retraction, enhanced bony removal, brain relaxation, wide arachnoid dissection, and most importantly, strategic use of dynamic retraction** of the normal brain during manipulation of the surgical target are key factors in successful operative planning.

## Supratentorial Approaches and Routes

The [[pterional-craniotomy]] and its modifications are the workhorses of the supratentorial approaches. An extended form through aggressive resection of the sphenoid wing and orbital roof is a *skull base approach* and mimics [[orbitozygomatic-craniotomy|orbitozygomatic osteotomy]].

The [[supraorbital-craniotomy]] is a lateral skull base approach suitable for the parasellar, parachiasmatic, and intrasylvian spaces. When combined with an eyebrow incision, it becomes a minimally invasive keyhole approach. The supraorbital subfrontal corridor affords longer operative working distance but potentially less brain manipulation and retraction.

The orbitofrontal and mediobasal cortices are often misleadingly considered "silent" and noneloquent. However, their functions are apparent to patients' families — subfrontal injury frequently leads to loss of inhibition caused by damage to the orbitofrontal region (cognitive processing and decision-making).

## Infratentorial Approaches

The [[retromastoid-craniotomy]] provides corridors for accessing the [[cerebellopontine-angle|cerebellopontine angle]]. Two main trajectories relative to the CN VII/VIII complex:

- **Supralateral cerebellar route** — for lesions above the CN VII/VIII complex
- **Infralateral cerebellar (infrafloccular) route** — for lesions below the CN VII/VIII complex

The [[telovelar-approach]] trajectory of operative dissection is in relation to the tonsils, vermis, and medial aspect of the cerebellar hemispheres.

## Clinical Pearls

- **Dynamic retraction** limits exposure of the entire lesion at once but reveals *just enough* of the *necessary* portion at each step — this obviates extensive cranial openings and brain retraction associated with higher postoperative morbidity
- As experience grows, the surgeon can accomplish more with less exposure — the definition of *"just enough"* is related to operative experience and skill
- **Working *angles*** are more important than **working *space*** — flexible operative working angles obviate the need for expanded operative spaces that lead to aggressive handling of normal structures
- The operator should be intimately familiar with all modifications of exposures, their advantages and limitations
- Minimally invasive keyhole and tailored approaches are discussed where appropriate
