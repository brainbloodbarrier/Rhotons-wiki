# Telovelar Approach

*Telovelar Approach to the Fourth Ventricle.*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.ch11*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol | **Pages:** 14 | **Figures:** 12

## Overview

Fourth-ventricular lesions sit beneath the cerebellar vermis and are accessible through two corridors:

1. **Transvermian** — transects the inferior vermis. Exposure limited to midline lesions; sacrificing the vermis produces **vermian split syndrome** (nystagmus, gait disturbance, truncal ataxia, equilibrium disturbance, head/neck oscillation); **cerebellar mutism** in pediatric patients.
2. **Telovelar** — uses **natural planes** (uvulotonsillar and medullotonsillar spaces, tela choroidea, inferior medullary velum). Preserves the vermis, reaches the **foramen of Luschka / lateral recesses**, and — with a C1 laminectomy — rivals transvermian rostral reach from obex to aqueduct.

Cohen-Gadol has **abandoned the transvermian route** in favor of the telovelar approach for all fourth-ventricular lesions. The telovelar approach is performed through a [[suboccipital-craniotomy|midline suboccipital craniotomy]].

## Indications

- **Ependymomas** (floor-sparing subtotal when tumor invades floor)
- **Medulloblastomas**, **hemangioblastomas**, **metastases**
- **Exophytic brainstem tumors**
- **Choroid plexus papillomas**
- **Cavernous malformations** of the fourth ventricular floor / pons (with facial nucleus mapping)
- **Arteriovenous malformations**
- Lesions extending into **foramen of Luschka / lateral recesses** (major advantage over transvermian)

## Preoperative Considerations

- **MRI** for rostral extent: need for **small caudal vermian split** or **C1 laminectomy** to reach superior pole
- **Floor invasion** assessed: plan **radical subtotal** leaving adherent tumor on floor — **do NOT manipulate the floor** during tumor resection (high morbidity: swallowing/ventilatory dysfunction, gastrostomy/tracheostomy)
- **Electrophysiologic mapping** of **facial nucleus** is reasonable for **intraparenchymal** lesions (cavernomas) to enable safe entry
- **Morbidly obese** patients: consider sitting position
- **Lower cranial nerve dysfunction** preoperatively → remain intubated postop; ENT consult before extubation/feeding
- **PICA encasement** (repeat operations) — plan subtotal resection
- **Patent occipital sinus** → brisk bleeding on dural opening; anticipate
- **Hemorrhagic mass with hydrocephalus** → consider preoperative EVD
- **Avoid aggressive supratentorial EVD** drainage when posterior fossa mass is acutely expanding → risks **upward cerebellar transtentorial herniation**
- **Defibrillator pads** for brainstem intraparenchymal lesions

## Operative Anatomy

### Fourth Ventricular Roof

- **Superior roof**: superior medullary velum + superior cerebellar peduncles
- **Inferior roof**: inferior medullary velum, **tela choroidea**, uvula, nodule
- **Lateral recesses** open to the cerebellopontine angle

### Tonsillar Spaces

- **Vallecula** — space between medial surfaces of the two tonsils
- **Cerebellomedullary fissure** — between anterior tonsillar surface and medulla
- **Uvulotonsillar space** — between medial tonsil and adjacent uvula (the operative plane)

### PICA

- Travels around the tonsils and through the inferior roof
- Turns into fissures between hemispheres, tonsils, and vermis
- **Medial and lateral trunks** identified at the PICA bifurcation — both preserved during retraction

### Fourth Ventricular Floor (high-risk zone)

- **Facial colliculus**, superior/inferior fovea, **striae medullares**, hypoglossal and vagal triangles, area postrema, obex, **funiculus separans**
- Manipulation associated with **facial weakness, diplopia, swallowing and ventilatory dysfunction**

## Patient Position

- **Modified park-bench**, head **flexed and turned toward the floor** — expands the space along the craniocervical junction
- **Ipsilateral shoulder mobilized inferiorly and anteriorly** (taped) — increases working space in the suboccipital area
- Lateral position uses gravity retraction, blood runs out of field, surgeon can sit

## Skin Incision

- **Linear midline** incision; short horizontal mark to note transverse sinus level
- Incision length reflects planned C1 laminectomy need

## Bone Work

- **Standard midline [[suboccipital-craniotomy|suboccipital craniotomy]]** — narrower than for hemispheric lesions, resembling Chiari decompression
- Exposes the **medial quarter to half of the cerebellar hemispheres**
- **Foramen magnum removed inferiorly**; craniocervical ligament resected
- **Transverse sinuses NOT exposed** in this approach
- Two burr holes on either side of the midline keel
- **C1 arch removal** if lesion extends into aqueduct or lateral recesses — facilitates inferior-to-superior trajectory
- Watch for **patent occipital sinus** — brisk bleeding

## Dural Opening

- **Curvilinear** dural incision, tacked superiorly

## Intradural Work — The Telovelar Dissection

1. **Expose** the inferior cerebellar surface and upper brainstem
2. **Open the uvulotonsillar and medullotonsillar spaces** — dissection plane between the **medial edge of the tonsil** and **adjacent edge of the uvula**
3. **Retract medial tonsil supralaterally**, **uvula supramedially** with **dynamic retraction** (no fixed retractors)
4. **Tela choroidea** becomes visible between tonsil and uvula — incise it longitudinally, parallel to the taenia
5. **PICA bifurcation** identified; medial and lateral trunks preserved; gentle **lateral retraction** of the bifurcation without branch sacrifice
6. Expose the **inferior medullary velum** via a nerve hook
7. **Superior vector** added to both retractors to open the ventricle
8. **Aqueduct and floor of the fourth ventricle** visualized
9. To reach the **superolateral recess / foramen of Luschka**: **divide the ipsilateral inferior medullary velum** — preserve the **vein of the cerebellomedullary fissure** near its rostral aspect
10. Begin microsurgical tumor resection:
    - **Floor-adherent tumor**: leave a thin sheet on the floor (ependymoma) — **do NOT aggressively dissect the floor**
    - **Intraparenchymal cavernoma**: facial-nucleus mapping guides entry through the safest floor zone

## Closure

- Standard [[suboccipital-craniotomy|suboccipital]] closure: watertight dura with pericranial autograft, neck muscles gently approximated, fascia watertight
- Bone plate replaced if no cerebellar swelling

## Postoperative Considerations

- **ICU 1-2 days**; longer if lower cranial nerve dysfunction
- Preserve intubation if lower CN palsies; **ENT consult** before extubation/feeding
- Watch for **cerebellar edema**, **hydrocephalus**, **CSF leak**

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Telovelar > transvermian** for all fourth ventricular lesions — no vermis sacrifice, better lateral recess exposure
- **Uvulotonsillar-medullotonsillar opening** — the key corridor; identify the plane carefully
- **Tela choroidea + inferior medullary velum** — the only structures cut; everything else retracted
- **Preserve PICA medial and lateral trunks** during retraction
- **NEVER manipulate tumor-invaded floor** — subtotal is the goal; a thin sheet on the floor is acceptable
- **C1 laminectomy** expands the rostral reach (obex → aqueduct)
- **Floor mapping (facial nucleus)** is reasonable for intraparenchymal cavernomas but not for ependymomas
- **Upward transtentorial herniation** risk if supratentorial EVD drained aggressively against an acutely expanding posterior fossa mass

## Complications

- **Facial weakness, diplopia, swallowing/ventilatory dysfunction** — floor manipulation
- **PICA territory infarct** — branch sacrifice
- **Cerebellar edema / mutism** (less than transvermian but still possible with aggressive retraction)
- **Hydrocephalus**
- **CSF leak**, **pseudomeningocele**
- **Lower cranial nerve palsies**
