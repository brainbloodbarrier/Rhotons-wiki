# Anterior Petrosectomy

*Source: NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol | also known as the Kawase approach*

The anterior petrosectomy is the natural extension of the middle fossa approach. It exchanges the limited IAC-lip exposure of the classical middle fossa craniotomy for access to the **upper third of the clivus, the ventrolateral upper pons and midbrain, and Meckel's cave** by removing the **petrous apex** — specifically the bone within **Kawase's (posteromedial / quadrilateral) triangle**. The labyrinth is preserved; this is its decisive advantage over [[retromastoid-craniotomy|retrosigmoid]] or posterior petrosectomy for appropriately selected lesions.

## Classification of Transpetrosal Approaches

Two fundamental osteotomies, infinitely recombined in the literature:

1. **Anterior petrosectomy** — removal of the **petrous apex** (this chapter).
2. **Posterior petrosectomy** — removal of the **petrous pyramid** (translabyrinthine/transcochlear/retrolabyrinthine variants).

More extensive "combined petrosal" osteotomies are conventional craniotomies plus one of these two. **Total petrosectomy** sacrifices the labyrinth and is rarely, if ever, necessary.

## Indications

**Preferred for:**

- Upper petroclival and Meckel's cave tumors — meningiomas, trigeminal schwannomas
- Intrapetrous lesions (anterior petrous cysts, [[dermoid|dermoids]], cholesteatomas)
- Cross-midline intracisternal dermoids and epidermoids
- Lateral/ventral **upper pons and midbrain** intra-axial lesions (cavernous malformations, tumors)
- **Low-lying basilar apex** and basilar trunk aneurysms
- Tumors entirely **medial to and above the IAC**
- Tumors that **span middle and posterior fossae**
- Younger patients in whom aggressive resection is desired

**Not suitable for:**

- Purely clival tumors (medial to the petroclival sulcus)
- Tumors extending caudal/posterior to the IAC → use posterior petrosectomy or [[retromastoid-craniotomy]]
- Tumor extension into cranial nerve foramina
- Purely lower pontine or medullary lesions
- Chordomas and chondrosarcomas — endoscopic transnasal route preferred
- Pituitary tumors — always endonasal

## Preoperative Considerations

- **MRI** defines tumor limits and candidacy — inflexible exposure punishes poor selection with subtotal resection
- **High-resolution temporal bone CT** — pneumatization of the petrous apex, intraosseous tumor extent, course of the petrous carotid
- **Lumbar drain** — highly advised (especially dominant side) for temporal lobe retraction relaxation; see [[lumbar-drain]]
- **Intraoperative neurophysiology** — facial nerve EMG and BAERs; inform team of thresholds that will trigger course change
- **Combined-corridor planning** — decide preoperatively whether posterior petrosectomy or [[retromastoid-craniotomy]] is needed in addition

## Operative Anatomy — Kawase's Triangle

The **posteromedial (quadrilateral / Kawase) triangle** on the floor of the middle fossa:

- **Anterior** — posterior margin of **Meckel's cave** containing V3
- **Posterior** — **arcuate eminence** (superior semicircular canal underneath)
- **Lateral** — **greater superficial petrosal nerve (GSPN)**
- **Medial** — **superior petrosal sinus**

Critical structures bounding the drilling zone:

- **Petrous internal carotid artery** — runs anterolaterally under the V3–GSPN interval; bone over the horizontal petrous ICA is the anterior limit of safe drilling
- **Cochlea** — at the genu of the petrous ICA, anteromedial to the geniculate ganglion; cochlear injury causes hearing loss and CSF otorrhea
- **Internal auditory canal (IAC)** — defines the posterolateral limit; drilling proceeds **medial** to the IAC
- **Geniculate ganglion** — often dehiscent; the **meningeal-orbital band** overlying the GSPN is elevated carefully to avoid traction on the facial nerve
- **Superior semicircular canal** — under the arcuate eminence; ~60° to petrous ridge; blue-lining identifies it before fenestration
- **Trigeminal nerve posterior root** — emerges medial to Meckel's cave, crosses the petrous apex toward the pons

## Position and Exposure

- **Supine**, head turned 45–60° contralaterally, slight neck extension, [[skull-clamp-placement|Mayfield]] fixation
- Temporal lobe uppermost in the field; gravity assists retraction
- [[temporal-subtemporal-craniotomy|Temporal / subtemporal craniotomy]] centered on the external auditory meatus
- Low temporal bone removal **to the floor of the middle fossa** — rongeur remaining bone flush to the skull base
- Dural tack-ups along the craniotomy edge

## Steps of the Operation

### 1. Extradural elevation of the middle fossa dura

- Elevate dura **posterior to anterior** (House's technique) to minimize facial nerve traction from a dehiscent geniculate ganglion
- Alternatively, **anterior to posterior** (Kawase/Fukushima) is safer when the GSPN runs superficially
- Identify landmarks in sequence: **middle meningeal artery (MMA)** at foramen spinosum → coagulate and divide → **V3 at foramen ovale** → **GSPN** → **arcuate eminence**

### 2. Identify Kawase's triangle boundaries

Expose V3, GSPN, arcuate eminence, and superior petrosal sinus as the drilling quadrilateral is mapped.

### 3. Drill the petrous apex

- **Diamond burr** with continuous irrigation
- Work from the posterior margin of V3 toward the medial edge of the IAC and clivus
- **Preserve** the cochlea (anteromedial to geniculate), petrous ICA (anterior), superior semicircular canal (posterior), and IAC (posterolateral)
- **Endpoint** — dura of the posterior fossa exposed between V3 and the IAC; thin cortical shell over the petrous ICA may be preserved

### 4. Dural opening

- Temporal dura opened longitudinally
- **Superior petrosal sinus** ligated and divided
- **Tentorium** incised **posterior to the entry of cranial nerve IV** (protect trochlear nerve); extend toward the tentorial incisura
- Posterior fossa dura opened over the drilled petrous apex

### 5. Intradural work

- Exposure includes ventrolateral pons/midbrain, upper clivus, basilar trunk, low basilar apex, CN III–V, and ipsilateral SCA/AICA
- Tumor resection follows standard microsurgical principles — see [[brain-tumors-general-principles]] and [[intraoperative-hemostasis]]

### 6. Closure

- Watertight dural closure; **fat graft** and **fibrin glue** to seal drilled air cells and prevent CSF leak
- Bone flap reattached; muscle/skin in layers
- Lumbar drain retained 3–5 days if significant dural exposure

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Cochlea first, ICA close second** — the cochlea at the genu of the petrous ICA is the most commonly violated structure; recheck position on every pass
- **Blue-line the superior semicircular canal** before any deep drilling posteriorly
- **GSPN preservation** matters — traction on the geniculate ganglion through the GSPN produces facial palsy; dehiscence is common
- Cut the **tentorium posterior to CN IV's dural entry** — 4th nerve injury is the signature avoidable complication
- **Temporal lobe retraction** is inevitable — [[lumbar-drain|lumbar drain]] + mannitol + gravity position reduce it
- **Subtotal resection** is preferable to cochlear, facial, or ICA injury
- Chordomas, pituitary tumors, and purely clival lesions belong to the **endoscopic endonasal** corridor, not here

## Alternatives and Complementary Approaches

- [[retromastoid-craniotomy]] — posterior fossa lesions caudal to IAC
- Posterior petrosectomy — lesions spanning petrous pyramid with hearing sacrifice tolerable
- [[pterional-craniotomy]] — high-riding basilar apex aneurysms
- Combined petrosal — very large petroclival meningiomas spanning middle + posterior fossa
- Endoscopic endonasal — purely clival, pituitary, chordoma/chondrosarcoma
