# Microscope-Guided Endonasal Transsphenoidal Approach

*Microscopic Transnasal Corridor to the Sella.*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.ch17.1*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol | **Pages:** 23 | **Figures:** 20

## Overview

The endonasal transsphenoidal approach has evolved since Cushing popularized the transnasal route in the early 20th century. Lighting, endoscopy, and instrumentation advances have made the **transsphenoidal approach the preferred route to the paramedian sellar and suprasellar region** (pituitary adenomas, meningiomas, craniopharyngiomas). Unlike pterional or orbitozygomatic approaches, the transnasal route **eliminates brain retraction**.

This chapter focuses on the **microscopic transnasal exposure** performed by the neurosurgeon (the endoscopic endonasal corridor is typically exposed by ENT colleagues and detailed separately in the Brain Tumors volume).

## Indications

- **Pituitary adenomas** (micro and macro)
- **Rathke's cleft cysts**
- Selected **parasellar meningiomas**, **craniopharyngiomas**, and **clival chordomas** confined to paramedian sella/suprasellar, medial to the ICAs and inferior to the subchiasmatic space
- Substantial vertical growth is **not** a contraindication
- Endoscopy extends reach to the anterior skull base (orbital groove, planum sphenoidale, tuberculum sella meningiomas; paraclival chordomas and chondrosarcomas)
- Tumors with significant suprasellar and middle fossa extension: initial transnasal resection, staged transcranial second stage

### NOT indicated for (microscopic corridor)

- Extensive **lateral dispersion beyond the cavernous sinus**
- **Medially ectatic carotid arteries** (midline displacement)
- Large disruption of bony landmarks where safe corridor cannot be verified by navigation

## Preoperative Considerations

- **Pituitary axis hormonal evaluation** — prolactin, cortisol, thyroxine mandatory
  - **Prolactinomas** can have atypical/asymmetric imaging mimicking meningioma → check prolactin **before surgery** to avoid unnecessary operation
  - Perioperative **glucocorticoids** eliminate hypocortisolemia; preexisting hypothyroidism can manifest acutely postop
- **Carotid anatomy review** on imaging — ectatic/medially displaced ICAs, invasive tumors destroying bony landmarks
- **Neuronavigation (CT-guided)** or at minimum lateral fluoroscopy is essential; invasive tumors and prior-surgery scarring distort normal landmarks
- **Prophylactic lumbar drain** if high postoperative CSF leak risk (reoperation, large diaphragm defect expected)
- **Abdomen prepared** for fat graft harvest — done **at the very beginning** to avoid contamination by nasal flora
- **"Cross-court" nostril selection**: tumors with left-sided suprasellar extension approached from the **right nostril** to expand working angle toward contralateral hidden corners

## Operative Anatomy

- **Sphenoid sinus**: carotid and optic prominences, **opticocarotid recess** — key landmarks
- **Pituitary gland** lies in variable position within the sella
- **Sphenoid septi**: simple midline, oblique, paired vertical — **oblique septi connect to the lateral sella where the ICA is covered by thin bone**. Paired vertical symmetrical septi are the most deceiving configuration: removing one can be mistaken for midline exposure. **Preoperative septal anatomy review is mandatory.**

## Patient Position

- **Supine, sniffing position** with neck slightly flexed on a horseshoe headrest
- **Skull clamp** required for neuronavigation — no intraoperative head adjustments possible afterward
- **Head tilted away from the surgeon** (right-handed operator on patient's right) so the shoulder does not obstruct the operator's body while viewing the sellar floor

### Operating Room Setup

- Surgeon and scrub tech positioned across from one another (handedness-dependent) for easy instrument transfer
- Neuronavigation / fluoroscope at the head of the table, across from the surgeon
- Anesthesiologist at the foot of the table

## Skin / Nasal Incision

- For midline tumors, select the **nostril with a contralaterally deviated, larger septum**
- **Hydrodissection** of septal mucosa with xylocaine + epinephrine near anterior quadrangular cartilage or vomer tip — hemostatic and less traumatic
- **Curvilinear subperiosteal incision** in anterior mucosa; mucosal flap elevated as large as possible while preserving the **nasoseptal artery** (pedicled nasoseptal flap for closure)

## Bone Work

### Septal Mobilization

- **#1 Penfield dissector** carries the submucosal dissection past the septal cartilage to the vomer
- Avoid mucosal perforations along the cartilage-bone junction
- **Quadrangular cartilage mobilized contralaterally** → access to both sides of vomer and perpendicular plate of ethmoid
- Contralateral septal mucosa elevated off the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid to expose **sphenoidal crest bilaterally**

### Sphenoidotomy

- **Bivalve nasal speculum** docked on the midline sphenoid crest; mucosa retracted laterally
- **Chisel** or **Kerrison rongeurs** (starting from sphenoid ostia) open the anterior sphenoid wall
- **Neuronavigation** confirms midline and exposure limits
- Midline septi removed with pituitary rongeurs; air drill if underpneumatized
- **All sinus mucosa exenterated thoroughly**

### Sellar Floor Osteotomy

- Entry through one nostril deflects the speculum toward the contralateral sella, **over the corresponding carotid** — **actively counteract this cross-court deflection**, keep speculum midline
- Small chisel removes midline sellar floor; Kerrison rongeurs expand the opening
- **Bone removal must extend laterally to the carotid arteries and anteriorly to the tuberculum sella** — **suboptimal osteotomy is the single most common reason for subtotal transsphenoidal resection**
- If sella is thin from tumor erosion, a blunt hook elevates the bone

## Dural Opening

- **Cruciate dural opening** for intrasellar + suprasellar lesions
- **Hand-held microdoppler** guides dural opening; if arteries are medially displaced, **accommodate the dural incision** accordingly — otherwise laceration
- **Venous channels** within dural layers sealed with thrombin-soaked gelfoam; avoid aggressive bipolar (extends lateral incisions into cavernous sinus)

## Intradural Work

- Microscopic tumor resection with curettes, ring curettes, pituitary forceps
- Details of adenoma resection in dedicated non-endoscopic adenoma resection chapters
- **Blind spots** lateral to the sella are minimized only by adequate dural opening

### Carotid Injury — Management

- Pack operative field with cotton, apply gentle pressure over visible laceration
- If bleeding controlled and vital resection required, may continue cautiously
- **If source cannot be immediately identified and sealed: pack, abort, immediate arteriogram** to rule out pseudoaneurysm

## Closure

### Sellar Reconstruction

- **Fat graft** (periumbilical, harvested at start of surgery) wrapped in Surgicel for easy handling and suction resistance
- Gently packed within sella (**avoid suprasellar compression**); pack **more anteriorly along/above the tuberculum sella** — occult CSF leaks typically occur here at the diaphragma-sella attachment
- **Method of packing matters more than quantity**
- **Dural replacement material** tucked under bony and dural edges along the sphenoid sinus floor
- If **large diaphragmatic tear** exposing suprasellar contents: rotate **pedicled septal mucosal flap**, formal skull base reconstruction, fibrin glue overlay

### Nasal Reconstruction

- Bony septum replaced, midline mucosae returned to midline
- **Antibiotic-coated "trumpet drains"** keep anterior septal mucosae along midline; removed on POD #1
- Both nostrils packed

## Postoperative Considerations

- **Lumbar drain** for several days if large diaphragm defects noted intraop
- Selective ICU monitoring; **Cushing's disease** patients → ICU for hypocortisolism watch (life-threatening)
- **Diabetes insipidus**: urine output > 400 cc for 2 consecutive hours → investigate with urine-specific gravity
- **Antibiotics** while nasal packing in place
- **Routine postoperative MRI** to assess extent of resection and drive the surgeon's learning curve

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **CT-guided neuronavigation** is especially important for poorly pneumatized sphenoids, reoperations, and invasive tumors
- Misplacement of the **nasal speculum into the sphenoid sinus** with forceful valve spread → anterior skull base fracture, optic nerve injury
- Bony and dural exposure along the **sellar floor** determines the extent of resection
- **Meticulous reconstruction** avoids CSF fistulas
- **Two symmetric vertical septi** are the most dangerous deceiving configuration — removing one misleads the operator off midline toward the ICA
- Constantly counteract the speculum's **natural cross-court deflection** toward the contralateral carotid
- Abandon microscope, convert to endoscopy, or stage transcranially when tumor lateralizes beyond the cavernous sinus

## Complications

- **ICA laceration** — most feared; pseudoaneurysm risk
- **CSF rhinorrhea** from diaphragmatic tear with inadequate reconstruction
- **Anterior skull base fracture**, **optic nerve injury** from speculum misplacement
- **Hypocortisolism** (Cushing's disease patients, unrecognized hypopituitarism)
- **Diabetes insipidus**
- **Epistaxis**, nasoseptal flap ischemia
- **Cavernous sinus bleeding** from aggressive lateral bipolar
