# Bifrontal Craniotomy

*Bifrontal Craniotomy for an Anterior Cranial Base Chondrosarcoma*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.ch08*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol | **Pages:** 17 | **Figures:** 14

## General Considerations

Pathologies along the falx in the anterior fossa can be reached through unilateral anterior parasagittal, [[pterional-craniotomy|pterional]], and [[orbitozygomatic-craniotomy|orbitozygomatic]] approaches. However, these may not provide adequate exposure of **large midline lesions with extradural extension** into ethmoid and sphenoid sinuses.

Cohen-Gadol does NOT believe expanded variations with bilateral orbital rim mobilization are necessary — they don't significantly broaden tumor exposure since enucleation often provides working space. These osteotomies increase risks of cosmetic deformity and CSF leakage.

> **Key point:** Bifrontal craniotomy is used **only for large tumors with anterior skull base invasion**. This chapter discusses **extradural anterior cranial base lesions**. Almost all intradurally confined tumors (including giant olfactory groove meningiomas) can be approached through a unilateral extended [[pterional-craniotomy|pterional]] or [[supraorbital-craniotomy|supraorbital]] corridor with anterior falx transection for contralateral access.

## Indications

- **Extradural tumors** with skull base invasion: esthesioblastomas, chondrosarcomas, frontal/ethmoid sinus tumors
- Bifrontal contusions with symptomatic cerebral edema (decompressive)
- Frontal sinus and anterior skull base fractures with CSF fistulas or cosmetic deformities
- Large tumors with nasal sinus extension where transnasal approach is not feasible (e.g., midline septal invasion destroying nasoseptal flap)

### Advantages of unilateral pterional/supraorbital OVER bifrontal

- Avoids frontal sinuses
- No sacrifice of anterior superior sagittal sinus
- No manipulation of both frontal lobes
- Allows early identification of optic nerve and carotid artery via lateral trans-Sylvian trajectory

## Preoperative Considerations

- **Lumbar drain** highly useful — CSF cisterns not accessed early, brain relaxation critical
- **MR/CT venogram** if meningioma possibly involving superior sagittal sinus
- Assess **frontal sinus morphology** — large sinuses increase CSF leak risk
- **Vascularized pericranial graft** routine for covering exenterated frontal/ethmoid sinuses
- Large skull base defects require additional bone reconstruction and flap coverage + prophylactic lumbar CSF drainage

## Operative Anatomy

- Anterior cranial fossa: frontal sinuses (asymmetric), olfactory bulbs adjacent to cribriform plate in a "valley" relative to orbital roofs
- Crista Galli proximity to olfactory structures
- Extradural dissection at crista galli/cribriform plate level leads to intradural space

## Steps of the Operation

### 1. Positioning
- Supine, head slightly extended or flexed (based on tumor vertical extent)
- No rotation — midline pathologies
- Single pin above/behind pinna, contralateral two pins bisecting above pinna
- Patient secured to table for potential position adjustments

### 2. Incision
- **Bicoronal incision** as anterior as possible while staying behind hairline
- Must reach at least to superior edge of pinna level
- Neck flexion tailored to tumor's inferior nasal sinus extension

### 3. Pericranial Flap
- Scalp incision leaves pericranium, temporalis muscle, and fascia intact
- **Vascularized pericranial graft** elevated as separate layer, based anteriorly on orbital rims
- Graft dissected/undermined more posteriorly beyond incision for larger piece
- Kept moist with wet sponge during operation

### 4. Craniotomy
- **Single burr hole** over anterior superior sagittal sinus
- Craniotomy height <10 cm, width does NOT need full span of orbital rims — a limited "low" bifrontal craniotomy suffices for most lesions
- Large craniotomies → risk of large bony defect if infected postoperatively
- Dura dissected with #3 Penfield
- Forehead burr holes avoided for cosmesis

### 5. Osteotomy Over Frontal Sinuses
- Craniotome for vertical bony cuts
- Final **horizontal osteotomy** over frontal sinuses with straight side-cutting burr — disconnect anterior then posterior sinus walls
- **Frontal sinus exenteration:** mucosae removed with pituitary rongeurs + monopolar electrocautery
- Pack with muscle, betadine/bacitracin-soaked bone wax, or betadine-soaked gelfoam

### 6. Sphenoid Ridge and Orbital Roof
- Overhanging inferior craniotomy edges and posterior frontal sinus walls removed to clear view toward basal frontal lobe

### 7. Extradural Tumor Resection
- Dynamic retraction with suction over olfactory bulbs
- Sharp dissection to disconnect bulb and expose extradural tumor extension into nasal cavities
- Bulb disconnection also leads to dural opening and disclosure of intradural tumor portions

### 8. Intradural Approach (for purely intradural tumors)
- Dura opened through two horizontal incisions parallel to upper craniotomy edge
- **Anterior superior sagittal sinus** ligated with 0-silk sutures in two spots and divided
- Anterior parasagittal bridging veins: considered dispensable but exercise caution
- Falx sectioned at sinus division point; inferior dural flap reflected anteriorly

### 9. Closure
- **Watertight dural closure** necessary — temporalis fascia for autograft
- Frontal sinus requires further exenteration
- Antibiotic-soaked gauze over dura; residual mucosa coagulated
- Frontal sinus packed with muscle, fat, or bone wax
- **Pericranial graft** reflected posteriorly to cover frontal sinuses and anterior skull base defect
- For large bony defects with ethmoid sinus exposure: **split calvarial bone graft** from craniotomy bone flap placed OVER (not under) the pericranial flap, fixed to orbital roof with titanium screws

### 10. Postoperative
- **Lumbar drain** for 48 hours to divert CSF
- ICU 1-2 days then ward
- **DANGER:** Pneumocephalus on postop CT without visible leak → suspect large leak. **Continuing lumbar drainage will reverse-suction air through nose** → worsening pneumocephalus → acute neurologic deterioration and infection. Requires repeat operation with careful dural closure and skull base reconstruction.

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- Lumbar drain useful for both intraoperative brain relaxation and postoperative short-term CSF diversion
- A relatively **small craniotomy is very effective** — large craniotomies risk large bony defect if infected
- Bifrontal craniotomy has **relatively limited indications** — most intradural tumors may be removed through the expanded [[pterional-craniotomy|pterional route]]
- **Fixation plates should NOT be placed over the region of the frontal sinus** — screws can invade sinus → CSF leak
