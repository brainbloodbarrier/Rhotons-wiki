# Occipital Craniotomy

*Occipital Craniotomy and Resection of a Peritorcular/Parafalcine Meningioma.*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.ch18*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol / Acioly | **Pages:** 9 | **Figures:** 8

## Overview

The occipital craniotomy exposes the occipital lobe, tentorium and posterior incisural space, splenium of the corpus callosum, medial and posterior temporal lobe, posterior thalamus, atrium of the lateral ventricle, and parieto-occipital area.

This approach is **highly extensible** and routinely combined with:

- **Posterior interhemispheric** (transcallosal intervenuous/paravenous variants)
- **Occipital transtentorial** route to the pineal region and precentral cerebellar fissure
- **Paramedian supracerebellar** (see [[paramedian-supracerebellar-craniotomy]])
- **Supratentorial infraoccipital** corridor for posteromedial temporal lobe lesions

## Indications

- **Intraparenchymal** occipital neoplasms (metastases, gliomas) and vascular lesions (AVMs, cavernous malformations)
- **Tentorial meningiomas** (peritorcular / parafalcine)
- **Occipital interhemispheric corridor**: parafalcine, medial occipital, falcotentorial, and splenial lesions
- **Occipital transtentorial corridor**: pineal region, precentral cerebellar fissure, posterior incisural space
- **Infraoccipital corridor**: mesial and posterior temporal lobe lesions

## Preoperative Considerations

- **MRI** routinely; **CT** if osseous involvement suspected
- **Functional MRI** for lesions near preserved visual cortex
- **MR venography**: patency of transverse sinuses, size, dominance, collateralization — crucial for approach selection
- **Digital subtraction angiography** rarely necessary; preoperative embolization seldom required (craniotomy allows early devascularization along tentorium and falx)
- **Sinus lumen infiltration**: preoperative cardiac workup, **TEE** + **transthoracic Doppler** intraop, low threshold for **paradoxical air embolism** suspicion
- **Neuro-ophthalmology**: detailed visual field testing
- **Cortical/subcortical visual mapping** has limited role — unstandardized protocols, patient cooperation issues, and most patients already have visual deficits

## Operative Anatomy

### Occipital Lobe — Three Surfaces

**Lateral:**
- Anterolateral limit: imaginary line between the **parieto-occipital arcus** (U-shaped gyrus projecting from parieto-occipital sulcus) and the **preoccipital notch**
- **Lambda** approximates the parieto-occipital sulcus projection, but the lambdoid suture slopes downward to the asterion, remaining posterior to the parieto-occipital junction
- **Lateral occipital sulcus** is the most consistent lateral sulcus, dividing into superior and inferior occipital gyri

**Medial:**
- **Calcarine sulcus** from occipital pole to splenium — divides into cuneus (superior) and lingula (inferior)
- **Opisthocranium** (most prominent point of the occipital bossa, 3 cm inferior to lambda) correlates with the distal calcarine sulcus
- Medial surface bounded by the **parieto-occipital sulcus**

**Basal:**
- Anterior limit: imaginary line from calcarine-POsulcus junction medially to preoccipital notch laterally
- Crossed by **occipitotemporal sulcus** and **collateral sulcus** — the collateral sulcus projects to the temporal horn as the **collateral eminence** and the atrium as the **collateral trigone**

### Sinus Anatomy

- **Transverse sinus** dominance (typically right)
- **Torcula** and SSS-transverse junction
- **Tentorial sinuses** along basal occipital path to tentorium
- Occipital lobe interhemispheric space is **mostly devoid of parasagittal bridging veins** — relatively safe entry

## Patient Position

- **Three-quarters prone (park-bench)** — lateral position allows gravity retraction and avoids nonphysiologic head turn
- **Dependent lobe** mobilized from midline by gravity for interhemispheric trajectory
- **Head turn/tilt** customized to lesion location:
  - Convexity: lesion at highest operative field point
  - Parafalcine occipital: ipsilateral side dependent; head tilted toward floor for ergonomic sitting microsurgery
- **Axillary roll** for contralateral axilla; ipsilateral shoulder pulled anteroinferiorly and taped out of working zone
- Patient firmly secured to table — intraoperative tilt must not displace

## Skin Incision

- **Linear paramedian transverse** or **longitudinal** (parallel to SSS) incision usually provides ample exposure
- **Horseshoe** (blue) for large convexity meningiomas/gliomas: starts at superior nuchal line, slopes upward over midline, turns laterally at lambda, descends to asterion — reserved for large occipital lesions and transtentorial access to pineal or tentorial cerebellum
- **Neuronavigation + preoperative MRV/angio** guide craniotomy positioning

## Bone Work

- **Convexity lesions**: single burr hole over occipital bone, guided by navigation
- **Paramedian / interhemispheric**: two burr holes over the SSS — early sinus identification drives bone flap planning
- **Penfield #3** to dissect between inner calvarium and SSS wall
- **Lumbar CSF drainage** mobilizes sinus wall and dura away from bone, preventing footplate injury
- If SSS adherent: add a **third burr hole**; all burr holes in continuity within epidural space
- **Last bone cut over the sinus** — allows rapid flap elevation if sinus injured
- For **supratentorial infraoccipital** or **occipital transtentorial** routes, **expose SSS, transverse sinuses, AND torcula**
- Sinus wall bleeding after flap elevation: thrombin-soaked gelfoam or **Surgicel Fibrillar** (left in place at closure)

## Dural Opening

- **Convexity / intraparenchymal**: cruciate or curvilinear, based on dural venous sinus
- **Interhemispheric space**: curvilinear dural opening centered over SSS
- **Pineal / basal occipital**: cruciate opening centered over **both SSS and transverse sinuses**
- Large draining veins at occipital lobe are lateral/outside working zone near the transverse sinus — **preserve**; occasional small draining vein may be sacrificed

## Intradural Work

- **Interhemispheric space** easily entered at this level (few parasagittal bridging veins)
- **Retraction sutures in superior falx** mobilize/rotate SSS to expand interhemispheric corridor
- **Infraoccipital space**: inspect basal occipital surface for bridging veins to tentorium and tentorial sinuses
- **Dynamic retraction only** (especially for interhemispheric route) — fixed retraction risks **visual field deficits**
- **Lumbar CSF drainage** allows early hemispheric mobilization away from falx
- Microsurgical tumor resection or vascular lesion handling follows approach-specific principles

## Closure

- **Ventricular drainage catheter** (briefly) if ventricle was entered — clears postoperative debris
- **Watertight dural closure NOT routine** for supratentorial craniotomies in this author's practice
- **Avoid dural allografts** (aseptic inflammation / infection risk)
- **No subgaleal drain** routinely
- Scalp closed standard; skin edges approximated without strangulation, absorbable sutures

## Postoperative Considerations

- **ICU for 1-2 days**, then ward
- Monitor for visual field changes and posterior fossa tension if transtentorial corridor used

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Very versatile** — interhemispheric, supracerebellar/infraoccipital, and transtentorial extensions
- **Three-quarters prone / park-bench** positioning facilitates gravity retraction and expands interhemispheric + infraoccipital corridors
- **Inappropriate retraction on medial occipital surface → visual field deficits** — use dynamic retraction + CSF drainage
- Occipital interhemispheric space has few bridging veins, but preserve any large vein; expose sinuses widely when the transtentorial or infraoccipital extension is planned
- **Last bone cut over the sinus** is a safety rule: enables rapid flap elevation in case of inadvertent sinus tear

## Complications

- **Visual field deficit** from medial occipital retraction or optic radiation injury
- **Venous sinus injury** (SSS, transverse, torcula)
- **Paradoxical air embolism** if tumor infiltrates sinus lumen
- **Venous infarction** from bridging vein sacrifice
- **CSF leak** (less risk than posterior fossa; watertight not mandatory supratentorially)
