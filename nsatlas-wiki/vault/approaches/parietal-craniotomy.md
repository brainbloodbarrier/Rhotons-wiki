# Parietal Craniotomy

*Parietal Craniotomy: Convexity, Parafalcine, and Periatrial Corridors.*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.ch03*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol / Acioly | **Pages:** 13 | **Figures:** 11

## Overview

The parietal craniotomy exposes the **mid-to-posterior hemisphere** while sparing:

- **Anterior sensorimotor cortices**
- **Posterior visual cortex**

Corridors:
- **Transcortical** via the more functionally "silent" **superior parietal lobule** (SPL)
- **Interhemispheric** — fewer parasagittal bridging veins in posterior parietal region provides access to deep midline lesions
- **Contralateral transfalcine** — longer and technically harder but spares ipsilateral white matter and avoids SPL disconnection (see [[contralateral-interhemispheric-transfalcine-transprecuneus-approach|PITTA]])

### Functional Considerations

- **Right (nondominant) parietal lobe**: spatial awareness, navigation → right-sided injury → **hemibody neglect** (improves but residual)
- **Left (dominant) parietal lobe**: language/mathematical symbols → damage → math, reading, writing, symbol deficits; **Gerstmann's syndrome** (dominant inferior parietal)
- **Bilateral** lesions → **Balint's syndrome** (simultanagnosia, oculomotor apraxia, optic ataxia)
- **SPL transgression** to the atrium may cause astereognosia and visual/speech processing deficits — impact quality of life depending on patient occupation

## Indications

- **Convexity, falcine, and parafalcine meningiomas** (most common)
- **Metastases, gliomas** of the parietal region
- **AVMs, cavernous malformations**
- **Parafalcine, medial parietal, splenial** lesions via interhemispheric corridor
- **Periatrial / atrial lesions** via transcortical SPL or (preferred) contralateral transfalcine route

## Preoperative Considerations

- **Cortical stimulation mapping** ("awake", "asleep", phase reversal) for anterior parietal intraparenchymal lesions near sensorimotor cortex
- **Lumbar drain** — low threshold even for large lesions with mass effect; CSF drained during dural opening to avoid transtentorial herniation in massive tumors with midline shift
- **MR/CT venogram** if interhemispheric corridor planned and large parasagittal veins suspected; confirms SSS patency in infiltrating meningiomas
- **Numerous ipsilateral parasagittal veins** → **contralateral transfalcine route** preferred for parafalcine lesions
- **SSS infiltration** → preoperative cardiac workup for **paradoxical air embolism**, TEE + transthoracic Doppler intraop

## Operative Anatomy

### Parietal Surface Structure

- **Superior parietal lobule** (Brodmann 5 + 7) and **inferior parietal lobule** (39 + 40), separated by the **intraparietal sulcus**

### Parietal Venous Drainage

- **Lateral ascending** group: central, postcentral, anterior parietal, posterior parietal veins → SSS
- **Lateral descending** group: parietosylvian veins → sylvian fissure
- **Medial ascending** group: paracentral, anteromedial, posteromedial parietal veins → SSS
- **Medial descending** group: posterior pericallosal veins → inferior sagittal sinus
- **Vein of Trolard (superior anastomotic)** crosses frontal/parietal lobes from sylvian fissure to SSS — typically **postcentral**, occasionally central or precentral

### SSS Location

- **Deviated right of sagittal suture** up to 11 mm
- **Parasagittal venous lakes** (lacunae) near posterior frontal/parietal region — drainage channels for meningeal veins

## Patient Position

- **Three-quarters prone** on OR table — avoids nonphysiologic head turn, facilitates gravity retraction, promotes ergonomic sitting for microsurgery
- **Parafalcine parietal** lesions: lesion side **dependent** for gravity retraction; tilt head away from floor for ergonomic sitting
- **Contralateral transfalcine** (periatrial): normal hemisphere dependent
- **Convexity** lesions: tilt head so lesion is at highest point
- **Axillary roll** (contralateral); ipsilateral shoulder pulled anteroinferiorly and taped out
- **Skull clamp** with swivel rocker — line from single pin to midpoint between opposite two pins must cross the equator; pins not near the vertex

## Skin Incision

- **Linear incision** (red) — ample exposure for most
- **Horseshoe** (blue) — reserved for large convexity meningiomas
- **Neuronavigation / MRV-angio** positions craniotomy and localizes parasagittal bridging veins, especially if interhemispheric dissection is planned

## Bone Work

### Convexity Lateral

- **Single burr hole** over occipital/parietal bone guided by navigation; craniotomy sized to lesion with ~2 cm margin

### Paramedian / Interhemispheric

- **Two burr holes directly over the SSS** (remember right deviation up to 11 mm)
- Early sinus identification drives bone flap planning
- **Penfield #3** over SSS wall; third burr hole if adherent
- **Lumbar CSF drainage** pulls sinus/dura away from bone → prevents footplate injury
- **Last bony cut over the sinus**
- Sinus bleeding → thrombin-soaked gelfoam or **Surgicel Fibrillar** (left in place through closure)
- **Avoid aggressive bipolar** on dura — propagates sinus tears

## Dural Opening

### Convexity / Intraparenchymal

- Circumferential opening **2 cm outside contrast-enhancing region** using navigation

### Interhemispheric

- **Curvilinear flap based on the SSS**
- **Preserve large draining veins**; occasionally a small vein may be sacrificed
- **Adjust opening** to protect any parasagittal vein's inlet into the sinus
- Bridging veins run **posterior → anterior** with multiple tributaries
- **Paramedian SSS extensions / venous lakes**: extend incision **parallel** to the sinus near midline (not perpendicular)
- Small **lateral SSS wall tears** during paramedian incision → close with **fine sutures**, not bipolar

## Intradural Work

### Interhemispheric Dissection

- **Untether parasagittal veins from arachnoid adhesions** — tedious, thick/adherent arachnoid
- **Lumbar CSF drainage** mobilizes hemisphere from midline
- **Retraction sutures in superior falx** rotate the SSS and expand the corridor
- **Never tension parasagittal veins** during microdissection

### Contralateral Transfalcine to Atrium

- **T-shaped falx incision**, flaps held with retention sutures
- **Contralateral precuneus corticotomy** + oblique white-matter dissection → entry into the atrium (see [[contralateral-interhemispheric-transfalcine-transprecuneus-approach|PITTA]])
- Falx, cortex, and white-matter divisions all **navigation-guided**

### Transcortical (SPL) to Atrium

- Used when contralateral transfalcine not feasible; risks SPL-related deficits (astereognosia, spatial awareness)

## Closure

- **Ventricular catheter** briefly if ventricle entered
- **Watertight dura NOT routine** (supratentorial)
- **Avoid allograft** dural substitutes
- **Dural closure must not kink parasagittal vein flow**

## Postoperative Considerations

- ICU 1-2 days, ward thereafter
- Watch for venous infarction, SPL-related spatial deficits, epidural hematoma from sinus bleeding

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Three-quarters prone** allows both convexity access and gravity-augmented interhemispheric corridor
- **SSS injury prevention** is paramount; low air-embolism threshold in semi-sitting
- **Parasagittal bridging vein sacrifice = catastrophic venous infarct** — respect them
- **Right-deviated SSS** up to 11 mm — navigate, don't guess
- **SPL transgression** has quality-of-life consequences — prefer contralateral transfalcine for periatrial lesions when possible
- **Sutures, not bipolar**, for SSS tears

## Complications

- **Venous infarction** (bridging vein sacrifice)
- **Air embolism** (SSS infiltration / elevated head)
- **Hemibody neglect** (right parietal injury)
- **Gerstmann / Balint syndromes**
- **Astereognosia, visual processing deficits** (SPL transgression)
- **Motor cortex / deep white matter injury** when craniotomy extends anteriorly
- **CSF leak, wound issues**
