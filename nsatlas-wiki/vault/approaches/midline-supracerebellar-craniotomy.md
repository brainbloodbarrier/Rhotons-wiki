# Midline Supracerebellar Craniotomy

*Midline Supracerebellar Craniotomy in the Sitting Position.*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.ch12*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol | **Pages:** 18 | **Figures:** 15

## Overview

The supracerebellar craniotomy exploits the **natural subdural space** above the cerebellum to reach the **pineal region, posterolateral mesencephalon, posterior third ventricle, posterior basal temporal lobe, and thalamus** without brain transgression. The corridor is narrow, deep, and technically demanding.

A **continuum** of supracerebellar infratentorial (SCIT) approaches exists, moving from midline → paramedian → far lateral. Other regional routes (occipital transtentorial, transcallosal interhemispheric) put supratentorial structures at risk and are avoided in Cohen-Gadol's practice — the infratentorial corridor is well tolerated despite cerebellar manipulation and can be expanded to reach almost any incisural lesion.

The **midline bilateral suboccipital supracerebellar** route is the traditional design for pineal region tumors, but has several drawbacks:

- **Steep trajectory over the apex of the culmen** (limits inferior visualization)
- **Tentorial angle** limits lateral visualization
- **Midline vermian bridging veins are invariably sacrificed** — not without risk (edema, vermian injury)
- **Bilateral dural venous sinus (transverse, torcula) at risk**

Cohen-Gadol now prefers the [[paramedian-supracerebellar-craniotomy|left-sided paramedian supracerebellar approach]] for even large midline pineal tumors — analogous to preferring a pterional over bifrontal for olfactory groove meningiomas. This chapter still describes the **traditional midline** technique for completeness.

## Indications

- **Pineal region tumors**: germ cell tumors, pineoblastomas, astrocytomas, meningiomas, epidermoid tumors, pilocytic astrocytomas
- **Posterior third ventricular tumors** (gentle pulvinar manipulation well tolerated)
- **Tentorial dural arteriovenous fistulae / malformations**
- **Posterolateral mesencephalic cavernous malformations, pilocytic astrocytomas**
- **Distal superior cerebellar artery aneurysms**
- **Transtentorial extension**: petrous apex meningiomas (supratentorial component), medial tentorial meningiomas, posterior hippocampal AVMs/cavernomas/astrocytomas/metastases — avoids second-stage subtemporal surgery

### Contraindications / cautions

- Deep venous structures (vein of Galen, internal cerebral veins, basal vein of Rosenthal) **posteriorly displaced** by posterior thalamic/vermian tumors mimicking pineal masses → supracerebellar is **contraindicated**
- Steep tentorial angle and obese patient with short neck — not a contraindication but consider **sitting position** with neck flexion

## Preoperative Considerations

- **Preoperative MRI**: tumor extent, need for transtentorial extension, deep venous relationships (vein of Galen, BVR, ICV, straight sinus)
- **Transverse/sigmoid sinus courses** evaluated — slight variability can affect craniotomy safety
- **Obstructive hydrocephalus**: prepare Keen's point or place preoperative frontal ventriculostomy
- **Sitting position**: strongest indication is pineal tumors (gravity mobilizes cerebellum inferiorly, expands corridor); requires:
  - **Right heart catheter**, **transthoracic Doppler**, **transesophageal echocardiography**
  - **Preoperative "bubble test"** to rule out patent foramen ovale
  - Ready management for **venous air embolism**

## Operative Anatomy

### Pineal Region Landmarks

- **Splenium of corpus callosum** lies anterior to the vein of Galen — following the tentorial contour can lead the operator inadvertently toward the vein of Galen; **redirect trajectory inferiorly** toward the pineal
- **Precentral vein** drains to the vein of Galen, lies posterior to thick arachnoid over diencephalic veins, connects to the culmen — **this vein is sacrificed**; all anterior diencephalic veins are preserved

### Midline vs Paramedian Corridor

- **Midline**: requires retraction of the culmen; steep
- **Paramedian**: reaches over the lower-lying **lateral cerebellum / quadrangular lobule**; shallower and more direct, avoids midline vermian veins, spares the dominant (typically right) transverse sinus
- **Cranial nerve IV** is at the lower edge of the dissection field in both

## Patient Position

- **Modified park-bench** or **sitting position**
- **Sitting**: gravity mobilizes cerebellum inferiorly, expands supracerebellar corridor; strongest for pineal tumors
- Head flexion ameliorates working angles over the cerebellum in patients with steep tentorium or short neck

### Operating Room Setup

- Surgeon and scrub tech positioned for efficient instrument transfer
- Burr hole site for ventricular catheter identified if needed
- **Midline incision**, may be extended superiorly for **pericranial harvest** for watertight dural closure

## Skin Incision

- **Midline linear incision** over occiput/suboccipital region
- Extended superiorly if pericranial autograft will be harvested for dural reconstruction

## Bone Work

- Burr holes placed to avoid dural sinus laceration; **B1 drill bit** used; **footplate avoided near sinuses**
- Craniotomy exposes the cerebellar hemispheres and skeletonizes the **transverse sinuses and torcula**
- After flap elevation, remaining bone over sinuses removed in layers with the drill — **skeletonization permits gentle sinus rotation** via tentorial retraction sutures
- **Foramen magnum bone removal is NOT required**

## Dural Opening

- **Curvilinear dural incision**
- **Midline occipital sinus** can be prominent with large paramedian venous lakes → **two separate dural incisions, one on each side of the sinus**; tie off the sinus with silk sutures and controlled transection
- **Cerebellar falx** cut; dural flap reflected superiorly
- **Tentorial retraction sutures** anchored into the tentorium elevate it and rotate/mobilize the transverse sinuses upward — **expands the working corridor meaningfully**
  - **Fixed retractors on the tentorium do NOT help** — they interfere with instrument angles despite sinus skeletonization

## Intradural Work

- **Sacrifice midline vermian bridging veins** (coagulated and cut) — the unavoidable cost of the midline route
- **Precentral vein** sacrificed; all other anterior diencephalic veins preserved
- Gently rotate transverse sinuses superiorly via tentorial retention sutures
- **Dissect thick arachnoid** over deep venous structures to expose the vein of Galen and associated vessels — **pathology may distort anatomy**
- **Redirect operative trajectory inferiorly** as you approach the tumor (inferior to vein of Galen) — without redirection the operator becomes disoriented and the deep veins are jeopardized
- Open arachnoid over tumor and begin microsurgical resection
- **Obstructive hydrocephalus with transtentorial occipital herniation** may be encountered — gently reduced; do not let it confuse regional anatomy

## Closure

- **Watertight dural closure** — posterior fossa tumor operations have significant postoperative CSF fistula risk
- **Pericranial autograft** preferred over allograft for dural reconstruction
- Bone flap replaced with cranial plates
- **Neck muscles gently approximated** — avoid tension/strangulation (muscle necrosis and uncontrolled postoperative pain)
- Fascia closed watertight

## Postoperative Considerations

- **ICU for 1-2 days**
- Prophylactic **steroids** to minimize aseptic meningitis risk
- Ventriculostomy (if placed) weaned off postoperatively
- **Cerebellar edema** from retraction → symptomatic posterior fossa tension can require decompression. If brain swollen at closure: **do not tension dural closure, do not replace bone flap**. Swelling can be compounded by partial transverse sinus thrombosis and vermian vein sacrifice

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Tentorial retraction sutures > fixed retractors** for expanding the supracerebellar corridor
- The [[paramedian-supracerebellar-craniotomy|paramedian variant]] is less invasive, protects bilateral dural sinuses, and affords a shallower trajectory — Cohen-Gadol's preferred route
- Use **two dural incisions** flanking a prominent midline occipital sinus to control bleeding
- **Redirect trajectory inferiorly** to avoid blind dissection around the vein of Galen
- **Sitting position** requires full air-embolism monitoring infrastructure
- Watch for **occipital transtentorial herniation** when obstructive hydrocephalus is present

## Complications

- **Venous air embolism** (sitting position)
- **Midline venous sinus laceration** during bone work
- **Vermian vein sacrifice** → cerebellar edema
- **Cerebellar mutism** (rare, vermian injury)
- **Vein of Galen / deep venous system injury**
- **CN IV injury**
- **Aseptic meningitis**, **CSF fistula**
- **Posterior fossa tension** requiring decompression
