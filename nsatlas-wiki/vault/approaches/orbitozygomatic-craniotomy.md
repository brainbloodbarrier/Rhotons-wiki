# Orbitozygomatic Craniotomy

The orbitozygomatic (OZ) craniotomy is an extension of the [[pterional-craniotomy]] that adds removal of the **superolateral orbital rim** (orbital osteotomy) and the **zygomatic arch** (zygomatic osteotomy). The additional bony removal flattens the operative corridor toward the anterior and middle skull base, shortens the working distance to deep parasellar targets, and widens the upward trajectory to the basilar apex and posterior clinoid region without additional brain retraction.

## Indications

The OZ is chosen when a pterional exposure is judged inadequate — typically for:

| Indication | Rationale |
|------------|-----------|
| **Basilar apex aneurysm** | Upward trajectory to posterior clinoid; less temporal lobe retraction |
| **Upper basilar trunk aneurysm** | Exposure above posterior clinoid |
| **Large parasellar tumor (craniopharyngioma, meningioma)** | Inferior-to-superior trajectory over chiasm |
| **Cavernous-sinus lesion** | See [[cavernous-sinus-anatomy]]; extradural exposure of anterior clinoid and lateral wall |
| **Orbital-apex / optic-canal lesion** | Optic-canal unroofing with dural preservation |
| **Petroclival lesion** (combined with petrosectomy) | Anterior reach of combined corridor |
| **High-riding basilar bifurcation** | OZ + transcavernous combination |

Lesions amenable to a pure pterional are **not** upgraded to an OZ — the added osteotomies incur cosmetic, temporalis, and infection risks.

## Variants

### One-Piece OZ

- Frontotemporal craniotomy and orbitozygomatic bar are elevated as a **single specimen**
- Advantages: fewer bone-flap seams, easier rigid fixation on reconstruction
- Disadvantages: requires more extensive periorbital and temporalis mobilization

### Two-Piece OZ

- Pterional bone flap elevated first; orbitozygomatic bar elevated as a **second specimen**
- Most common variant; safer periorbital dissection
- Reconstruction uses mini-plates across both flaps

### Modified "Supraorbital" (Single-Piece Orbital) Variants

- Orbital osteotomy without zygomatic arch ("fronto-orbital")
- Used for predominantly anterior cranial fossa targets (e.g., [[supraorbital-craniotomy]] family)

## Positioning and Incision

- **Supine**, ipsilateral shoulder bump; head rotated 20–30° away from lesion; vertex slightly below horizontal to drop frontal lobe away from roof of orbit
- **Curvilinear scalp incision** starting above zygomatic arch (preauricular, 1 cm anterior to tragus to protect the frontotemporal branch of CN VII), sweeping superiorly and crossing the midline at the hairline
- **Interfascial or subfascial dissection** of the temporalis fascia to protect the frontotemporal facial branch — defining OZ-safe technique

## Operative Sequence

1. Scalp incision and interfascial dissection
2. Temporalis elevation — reverse-direction retrograde flap
3. **Key keyhole (MacCarty)** burr hole at the frontozygomatic suture — exposes frontal and temporal dura + periorbita
4. Pterional craniotomy (frontotemporal bone flap)
5. **Orbital osteotomy** — superior rim and lateral wall, into the MacCarty keyhole
6. **Zygomatic osteotomy** — anterior at frontozygomatic suture, posterior across zygomatic root
7. Extradural drilling of the sphenoid ridge, anterior clinoid (optic strut), and optic-canal unroofing as needed
8. Dural opening: curvilinear, based on the sylvian fissure or the sphenoid ridge

## Key Anatomic Steps

- **Periorbita preservation** — any periorbital tear = orbital fat prolapse, which obstructs exposure; repair with fibrin glue and small patch graft
- **Anterior clinoidectomy** (extradural or intradural) — opens the carotid-oculomotor triangle and the roof of the [[cavernous-sinus-anatomy]]
- **Optic-strut drilling** — essential for paraclinoid carotid exposure
- **V2/V3 preservation** — zygomatic osteotomy must stay lateral to the foramen ovale and rotundum

## Comparison with Alternatives

- **[[pterional-craniotomy]]**: baseline; adequate for most ICA bifurcation, MCA, and ACoA lesions
- **OZ**: adds ~10° of upward/inferior trajectory and ~15–20 mm of vertical exposure — net benefit is maximal for lesions behind/above the posterior clinoid
- **[[supraorbital-craniotomy]]** (eyebrow): minimal variant; loses basilar-apex reach but cosmetically superior for tuberculum sellae, olfactory-groove meningioma
- **[[bifrontal-craniotomy]]**: midline ACF, olfactory groove, large planum; not a substitute for lateral OZ

## Complications

| Complication | Mechanism |
|--------------|-----------|
| Frontal hollowing (temporalis atrophy) | Aggressive temporalis stripping |
| Frontotemporal branch (CN VII) palsy | Inadequate interfascial dissection |
| Diplopia / enophthalmos | Periorbital tear, orbital volume loss |
| CSF leak (frontal sinus breach) | Over-anterior orbital cut |
| Palpable / cosmetic bone-flap deformity | Poor reconstruction |
| Mastication pain | Zygomatic reconstruction malalignment |

## Reconstruction

- Meticulous re-fixation of all osteotomies with mini-plates
- Temporalis resuspension with drill-holes in the superior temporal line to prevent hollowing
- Orbital rim alignment is cosmetically critical — rotation of a few degrees produces visible asymmetry
