# Transtentorial Approach to Parahippocampal Lesions

*Supracerebellar Transtentorial Approach for Resection of a Posterior Parahippocampal Tumor.*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.ch14*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol | **Pages:** 17 | **Figures:** 16

## Overview

Access to the **posterior medial temporal lobe and parahippocampal regions** is challenging — vital overlying cortices and no ideal skull base corridor.

### Alternatives and their limitations

- **Subtemporal**: excessive temporal lobe retraction, **vein of Labbé** at risk (see [[temporal-subtemporal-craniotomy]])
- **Transtemporal**: transgresses posterior temporal neocortex (dominant side eloquence); disrupts **optic radiations**
- **Transsylvian / transinsular / transcisternal**: deep narrow channel; posterior medial temporal reach unacceptably limited
- **Interhemispheric parieto-occipital**: possible but disrupts additional normal tissue

**Cohen-Gadol's preferred route:** **paramedian supracerebellar transtentorial approach**. Tentorium is transected through a paramedian supracerebellar corridor, reaching the basal posteromedial temporal lobe **from below** while leaving supratentorial cortices and optic radiations intact.

The corridor is **long and narrow** but has favorable working angles. Exposure is limited — select cases judiciously. Large lesions extending superiorly and anteriorly may be unsuitable.

## Indications

- **Intraparenchymal lesions** in the **posterior hippocampus / parahippocampal gyrus** at or just posterior to the cerebral peduncle / uncus
- **Tumors, AVMs, cavernous malformations**
- **Distal posterior cerebral artery aneurysms**
- **Multicompartmental pineal region masses**
- **Medial tentorial extra-axial tumors** (meningiomas)
- **Petrous apex meningiomas** (supratentorial extension)

### NOT suitable for

- Large lesions extending superiorly/anteriorly beyond the corridor's reach
- **Anterior** hippocampal / parahippocampal lesions (anterior to cerebral peduncle, within uncus) — use **limited anteromedial temporal lobe resection** instead

## Preoperative Considerations

- **T2-weighted MRI**: study the **posterior cerebral artery** branches — at direct risk
- **Transverse + sigmoid sinus** courses — variable, study for safety
- **Tentorial venous channels / straight sinus tributaries** — if large tentorial veins present, tailor tentorial incision or avoid this approach; **basal vein of Rosenthal** rarely drains into tentorial channels but must be checked
- Steep tentorial angle / obese / short-neck patients: **sitting position** with neck flexion
- **Obstructive hydrocephalus**: prepare Keen's point or preoperative frontal ventriculostomy — covered by paramedian incision
- **Modified park-bench** standard positioning
- **Lumbar drain** (no obstructive hydrocephalus) for cerebellar decompression

## Operative Anatomy

### Tentorial Landmarks

- **Trochlear nerve (CN IV)** enters the tentorium along its free edge — must be identified and preserved during tentorial transection
- **Superior petrosal sinus** along the petrous ridge — edge of lateral tentorial incision
- **Straight sinus** and tributaries — preserve during medial tentorial cut
- **Bridging occipital-to-tentorium veins** — may be sacrificed selectively

### Mediobasal Temporal Landmarks (after tentorial transection)

- **Parahippocampal gyrus (PHG)**, fusiform gyrus, lingual gyrus, isthmus
- **Pulvinar**, basal vein, internal occipital vein
- **Distal PCA (P3) branches**, **posterior temporal artery** — **numerous en passage branches** — meticulously preserve
- **Thalamoperforating arteries** — indiscriminate coagulation → thalamic and occipital lobe ischemia
- **Vein of Galen**, SCA, CN IV at the inferolateral corner

## Patient Position

- **Modified park-bench** (standard — same as [[paramedian-supracerebellar-craniotomy]])
- Skull clamp, neck flexed, head **15-20° toward floor**
- Ipsilateral shoulder forward/taped out of the surgeon's zone
- **Neuronavigation** for midline and sinuses

## Skin Incision

- **Paramedian vertical linear incision** halfway between **inion** and **mastoid groove**
- **One-third above, two-thirds below** the transverse sinus
- **~7-8 cm** long; Keen's point at upper edge
- Horizontal mark to note transverse sinus level

## Bone Work

- **Single burr hole** at inferior edge of transverse sinus, ~2 cm lateral to midline/torcula
- **Small bone flap** elevated; **full width of transverse sinus** exposed for rostral mobilization
- **Bilateral craniotomy is NOT needed**
- Same technique as [[paramedian-supracerebellar-craniotomy]]

## Dural Opening

- **Single curved dural flap based on the transverse sinus**
- **Two retraction sutures** along posterior tentorium rotate the transverse sinus superiorly, expanding the corridor
- Microdoppler confirms sinus patency during retraction
- One or two paramedian bridging veins sacrificed; midline vermian veins spared
- Gradual **lumbar / ventriculostomy CSF** release mobilizes the cerebellar hemisphere caudally

## Intradural Work

### Tentorial Incision

- Dura may be cut in a **"T"-shape** (smaller lesions, Figure 9) or **"U"-shape** (larger lesions, Figure 10) — navigation-guided
- The **"T"-shaped tentorial window** or **"U"-shaped tentorial flap** is placed over the lesion projection
- **Right-angled dissector** elevates the tentorial edge; microscissors continue the transection; small curved knife cuts toward the operator
- **Retraction sutures through the tentorium** + **cottonoid patties** protect the cerebellar surface
- **Laterally**: follow the border of the **petrosal sinus / petrous ridge** until the **trochlear nerve is visualized entering the tentorium's free border**. **Cut before the trochlear entry point** while the nerve is in direct view. (See Figure 13 — red dashed line sacrifices the nerve; black dashed line preserves it.)
- **Medially**: preserve the **straight sinus** and tributaries
- **Venous lakes** within tentorial leaflets → thrombin-soaked gelfoam; **do NOT bipolar** (shrinks and tears tentorial edges)
- **Posterior petrosal vein** and other bridging veins protected during sectioning

### Tumor Resection (Intra-axial)

- After tentorial opening, arachnoid over the **medial dorsolateral mesencephalon** dissected to mobilize cerebellum inferiorly
- Navigation guides **corticotomy** if lesion is not apparent on the pial surface
- **Dynamic retraction with the suction apparatus** — no fixed retractors; better working-zone visibility
- **Inferior-to-superior trajectory** is ideal for tumors extending to the temporal horn and calcar avis
- **Preserve distal PCA en passage branches** meticulously
- Small **overlying cortical arteries** may be sacrificed

### Tumor Resection (Extra-axial, medial tentorial / petrous apex meningiomas)

- Park-bench position
- **Early identification of the dorsolateral brainstem and neurovascular structures** at the tentorial incisura — microdissect away from the tumor **before debulking** (field will become bloody)
- **Extradural devascularization** via cauterization of the **undersurface of the tentorium**
- Generous tentorial incision from petrous ridge to midline **preserving trochlear nerve**
- Preserve **straight sinus** medially
- Tentorial resection yields a wide corridor to **basal occipital and posteromedial temporal** regions

## Closure

- **Tentorium gently reflected back** to original position — **NOT sutured**
- **Watertight dura** with **pericranial autograft** (avoid allograft)
- Bone plate replaced with cranial plates if no cerebellar swelling
- **Neck muscles gently approximated** — minimize strangulation by deep sutures
- **Fascia watertight**

## Postoperative Considerations

- **ICU 1-2 days**, ward thereafter
- **Prophylactic steroids** (aseptic meningitis)
- Ventriculostomy removed during recovery
- Watch for **cerebellar edema** (retraction) and posterior fossa tension — partial transverse sinus thrombosis + paravermian vein sacrifice compound risk; leave bone off and avoid dural tension if swollen

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Long, narrow corridor** with favorable working angles — requires special microsurgical expertise
- **Preserve the trochlear nerve** — identify it entering the tentorial free edge and **cut medial to it**
- **PCA en passage branches** are numerous — avoid indiscriminate coagulation (thalamic / occipital lobe ischemia)
- **Thalamoperforators** can be injured during tumor manipulation
- **Venous lakes in the tentorium** → gelfoam, **no bipolar**
- **Dynamic retraction with suction** > fixed retractors
- **Intraoperative navigation** is essential — unusual angles disorient the operator easily
- **Early microdissection of brainstem neurovascular structures** for extra-axial tumors — before bleeding obscures the field
- **Inferior-to-superior trajectory** excels for lesions at temporal horn / calcar avis level

## Complications

- **Trochlear nerve injury** during tentorial transection
- **PCA territory infarct** (occipital, thalamic)
- **Thalamoperforator injury** → deep ischemia
- **Straight sinus / vein of Galen / basal vein injury**
- **Cerebellar retraction edema**, posterior fossa tension
- **CSF fistula**, **aseptic meningitis**
- **Visual field deficit** (occipital cortex manipulation)
