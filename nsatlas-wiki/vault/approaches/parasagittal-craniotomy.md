# Parasagittal Craniotomy

*Parasagittal Craniotomy: Nuances of Technique.*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.ch06*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol | **Pages:** 11 | **Figures:** 10

## Overview

The parasagittal craniotomy exposes lesions along the **paramedian hemispheres and superior sagittal sinus (SSS)**. The basic exposure provides access to the SSS itself and can be extended across the midline for complete sinus control.

**Key regional hazards:** numerous bridging veins and unpredictable parasagittal venous lakes (lacunae) → risk of profuse blood loss and **life-threatening venous air embolism**.

### Critical anatomical fact

The **sagittal suture does NOT define the exact location of the SSS**. The sinus is typically **deviated to the right of the sagittal suture**, maximum deviation usually ≤ **11 mm**.

The interhemispheric extension is covered in [[interhemispheric-craniotomy]].

## Indications

- **Parasagittal meningiomas** (most common)
- **Metastases, gliomas** adjacent to the SSS
- **Arteriovenous malformations, cavernous malformations** intimately associated with the SSS and superior falx

## Preoperative Considerations

- **Neuronavigation** for craniotomy planning
- **MR or CT venogram**:
  - Locate large bridging veins and lacunae
  - Confirm **SSS patency** (especially when meningioma infiltrates)
- If numerous parasagittal veins prohibit ipsilateral interhemispheric work → consider [[contralateral-interhemispheric-transfalcine-transprecuneus-approach|contralateral interhemispheric transfalcine route]]
- **Air embolism workup** when tumor invades sinus lumen: TEE/transthoracic Doppler intraop, low threshold of suspicion. High-risk cases → **central venous catheter + precordial Doppler**
- **Lumbar CSF drainage**:
  - Decompresses dura away from bone, protects sinuses during drilling
  - Used routinely regardless of tumor size
  - For large tumors with midline shift, CSF drained **while dura is being opened** to decrease brain shift risk
- **Mannitol + steroids** for significant vasogenic edema

## Operative Anatomy

### Superior Sagittal Sinus and Bridging Veins

- Caliber **increases from anterior to posterior**
- **Paramedian venous lakes (lacunae)** can extend laterally beyond the sinus edges → **profuse bleeding** during dural opening near midline
- **Bridging veins** travel posterior → anterior
- **Anterior third**: occasional sacrifice of 1-2 small bridging veins tolerated
- **Middle and posterior thirds**: bridging veins are **indispensable** — sacrifice → serious risk of venous infarction

## Patient Position

- **Anterior/middle third lesions**: supine, head neutral
  - Lesion at highest point of operative field, but avoid overzealous head elevation (air embolism risk)
- **Posterior third lesions**: lateral position — avoids extreme head elevation while allowing gravity clearance of blood from the field
- **Skull clamp** with flexion calibrated to lesion location:
  - Posterior middle-third: **neck flexed**
  - Anterior: head neutral

## Skin Incision

- **Linear incision** preferred for tumors of all sizes (Cohen-Gadol's preference)
- **U-shaped** incision optional for large lesions
- If SSS exposure required: **incision crosses the midline**
- **Neuronavigation / preoperative venogram** to localize and protect parasagittal bridging veins

## Bone Work

- **Two burr holes directly over the SSS** (remember right-sided deviation up to 11 mm)
- Early sinus identification guides bone flap planning
- **Penfield #3** to strip inner-table dura from SSS wall
- If SSS wall adherent: place a **third burr hole**; all burr holes in epidural continuity
- **Lumbar CSF drainage** mobilizes sinus wall/dura away from inner table → prevents footplate injury
- **Last bone cut over the sinus** → allows rapid flap elevation if sinus is torn
- If lesion abuts interhemispheric fissure, **expose corresponding length of SSS**
- Mild/moderate sinus wall bleeding after flap elevation: thrombin-soaked gelfoam or **Surgicel Fibrillar** left in place through closure
- **AVOID aggressive dural coagulation** — expands sinus tears
- **Highest risk step for air embolism**: unexplained hypotension, decreased ETCO₂, dramatically "dry" field = suspect air embolism

## Dural Opening

- **Curvilinear dural flap based on the SSS** for interhemispheric work
- Initial dural opening **away from the SSS** to identify cortical veins and midline drainage patterns first
- If a parasagittal vein is encountered → **adjust dural incision** to protect the vein's inlet into the sinus
- If the SSS edge is inadvertently cut during medial dural incision → **close with fine sutures**; **do NOT bipolar** (shrinks dural edges, expands tear, profuse bleeding)
- When tumor-infiltrated dura must be removed, still **respect parasagittal veins**
- Paramedian venous lakes may force the dural incision to run **parallel** to the sinus/lake rather than perpendicular near midline

## Intradural Work

- **Release bridging veins from arachnoid adhesions** and untether for mobilization; arachnoid can be thick and highly adherent — lumbar CSF drainage eases this
- **Retraction sutures in the superior falx just below the sinus** gently mobilize and rotate the SSS — expands operative corridor and working angles within the interhemispheric space
- **Do NOT tension parasagittal veins** during microdissection
- Proceed with convexity tumor resection or interhemispheric microdissection depending on pathology
- For meningiomas: devascularize early along falx and/or dura

## Closure

- **Watertight dura preferable** but often simply approximated for supratentorial lesions (author's practice)
- **Do NOT place tack-up sutures close to the SSS**
- **Hemostasis along the sinus is cardinal** — prevents compressive postoperative epidural hematoma

## Postoperative Considerations

- ICU observation for 1-2 days
- Monitor for venous infarction (especially if middle/posterior bridging vein sacrificed)
- Watch for compressive epidural hematoma from sinus bleeding

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **SSS injury must be prevented at all costs** — low threshold of suspicion for air embolism in sitting/semi-sitting cases
- **Middle and posterior third bridging veins are indispensable** — higher venous infarct risk than operators commonly assume
- **SSS deviates right** up to 11 mm — confirm location with navigation, do not assume sagittal suture = sinus
- **Last bone cut over the sinus** — safety rule for rapid flap elevation on injury
- **No bipolar on SSS tears** — sutures only
- **Dural incision runs parallel** (not perpendicular) to venous lakes near the midline
- **Retraction sutures rotate the SSS** to open the corridor; fixed retractors on the sinus are ineffective and harmful
- Consider **contralateral transfalcine** approach when ipsilateral parasagittal veins are dense

## Complications

- **Venous air embolism**
- **SSS laceration** with torrential bleeding
- **Venous infarction** from bridging vein sacrifice (hemiparesis, seizure)
- **Postoperative epidural hematoma** from inadequate sinus hemostasis
- **Cerebral herniation** during dural opening in large edematous tumors (mitigated by lumbar drainage timed to dural opening)
