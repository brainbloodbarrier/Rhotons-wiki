# Posterior Petrosectomy

Posterior petrosectomy is a family of skull-base approaches in which progressively more of the posterior temporal bone is drilled away to expose the petroclival region, the [[cerebellopontine-angle]], the clivus, and the ventrolateral pontomesencephalic junction. The three canonical variants — **retrolabyrinthine**, **translabyrinthine**, and **transcochlear** — represent an escalating trade between exposure and preservation of hearing, vestibular function, and facial nerve. Combined with a temporal craniotomy and division of the tentorium, they form the **combined supra-infratentorial (combined petrosal) approach** popularized for petroclival meningiomas and large upper-pontine lesions.

## Surgical Anatomy

Key temporal-bone structures drilled or preserved:

- **Mastoid antrum and air cells** — entry corridor.
- **Sigmoid sinus** — skeletonized and decompressed; preserved in every variant.
- **Endolymphatic sac and duct** — landmark for the posterior semicircular canal; preserved in retrolabyrinthine.
- **Labyrinth** (three semicircular canals + vestibule) — preserved in retrolabyrinthine; sacrificed in translabyrinthine and transcochlear.
- **Internal auditory canal (IAC)** — unroofed in trans/transcochlear variants.
- **Cochlea** — preserved in retro- and translabyrinthine; sacrificed in transcochlear.
- **Facial nerve (CN VII)** — preserved in all; transposed anteriorly in transcochlear.
- **Jugular bulb** — decompressed; a high bulb limits exposure.

## The Three Variants

### Retrolabyrinthine

- Drilling stops at the semicircular canals, preserving hearing and vestibular function.
- Exposure limited to the dura between the sigmoid sinus and the labyrinth (a narrow triangular window).
- **Indications:** vestibular nerve section for Ménière disease, microvascular decompression of CN V or VII when a simple retrosigmoid is inadequate, small CPA lesions with useful hearing.

### Translabyrinthine

- Sacrifices the labyrinth to open a wider window over the IAC and CPA.
- **Preserves the facial nerve** (identified and followed through the fallopian canal from geniculate ganglion to stylomastoid foramen).
- **Destroys hearing** irreversibly.
- **Indications:** [[acoustic-neuroma]] with non-serviceable hearing, large CPA lesions, neurofibromatosis 2 tumors after NF2 protocols, CPA meningiomas extending into the IAC.

### Transcochlear

- Extends translabyrinthine by drilling the cochlea and transposing CN VII posteriorly.
- Exposes the **prepontine cistern**, clivus, basilar artery, and contralateral CPA.
- Sacrifices hearing permanently and incurs significant facial weakness risk.
- **Indications:** petroclival meningioma with clival extension, large epidermoids, midbasilar aneurysms (rare).

## Combined Petrosal Approach

For **petroclival [[lateral-sphenoid-wing-meningioma]] extension** or true petroclival tumors, a combined approach adds:

- **Temporal (subtemporal) craniotomy** above the transverse sinus and tentorium.
- **Petrosectomy** (usually retrolabyrinthine or translabyrinthine) below.
- **Tentorial incision** parallel to the petrous ridge, sparing the vein of Labbé superior drainage and the superior petrosal sinus.
- Provides a wide corridor from the upper clivus to the lower clivus with simultaneous supra- and infratentorial access.

## Positioning and Incision

- **Supine with head rotated** or **park-bench** on the lesion side.
- **C-shaped retroauricular incision** extending from the superior temporal line, behind the pinna, to the mastoid tip.
- Identify landmarks: asterion, mastoid tip, zygoma, digastric groove; external auditory canal preserved.
- Craniotomy crossing the transverse and sigmoid sinuses as needed for combined petrosal.

## Pitfalls and Complications

- **Facial nerve injury** — greatest in transcochlear; identified early with nerve stimulator and preserved with diamond burs under copious irrigation.
- **CSF leak** — high rate in translabyrinthine; obliterate eustachian tube and middle ear with fat/muscle; multilayer closure.
- **Vein of Labbé injury** — during temporal lobe elevation in combined approach; temporal lobe venous infarction can be catastrophic.
- **Sigmoid / jugular bulb injury** — profuse venous bleeding; bone wax, Surgicel, gentle compression.
- **Chronic otologic sequelae** — wound infection, mastoid cavity issues, balance dysfunction.

## Outcome Considerations

- For vestibular schwannoma, facial nerve preservation rates are high (>90% House-Brackmann I–II in experienced hands) in translabyrinthine if tumor has not invaded the nerve.
- Hearing preservation is **impossible** in translabyrinthine and transcochlear — patient selection is mandatory.
- Petroclival meningioma resection remains incomplete in many cases; subtotal resection + radiosurgery is a common modern strategy.
