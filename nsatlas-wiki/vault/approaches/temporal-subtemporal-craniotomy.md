# Temporal / Subtemporal Craniotomy

*Techniques for Performance of Temporal Craniotomy.*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.ch05*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol | **Pages:** 13 | **Figures:** 11

## Overview

Temporal craniotomy is a simple approach with vast applicability to intra- and extra-axial pathologies. The **subtemporal** corridor opens the floor of the middle fossa, upper petroclival region, and associated cisterns — extended via **anterior petrosectomy** to reach the anterior upper brainstem.

### Functional considerations

- **Dominant superior and posterior middle temporal gyri**: language functions — avoid transgression
- **Anterior middle temporal gyrus**: possibly distance contemplation, face recognition, reading word meaning
- **Inferior temporal gyrus**: visual processing, complex object features, face perception, number recognition

## Indications

### Temporal (lateral/convexity)

- Mid-to-posterior intraparenchymal and convexity temporal lobe tumors
- **Mid hippocampal** lesions via transsulcal approach
- **Lateral thalamic** tumors and **basal cisterns** via transcortical transventricular transchoroidal pathway
- Sylvian fissure exposure is **limited**

### Subtemporal

- **Middle fossa meningiomas**
- Small **acoustic tumors** and **trigeminal schwannomas**
- Low-lying basilar caput / **upper basilar artery aneurysms**
- **Upper petroclival meningiomas** (anterior petrosal approach)
- **Superior semicircular canal dehiscence** repair
- Combined with extended pterional for complex interpeduncular vascular abnormalities and multi-compartment medial incisural / parachiasmatic meningiomas

## Preoperative Considerations

- **Vein of Labbé drainage site**: MR venogram — **~1 cm superior** to the superior zygomatic arch and **2-5 cm (mean 2.9 cm) posterior** to the external auditory meatus opening
- Define arterial/venous displacement along medial tentorium
- Plan **combined supra- + infratentorial** approach if needed
- **Mannitol 1 g/kg** at skin incision for expected "tight" brain
- **Cold lactated Ringer's** if cortical stimulation planned
- **Lumbar drain** for most subtemporal cases regardless of tumor size — early CSF drainage minimizes temporal lobe retraction injury (basal cisterns not reached until after lobe elevation)

## Operative Anatomy

### Surface Landmarks

- **Root of the zygoma** marks the level of the **middle fossa floor** — critical for burr hole and inferior craniotomy edge
- Middle fossa floor is **oblique, slopes slightly superior from anterior to posterior**
- **Squamosal suture** overlies the Sylvian fissure and delineates the superior temporal lobe limit
- **Superficial temporal artery**: one finger-breadth anterior to the tragus, bifurcates ~5 cm above the zygoma
- **Temporal branch of the facial nerve**: anterior over the zygoma, superficial to the two layers of anterior superficial temporal fascia

### Vein of Labbé

- Variable course toward the transverse sinus
- **Preservation is essential** — particularly for combined approaches to the posterior temporal region
- **Do NOT confuse with the middle temporal vein**
- Sacrifice → temporal lobe **venous infarction**

## Patient Position

- **Supine** (if neck supple), head rotated with a **large ipsilateral shoulder roll** to minimize neck torsion; nonphysiologic rotation compromises jugular return and causes postoperative neck pain
- **Lateral**: older / heavy-set patients, significant cervical spondylosis
- Head tilted **~20° toward the floor** for gravity-based mobilization of temporal lobe off middle fossa floor

## Skin Incision

- **Linear incision** — preferred when possible (heals better); horizontal temporalis disconnection from superior temporal line maximizes flap retraction
- **Horseshoe** — large subtemporal lesions
- **Reverse question mark** — when anterior temporal pole access required
- Incision extending to zygoma level must spare the **main trunk of the STA** (palpate before marking)

## Bone Work

- **Generous single burr hole just above the root of the zygoma**
- **Penfield #3** mobilizes dura from inner calvarium
- Multiple burr holes if dura adherent
- **Lumbar drainage ~30-40 cc CSF** (in 10-20 cc aliquots) relaxes brain, eases dural-calvarium dissection, reduces tear risk — **especially important for extradural approaches** where dural injury would breach lobe protection
- **Craniotome** completes the flap
- **Inferior craniotomy edge**: as close to the middle fossa floor as possible — **root of zygoma upper edge** = floor level
- **Overhanging inferior bone** removed with **Leksell rongeur** and handheld drill until craniotomy edge matches floor level
- **Mastoid air cells and temporal bone** thoroughly **waxed** to prevent postoperative CSF fistula
- **Dural tack-up sutures** placed

## Dural Opening

- **Standard curvilinear** opening for intraparenchymal lesions
- **Extradural dissection** (for skull base / anterior petrosal) resumed without violating dura
- **Dural opening adjusted for vein of Labbé protection**
- Additional lumbar CSF may be released to relax the lobe before retraction

## Intradural Work

- Intraparenchymal / convexity lesion: standard microsurgical resection
- **Transsulcal corridor** for mid hippocampal targets
- **Transcortical-transventricular-transchoroidal** pathway for lateral thalamic / basal cistern reach
- **Subtemporal corridor**: elevate the temporal lobe gently off the floor (with CSF drainage); access to middle fossa floor, Meckel's cave region, and tentorial notch
- For **anterior petrosal approach** continuation, see the dedicated anterior petrosectomy chapter

### Extended Subtemporal Transtentorial Approach (to Ventrolateral Upper Brainstem)

**Purpose:** replace more radical skull base approaches (anterior petrosectomy) for ventrolateral upper brainstem access.

**Key steps for anterolateral medial tentorial flap reflection:**

1. **Dissect the trochlear nerve from its dural canal** up to its entrance into the cavernous sinus (**mean distance 8 mm**, range 3.4-14.9 mm)
2. **Extend the tentorial incision up to Meckel's cave**

- Two **tentorial retraction sutures** mobilize the tentorium
- Microscissors open the medial border of the **trochlear dural canal** — nerve is not adherent; microdissector gently mobilizes it toward the brainstem
- Tentorial incision oriented toward the **superior petrosal sinus** at the area just above the trigeminal root entry to Meckel's cave
- Result: **supra- and infratrochlear working window** with trochlear nerve free of tentorial flap
- Extended tentorial incision exposes **trigeminal root** and **AICA origin** laterally at Meckel's cave

## Closure

- Middle fossa bone defect: meticulous wax sealing of mastoid air cells
- Watertight dural closure for subtemporal / extradural approaches (CSF fistula risk via petrous)
- Temporalis reattachment to superior temporal line; single-layer myocutaneous preferred
- Standard scalp closure

## Postoperative Considerations

- ICU observation
- Watch for **temporal lobe venous infarction** (if Labbé compromised) → aphasia (dominant side), seizures, swelling
- Monitor for **CSF leak / otorrhea / rhinorrhea** via petrous air cells

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Root of zygoma = middle fossa floor** — align the inferior craniotomy edge here; use rongeur/drill to remove overhang
- **Wax the mastoid air cells** aggressively — otherwise postoperative CSF fistula
- **Lumbar drain is standard** for subtemporal — basal cisterns are downstream of lobe elevation
- **Vein of Labbé** is the most critical preoperative imaging task for posterior subtemporal cases
- **Do NOT confuse middle temporal vein with vein of Labbé**
- **Linear incision heals better** than horseshoe when feasible
- **Shoulder roll** essential for supine neck rotation — prevents jugular compromise
- **Extended subtemporal transtentorial** (with trochlear nerve unroofing + tentorial incision to Meckel's cave) can **replace anterior petrosectomy** for ventrolateral brainstem access

## Complications

- **Vein of Labbé sacrifice → temporal lobe venous infarction** (dysphasia, seizures, swelling)
- **CSF rhinorrhea / otorrhea** from unwaxed petrous air cells
- **Trochlear nerve injury** during tentorial flap dissection
- **Temporal lobe retraction injury** (mitigated by lumbar drainage)
- **Facial nerve temporal branch injury** (frontalis weakness)
- **Jugular compromise** from excessive neck rotation
