# Paramedian Supracerebellar Craniotomy

*Paramedian Supracerebellar Craniotomy for Brainstem Pilocytic Astrocytoma.*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.ch13*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol | **Pages:** 15 | **Figures:** 14

## Overview

A **variation of the supracerebellar craniotomy** — the paramedian supracerebellar (usually **left-sided**) approach has become Cohen-Gadol's preferred route to the **pineal region, posterolateral mesencephalon, posterior third ventricle, and posterior basal temporal lobe**, even for large midline pineal tumors.

### Advantages over [[midline-supracerebellar-craniotomy|midline bilateral suboccipital]]

1. **Less invasive craniotomy** — does not place the frequently dominant **right transverse sinus and torcula** at risk
2. **Only one cerebellar hemisphere** manipulated
3. **Vermian bridging veins often protected** (midline route sacrifices nearly all)
4. **Lower slope of the lateral cerebellum** → more inferior trajectory to the inferior pole of the tumor
5. Intact **contralateral supracerebellar route remains available** for reoperation

The analogy: paramedian vs midline = pterional/frontolateral vs bifrontal for olfactory groove meningiomas.

## Indications

- **Pineal region tumors** — germ cell tumors, pineoblastomas, astrocytomas, meningiomas, epidermoid tumors, pilocytic astrocytomas
- **Posterior third ventricular tumors** — gentle pulvinar manipulation well tolerated
- **Tentorial dural arteriovenous fistulae / malformations**
- **Posterolateral mesencephalic cavernous malformations, pilocytic astrocytomas**
- **Distal superior cerebellar artery aneurysms**
- **Transtentorial extension** (wide tentorial section):
  - Petrous apex meningiomas with supratentorial extension (avoids staged subtemporal)
  - **Medial tentorial meningiomas** (avoids temporal lobe retraction of subtemporal route)
  - Posterior hippocampal AVMs, cavernomas, astrocytomas, metastases — **resect supratentorial tumors through an infratentorial craniotomy**

### Contraindication

- Deep venous structures **posteriorly displaced** by posterior thalamic/vermian tumor mimicking a pineal mass

## Preoperative Considerations

- **MRI** for tumor extent, deep venous relationships (vein of Galen, BVR, ICV, straight sinus), need for transtentorial corridor
- **Germ cell markers** — serum and CSF
- **Obstructive hydrocephalus**: prepare Keen's point or preoperative frontal ventriculostomy (paramedian incision uncovers Keen's point burr hole region)
- **Transverse + sigmoid sinus** courses studied
- **Modified park-bench** preferred; consider sitting in steep-tentorium / short-neck / obese patients
- **Lumbar drain** (if no obstructive hydrocephalus) → cerebellar decompression and mobilization

## Operative Anatomy

### Pineal Region

- **Splenium anterior to vein of Galen** — following tentorial contour temptingly leads to vein of Galen; redirect trajectory **inferiorly** toward pineal
- **Posterior basal temporal lobe** and **occipital lobe** become available with tentorial section

### Midline vs Paramedian Trajectory

- **Midline** requires **culmen retraction** (steep)
- **Paramedian** reaches over the **lateral cerebellum / quadrangular lobule** — lower, less steep, more direct
- **CN IV** at the inferolateral corner of the operative field

## Patient Position

- **Modified park-bench** (Cohen-Gadol's preference)
- **Skull clamp**, neck flexed, head turned **15-20° toward the floor**
- **Ipsilateral shoulder** falls forward, taped out of surgeon's working zone
- **Intraoperative neuronavigation** to locate midline, transverse sinus, sigmoid sinus

## Skin Incision

- **Paramedian vertical linear incision** halfway between the **inion** and the **mastoid groove**
- **One-third above, two-thirds below** the transverse sinus
- **~7-8 cm** long
- **Keen's point** lies within the upper edge of the incision

## Bone Work

- **Single burr hole** at the inferior edge of the transverse sinus, ~2 cm lateral to midline/torcula
- **Small bone flap** elevated; **entire width of the transverse sinus exposed** to allow rostral sinus mobilization
- Avoid drill footplate on dura/sinus — strip dura first with Penfield #3

## Dural Opening

- **Single curved dural flap** based on the transverse sinus
- **Two retraction sutures** through the posterior tentorium mobilize and gently rotate the transverse sinus **superiorly**, expanding the supracerebellar corridor
- **Fixed tentorial retractors do NOT help** — interfere with instrument angles
- Gradual **lumbar / ventriculostomy CSF release** → gentle caudal mobilization of the lateral cerebellar hemisphere

## Intradural Work

- **One or two paramedian bridging veins** may be sacrificed; **large midline bridging veins are spared**
- **Microdoppler ultrasonography** confirms transverse sinus patency after retraction
- Dissect arachnoid over **medial dorsolateral mesencephalon** — mobilizes cerebellum downward, opens corridor to midline
- **CN IV** at inferolateral corner — protect
- **"Cross-court" trajectory**: tentorial surface of cerebellum is highest at its anteromedial apex and slopes down laterally → the lateral paramedian corridor provides a more inferior trajectory to posterior midline and posterolateral mesencephalon than the midline route
- Redirect **inferiorly** as the tumor is reached (below vein of Galen)
- Open arachnoid over tumor, begin microsurgical resection
- **Transtentorial modification** (if needed): wide tentorial incision exposes the posterior ambient cisterns, basal temporal lobe, parahippocampus, and distal PCA branches

## Closure

- **Watertight dura** (posterior fossa → CSF fistula risk) — pericranial autograft preferred over allograft
- Bone flap replaced with cranial plates
- **Neck muscles gently approximated** (avoid tension → muscle necrosis / pain); fascia watertight
- **Subgaleal drain** not routine

## Postoperative Considerations

- **ICU 1-2 days**, then ward
- **Steroids** prophylactically (aseptic meningitis)
- Ventriculostomy weaned postoperatively
- Watch for cerebellar edema / posterior fossa tension — especially if transverse sinus partially thrombosed; if brain swollen at closure, **do not tension dural closure, do not replace bone flap**

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Left-sided** variant protects the dominant right transverse sinus and torcula
- **Tentorial retraction sutures** rotate the transverse sinus superiorly and expand the corridor — **far superior to fixed retractors**
- **Cross-court lateral trajectory** is shallower than the midline culmen route
- Redirect trajectory **inferiorly** to avoid blind dissection around vein of Galen
- **Microdoppler** monitors sinus patency during retraction
- **Transtentorial extension** handles supratentorial pineal/temporal/falcotentorial components without staged subtemporal surgery
- **CN IV** at inferolateral exposure — identify early, protect

## Complications

- **Venous air embolism** (sitting position)
- **Transverse sinus laceration / thrombosis**
- **Cerebellar edema** (retraction); posterior fossa tension
- **Vein of Galen / deep venous system injury**
- **CN IV palsy**
- **Visual field / hemianopia** (medial occipital retraction in transtentorial extension)
- **CSF fistula**, **aseptic meningitis**
