# Retromastoid Craniotomy

*Retromastoid Craniotomy for Acoustic Tumor*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.ch11*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol | **Pages:** 22 | **Figures:** 18

## General Considerations

As the [[pterional-craniotomy]] is the workhorse for supratentorial parasellar lesions, **the retromastoid craniotomy is the workhorse of infratentorial approaches** for reaching the [[cerebellopontine-angle|cerebellopontine (CP) angle]] and ventrolateral brainstem.

Cohen-Gadol defines the **extended retromastoid approach** as a modification that includes partial removal of the bone over the sigmoid sinus. This "untethering" allows lateral mobilization of the sigmoid sinus using retraction sutures after dural opening, expanding the lateral operative trajectory toward the CP angle while **reducing retraction on the cerebellar hemisphere**.

The operative corridor extends from the ventrolateral midbrain and upper clivus to the ventrolateral medulla and lower clivus.

> Petrosectomy exposures to the ventrolateral and anterior brainstem are most likely **overutilized** — the extended retrosigmoid route offers many of the same advantages with minimal morbidity. However, tumor texture matters: soft/suckable lesions (epidermoid) → retrosigmoid OK; fibrous meningiomas with vascular encasement → may require [[anterior-petrosectomy|petrosectomy]].

## Indications

- Almost all lesions within the CP angle and ventrolateral brainstem
- [[microvascular-decompression-for-trigeminal-neuralgia|Microvascular decompression]] (trigeminal neuralgia, [[hemifacial-spasm]], [[glossopharyngeal-neuralgia]])
- Acoustic neuromas (vestibular schwannomas)
- CPA epidermoid tumors and meningiomas
- Ventrally located epidermoid tumors crossing the ventral brainstem

### Contraindication
- **Contralateral hearing loss** — may be a contraindication for retromastoid craniotomy

## Preoperative Considerations

- **BAERs monitoring** during MVD and lesions affecting CN VII/VIII — latency of peak V is the best electrophysiologic indicator for cochlear nerve injury
- **Transverse and sigmoid sinuses** have variable location along temporal/occipital bones — review preop imaging
- Short necks/mild brachycephaly: short sigmoid sinus with inferiorly displaced transverse-sigmoid junction — anatomical variations can disorient the surgeon
- **Generous mastoid air cells** increase CSF fistula risk

## Operative Anatomy

Key structures via the retromastoid corridor:
- **Bony:** temporal lobe, occipital lobe, vein of Labbe, Trautman's triangle, otic capsule, condylar fossa, sigmoid sinus, jugular bulb, mastoid groove
- **Neural:** CN V (deeper, further from surgeon), CN VII/VIII (more superficial, oblique inferior-to-superior trajectory), CN VI, CN IX, X, XI
- **Vascular:** AICA, PICA, labyrinthine artery, superior petrosal vein, subarcuate artery, vertebral artery, internal jugular vein, ICA

## Steps of the Operation

### 1. Positioning — Park-Bench (Preferred)

- **Park-bench** preferred over supine — prevents non-physiologic neck posture, reduces venous congestion, *decreases postoperative neck pain*
- **Lumbar puncture BEFORE draping** — drain 35-40cc CSF for posterior fossa decompression. This critically facilitates safe dural opening and "going around" the cerebellum to reach CPA cisterns
- Ipsilateral shoulder falls forward, mobilized caudally (critical for obese/short-necked patients)
- Avoid tape directly over fibula head → peroneal nerve palsy
- **Mastoid bone = highest point** in the operative field
- Head slightly flexed and turned toward floor; bed flexed 15° up, then Trendelenburg 10° → brings operative area into horizontal plane
- For midline clival tumors: do NOT rotate toward floor (overhanging lateral cerebellar hemisphere compromises angle)

### 2. Incision — Modified Reverse "U" (Walter Dandy)

Summit marked just below the presumed transverse-sigmoid junction (where inion-to-root-of-zygoma line crosses mastoid groove). For large acoustic tumors, widen the base.

**Advantages over linear incision:**
1. No caudal muscle dissection → less postoperative suboccipital pain
2. Reflects myocutaneous flap **inferiorly, out of the surgeon's working zone** (linear incision accumulates scalp/muscle under retractor, increasing working distance)

### 3. Craniotomy

- **Single burr hole** at edge of dural sinus junction — place with caution, enlarge if needed
- Asterion is NOT a constant landmark — burr hole over it may expose entire transverse sinus width → injury risk
- Dissect dura from inner skull, detach from dural sinuses carefully
- **Sigmoid sinus may be embedded in inner cortical bone** — leave alone, "egg-shell" in second step
- MVD: craniotomy/craniectomy ~1.5-2x quarter coin
- Older patients/adherent dura: plan craniectomy (safer than craniotomy)
- When in doubt, plan craniectomy

### 4. Extended Retromastoid — Sigmoid Sinus Exposure

> **Exposing the posterior half of the sigmoid sinus is the cardinal step** to expand the operative corridor.

- Drill portion of mastoid bone exposing posterior sigmoid sinus edge along its length
- Bevel out overhanging bone edges to improve lateral mobilization/rotation during dural reflection
- Enables more anterolateral trajectory toward CPA, reducing cerebellar retraction
- "Egg-shelling" technique: careful drilling, Kerrison rongeurs with "mouth" pointed AWAY from sinus
- If sinus tear: bone wax to seal against mastoid edge (do NOT pack Gelfoam INTO sinus → thrombosis)
- **Mastoid air cells:** wax thoroughly after craniotomy AND at closure. Irrigate epidural space to prevent bone dust entering intradural space (aseptic meningitis risk)

### 5. Operative Corridors

#### Supralateral Route (Lesions Posterior/Superior to CN VII/VIII)

- Dural incisions parallel to sigmoid and transverse sinuses, a few mm from edge
- Retraction sutures along sigmoid sinus dura → gently mobilize and rotate anterolaterally → expands lateral corridor toward CPA
- **"Rubber dam" technique:** piece of glove (slightly larger than cottonoid) protects cerebellar hemisphere from friction during "going around" maneuver
- Advance cottonoid parallel to petrous-tentorial junction (groove) toward petrous apex
- **CRITICAL:** Avoid purely medial cerebellar retraction (vector parallel to CN VIII → hearing loss)
- Retract parallel to CN V and IX direction only
- Dynamic retraction with suction at CN V root entry zone → obviate fixed rigid retractors and sacrifice of superior petrosal vein

#### Infralateral Route (Lesions Anterior/Inferior to CN VII/VIII)

- Dura incised parallel to sigmoid sinus and posterior fossa floor
- Same rubber dam technique
- Retraction vector parallel to CN IX
- Identify petrous bone-posterior fossa floor junction to prevent disorientation

### 6. Closure

- MVD: primary dural approximation (NOT watertight — very low CSF leak rate, minimal risk of increased CSF pressure)
- **Tumor resection: watertight dural closure mandatory**
- Mastoid air cells rewaxed thoroughly ("wax in, wax out")
- Methyl methacrylate cranioplasty or bone flap replacement
- Muscle and scalp closed in anatomical layers

## Transtentorial Extension

The retromastoid and supracerebellar routes can be combined: expose tentorium → excise tentorium → access ventrolateral petroclival area, posterior parahippocampus, and middle fossa floor. Facilitates single-stage resection of posterior fossa tumors extending into middle fossa.

## Alternative Incisions

**Linear incision:** marked 1/3 above and 2/3 below the transverse sinus. Most commonly used for MVD and small CPA tumors. **Shortcomings:** accumulates tissue under retractor, increases working distance.

## Postoperative Considerations

- ICU overnight, ward 2-3 days
- **Steroids** to prevent aseptic meningitis and minimize nausea/headaches
- **Delayed facial palsy and hearing loss** may occur after MVD/epidermoid resection — usually temporary, responds to tapered dexamethasone (1 week)

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- The extended retromastoid is the **flexible workhorse for CPA lesions** — can sometimes obviate petrosal osteotomies
- Cerebellum should be retracted **only parallel to the direction of CN V and IX** to avoid direct traction on CN VIII
- Sharp arachnoid dissection and strategic dynamic cerebellar retraction will facilitate mobilization without placing cranial nerves at risk
