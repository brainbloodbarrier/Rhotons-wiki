# Pterional Craniotomy

*Pterional Craniotomy: Improving the Efficiency of Exposure*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.ch02*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol / Acioly | **Pages:** 23 | **Figures:** 20

## General Considerations

The pterional or frontotemporal craniotomy is the **workhorse of the supratentorial approaches**. Cohen-Gadol uses the *extended pterional approach*, defined as a standard pterional craniotomy supplemented and expanded by:

1. **Osteotomy** along the lateral sphenoid wing to the level of the superior orbital fissure
2. **Drilling** along the roof of the orbit to flatten its surface
3. If necessary, **rongeuring** temporal squama towards the floor of the middle fossa

These modifications provide unobstructed operative working angles toward the parasellar and subfrontal targets. The osteotomy along the orbital roof provides some advantages of an [[orbitozygomatic-craniotomy]] while being more efficient and with less cosmetic deformity.

The [[supraorbital-craniotomy]] through the eyebrow incision is a minimally invasive keyhole alternative. A *minipterional* approach centered on the sylvian fissure axis produces similar exposure to the standard pterional.

> *"Therefore, routine indiscriminate use of one approach should be avoided."* The operator should be intimately familiar with all modifications of exposures to the subfrontal corridor.

## Indications

- Parasellar lesions around the [[circle-of-willis|circle of Willis]] and optic apparatus
- Anterior skull base tumors (including orbital lesions)
- Interpeduncular and intrasylvian pathologies
- Giant midline anterior cranial fossa intradural lesions (olfactory and cribriform plate meningiomas) — has **replaced** [[bifrontal-craniotomy]] for these in Cohen-Gadol's practice
- Contralateral lesions in select cases: contralateral ophthalmic, MCA, and P1 artery aneurysms

### NOT indicated for

- **Third ventricular tumors**, especially craniopharyngiomas — use endoscopic endonasal route
- **Pituitary tumors** of any size or texture — always endonasal approach
- **Fibrous superior petroclival meningiomas** and large clival tumors — use [[anterior-petrosectomy|petrosal osteotomies]]

## Preoperative Considerations

- **Antiepileptic medications** for 7 days postop (terminate if no seizures)
- **Lumbar drain** for CSF drainage and brain relaxation in large skull base lesions blocking basal cisterns
- **Mannitol** depending on mass effect; **dexamethasone** for significant vasogenic edema
- **Embolization** sparingly (AVMs, meningiomas)
- **Neuronavigation** routinely used
- **EEG** for burst suppression confirmation during temporary parent vessel occlusion (aneurysms)
- **Blood pressure**: normotensive throughout, except AVMs (systolic kept ~20-30% below preoperative baseline)

## Operative Anatomy

### Scalp Layers

Five concentric layers over the scalp: skin, subcutaneous tissue, galea aponeurotica, loose connective tissue, and pericranium.

### Temporal Region

The temporoparietal fascia extends over the temporal area as part of the galea. The temporal fascia splits into deep and superficial laminae involving the superficial temporal fat pad, adhering to the superior margin of the zygomatic arch. The **frontotemporal branches of the facial nerve** are found in the fatty-fibrous tissue in front of the inferior temporal septum — preservation requires interfascial or subfascial dissection.

## Steps of the Operation

### 1. Positioning

- Supine, knees flexed, head of table elevated 15-20 degrees
- [[skull-clamp-placement|Skull clamp]]: dual pins along contralateral superior temporal line, single pin on ipsilateral mastoid bone
- Head turned 20-45 degrees away from approach side, moderately hyperextended
- **Key principle:** *The closer the pathology to the midline and the further anteriorly the lesion is located, the less the head is turned* (~30° for AComA, ~45° for MCA aneurysm)
- Malar eminence must be highest point on the head

### 2. Skin Incision

- Begins **1 cm anterior to the tragus** at the level of the zygoma
- Initial straight line perpendicular to the superior temporal line
- Proceeds behind the hairline to midline or contralateral mid-pupillary line
- Hashed line connecting incision ends should remain within 1 cm of the keyhole
- **Do NOT extend below the zygoma** — risk to facial nerve

### 3. Scalp Flap and Soft Tissue Dissection

- Incise through galea to pericranium level
- [[hemostasis|Hemostasis]] with Raney clips or bipolar electrocautery
- At the superior temporal line: periosteal elevator under subcutaneous tissue to protect STA and temporalis
- Identify STA bifurcation — coagulate and divide frontal branch, **preserve parietal branch** (important if bypass contemplated)
- **Single myocutaneous layer flap** (Cohen-Gadol preference) — no muscle cuff left behind along superior temporal line
- Rolled gauze underneath flap to prevent kinking and ischemic damage

### 4. Temporalis Muscle

- Incise with monopolar electrocautery in two directions: along superior temporal line and inferior aspect
- Reflect anteroinferiorly, **preserve deep temporalis fascia** (avoid monopolar on this layer — contains neurovascular supply)
- Secure with fishhooks over the zygoma
- Expose frontal process of zygoma anterior to keyhole

### 5. Craniotomy

- **Single burr hole** just inferior to most exposed posterior aspect of superior temporal line
- Dissect dura from inner skull with #3 Penfield dissector
- **4-step craniotome technique**: first bony cut (#1), turn drill 180° at pterion (#2), remove and reposition (#3), second osteotomy (#4)
- Extent varies by pathology: larger for AComA, subfrontal tumors require more medial extension

### 6. Sphenoid Ridge Removal (Extended Pterional)

- Disconnect bone flap from lateral sphenoid wing with B1 bit
- Strip dura from orbital roof with Penfield #1
- **Aggressively remove** lateral and mid sphenoid ridge — rongeur first, then air drill with side-cutting burr
- Drill orbital roof flat and supraorbital edge of craniotomy
- These maneuvers define the **"extended" pterional craniotomy** — critical for unobstructed subfrontal view
- Three dural tack-up sutures around craniotomy edges

### 7. Dural Opening

- **Curvilinear incision**, reflected anteriorly
- Dural retraction sutures placed as close to brain as possible to mobilize dura and muscle away from subfrontal route
- May secure dural flap to myocutaneous flap with sutures

### 8. Closure

- **Dura**: approximated primarily; watertight closure NOT required for supratentorial craniotomies unless ventricle entered or hydrocephalus/increased CSF pressure suspected
- **Bone flap**: reattached with at least **three fixation miniplates**
- Burr hole behind hairline preserves bone around keyhole, minimizes cosmetic deformity
- **Temporalis fascia**: reattached at fascial cuff to superior temporal line (posteriorly only for single-layer flap); muscle not included, fascia gently approximated to minimize jaw discomfort
- **No subgaleal drain** routinely (unless hemostasis problematic)

## Interfascial and Subfascial Techniques

### Interfascial Technique

~4 cm above the lateral orbital rim, incise through the superficial layer of temporal fascia at the upper edge of the interfascial fat pad. This mobilizes the superficial layer with facial nerve branches on its outer surface along with the scalp flap. The safe incision avoids transecting frontalis nerve branches.

### Subfascial Technique

Elevates the deep surface of temporal fascia from the outer surface of temporalis muscle in continuity with frontal pericranium. Superficial temporalis fascia, fat pad, and deep temporalis fascia all mobilized with the scalp flap.

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- The *extended pterional* with sphenoid wing + orbital roof resection provides unobstructed working angles toward the parasellar area
- Orbital roof osteotomy mimics some advantages of [[orbitozygomatic-craniotomy]] with less cosmetic deformity
- **DANGER:** Orbital and sphenoid wing meningiomas cause hyperostosis. Any intentional fracture over the lateral sphenoid wing during bone flap elevation can unintentionally **extend to the optic canal and lead to blindness**
- NOT for fibrous superior petroclival meningiomas — [[anterior-petrosectomy|petrosal osteotomies]] provide safer, more expanded access
