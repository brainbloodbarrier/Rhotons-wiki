# Operating Room Setup and Workflow

*Principles of Cranial Surgery — Operating Room Setup and Workflow*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Captain of the Ship

The surgeon is **"the captain of the ship"** — responsible for all actions and workflow in the OR. Leads by example.

> *"The factor that distinguishes great from average surgeons is the ability to succeed in the face of adversity, technical difficulty, or crisis while unifying the team to secure an excellent outcome."*

### Crisis readiness

- **Anticipate** technical difficulty and prepare
- **Decision-making must be flexible** — accommodate intraoperative findings
- **The pathology dictates the plan** — not the surgeon's agenda
- **Periodically step back** — microscopes magnify errors too; don't miss the forest for the trees
- **Scrutinize surgical intuition** — don't dismiss it, but test it against the findings

## OR Layout — General

| Role | Position |
|---|---|
| Surgeon | Across from nurse; seated for microsurgery |
| Surgical technician/nurse | Across the patient from surgeon — seamless exchange |
| Anesthesiologist | Head of table (or foot if room constraints demand) |
| Surgical assistant (resident/fellow) | Opposite end of table from anesthesia |
| Circulating nurse(s) | Free movement around room |
| Mayo stand | Organized for fluid instrument exchange |
| Monitors | Multiple — assistants must see operative field |

### Consistency matters

**Experienced tech's anticipation of the next maneuver is invaluable — only possible if the same staff operate with the same surgeon consistently.**

### Approach-specific layouts

- **Pterional:** mirror of retromastoid; see [[pterional-craniotomy]]
- **Retromastoid:** opposite mirror; see [[retromastoid-craniotomy]]
- **Left-handed surgeon:** fully mirrored setup
- **Percutaneous trigeminal rhizotomy:** X-ray tech + X-ray machine + stimulator integrated; anesthesiologist near airway
- **Endoscopic transnasal:** dedicated monitors for surgeon AND assistant

### Dedicated space per team member

- Enhances workflow, allows unobstructed circulating movement
- **Roaming visitors compromise workflow and raise infection risk** — assign spots

## The Team

- Surgical: attending + resident/fellow/students
- Anesthesia: attending + resident/fellow + CRNA + anesthesia tech
- Nursing: 1-2 circulating + surgical tech
- Accessory: neurophysiological monitoring tech, X-ray tech, visitors

## Pre-Surgery Workflow (Cohen-Gadol)

1. **Arrive after anesthesia induction**
2. **Position the patient** and **mark the incision** (see [[patient-positioning]], [[scalp-incisions]])
3. **Team brief:** walk through operative steps and anesthetic needs with full team while wound is prepped and draped
4. Review **preoperative imaging and plan** with the team
5. Confirm **special supplies** (e.g., femoral artery sheath for intraop angiography in AVMs) **before patient enters the room**
6. **Check microscope balance** and **mouthswitch** height/config — see [[microscope-and-mouthpiece]]

**Standardize and simplify** the OR setup — inconsistent requests confuse the staff.

## Marking the Incision

1. Immobilize head in skull clamp — see [[skull-clamp-placement]]
2. **Neuronavigation** for incision planning
3. **Always verify with superficial landmarks** (ear, zygoma, sagittal suture, inion, superior nuchal line) — navigation errors are catastrophic
4. **Clip (not shave)** a hair strip around the incision — no evidence that extended shaving reduces infection

## Skin Prep and Drape

- Scrub with **alcohol first, then ChloraPrep** (rapid-acting, persistent)
- **Lidocaine + epinephrine** subcutaneous injection along the incision line — reduces bleeding, may precondition for postoperative pain control
- **Reprep with ChloraPrep**, wait until fully dry
- Standard sterile drape
- If ventriculostomy or drain anticipated, **prep tunneling area** adjacent to incision — see [[external-ventricular-drain]]

## Intraoperative Workflow

- **Move quickly through exposure/craniotomy** — save energy for intradural critical steps
- **Think ahead** — request the next instrument **before** you need it; it arrives ready
- **Receive instruments without looking up** — eyes stay on the field
- **Minimize exchanges; multifunctional instruments**
- **Standard, limited repertoire** — see [[instrumentation]] and [[use-and-misuse-of-instruments]]
- **Mouthswitch** is paramount — see [[microscope-and-mouthpiece]] and [[surgeons-philosophy-and-operating-position]]
- **Describe reasoning** to trainees throughout
- **Sit** during microsurgery; ergonomic chair with armrests (wrapped in sterile drapes)

### Intraop angiography

- OR setup must accommodate **fluoroscopy space**
- **Radiolucent head holder NOT required** (Cohen-Gadol) — regular skull clamp allows adequate images

## After the Case — Review

- **Record every surgery** with microscope-integrated camera
- **Review own videos** regularly — most important source of learning
- **Consult colleagues** on complex plans days before — never disappointed
- **Walk trainees through plan** the night before

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **OR setup is crucial** for safety, efficiency, organization, teamwork
- **Captain-of-the-ship** — surgeon owns all details
- **Simplify and standardize** — complexity and inconsistency breed error
- **Think ahead, ask ahead** for the next instrument
- **Eyes on the field** during instrument exchange
- **Record and review** all cases
- **Consult colleagues** for complex plans
