# Surgeon's Philosophy and Operating Position

*Principles of Cranial Surgery — Surgeon's Philosophy and Operating Position*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.09*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

> *"Microsurgery is a dance of elegant individual artistic maneuvers with properly executed transitions. Practice it, master it, and pursue its excellence."*

## Why Body Posture Is a Patient-Safety Variable

Surgeon-level ergonomics are a **direct input to patient outcomes**, not a personal-comfort extra:

- **Fatigue lowers patience and self-control** — the very traits required for calculated, deliberate microsurgical movements during the critical hours.
- **Exhaustion and burnout** unconsciously compromise decision-making long before the surgeon notices.
- **Arm and shoulder stabilization** performed by muscles (instead of an armrest) produces **tremor** that shows up at the instrument tip.
- **Nondeliberate movements** — the half-moves between intentional maneuvers — accumulate over long cases and cause cortical and vascular injuries that were never planned.
- The lineage from posture to outcome is short: **bad posture → muscle fatigue → tremor and imprecision → iatrogenic injury.**

## The Fatigue Problem

Microsurgery is technically demanding, treacherous, and precarious. **The most critical and demanding parts of an operation often occur hours into the case, when fatigue has worn down the surgeon's patience and self-control** — the very qualities needed for calculated microsurgical movements.

**Exhaustion and burnout** unconsciously compromise decision-making and judgment.

## Cohen-Gadol's Six Principles for Composure

1. **Master technical efficiency** to shorten lengthy operations
2. **Move quickly through exposure and less-risky parts** — save energy for critical microsurgical dissection
3. **Use the mouthpiece** as much as possible — minimizes nondeliberate movement
4. Replace hesitant, timid, exploratory maneuvers with **deliberate, efficient, purposeful** ones
5. **Microsurgery is a dance** — individual maneuvers with properly executed transitions
6. **Every next operation must be more proficient than the last** — review all own operative videos

## Sit for Intradural Work

After craniotomy and exposure:

- **Sit** during intradural microsurgery — minimizes fatigue
- Standing causes arm and hand fatigue
- Sitting + armrest = relaxed, steady hands and elimination of nondeliberate dissection maneuvers
- The surgeon's comfort is **the single most overlooked consideration in patient safety**

## The Mouthswitch — Indispensable

Why it matters for fatigue and workflow:

- **Adjust scope without moving hands** from the operative field
- Without it: hand instruments to assistant, readjust scope, return instruments — slow, wastes mental context
- **Loss of dissection context** at every scope adjustment is a primary cause of inefficient long cases
- **"The microscope is a part of and extension of my face — as if I'm wearing glasses"**
- **Scope adjusts to surgeon**, not surgeon to oculars
- Lever-arm technology has a learning curve; non-neutral ocular positions require neck adjustment to keep the mouthpiece in the jaws
- Adjust mouthpiece **before draping the microscope**

See [[microscope-and-mouthpiece]].

## The Armrest — Anti-Tremor

Another major fatigue source: **overuse of arm muscles for stabilization**.

### Ergonomic setup

- **Comfortable operating chair** — delays fatigue
- **Slight recline** — relaxes core muscles
- **Armrest extends just short of the wrist** — allows wrists to dangle over the field
- **Arms slightly extended** (not stretched) at chest level
- Armrest **replaces upper arm and shoulder stabilization** — key to tremor-free dissection
- **Operative field below armrest level** — wrists dangle, maximum maneuverability
- Sterile drape over surgeon's lap
- Minimizes need for fixed retractors — surgeon adjusts hand, not brain

## Workflow Efficiency as Energy Management

The "efficient long case" is not about speed per maneuver — it's about **preserving energy for the critical microsurgical phase**. Practical expressions:

- **Focus mindset sets the workflow** — think about dissection, not exposure. Exposure is a transport mechanism; treat it as a means and move on.
- **Eliminate the scope-handle context switch** — every hand-off of instruments to adjust the microscope with handles is a mini-interruption that forces cognitive re-entry into the dissection.
- **The microscope adjusts to the surgeon, not vice versa** — non-neutral oculars and ongoing manual scope adjustments burn calories and concentration.
- **Video review retrospectively** reveals hours of inefficient motion the surgeon did not perceive live — the correction is made case-by-case.

> *"I consider the microscope a part of and extension of my face, as if I am wearing a pair of glasses."* — Cohen-Gadol

## Operating Room Setup

- **Surgeon sits across the table from the assistant** → efficient instrument transfer while keeping focus on the operative field
- **Anesthesiologist** at head of table
- **Nurse / Mayo stand** positioned for instrument flow from assistant to surgeon
- Standard layouts differ by approach — pterional and retromastoid mirror each other
- See [[operating-room-setup-and-workflow]]

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Surgeon's comfort IS patient safety** — not a luxury
- **Master the mouthpiece** — operative efficiency multiplier
- **Don't let case intensity consume energy reserved for critical steps**
- **Video review** is a lifelong learning practice
- **Efficient workflow** = focus on dissection, not on exposure/scope/instrument exchange
- **"Every next operation more proficient than the last"** — mindset
