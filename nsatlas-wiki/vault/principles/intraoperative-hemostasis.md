# Intraoperative Hemostasis

*Principles of Cranial Surgery — Intraoperative Hemostasis*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.13*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

Successful microsurgery is founded on **strict hemostasis** and a clear, bloodless operative field. **One of the most common reasons for long operations is perpetual attempts at bleeding control caused by poor planning.**

## Prevention First

Hemostasis is best secured through:

- **Devascularizing the tumor base** or interrupting its vascular pedicle early
- Using **natural interhemispheric and arachnoid planes** — minimize pial invasion
- **Avoiding aggressive fixed retraction** — causes brain congestion and venous bleeding (under-recognized)
- **Avoiding aggressive suction on brain surfaces** — causes pial injury and constant oozing
- **Constant low-flow irrigation** — clears field, prevents heat injury from microscope light
- **Preoperative embolization** of highly vascular tumors when feasible

## Pre-Dural-Opening Hemostasis

**Immaculate scalp, bone, epidural, and dural hemostasis BEFORE opening the dura.** Slow blood drainage from epidural space into deep field is a major operative impediment.

- **Pterion area** — stripped dura exposes small intradiploic veins causing slow bleeding; **thrombin-soaked Gelfoam powder** plugs these readily
- **Tack-up stitches** (2-3) — prevent aggressive dural mobilization from producing postoperative epidural hematomas
- **Bone wax** — cancellous bone edges

## Coagulation Methods

### Electrical — Monopolar (Bovie)

- Harvey Cushing introduced Bovie electrocautery to neurosurgery (first use: his own tumor patient)
- Useful for muscle and fascial dissection during craniotomies
- **Not intradural** — indiscriminate current
- Uses: devascularizing bony/dural attachments of superficial meningiomas, hemangioblastomas, hemangiopericytomas; loop electrodes for giant meningiomas (char quickly)
- **CONTRAINDICATED in pacemaker patients** — current interferes with pacing

### Electrical — Bipolar

The **most practical, adaptable, multifunctional** microsurgical instrument.

**Uses beyond coagulation:**

- **Sharp-tip spring action** → arachnoid dissection (Sylvian split)
- **Closed-tip** → fine dissector substitute for mobilizing Sylvian arteries
- **Fine tips** → grab arachnoid encasement of cranial nerves to displace away from tumor — without touching the nerves

**Technique rules:**

- **Don't fully approximate tips** — leave a small tissue bite for coagulation to work
- **Irrigate tips** — minimizes charring and heat dissipation
- **Char = ineffective** — replace or clean
- **High currents** char and avulse without hemostasis
- **Don't force blunt dissection** of thick arachnoid/adherent pia — use microscissors instead

**Most common error:** indiscriminate cortical coagulation of a bleeding source. **Correct approach:** controlled irrigation pinpoints the exact source; apply forceps along the source axis, protecting neighbors from heat injury.

### Cotton Tamponade — The Life-Saving Trick

For cortical ooze OR inadvertent **perforator avulsion** from a parent artery:

- **DO NOT coagulate** on an indispensable parent artery — causes irreversible injury
- **Small cotton pledget** over the tear; gentle tamponade lets the patient's own hemostatic cascade seal the defect
- **Can be left in place** at case end
- This maneuver has been **life-saving** (Cohen-Gadol) for preserving vital parent vessels

### Mechanical

- **Silver / Weck clips** — ligation of large vessels, occipital/superior petrosal sinuses (limited microsurgical use)
- **Bone wax** — seals bleeding cancellous bone; obliterates air sinuses to prevent CSF leak
- Mix with **Gelfoam powder** or **Surgicel fibrillar** for foramen spinosum bleeding

### Chemical

- **Gelfoam powder / Surgifoam + thrombin solution** rolled into small spheres — seals small low-pressure venous channels around epidural space
- **DO NOT pack sinus lumen** — causes dural venous sinus thrombosis
- **Thrombin solution alone** → irrigation for slow venous ooze after glioma resection; safe even in ventricles
- **Surgicel fibrillar** (oxidized regenerated cellulose) — venous sinus wall defects (overlaid, not intraluminal)
- **Floseal powder** — bone and venous bleeding, especially endonasal endoscopic surgery
- **Fibrin glue** — cavernous sinus bleeding

## Types of Bleeding

### Arterial

- Bipolar coagulation is the traditional workhorse
- **Primary closure with fine sutures** if artery is important and caliber permits
- **Small tear / perforator avulsion in indispensable vessel** → cotton pledget tamponade (see above). Impatience = irreversible injury

### Venous Sinus

**Sinus wall tear:**

- **Small tear** → overlay **Gelfoam sponge or Surgicel fibrillar** on top (NOT intraluminal); scaffolding for hemostatic cascade
- **Large tear** → ideally primary closure with fine sutures, reinforced with **pericranium or muscle**
- Alternatively: **reflect dura over the tear** to reconstruct the sinus roof
- **Cavernous sinus** → fibrin glue injection

### Diffuse Venous (DIC)

- Severe TBI or massive transfusion → coagulopathy
- **Thrombin-soaked Gelfoam** gives transient control
- **Correct the underlying cause** (DIC)
- **Abort the operation** — return in 1-2 weeks for second stage if uncontrollable

### Bone

- **Bone wax** — first-line
- **Floseal** — alternative, especially endonasal endoscopic

## Why Prevention Beats Treatment

Operations lose hours to perpetual bleeding-control cycles that could have been prevented with better planning. The dominant sources of preventable bleeding are:

1. **Aggressive fixed retraction** → venous congestion → cortical bleeding. Dynamic retraction eliminates the cause; see [[brain-mobilization-and-protection]].
2. **Aggressive suction on pial surfaces** → pial tears → relentless oozing.
3. **Forced blunt dissection through thick arachnoid** → perforator avulsions that then need cotton tamponade or worse.
4. **Inadequate pre-dural hemostasis** → epidural blood drains into the deep field throughout the case.
5. **Missed tumor devascularization** → tumor bleeds continuously during piecemeal removal.

The fix is almost always upstream of the bleed: plan the corridor, devascularize early, use natural arachnoid planes, keep retraction dynamic.

## Why Cotton Tamponade Works on Perforators

- **Perforator caliber** is small enough that **the patient's own platelet/clotting cascade can seal it** within minutes if the tear is not enlarged by bipolar current.
- **Bipolar on an indispensable parent vessel** risks stenosis, thrombosis, or wall injury — all irreversible.
- The cotton pledget provides mechanical stasis without thermal or mechanical injury to the vessel wall.
- **Leaving the pledget in place** at case end is acceptable when hemostasis is secure and it sits in a non-critical space.

## The Cortical-Coagulation Error

The single most common hemostasis mistake is **indiscriminate cortical coagulation** of a bleeding point:

- Surgeon panics at a visible bleed → large-arc bipolar applied broadly to cortex.
- Result: pial injury extends, bleeding source still active, heat transmits to adjacent perforators.

Correct approach:

1. **Irrigate** — identifies the exact source (seconds of patience beats minutes of bipolar).
2. **Align bipolar tips along the source axis**, not across cortex.
3. **Tips 0.5–1 mm apart** with a small tissue bite between them — full approximation prevents current from doing the work.
4. **Short bursts at low current** — high current chars without hemostasis.

## The Irrigation Rule

- **Constant low-flow irrigation** clears blood without forceful suction
- Prevents **heat injury** to cortex from microscope light
- Pinpoints the exact bleeding source
- **Use generously**

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Prevention beats treatment** — devascularize early, respect natural planes
- **No aggressive fixed retraction** — brain congestion and venous ooze
- **Bipolar forceps are multifunctional** — forceps, scissors, dissector, coagulator
- **Cotton pledget > bipolar** for perforator avulsion on indispensable vessels
- **Thrombin-Gelfoam over sinus tears**, never intraluminal
- **No monopolar in pacemaker patients**
- **Irrigate bipolar tips** — charring kills coagulation
- **Pterion intradiploic veins** — thrombin-Gelfoam plugs pre-dural opening
- **Bleeding you can't control** — abort and stage
