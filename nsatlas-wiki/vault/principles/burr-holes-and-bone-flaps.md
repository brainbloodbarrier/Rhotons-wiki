# Burr Holes and Bone Flaps

*Principles of Cranial Surgery — Burr Holes and Bone Flaps*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.11*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol / Parish

## The Five Precautions of Burr Hole Creation

Every burr hole must address:

1. **Protection of the dural venous sinuses**
2. **Preservation of dural integrity**
3. **Avoidance of air sinuses** (frontal, ethmoid, mastoid)
4. **Careful handling of lesions infiltrating skull** that obscure epidural landmarks
5. **Smooth, gentle, "effortless" drill use** to avoid vibration transmission to the brain — vibrations cause contusions and subarachnoid hemorrhage

Forceful drilling against the inner cortex, or plunging into dura or brain, causes avoidable cortical injury.

## Number, Size, Location

Factors determining number and size of burr holes:

- Size and location of the craniotomy (over sinuses? over sutures?)
- Nature of the lesion (does it involve bone?)
- Patient's age (young patients — dura is more adherent and more prone to epidural hematoma)
- Overall adherence of dura to inner table

Cohen-Gadol's preferences:

- **Place burr holes behind the hairline** when possible — minimizes cosmetic deformity
- **Generous** burr holes — allow the dural dissector to travel effectively beyond the hole
- **Manipulate dura only underneath** the planned craniotomy borders — separating beyond the edges risks occult epidural hematoma, especially in young patients

## Burr Hole Technique

1. Drill outer table and cancellous bone under ample irrigation
2. **Carefully thin the inner cortical layer** and expose a small island of dura
3. Use **Kerrison rongeur or curette** to remove the thin shell of inner cortex — this **undercuts the inner table**
4. The undercut rim lets the dural dissector (Penfield #3) achieve acute working angles while staying tangential to the inner table — avoiding dural tear
5. Dural dissector can now travel well beyond the burr hole

These maneuvers allow elevation of large bone flaps with only **one or two burr holes**.

## Uneven Skull Thickness — The Trap

The acorn-shaped drill bit can reach dura on the thinner side of the hole while the tip is still working on the thicker adjacent bone — causing cortical or sinus injury.

Key danger zones:

- **Pterional keyhole** — frontal bone is thicker than temporal; acorn tip reaches temporal dura prematurely. Cohen-Gadol prefers a single burr hole just inferior to the superior temporal line
- **Parasagittal** (near superior sagittal sinus) — sinus wall is adjacent to thinner bone
- **Retromastoid** (near transverse/sigmoid) — sinus embeds in inner table
- **Frontal sinus region** (bifrontal craniotomy) — skull is very thick; use B1 bit without footplate parallel to skull base

## Burr Holes Near Dural Venous Sinuses

- Placement over **transverse sinus** is relatively safe — correct footplate tip contour produces a single-piece flap exposing sinus roof
- Fear of sinus injury should NOT deter generous exposure — allows mobilization of sinuses for supracerebellar corridors
- Cohen-Gadol does **NOT routinely place two burr holes flanking the sinus**. A single burr hole + effective dural separation is typically enough
- **Exception: sigmoid sinus** — highly adherent and embedded in mastoid bone, requires extra care
- **Parasagittal craniotomy**: the **last bony cut is performed over the sinus** so the flap can be lifted rapidly if sinus injury occurs

## Handling Adherent Dura

- Gently **toggle the drill footplate** during craniotomy to separate dura with the footplate tip — especially at suture lines
- If dura is torn during burr hole creation, **expand the hole** to expose intact dura as a landmark for continued separation
- If the B1 + footplate drill will not advance smoothly, either the footplate is not tangential to the inner surface or the dura is unduly adherent — **pause and resolve**; never force the drill
- When dura is extremely adherent, use B1 without footplate to create a trough down to the thinned inner table; complete with **fine Kerrison rongeurs** or gentle fracture

## Craniotome Technique (Footplate)

Principles for connecting burr holes with B1 + footplate:

1. **Wide turns only** — the footplate cannot make sharp turns; wide turns minimize dural tear risk
2. **Skull base anatomy matters** — consider lateral sphenoid wing, orbital roof, frontal sinuses, oblique middle fossa floor, foramen magnum
3. **Drill handle perpendicular** to skull surface — keeps the footplate tangential to inner table. Tilted handle = struggle to advance = dural tear

## Vibration — The Silent Complication

With raised ICP, drill vibrations transmit to brain pushed against skull, causing:

- Subdural hematoma
- Subarachnoid hemorrhage
- Cortical contusion (visible reddish bruise directly under the drill site)

Mitigations:

- Smooth, controlled footplate maneuvering
- **Lumbar CSF drainage** dramatically decreases the risk — see [[lumbar-drain]]

## Bleeding Control During Bone Work

- **Bone wax** — stops cancellous bleeding along craniotomy edges
- **Thrombin-soaked gelfoam powder** — epidural venous bleeding
- Tack dura up to craniotomy edges once elevated
- **Tense bluish dura** after flap removal = subdural hematoma from bone work → open immediately and evacuate
- See [[intraoperative-hemostasis]]

## Special Situations

### Bifrontal Craniotomy

- Single burr hole when dura is easily separable
- **B1 without footplate** for the inferior osteotomy parallel to skull base — allows controlled transection of anterior and posterior frontal sinus walls
- Footplate dangerous near frontal sinus because skull is very thick

### Pterional Craniotomy

- Single burr hole inferior to superior temporal line
- See [[pterional-craniotomy]] for 4-step craniotome technique

### Parasagittal Craniotomy

- Final bony cut over the sinus for rapid rescue

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- Consider skull anatomy **before** drilling to prevent dural and cortical injury
- **Uneven skull thickness** (keyhole, parasinus regions) places dura at risk
- **Smooth drill use** — forceful handling = cortical injury from vibration transmission
- **Generous burr holes** with inner-table undercutting let the dissector travel far beyond the hole
- **Never push a drill that isn't advancing** — pause, reassess, resolve
- Lumbar drain reduces vibration-related cortical injury in tight brains
