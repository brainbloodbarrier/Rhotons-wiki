# Suturing and Closure

*Principles of Cranial Surgery — Suturing and Closure*
*DOI:* NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol / Hendricks

## Suture Properties

Every suture selection balances:

- **Filament** (mono vs multi)
- **Capillarity**
- **Diameter (size)**
- **Tensile and knot strength**
- **Memory and elasticity**
- **Tissue reactivity**

**Ideal suture:** max tensile strength, knot security, low capillarity, smoothness, minimal reactivity.

### Monofilament vs Multifilament

| Monofilament | Multifilament (woven) |
|---|---|
| Less tissue trauma | Higher tensile strength |
| Fewer bacteria, lower capillarity | Easier stronger knots |
| Harder to tie, less elastic | More capillarity (bacteria harbor) |
|  | More tissue trauma on passage |

### Absorbable vs Nonabsorbable

**Absorbable biological:** chromic gut, plain gut

**Absorbable synthetic:** Vicryl (polyglactin 910), Vicryl Rapide, coated Vicryl, Monocryl (poliglecaprone 25), PDS II (polydioxanone)

**Nonabsorbable biological:** Perma-hand silk

**Nonabsorbable synthetic:** Mersilene, Ethibond (Dacron), Prolene (polypropylene), Pronova, Ethilon (nylon), Nurolon, stainless steel

## The Needle

### Anatomy

- **Point, body, swage** (suture attachment)
- **Chord length** (straight-line point-to-swage) ≠ **length** (arc)
- **Extent of curvature** = fraction of circle (1/4, 3/8, 1/2, 5/8)

### Shapes

Straight, half-curved (ski), 1/4, 3/8, 1/2 (CT, CT-1, CT-2, CT-3), 5/8, compound curve, canoe.

### Point types

| Type | Use |
|---|---|
| **Taper point** | Soft tissue, subcutaneous, dura — dilates rather than cuts |
| **Taper cutting** | Dense tissue without exceeding suture diameter |
| **Conventional cutting** | Skin closure — sharp apex, max cutting |
| **Reverse cutting** | Skin, tendons — resists deformity, less pull-through |
| **Blunt** | Abdominal organs — rare in neurosurgery |

### Common neurosurgical needles

**Tapered:**

- CT (circle-taper) — 1/2, 40 mm
- CT-1, CT-2, CT-3, CTX, SH series
- BV (blood vessel) 50-3, 75-3, 100-4 — 3/8 circle, 3-5 mm for microvascular

**Cutting:**

- PS-1/2/3, PSL, PSLX (plastic-skin)
- FS-1/2, FSL, FSLX (for-skin)
- P-3

## Needle Driver Technique

- Match driver size to needle and field depth
- **"Palm" the driver** — thumb + index stabilize shaft; 3rd-5th digits wrap for stability
- **Thenar eminence** controls ratchet
- **Load needle 1/4 - 1/2 from swage** (optimally 1/3) — outside this range risks weakening and breakage
- **Castroviejo drivers** for fine sutures (6-0 Prolene)

## Basic Suturing Techniques

### Simple stitch

- **Simple interrupted** — low-tension approximation
- **Simple continuous (running)** — faster, less material
- Needle enters perpendicular, passes through full dermis, exits perpendicular
- **Cohen-Gadol: 3-0 Monocryl continuous for scalp skin closure** — see [[scalp-incisions]]

### Horizontal mattress

- **High tension** or fragile/friable margins (redo operations)
- Cosmetically suboptimal — combine with simple interrupted for cosmesis

### Vertical mattress

- **Better eversion** — good for tenuous tissue
- Deep dermal + epidermal suture in one throw
- **Cohen-Gadol use:** redo craniotomies with marginal skin edges

### Subcuticular

- **Standard for cosmetic skin closure**
- **Interrupted:** inverted technique — knot buried subcutaneously
- **Continuous:** anchor tie or knotless with apex start; uniform-depth bites parallel to incision
- Final exit-stitch or continued into epidermis

## Tissue Approximation — Common Errors

| Error | Problem |
|---|---|
| **Unequal spacing** | Uneven tension across wound |
| **Excessive tension** | Tissue strangulation, ischemia |
| **Skin inversion** | Edges rolled under — impaired epithelialization |
| **Skin overlap** | Folded edges — poor healing, infection risk |

**Correct technique:** even parallel stitches with uniform tension — see [[scalp-incisions]] wound-healing principles.

## Layered Closure of a Cranial Incision

### Fascia (cervicothoracolumbar)

- **0 Vicryl** with CT or CT-1 needle
- **Interrupted**, ~1 cm spacing

### Muscle

- **Close before fascia** to reduce dead space and seroma
- **0 Vicryl**, CT or CT-1
- **Interrupted**, ~2-3 cm spacing
- Muscle approximates poorly — **over-tight = necrosis and severe postop pain**

### Subcutaneous fat

- **2-0 or 3-0 Vicryl interrupted**
- Reduces dead space, assists edge approximation (fat doesn't hold sutures well)

### Galea (essential for cranial surgery)

- **2-0 Vicryl, inverted interrupted**
- **0.5-1 cm spacing**
- **Minimize dense connective/adipose tissue inclusion** — doesn't provide support and threatens skin vascularity and approximation

### Skin — scalp

- **Staples** 0.5-1 cm apart — most common
- Alternative: **running 2-0 or 3-0 Ethilon** with ~1 cm loops
- Alternative: **running Vicryl Rapide** — absorbs rapidly, no removal
- Cohen-Gadol: **3-0 Monocryl continuous** (no removal, pediatric friendly)

### Skin — cervical/thoracic/lumbar

- **Simple running 2-0 Ethilon** removed 7-10 days, OR
- **Running subcuticular 3-0 Monocryl + Dermabond** (common), OR
- **Staples**

## Removal Timing

- **Scalp staples/sutures:** 10-14 days
- **Spinal sutures:** 7-10 days
- **Cohen-Gadol** uses absorbable → no removal visit
- **Remove before tract epithelialization** — epithelized tracts harbor infection

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Monofilament for dirty wounds** (Prolene, Monocryl) — less bacterial harbor
- **Taper needle for dura/subcutaneous**, **cutting for skin**
- **Load needle 1/3 from swage**
- **Inverted buried knots** for subcuticular closure
- **Skin inversion and overlap** are common and avoidable — check every stitch
- **Muscle over-tight = necrosis** — less tension, more spacing
- **Galea inverted interrupted** at 0.5-1 cm — the cranial-closure workhorse
- **Monocryl running** skin closure avoids staple removal, excellent in kids
