# Skull Clamp Placement

*Principles of Cranial Surgery — Skull Clamp Placement*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.07*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol / Hendricks

The Mayfield skull clamp has stood the test of time; its sophistication relies on its simplicity. The initial step is firm application of the **two-point arm** to the cranium *before* applying pressure with the contralateral **single pin**.

## The Three Universal Axioms

1. **Sweatband rule** — keep pins within the "sweatband" region (along superior temporal and superior nuchal lines). This avoids placement within muscle and minimizes slippage
2. **Sagittal-crossing rule** — place the clamp so the opposing arms cross the vertical/sagittal plane defined by the superior sagittal sinus
3. **Bisection rule** — the vertical plane of the single pin should bisect the two-pin arm

Key conceptual phrase: **"the head falls into the pins"** — gravity plus pin pressure stabilize the head in space. Two of three pins below the cranial equator lets gravity assist fixation even if pin pressure is suboptimal.

## Structures to Avoid

**Arterial/nerve:**

- Superficial temporal artery
- Occipital artery
- Supraorbital artery and nerve

**Bone:**

- **Temporal squamosa** — lacks integrity for aggressive pinning
- **Transverse and sigmoid sinuses** — avoid pins overlying these in patients with thin skulls

## Pin Pressure

| Population | Pressure |
|---|---|
| Adults (standard) | 60-80 pounds |
| Adults (awake craniotomy) | up to 80 pounds — ensures fixation during agitation |
| Pediatric >3 years | 30-40 pounds (Mayfield Infinity horseshoe + clamp) |
| Pediatric <3 years | **CONTRAINDICATED** — risk of skull fracture |

Each lateral line on the torque knob = 20 pounds.

## Placement Patterns by Approach

### Pterional Craniotomy — see [[pterional-craniotomy]]

- Double-pin arm **ipsilateral** to pterion
- One pin on mastoid process, the other superolateral to inion
- Contralateral single pin on superior temporal line
- **Two pins below cranial equator** → gravity-stabilized
- Single pin placed lateral to supraorbital artery/nerve

### Temporal / Subtemporal — see [[temporal-subtemporal-craniotomy]]

- Head turned contralaterally, ipsilateral shoulder on gel rest
- Single pin: **ipsilateral supraorbital region**, at least 1 cm superior to eyebrow and lateral to supraorbital notch
- Double-pin arm: posterior, just superior to nuchal line — one pin adjacent to inion, the other lateral
- Danger: single pin slippage into orbital region or frontal sinus

### Frontal Craniotomy

- Double-pin arm along posterior superior temporal line
- Contralateral single pin superoposterior to pinna, posterior margin of temporal fossa — avoid temporalis
- All three pins below cranial equator → gravity assist
- Neutral anatomic posture for interhemispheric; lateral positioning uses gravity retraction

### Parietal Craniotomy

- All three pins **above the ears**
- Single pin in thin posterior temporalis superior to ipsilateral ear
- Double-pin arm both pins in axial plane superior to contralateral pinna
- **Hazard:** clamp arms contacting face — particularly nose (compression from swelling). Confirm clearance; pad the nose before draping

### Bifrontal Craniotomy — see [[bifrontal-craniotomy]]

- Vertically-rotated double-pin arm, both pins above ear
- Contralateral single pin superoposterior to pinna
- Vertical rotation provides draping space for the large incision
- Poor implantation → posterior slide → scalp retraction complicating closure

### Retrosigmoid (Retromastoid) — see [[retromastoid-craniotomy]]

- All three pins **superior to ears**
- Single pin above superior temporal line, along hairline (clamp rotated to avoid forehead pinning — cosmetic)
- Double-pin arm parallel and just posterior to superior temporal line

### Suboccipital — see [[suboccipital-craniotomy]]

- Pins superior to pinna bilaterally, slightly anterior to facilitate neck flexion
- At or above superior temporal line to avoid temporalis
- Watch face clearance (same hazard as parietal)

### Large / Trauma Scalp Flaps

- Use pterional pattern
- Single pin on **ipsilateral mastoid** to keep scalp exposure free of pin interference
- Double-pin arm along contralateral superior temporal line

### Cervical / Prone Procedures

- All three pins along superior temporal line (avoid temporalis)
- **Forceful overflexion** of neck causes fixation failure — avoid

## Complication Avoidance — Summary

1. Pins near vertex (outside sweatband) increase dislodgment risk. If forced outside sweatband, keep **≥2 pins within/below** sweatband
2. Single-pin vertical plane must **bisect** double-pin arm
3. Avoid temporalis / suboccipital muscle — scalp thickness at these sites risks **pressure necrosis** at pin shoulders
4. Respect the 80-pound ceiling (adults) / 30-40 pound ceiling (pediatric >3 yr)

## Pediatric Considerations

- <3 years: skull clamp **contraindicated**
- 3+ years: Mayfield Infinity (horseshoe + clamp combination) reduces required force
- Lower pin pressures (30-40 lb) to avoid depressed fracture

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Sweatband region** (superior temporal line + superior nuchal line) maximizes pin safety
- **Never pin through temporalis or suboccipital muscle** — thick scalp at these sites causes pressure necrosis and inadequate fixation
- **Skull clamp contraindicated under age 3**
- "Head falls into the pins" — position first, then clamp
- Single-pin plane bisects double-pin arm — non-negotiable
- In awake craniotomies use the full 80-lb ceiling for agitation resistance
