# Lumbar Drain

*Principles of Cranial Surgery — Lumbar Drain*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.16*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Applications

- Decompression of CSF spaces (brain relaxation)
- Management of intracranial hypertension
- Repair of CSF leak / fistula
- **One-time lumbar puncture** — diagnostic or short-term drainage (e.g., ~35 cc CSF before retromastoid craniotomy)

Cohen-Gadol has a **low threshold for intraoperative lumbar drainage** to achieve brain relaxation.

## Indications — Pre-Craniotomy Placement

Use when CSF cisterns are not accessed early and immediate brain mobilization is necessary, or when the tumor has partially filled cisterns:

1. Subtemporal approaches, including extradural middle fossa osteotomy and anterior petrosectomy
2. Interhemispheric dissection without early access to the ventricle
3. Extradural clinoidectomy
4. Supracerebellar trajectories
5. Bifrontal basal approaches
6. Retromastoid and subfrontal trajectories with raised ICP

Liberal use for:

- Deep meningiomas without ready cistern access (parafalcine, large parasagittal)
- Tumors filling basal cisterns (large clinoidal, tuberculum sellae) — enables early base devascularization

## Contraindications / Cautions

- **Obstructive hydrocephalus** — contraindicated
- **Tumor size and moderate midline shift** are NOT prohibitive if CSF spaces are communicating
- **Simultaneous EVD + lumbar drain** — AVOID; dramatic CSF dynamic shifts → pneumocephalus and neurologic deterioration
- **Herniation risk**: rare but real; never witnessed by Cohen-Gadol, reported by others with overzealous drainage

## Intraoperative Volume Management

- **Open at dural opening** (not earlier) in large-mass-effect tumors — minimizes herniation syndrome during craniotomy
- **10-20 cc aliquots**, max **60 cc total** during dural opening + procedure
- Once CSF cisterns are reached → **clamp for rest of operation** (avoid symptomatic pneumocephalus)
- Remove at end of case **UNLESS** air sinuses violated (CSF fistula risk)

## Postoperative Use (Skull Base CSF Leak Prevention)

- **~48 hours** of drainage after large dural defects joining nasal sinuses (expanded endonasal, bifrontal transcribriform, transtuberular osteotomies)

### DANGER — the pneumocephalus cascade

If postop CT shows **non-resolving pneumocephalus**:

- **Suspect a large leak**
- Continuing lumbar drainage **"reverse-suctions" air through the nose** → dramatic worsening → acute neurologic deterioration + infection
- **Repeat operation** with careful dural closure and skull base reconstruction (vascularized flap) is required

## Technique

### Equipment

- **14-gauge Tuohy needle**
- Catheter over guidewire
- Drainage system (Integra bag with 5-75 mmH₂O pressure transducer)

### Positioning

- **Lateral decubitus, fetal position** — expands interlaminar space
- **Torso NOT rotated** — rotation confuses midline reference in coronal plane
- Palpate **iliac crest** → estimate L4-5 interspace

### Needle insertion

- **Midline, interspinous, bevel cephalad**
- Slight cephalad angulation, follow spinous process contour
- **"Walk"** off lamina with fine adjustments until needle falls into interlaminar space
- **Paraspinal technique** (angled lateral-to-medial) as alternative

### Cannulation failures — four causes

1. **Narrow interlaminar space** (degenerative change in elderly) — spinous processes deflect the tip without the operator knowing
2. **Off-midline tip** — slight torso rotation disorients operator
3. **Obese patients** — longer needle required
4. **Decompressed sac** from prior attempts — cannot be reliably entered

Resistance changes **after interlaminar ligament** — cue to stop short of sac.

### Catheter threading

- **Rotate bevel cranially** once sac is entered and CSF obtained
- **Try catheter alone first**; if resistance → use catheter over wire
- If **catheter won't advance**:
  - Tip too deep → slightly withdraw (posterior vertebral body blocks progress)
  - Wrong angle → withdraw and reposition
- **Advance to T12-L1** (4-5 cm beyond needle tip)
- Remove wire and needle
- **Secure with tape**, sterile occlusive dressing, connect to drainage bag

### Anti-kinking

- **Two sponges straddling the catheter at skin exit** held with Tegaderm
- If drainage stops in supine position → suspect kinking at skin exit → pad more

## Alternative — Needle Only (No Catheter)

- For patients in **lateral position during surgery**
- Insert Tuohy needle, tape to skin, connect IV tubing directly to hub
- **Large blankets straddle the needle** to prevent intraop displacement

## Drain Management — Two Strategies

### Volume-based (preferred)

- Drain **8-15 cc per hour**
- Intermittent clamping maintains the volume

### Pressure-based

- Set drain at a specific level above tragus or shoulder
- CSF drains only when CSF pressure exceeds leveled pressure
- **Clamp when patient is out of bed**; re-level with position
- **Labor-intensive, error-prone** for nursing staff

## Complications — Recognition Required

- **Tension pneumocephalus**
- **Transtentorial herniation**
- **Subdural or intracerebral hematoma**
- **Chiari-like tonsillar descent** and **cerebral settling** from intracranial hypotension

These cause acute neurologic deterioration — recognize symptoms immediately.

## Long-Term Use

- Cohen-Gadol has left lumbar drains in place **up to 10 days** without issue
- **Antibiotics continued** while drain in place
- **Accidental disconnection on floor**: clean ends with Betadine, reconnect — don't replace the entire system

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Low threshold** for intraop lumbar drainage in skull base and interhemispheric procedures
- **Contraindication:** obstructive hydrocephalus
- **Don't combine EVD + lumbar drain simultaneously**
- **Max 60 cc** during case; then clamp
- **Non-resolving pneumocephalus** = reoperate; do NOT continue drainage (worsens)
- **Over- and underdrainage** can be fatal — recognize symptoms fast
- **Volume-based drainage** (8-15 cc/hr) > pressure-leveling
- **Kinking at skin exit** — pad with sponges
