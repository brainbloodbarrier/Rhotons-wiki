# Use and Misuse of Instruments

*Principles of Cranial Surgery — Use and Misuse of Instruments*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.06*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

> *"It is ultimately the handling of the instrument that defines surgical finesse, craft, or mastery."*

The notion that a surgeon should operate "in the most comfortable manner" should be **challenged**. Instruments must be selected and used to **protect normal structures and handle pathology thoroughly and efficiently** — not to accommodate surgeon comfort. This topic is underrepresented in textbooks and deserves much attention.

## Four Guiding Principles

1. **Shorter, finer, sharper** instruments = more precise and less traumatic
2. **Minimalism** — clutter hurts efficiency and confuses assistants; one instrument executes many maneuvers consecutively
3. **Understand the reason behind every design feature** — choice + application determines outcome
4. **Refine direction, force, and transitional motion** with experience

The instrument is an **extension of the hand**. Ambidexterity, flexible angles, and gentle force produce precise manipulation.

## Scalpel — Most Common Unintentional Misuse

- **Belly of the blade**, not the tip, makes the incision
- **Blade perpendicular** to skin (90°)
- **Tilting the blade** → overlapping edges at closure, impaired reepithelialization, more scarring and infection

## Drill Use

### Core tenets

- **Paintbrush touch** over cranial nerves
- **Sharp bit** — replace as needed to avoid forceful, blunt drilling
- **Smooth, systematic** motions
- **Loose objects (cottonoid patties)** within drill range become projectiles
- **Sharp outer-table edges** can cause the drill to "jump" or slide — stabilize both hands

### Plunging prevention

**The most important risk** — usually occurs with **one-handed drilling**.

- **One hand braces** the drill shaft; the other stabilizes at the side
- **Perforator clutch bits** have spring auto-stop on dura — BUT unreliable on uneven surfaces → Cohen-Gadol has **abandoned perforators** in favor of **acorn-shaped bits** (more controlled)

### Minimize drilling time

- Once inner table is breached, switch to **curette + thin-footplate Kerrison**
- Drill **vibrations** cause subarachnoid/subdural hematomas in raised ICP
- If dura is adherent OR sinuses are nearby → **multiple burr holes**, not forceful drilling
- **Dura separation with #3 Penfield** — forceful blind motion = dural tear + cortical contusion
- See [[burr-holes-and-bone-flaps]]

## Craniotome / Bone Flap

- **Handle perpendicular to skull** — tilting disengages footplate
- **NOT ADVANCING** = do NOT push; transmission of violent vibrations → SDH / SAH / ICH (common junior-resident mistake)
- **No sharp turns** — wide planned turns only (classic error on the frontal limb of pterional)
- **Constant irrigation**
- **Connect burr holes along outer edge** (not inner) — maximizes exposure for a given hole configuration
- **Thick skulls** (acromegaly) → long B1 or C1 bits to deepen grooves between holes
- **Footplate tangential to inner table** — dural sinuses embed in the inner table; respect the gentle concavity — ignored by juniors → massive hemorrhage
- **Stabilize and rest the hand** during drilling — the most common cause of injury is unsteady hands + forceful handling of a blunt burr
- **#1 Penfield** strips dura off anterior/middle fossa floor before lateral sphenoid wing resection
- **Brain ribbon or suction** retracts dura during drilling/rongeuring

## Bipolar Coagulation of the Dura

- **Dura shrinks** when coagulated
- For **small dural sinus defect bleeding** — minimize bipolar — the defect edges retract and **bleeding worsens**
- **Epidural hemostasis (cut dura edges)** is essential before intradural work — trainees skip this out of enthusiasm

## Dural Retraction — The Underestimated 2-3 mm

- Retention sutures along the **fold closest to the brain/skull base**, NOT along the dural flap edge
- Mobilizes **underlying soft tissue** (temporalis, periorbita in orbitozygomatic)
- **Prerequisite:** generous skull base bone removal

## Bipolar Coagulation — Three Failure Modes

1. **Coagulum / charring** at tips → ineffective
2. **Indiscriminate coagulation** → regional tissue damage
3. **Vessel adherence** to tips → vascular avulsion injury

### Discipline rules

- **1-2 second bursts** only (exception: thick-walled AVM vessels)
- **Do NOT fully close** the tips; **intermittent grasping** during coagulation — minimizes char
- For arterial bleed: **open and close tips on the vessel** with short current intervals; static current = vessel sticks = avulsion
- **Lowest workable current** — insulated tips help
- **Steady minimal irrigation** — never flood, never dry. **Irrigating bipolar forceps** are ideal
- **Spring action for arachnoid** requires adequate opening tension; forceful spread of thick arachnoid = pial injury — **sharp dissection is safest**

See [[intraoperative-hemostasis]].

## Fixed Retractors — Cohen-Gadol's Position

> *"I avoid fixed retractors at all cost."*

Reasons:

- Interfere with microsurgical flow, can dislodge
- **Restrict instrument working angles**
- **Wide blades** exert fixed undesirable vectors

### When transient fixed retraction is acceptable

- Bypass procedures within the fissure
- Permanent clip application in deep corridors
- As a "third hand" to hold vital structures in view

### Principles when used

- Blades are **holders / protectors**, not retractors
- **Brain herniation around blade edges** = signal to use alternative mobilization (skull base osteotomy, wider arachnoid opening, brain relaxation) — NOT more retraction
- **Position for safe failure** — accidental movement must not cause injury
- **Limited working area exposed** only
- **Intermittent release** to restore perfusion
- See [[brain-mobilization-and-protection]]

## Blunt vs Sharp Dissection

- **Sharp dissection under high magnification** = safest for arachnoid and pial adhesions
- **Blunt is tempting** (less taxing, faster) but dangerous — especially in aneurysm surgery
- **Fine blunt dissectors** are OK to **mobilize** arachnoid in preparation for sharp transection — protects entangled perforators
- **Microscissor tip always in view** — blind sharp cuts = catastrophe
- **Inferior blade** elevates arachnoid away from vessels before completing the cut

## Ultrasonic Aspiration

- **Can avulse, penetrate, shred** vessel walls if used outside the tumor capsule
- Vibrations can disrupt arteries at some distance from the tip
- The claim that ultrasonic aspirators "leave vessels untouched" is **unfounded**

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Lifelong video review** of own operations is the single best source of instrument-use improvement (Cohen-Gadol)
- **Misuse is underreported and underappreciated**
- **Sharp beats blunt** for microsurgery — always
- **No static bipolar on a vessel** — pulsed grasping or avulsion injury
- **No one-handed drilling** — plunge risk
- **No perforator bits** over sinuses (Cohen-Gadol) — acorn bit instead
- **No fixed retractor vs tight brain** — use bone work, arachnoid dissection, and brain relaxation first
- **Belly of the scalpel, blade perpendicular, no tilt**
- **Retention sutures on the base of the dura**, not the edge — 2-3 mm of exposure gained
