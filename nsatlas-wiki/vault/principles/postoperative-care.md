# Postoperative Care

*Principles of Cranial Surgery — Postoperative Care*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.25*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol / Hendricks

## Immediate Postoperative Management

- Most complications present in the **first hours post-op**
- PACU or neurosurgical ICU
- **Extubate immediately** when safe → enables thorough evaluation
- **Interval protocolled neurologic exam** is the cornerstone
- **Multisystem physical** to confirm resuscitation adequacy
- **Vigilance for early signs:** impaired consciousness, seizure, stroke, hematoma with focal signs
- **Routine postop CT is debated** — questionable benefit vs cost (Fontes et al. 2014)

## Pain Control — Multimodal

~80% of patients experience pain. **Multimodal regimens** give better control with lower opioid exposure.

### Agent categories

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| IV opioids | Morphine 1-2 mg q2-4h PRN, fentanyl 12.5-50 µg q1-2h PRN, hydromorphone 0.5-1 mg q1-4h PRN |
| Oral opioids | Oxycodone 5-20 mg q3-6h, hydrocodone/APAP 5/325 q6-8h |
| NSAIDs | Ibuprofen 600-800 mg PO q8h, ketorolac 15 mg IV q6h or 10 mg PO q6h |
| Acetaminophen | 500-1000 mg q4-6h, max 4000 mg/day |
| Muscle relaxants | Methocarbamol, diazepam, baclofen, tizanidine |
| Steroids | Dexamethasone 4 mg PO q6h or Medrol dose pack |
| PCA | Rarely needed for cranial surgery |

**Ketorolac does NOT increase postoperative bleeding** (27-RCT meta-analysis).

### Penn Neurosurgery ERAS Protocol

Three phases:

- **Preop:** education/text reminders, surgical site education, nutrition optimization, diabetes management, smoking cessation, chronic opioid screen, OSA screen, discharge planning
- **Periop:** metabolism management, multimodal analgesia, safety checklist, early mobilization, wound care
- **Postop:** team communication, wound care, post-acute triage pathway

**Tiered pain management** across: Preop Clinic → Preop Holding → Intraop → PACU/Postop Floor → Home. Medications include gabapentin, NSAIDs, opioids, acetaminophen, bupivacaine local, dexamethasone, muscle relaxants.

**Results:** lower opioid use both intraop and for 1 month post-op; improved ambulation.

## Hypertension Control

Critical for AVM, unsecured aneurysm, post-tumor resection.

- **Prevent rapid fluctuations**; often need **tight systolic range**
- Agents: **Labetalol** 10-20 mg IV push q30-60 min, **hydralazine** 10 mg IV push q30-60 min, **nicardipine infusion** (titratable)
- **Arterial line** for continuous feedback during tight control

## Nausea and Vomiting

~50% after neurosurgery.

- **Supratentorial:** ondansetron 4-8 mg q6h (caution: serotonin syndrome), promethazine 6.25-25 mg q6-8h (sedating), prochlorperazine, metoclopramide, haloperidol, diphenhydramine
- **Infratentorial (vestibular nerve manipulation):** meclizine 6.25-12.5 mg PO q4h, diazepam, scopolamine patch

## Shivering

~30% of patients. **200-400% increase in oxygen consumption** in hypothermic shivering.

- **Warm patient to normothermia** (blankets, forced-air warmer)
- **Meperidine 25 mg q3-4h** PRN
- **Low-dose propofol** in intubated patients

## GI Prophylaxis

- **Famotidine 20 mg IV/PO BID** vs **pantoprazole 20 mg PO QD** — equal efficacy
- **Famotidine → 17% thrombocytopenia**; pantoprazole → 4%
- Use **PPI** in clinically significant preop thrombocytopenia

## Thromboembolism Prophylaxis

- **Compression stockings + prophylactic anticoagulation** > stockings alone (Agnelli et al.)
- **Enoxaparin 40 mg daily** — standard dose
- **Timing 24 h postop** typically; delay to POD 2+ for:
  - Subtotally resected tumor (schwannoma, glioma)
  - Posterior fossa surgery
  - High-volume blood-loss deformity surgery

## ICU Admission Triggers

Standard ICU after:

1. Most craniotomies for lesion resection
2. Aneurysm clipping
3. Revascularization
4. Craniofacial
5. Transsphenoidal
6. CEA
7. Major spine with long duration or high blood loss
8. Endovascular
9. Any procedure with complicating comorbidity or intraop complication

**Delayed ICU admission does NOT worsen outcomes** — stepwise triage is acceptable.

### Neurocritical parameters to monitor

- Clinical exam; systemic hemodynamics
- **Cerebrovascular autoregulation**
- **ICP, CPP, CBF**
- Cerebral metabolism
- Nutrition and glucose
- Electrophysiology
- Hemostasis
- Temperature and inflammation

## Procedure-Specific Postoperative Care

### Aneurysm Clipping (elective)

- ICU overnight
- **Systolic BP <160 mm Hg** (liberal since aneurysm secured); up to <200 mm Hg if higher vasospasm risk
- Cranial dressing off **POD 1**; subgaleal drain out POD 1
- **Absorbable sutures** → no removal visit; 1-month follow-up
- **Ruptured SAH patients** — much longer ICU: vasospasm monitoring, electrolytes, BP
- **Failed EVD wean** → ventriculoperitoneal shunt

### AVM Resection

- **Normotension to mild-moderate hypotension** to minimize hemorrhage and perfusion breakthrough
- **Difficult case → keep intubated + sedated overnight** to avoid coughing-induced BP spikes
- **Normal perfusion pressure breakthrough** — edema, hyperemia, perinidal hemorrhage from chronic dilation + lost autoregulation
- **Giant AVMs:** systolic BP **<110 mm Hg** for days; intubated 24-48 h
- **Mandatory postop angiogram** — small residuals can appear despite clean intraop angio
- See [[brain-mobilization-and-protection]], [[preoperative-evaluation]]

### Brain Tumor Resection

- Immediate neuro exam in OR
- **Difficult arousal or unexpected focal deficit → CT**
- Diet: ice chips → advance as tolerated
- **Hourly vital/neuro checks** overnight for selected cases; otherwise q2-4h
- Transfer to floor **POD 1** typically
- **Antibiotics off 24 h** unless indicated
- **Steroids:** continue dexamethasone 4 mg q6h; individualized taper over **1-2 weeks starting POD 4-5**
- **Prophylactic AEDs:** stop 1 week postop if no seizure
- **MRI within 48 h** for residual tumor
- PT/OT consults
- Discharge **~POD 3**

### Carotid Endarterectomy

- Institutional variability (ICU vs floor if stable)
- **Systolic BP <140 mm Hg** to prevent hyperperfusion injury and hematoma
- **Neck circumference monitoring** — drain output informative
- **Compressive hematoma → bedside re-exploration** (tracheal decompression emergency)
- Most hematomas: venous ooze / inadvertent IJ puncture
- **Lifelong ASA 81 mg** + **clopidogrel 75 mg × 1 month** in select
- **Statin** per standard of care
- Some: preop-discharge carotid duplex to exclude occlusion

### Dural AVF Obliteration

- **Systolic BP <160 mm Hg × 24 h**
- **Postop diagnostic angiogram mandatory** — hemodynamic changes risk hemorrhage

### Revascularization (Bypass)

- ICU 24-48 h; watch for vasospasm, ischemia
- **Noncompressive head bandage**
- **Avoid sleeping on anastomosis side** for first few days
- **Antiplatelets essential:**
  - Standard: **ASA 325 mg daily** starting 6 h postop or POD 1 morning
  - Higher thromboembolic risk (flow reversal): add **clopidogrel**
- **Patient-specific BP goals** — baseline higher in revascularization candidates; universal parameters risk hypoperfusion
- **Postop angiography** for patency; **CT perfusion** optional reference for later decline
- **Hyperperfusion** → cortical dysfunction
- **Hypoperfusion** (especially Moyamoya) → temporary neuro decline even without imaging findings

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Immediate postop hours are the most critical**
- **Multimodal analgesia** > opioid monotherapy; **ketorolac is safe**
- **Tight BP control** for AVM/unsecured aneurysm/post-tumor
- **Normothermia** — shivering dramatically raises O₂ consumption
- **PPI over H2 blocker** in thrombocytopenia
- **Enoxaparin within 24 h** except subtotal tumor, posterior fossa, high-blood-loss spine
- **Postop angiogram** after AVM or dAVF — even when intraop looked clean
- **Mandatory postop CT** after unexpected deficit or difficult arousal
- **Discharge plan starts preop** (ERAS)
