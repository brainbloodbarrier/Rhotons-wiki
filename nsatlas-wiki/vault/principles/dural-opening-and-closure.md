# Dural Opening and Closure

*Principles of Cranial Surgery — Dural Opening and Closure*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Two Principles of Dural Opening

1. **Generous operative corridor** — unrestricted by dural flaps, while protecting venous sinuses and associated veins
2. **Plan the opening with the closure in mind** — saves time and avoids unexpected challenges at closure

## Supratentorial Craniotomies

### Opening

- **Curvilinear** whenever possible — easiest to close
- **Cruciate** for convexity craniotomies when watertight closure is NOT mandatory
- Tailor the incision to the underlying pathology
- **Mobilize the base of the dura** with retention sutures implanted near the brain — sutures along the flap edge do not effectively retract the dura or underlying soft tissue
- Tent up remaining edges to prevent epidural blood drainage into the operative space

### Parasagittal craniotomies

- **U-shaped flap** based on the **sagittal sinus**
- Retraction sutures in the superior falx **rotate the venous sinus laterally** — expands interhemispheric reach
- Lateral positioning + gravity retraction aids hemisphere mobilization

### Reoperations

- Use **limited, pathology-tailored** dural openings — avoid dissecting adherent pia, veins, arteries
- Example: repeat pterional — do **not** reopen the prior curvilinear dural incision; instead incise dura along the frontal base for a subfrontal trajectory. Avoids dissection of adherent dura over lateral frontal lobe
- Microsurgical technique mandatory for dissecting cortical vessels off inner dura

## Infratentorial Craniotomies

- **Watertight closure highly recommended** for posterior fossa tumors and vascular lesions — postoperative CSF pressures and gravity predispose to CSF fistula
- **MVD** (microvascular decompression) is an **exception** — pristine field, low postoperative CSF pressure

### Retromastoid ([[retromastoid-craniotomy]])

- Incise dura **along transverse and sigmoid sinuses**, a few millimeters away from sinus edge
- Leaves most dura over the cerebellum — keeps it hydrated, protects from microscope light — **no retraction needed**
- Retraction sutures along sigmoid sinus dura **rotate the sinus laterally** — expands CPA corridor
- Unlike parasagittal (parasagittal veins prevent this), the retromastoid can safely incise parallel to the sinuses

### Supracerebellar craniotomies

- Midline or paramedian openings
- Retraction sutures along **posterior tentorium** mobilize transverse sinus superiorly — expands supracerebellar angles
- Dura often retracts → **pericranium graft** required for effective closure

## Dural Closure — The Watertight Algorithm

A watertight closure must be done **judiciously** — unnecessary watertight closure wastes operative time. Running sutures are generally best.

### Cohen-Gadol's algorithm

| Situation | Closure |
|---|---|
| Supratentorial craniotomy (general, even with ventricle entry) | **NOT watertight** — low leak rate |
| Supratentorial with air sinuses violated | **Watertight** |
| Posterior fossa tumor/vascular | **Watertight (mandatory)** |
| MVD | **NOT watertight** |

### Technique

- Few stitches to approximate dural edges
- **Gelfoam** over epidural space — scaffolding for scar formation
- Running sutures for watertight when required

## Air Sinus Management

Critical for CSF leak prevention:

- **Mastoid air cells** thoroughly **waxed**
- **Frontal air sinuses** **exenterated** (strip mucosa, obliterate)
- Details in [[retromastoid-craniotomy]] and [[bifrontal-craniotomy]]

## Dural Substitutes — Cohen-Gadol's View

- **Avoids dural substitutes (allografts)** — risk of aseptic and septic inflammation
- **Pericranium** harvested at the **start** of the operation for augmentation
- **Muscle plug** for small defects
- **Fat globules** — very effective for preventing CSF leak when watertight closure is impossible (skull base — see [[anterior-petrosectomy|anterior petrosectomy]])

## Tackup Sutures

- Placed along **craniotomy edges** to prevent epidural fluid collections
- Through **oblique holes** — perpendicular tacking holes complicate needle passage
- Needle transverses **only the outer layer** of dura

## Bone Flap Fixation

- **Plates and screws** — usually 3-point fixation sufficient for simple pterional
- **Keyhole** filled with **cranioplasty** material for cosmesis
- **Screws should NOT violate cortical bone over air sinuses**
- For orbitozygomatic osteotomies, more fixation points needed

## Soft Tissue Closure

- **Temporalis fascia** approximated with absorbable sutures
- **Do NOT include muscle** in the suture line — strangulation causes necrosis and postop discomfort
- **Fat pad reflected into position**, not sutured
- **Galea**: 3-0 absorbable
- **Skin**: 3-0 Monocryl
- Minimize coagulation on scalp edges — necrosis impairs healing, especially for reoperation
- See [[scalp-incisions]] and [[suturing-and-closure]]

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Plan opening with closure in mind**
- Watertight closure done **judiciously** — not reflexively
- **Meticulous closure** dramatically decreases complications
- **Base-of-dura retention sutures** work; edge sutures don't
- **No dural substitute allografts** — pericranium, muscle, fat instead
- **Air sinuses must be sealed** — mastoid wax, frontal exenteration
- Posterior fossa (except MVD) = **watertight, mandatory**
