# Preoperative Evaluation

*Principles of Cranial Surgery — Preoperative Evaluation*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.01*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol / Parish

## Goals

1. **Establish rapport** with patient and family; describe surgery and recovery
2. **History**: medical, surgical, personal, family, social, medications, allergies
3. **Physical exam + labs**; arrange needed investigations and consults
4. **Risk-stratify** using ASA (1 healthy → 6 brain-dead)
5. **Resource planning** for safety and recovery
6. **Informed consent** documented
7. **Anesthesia team communication** — clear pre-brief

Elective patients → preoperative assessment team (internists + family medicine) for anesthesia clearance.

## General History Red Flags

- **Prior anesthesia complications** (airway, IV access, pain)
- **Allergies** (latex, antibiotics, pain meds)
- **Steroid use** — adrenal suppression, hyperglycemia
- **Anticonvulsants** — resistance to nondepolarizing muscle relaxants
- **Anticoagulants / antiplatelets** — see discontinuation table
- **Smoking / alcohol / drug use**
- **Family history** of anesthesia complications or coagulopathy
- **Religious beliefs** (Jehovah's Witness — blood products)

## Airway and Spine

- **Assess airway** (anesthesiologist)
- **Cervical instability / cervical spine lesions** complicate airway
- **Incidental myelopathy on preop exam** → order spine imaging **BEFORE** prone positioning (non-physiologic neck extension = severe cord injury)
- **CN IX, X dysfunction** → high aspiration risk; post-op swallowing evaluation required before feeding
- **Lower CN dysfunction** → may require pre/post-op **gastrostomy** for nutrition and wound healing

## System-Based Evaluation

### Neurologic

- **Depressed LOC** → delayed emergence, aspiration risk, possible overnight intubation, assisted ventilation
- **Preexisting motor deficits** — risk of **life-threatening hyperkalemia with succinylcholine**

### Respiratory

- **PFTs + ABG** for any suspected pulmonary disease
- **Elevated PaCO₂ / low PaO₂** → predicts postop complications → correct preop
- **Significant disease** → plan postoperative mechanical ventilation
- **Home CPAP** → must be available throughout admission
- **Transsphenoidal patients** with CPAP → special arrangements

### Cardiovascular

- **Perioperative beta-blockade** for ischemic heart disease, HF, DM, renal insufficiency, cerebrovascular disease
- **NEVER initiate new beta-blocker** on day of surgery (POISE trial — increased stroke/mortality) — continue if already taking
- **Wait ≥4-6 weeks after MI** (acute: <7 days; recent: 7-30 days) before elective surgery
- **CHF** → mannitol sparingly (volume overload exacerbates failure)
- **Chronic HTN** → CBF autoregulation shifted to higher pressures → poor tolerance for acute hypotension
- **Aneurysm + coarctation + HTN** — known association

### Renal

- **Adjust renally-cleared medications**
- **Volume monitoring** via arterial / CVP catheters
- **Avoid**: NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, aminoglycosides, contrast dye, volume depletion
- **Mannitol contraindicated in anuric patients**
- **Preexisting acidosis** on postop ventilation → monitor for hypercapnia → worsens acidosis → hyperkalemia → circulatory depression

### Hematologic

- **Correct coagulopathy; blood products/factors/platelets available**

#### Antithrombotic discontinuation

| Agent | Stop before surgery |
|---|---|
| Warfarin (Coumadin) | >7-10 days |
| Clopidogrel (Plavix) | 5-7 days |
| Aspirin | 7-10 days |
| NSAIDs | 5 days |

Coordinate with cardiologist for TIA, recent stents, atrial fibrillation — **risk/benefit documented**.

### Endocrine

- **Diabetes**: insulin for strict euglycemia; **hyperglycemia worsens outcome after focal/global ischemia**
- **Metformin, sulfonylureas** → stop 24-48 h preop (long half-lives → hypoglycemia)
- **Cushing's disease**: hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis — correct electrolytes + volume before transsphenoidal
- **Adrenal insufficiency / chronic steroids**: perioperative stress-dose steroids
- **Hyper/hypothyroidism**: make euthyroid (mild-moderate not absolute contraindication)

## Laboratory Standard Set

Most neurosurgical patients need:

- **Hemoglobin/hematocrit**
- **Serum glucose + electrolytes**
- **Coagulation studies**
- **Type and screen** (minor) or **type and cross** (major blood loss expected)
- **Anticonvulsant levels** — aim for **upper end of therapeutic** (perioperative metabolism increases)
- **Pregnancy test** for women of childbearing age with unknown status
- **ECG** for known cardiac disease or risk factors
- **CXR** for pulmonary / cardiac / recent URI
- ASA Task Force: labs/history within **6 months** are acceptable if no substantial change

## Specific Neurosurgical Scenarios

### Intracranial Tumors

- **Preop steroids** for peritumoral edema
- Long-term steroids → HPA axis suppression → **stress-dose coverage**
- **Mannitol** for intraop ICP reduction; **volume monitoring** to prevent hypotension
- **Early lumbar drainage** for large posterior fossa mass — prevents herniation on dural opening — see [[lumbar-drain]]
- **Propofol** provides better brain relaxation than isoflurane/sevoflurane — ask anesthesia for propofol in tight brains, especially posterior fossa
- **Acromegalics**: DM, HTN, cardiomegaly; **difficult intubation**; hypertrophied ligaments complicate radial artery catheterization
- **Informed consent**: bleeding, seizures, stroke, coma, death, hydrocephalus, meningitis, deficits

### Ischemic Cerebrovascular (CEA)

- Multiple comorbidities typical (CAD, HTN, PVD, COPD, DM, renal)
- **NASCET**: MI history, unstable angina, HTN = independent risk factors
- **Aggressive preop BP control → improved outcome**

### Aneurysmal SAH

- **Hunt-Hess grade** correlates with vasospasm, ICP, autoregulation impairment, arrhythmia, MI-like myocardial dysfunction, hypovolemia, hypernatremia
- **Cerebral salt wasting** → monitor for hyponatremia → **hypertonic or isotonic saline correction**
- **ECG changes in 40-60%** (ST, T-wave inversion) — often no cardiac ischemia on workup, adrenergic stress mechanism
- **Postpone repair ONLY if:** hemodynamically unstable, EF <30%, clinical heart failure
- **Pulmonary aspiration risk** from LOC at ictus; neurogenic pulmonary edema compounds
- Associated conditions: HTN, smoking, aortic coarctation, polycystic kidney disease, fibromuscular dysplasia

### Arteriovenous Malformations

- **Massive intraop blood loss possible** — type/cross, generous vascular access
- **Spetzler-Martin grade** predicts surgical risk
- **Post-AVM resection normal perfusion pressure breakthrough** → keep systolic BP **20-30% below baseline** for 2-3 days postop → minimizes hematoma/edema
- **Supratherapeutic anticonvulsant levels** — prevent perioperative seizures → BP spikes

### Posterior Fossa

- **Lateral positioning** required → cardiopulmonary demand
- **Brainstem compression signs** — evaluate preop
- **CN V, IX, X manipulation** + tentorium/petrous dura → transient **asystole or arrhythmia** — precautions ready
- **Electrophysiologic monitoring**: SSEP, MEP, EMG, BAER — coordinate with anesthesia (avoid paralytics during motor monitoring)

### Head Injury

- **Emergency surgery** — limited preop workup
- **ABC first**; avoid secondary injury from hypoxemia, hypercapnia, hypo/hypertension, hyperglycemia, raised ICP, seizures, vasospasm
- **Cervical spine injury** complicates intubation
- **CPP >60 mmHg** target
- **Type and cross** ASAP

### Epilepsy

- **Awake craniotomy** under local + minimal IV sedation (electrocorticography mapping)
- **Phenytoin** → resistance to nondepolarizing NMBs
- **Anticonvulsant levels** carefully adjusted

## Emergency Surgery (Limited Preop)

Minimum set:

- Hematocrit, electrolytes
- Renal function
- ECG

## Other Considerations

- **Prior surgery by another surgeon** → **phone call** (op notes alone are often incomplete/confusing)
- **Prior surgery by same surgeon** → review your own op notes
- **Imaging >3 months old** → repeat (lesion progression alters planning)
- **Review plan + physiologic targets** (BP, volume, burst suppression needs, neuromonitoring) with anesthesia immediately preop
- **Awake craniotomy**: brief anesthesia team a day before
- **Poor nutritional status** → serum prealbumin + nutritionist consult to optimize before surgery

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **ASA classification predicts outcome** — stratify every patient
- **Myelopathy before prone positioning** = imaging required
- **No succinylcholine in preexisting motor deficits** (hyperkalemia)
- **Don't start new beta-blocker on surgery day** (POISE)
- **Mannitol sparingly** in CHF and anuric patients
- **Propofol beats volatiles** for tight brain — posterior fossa especially
- **Anticonvulsant levels at upper therapeutic range** perioperatively
- **SAH + EF<30% or HF** → postpone; otherwise repair
- **AVM post-resection BP 20-30% below baseline** for 2-3 days
- **Phone the prior surgeon** — op notes are often insufficient
