# Techniques of Sylvian Fissure Split

*Principles of Cranial Surgery — Techniques of Sylvian Fissure Split*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v3.ch01.5*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## How Much Fissure?

The extent of dissection depends on:

1. Location and texture of the underlying pathology
2. **Brain compliance** (atrophy, absence of swelling)
3. Surgeon skill with dynamic retraction (avoiding fixed retraction)

| Scenario | Fissure split |
|---|---|
| Large insular tumors, giant MCA aneurysms | **As wide as possible** to peri-insular sulci; posterior extent limited by tumor pole and dominant hemisphere safety |
| Anterior skull base tumors, anterior circulation aneurysms | **Anterior limb only** (cistern just anterior to M1); extend intraop if needed |

**Some degree of atraumatic fissure dissection is mandatory for the lateral subfrontal trajectory.**

## Sylvian Cistern Anatomy

Three components:

- **Fissure** (arachnoid channel)
- **Opercular sulci** (between opposing gyri)
- **Sylvian fossa** (houses MCA, lateral to insula)

### Compartments

- **Anterior (stem):** anterior perforated substance → temporal pole → **Sylvian point** (confluence of ascending, horizontal, posterior rami)
- **Posterior (insulo-opercular)**

### Sylvian point

- Marks the division of the inferior frontal gyrus into **pars orbitalis, pars triangularis, pars opercularis**
- **Proximal fissure** = proximal to Sylvian point (most cases need this)
- **Distal fissure** = distal to Sylvian point

### Three-segment structural division

| Section | Length | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Proximal (sphenoidal)** — A | 3-4 cm over planum polare | Adherent pia, few vessels, gentle microdissection |
| **Middle (insular)** — B | 6-7 cm limen insula → posterior insular point | Less interdigitated sulci, easier split |
| **Posterior (retroinsular)** — C | 4-5 cm, deep, under supramarginal/transverse temporal/parietal | Complex interdigitations — challenging |

### Vallecula

The proximal "valley" houses:

- **ICA bifurcation**
- **Limen insula** (where MCA bifurcates into M1 superior and inferior trunks)
- **Lateral lenticulostriate perforators**
- **Deep Sylvian vein**

Opening the vallecula accesses proximal MCA and ICA bifurcation.

### MCA segments

- **M1** — posterior and parallel to sphenoid ridge (3-4 cm, C/S-shaped in proximal fissure)
- **M2** — on limen insula
- **M3** — spreads over frontotemporoparietal opercula
- **M4** — cortical convexity branches

**Dissect along the inferior and anterior aspect of M1** — preserves lenticulostriates.

## Positioning and Setup

- **Supine**, head rotated **30°** away
- **More than 30°** → temporal opercula rotate into fissure angle, obstructing dissection
- See [[patient-positioning]]
- After dural opening: gentle anterior frontal elevation + arachnoid opening over opticocarotid cisterns → CSF outflow
- **Small cotton ball** under the frontal lobe maintains CSF outflow during fissure dissection
- **Moist Telfa** covers the brain (except peri-insular areas) — heat-shield from microscope

## Vein Preservation Rule

**Dissect ABOVE the superior Sylvian vein** (which lies ~4 mm below the fissure in >80% of hemispheres).

- Arachnoid opened on the **frontal side** of the veins → veins don't cross the fissure when frontal lobe is elevated
- If **two superficial Sylvian veins** exist: **dissect between them** — arachnoid bands are more robust than pia and bridging veins are rare
- Preservation prevents venous infarction — especially critical when Sylvian veins are dominant and parasagittal veins are sparse on angiography

## The Inside-to-Outside Technique (Cohen-Gadol / Yasargil)

### The orange analogy (Yasargil)

> It's hard to separate orange slices from the outside. It's easy to put a finger into the middle of the orange and radially separate the wedges from inside.

### Why inside-to-outside

- Early identification of **MCA branches** as landmarks
- Adjust plane along interdigitating opercula while keeping fissure depth as orientation
- Avoids the "outside-to-inside" error (no landmarks, adherent opercula, frontal lobe retraction)

### Where to start

At the **Sylvian point** — widest arachnoid corridor where pars triangularis naturally retracts superiorly, and the insular apex serves as landmark.

### Technique step-by-step

**Step 1:** Make a **3-mm opening** above and along the superficial Sylvian vein with a **round (beaver) knife**. Incise arachnoid at several points. Extend opening by holding edges with jeweler forceps.

**Step 2:** Disrupt the superficial thick arachnoid over veins with fine forceps or microscissors. Slip a **1×1 mm cotton pledget** between the pial membranes.

**Step 3:** Extend deep — identify **distal MCA branches on the insula** as landmark. Open fissure from **deep-to-superficial, posterior-to-anterior** (inside-to-outside).

**Step 4:** **Planum polare** may be very adherent — use round knife for sharp dissection of pial planes.

### Pledget mechanics

- Gradual gentle compression by **fine suction tube** + **bipolar spread** opens the plane
- Larger pledget replaces smaller as fissure deepens → keeps fissure open without retractors
- **No blood from pial surface** = no bipolar coagulation; gelfoam + cotton pledget instead

### Instrument pattern

- **Straight (non-bayoneted) microscissors** + 5-mm-tip bipolar forceps for deeper dissection
- **Bipolar spreads thin arachnoid, scissors cut thick bands**
- Forceful blunt spreading of thick arachnoid = pial injury → bipolar on pia = more cortical injury
- **Jeweler forceps** grab and separate more superficial thick bands
- **Saline injection** via syringe into Sylvian fossa expands the fossa and reveals arachnoid planes

## Proximal Extension to Opticocarotid Cisterns

- Open vallecula along **M1** → expands corridor to opticocarotid cisterns
- **Posterior frontal operculum** tends to herniate — aggressive retraction causes venous congestion and spontaneous cortical bleeding; avoid
- A **thick arachnoid band with occasional vein** tethers frontal and temporal lobes just before the cisterns — transect
- **"T"-shaped arachnoid incision** over opticocarotid cisterns:
  - **Anterior limb** lateral to optic nerve and over carotid, extended medially to disconnect posterior gyrus rectus from chiasm
  - **Posterior limb** parallel to PComA, connects to medial Sylvian arachnoid opening

## Distal Fissure Dissection

- **Indicated for:** large insular tumors, M2/M3 aneurysms, giant MCA bifurcation aneurysms
- Posterior opercula adherent — **superior temporal gyrus at risk**, especially dominant hemisphere
- **Round arachnoid knife** for adherent pial surfaces
- Gentle dynamic retraction + anterior-to-posterior angles give good exposure of retrosylvian fossa, posterior insular cortex, peri-insular sulci, posterior M2 branches

## Direction — Proximal-to-Distal vs Distal-to-Proximal

**Cohen-Gadol preference: distal-to-proximal (via inside-to-outside).**

- Proximal-to-distal requires significant frontal lobe retraction (M1 identified early, followed distally)
- Distal-to-proximal = **no frontal lobe retraction**
- **But** distal-to-proximal does **NOT give early proximal control** — risky in ruptured aneurysm
- The inside-to-outside variant compromises: enter at Sylvian point deep, identify MCA, then open outward

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Don't dissect proximal-to-distal** — frontal retraction penalty
- **Don't repeat pia strokes** with suction shaft — park it strategically, move only when necessary
- **Closed microscissor tips** = dissector; **jaws** = transector
- **Fissure is oblique, not perpendicular to insula** — as you go posterior-to-anterior, don't violate frontal pial banks
- **Fundamental ingredients** of efficient split: dynamic retraction + sharp dissection + microscope mouthswitch + **inside-to-outside**
- **Cortical malformation** can transform the fissure into a sulcus series — know this preoperatively
- **80° rule** — head rotation ≤ 30° to keep temporal opercula out of the angle
