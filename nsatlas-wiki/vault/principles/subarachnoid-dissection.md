# Subarachnoid Dissection

*Principles of Cranial Surgery — Subarachnoid Dissection*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v3.ch01.4*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol / Weyhenmeyer

> *"Aneurysm surgery is essentially microsurgery of the subarachnoid space."*

## The Subarachnoid Space

- Between **arachnoid mater** (outer) and **pia mater** (inner)
- Pia follows every gyrus and sulcus
- Meningeal dura encloses the arachnoid; small potential **subdural space** between them
- Gyri encroaching on arachnoid create **membranes** that partition the space into **cisternae**

### Cisterns = the map of aneurysm surgery

Each cistern houses specific vessels and nerves:

- **Sylvian cistern** — middle cerebral artery
- **Lamina terminalis cistern** — anterior communicating artery complex
- **Basal cisterns** — circle of Willis
- **Prepontine, cerebellopontine, ambient, quadrigeminal**, etc.

CSF is NOT freely flowing — it traverses **pores and trabeculations** between cisterns. After **aSAH**, these pores become occluded → CSF buildup → hydrocephalus. The cisternal architecture also dictates the microsurgical dissection sequence.

## Microsurgical Instrumentation

**Cohen-Gadol's "special set" philosophy:** master a limited set, use each tool to full potential. As experience grows, the set shrinks.

Core instruments:

- **Bayonetted bipolar forceps** in multiple tip sizes (sharp for dissection, blunt for coagulation)
- **Sharp arachnoid knives**
- **Round arachnoid knife** — splits superficial arachnoid
- **Fine dissectors** (Rhoton series)
- **Microscissors** (straight, curved, angled)
- **Angled, malleable suction devices**
- **Self-retaining retractors** — transient "third hand" during final clip application
- See [[instrumentation]]

## Planning the Dissection Trajectory

Before incising anything:

1. **Plan the subarachnoid trajectory** from cistern to cistern, without pial transgression or significant retraction
2. The **aneurysm location defines the operative path** — each artery has a preferred safe pathway (smooth contour, minimal branching, few perforators)
3. **Never approach the aneurysm directly** — risk of premature rupture
4. **The dome must NOT lie on the dissection path** — increases rupture risk
5. **Perforators obliterate the dissection plane** — plan around them

### Example: MCA preferred pathway

- **Inferior and anterior** MCA — smooth, few perforators → dissect here
- **Superior** MCA — lenticulostriate perforators → violation = postoperative deficit

### Single-lobe artery rule

Most arteries supply **one lobe**. Once identified, the artery can be held in close apposition to its lobe without strain during retraction. Example: an M3 loop may appear to feed both frontal and temporal lobes, but its feeding vessels belong to only one — mobilize the loop accordingly to expand the transsylvian corridor.

## Vascular Control

**Proximal control is the sine qua non of vascular surgery.** Plan its location before opening.

### The ideal

- Temporary clip **just proximal to aneurysm neck**
- Isolates the sac without greatly affecting normal parenchymal flow
- Minimal perforator manipulation

### Multi-feeder aneurysms (AComA / PComA complexes)

**Cohen-Gadol's low-flow strategy:**

- Temporarily occlude **only the dominant feeder** (dominant A1, ICA)
- Allow non-dominant A1 and PComA to supply distal territories for protection
- **All feeders dissected and available** for additional temporary occlusion if needed

### If proximal control fails

- Fall back to more proximal level (more ischemia risk, but necessary)
- **Cervical ICA** preparation for paraclinoid aneurysms
- **Femoral access + balloon-tipped catheter** for basilar bifurcation aneurysms
- **Adenosine-induced transient asystole** (30-60 s) — safe for predissection rupture and giant aneurysms without anatomic proximal control

### Distal control

- **Expose, but don't routinely clip**
- Indications for distal clip ligation:
  1. Overwhelming retrograde bleeding from ruptured aneurysm
  2. Persistent turgor preventing deflation
  3. Trapping for suction decompression
  4. Opening the sac for thrombectomy / coil removal

Distal control occurs naturally during circumdissection — identify all efferent arteries **before final clip ligation**.

## Dissection Technique

### After SAH — clot management

- SAH spoils subarachnoid planes and visualization
- Begin clot evacuation at **known arterial landmarks**, work outward
- **Irrigation + gentle suction** — never forceful
- See [[intraoperative-hemostasis]]

### The arachnoid → vessel sequence

1. **Dynamic retraction** via bipolar spring action + dissector tips — see [[brain-mobilization-and-protection]]
2. **Probe tight spaces** with fine blunt dissectors to find invisible arachnoid planes
3. **Widen natural planes** with spring action of forceps
4. **Microscissors** break remaining bands — bipolar-separate, then cut
5. **CSF egress** — the single most effective brain-mobilization maneuver

### Scissor technique

- **Tip first** — separate the arachnoid from vascular elements
- **Lower jaw lifts** the arachnoid band — ensures no hidden structures in the jaws
- **Blades then transect** the isolated band
- Minimal instrument exchange = operative efficiency

### Bipolar forceps for arachnoid (non-coagulating use)

- Forceps with **added tensile strength during closure** of tines
- **Spring opening** parallels vessels and fissure — NOT perpendicular
- Opening force distributed across sizable surface to prevent pial injury
- **Thick arachnoid → sharp dissection mandatory** — never force blunt opening

### The endgame

Trace cortical/fissure arteries down to basal cisterns and circle of Willis. **The anatomy of the circle of Willis is the decisive map for the aneurysm surgeon.**

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Intimate cisternal anatomy** is imperative — not negotiable
- **Trajectory first, dissection second** — plan before cutting
- **Never approach the dome directly** — preserve the path as perforator-free as possible
- **Single-lobe artery rule** — know which lobe each vessel supplies
- **Proximal control before aneurysm discovery** — always
- **Low-flow partial occlusion** (dominant feeder only) for multi-feeder complexes
- **Sharp dissection beats blunt** for thick arachnoid — pia injury is avoidable
- **Tips first, blades second** with microscissors
- **Adenosine asystole** rescues unclamped rupture; **balloon occlusion** rescues basilar
