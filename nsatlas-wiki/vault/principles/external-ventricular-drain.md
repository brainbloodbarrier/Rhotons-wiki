# External Ventricular Drain

*Principles of Cranial Surgery — External Ventricular Drain*
*DOI: 10.18791/nsatlas.v2.15*

**Source:** NSAtlas, Cohen-Gadol

## Role

- **Diagnostic + therapeutic** — unlike intraparenchymal ICP monitors (which only measure)
- Drains CSF → relieves intracranial hypertension from CSF overproduction or underabsorption
- **More invasive** than intraparenchymal monitors → higher infection (ventriculitis) and hemorrhage risk
- **Blind procedure** based on external landmarks; distorted ventricles require trajectory adjustment from CT
- Small ventricles → consider **stereotaxy**

## Side Selection

- **Right frontal (nondominant)** is default
- **Left-sided** indications:
  - Left frontal pathology forces preservation of right frontal lobe function
  - Right frontal horn "blood-casted" → immediate right EVD clogging
  - Trapped left lateral ventricle

## EVD vs Intraparenchymal ICP

- **TBI + GCS <8** → intraparenchymal first (most have physiologic ICP, no hydrocephalus)
- **EVD** reserved for hydrocephalus or medically intractable raised ICP requiring CSF drainage

## Landmarks

| Landmark | Role |
|---|---|
| Midline (nasion-inion) | Sagittal plane reference |
| Nasion | Anterior starting reference |
| Ipsilateral tragus | Sagittal trajectory target (~1 cm anterior to tragus) |
| Medial canthus | Coronal trajectory target |
| Ipsilateral pupil | Midpupillary line guide |
| Coronal suture | 1 cm posterior to Kocher's |

### Kocher's point

- **10-11 cm posterior to nasion**
- **3 cm lateral to midline**
- **1 cm anterior to coronal suture**, along midpupillary line
- **Cohen-Gadol variant:** place burr hole **1 cm lateral** to standard Kocher's — avoids parasagittal veins

## Trajectory

- **Coronal plane:** aim at medial canthus
- **Sagittal plane:** aim at ~1 cm anterior to tragus
- **Drill perpendicular to skull** — critical
- **Target:** foramen of Monro — the "zero point" for intracranial pressure measurement

## Patient Position

- **Supine**, head of bed elevated **30-45°**
- **Neutral head** — any rotation disorients landmarks
- **Assistant stabilizes head** (patient lethargic/sedated)

## Procedure

1. Clip hair at Kocher's point; verify midline from nose bridge
2. Mark Kocher's and draw 1-2 cm linear incision
3. Local anesthetic along incision AND along **planned subgaleal tunneling path**
4. Small self-retaining retractor; dissect periosteum off calvarium
5. **Drill perpendicular to skull, same trajectory as planned catheter path**
6. Use a **larger-diameter bit** and **advance/retract multiple times** to ensure smooth inner-table edges (no bony ledge)
7. If drill didn't breach dura → **blunt needle durotomy**
8. Pass drain with stylet to **6-6.5 cm** from scalp level
9. Feel "pop" as ependyma is penetrated → confirm CSF flow
10. Remove stylet, advance catheter **1 cm more** within ventricle (tip at foramen of Monro)
11. Tunnel distal drain subgaleally (Cohen-Gadol uses non-toothed forceps to hold catheter during tunneling)
12. **Secure to skin with staples at multiple points** to prevent pullout
13. Close incision — **do NOT sew into the drain**

## The "Inadequate Craniostomy" Trap

- **Small burr hole with irregular inner-table edges** deflects the flexible catheter tip
- The operator manipulates only the distal end — **cannot detect deflection** once catheter is in brain
- Prevention:
  1. **Larger drill bit**
  2. **Multiple passes** through skull thickness to smooth inner table

## When CSF Doesn't Return

- **Don't immediately remove**
- **Low intraventricular pressure** can mimic malposition
- **10-cc syringe** — gently withdraw fluid from catheter; confirms correct placement
- If CSF confirmed by aspiration, tip is good

## If Catheter Advances Beyond 7 cm Without CSF

Wrong location — likely:

- Temporal horn
- Interhemispheric fissure
- Third ventricle
- Sylvian fissure
- Basal cisterns

**Action:** remove catheter, re-aim **slightly more medial and posterior**.

## Three-Pass Rule

**If 3 consecutive passes fail to cannulate:**

- **Abort** the procedure
- Request senior/experienced colleague OR use stereotactic guidance
- Intracranial hemorrhage risk **significantly increases** after 3 attempts

## Imaging After Placement

If multiple passes or malposition suspected → **CT scan immediately** to confirm placement and exclude hematoma.

## Postoperative Pressure Management

| Clinical scenario | Minimum drainage pressure |
|---|---|
| Standard | 5-10 cm H₂O |
| **Unsecured ruptured aneurysm** | **≥10 cm H₂O** — lower risks rerupture from transmural pressure change |
| **Huge posterior fossa mass with hydrocephalus** | **≥15 cm H₂O** — prevents life-threatening upward transtentorial herniation by preserving supratentorial back-pressure |
| **Skull base / spinal CSF leak** | **Do NOT drain aggressively** — worsens pneumocephalus; infection spread to ventricles |

## Other Key Principles

- **Simultaneous EVD + lumbar drain = avoid** (see [[lumbar-drain]]) — dramatic CSF dynamic changes
- **Long-term EVD (>2 weeks)** → increased infection / ventriculitis
- **Active CSF infection** → avoid EVD placement if possible

## EVD Malposition Example

Slight deflection by inner burr-hole edge → catheter misses ventricle → operator advances beyond allowed depth → tip too deep in parenchyma → hemorrhage risk.

## Pearls and Pitfalls

- **Kocher's + 1 cm lateral** avoids parasagittal veins
- **Drill perpendicular, same trajectory as catheter**
- **Larger bit + multiple passes** = smooth inner table — THE most important technical principle
- **"Aim slightly medial and posterior"** if previous pass missed
- **Don't pull a probably-in catheter** — 10-cc syringe aspiration confirms low-pressure placement
- **3-pass rule** — abort and escalate
- **Stateful pressure management** — ruptured aneurysm ≥10, posterior fossa mass ≥15, CSF leak = clamp or minimize
- **Staples at multiple points**, tunnel subgaleally, **don't sew into drain**
