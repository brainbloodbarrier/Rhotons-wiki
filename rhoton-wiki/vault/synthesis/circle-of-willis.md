# Circle of Willis

The circle of Willis (circulus arteriosus cerebri) is the principal arterial anastomosis at the base of the brain, connecting the anterior and posterior cerebral circulations. It encircles the [[optic-chiasm]], infundibulum, and [[interpeduncular-cistern|interpeduncular fossa]].

## Components

### [[anterior-circulation]]
- **[[internal-carotid-artery]]** (bilateral) — C4 supraclinoid segments form the lateral walls
- **[[anterior-cerebral-artery]]** — A1 segments connect the ICA to the ACoA
- **[[anterior-communicating-artery]]** — completes the anterior circle, connecting bilateral A1 segments

### Posterior Circulation
- **[[basilar-artery]]** — apex provides the posterior midline input (see [[posterior-circulation]])
- **[[posterior-cerebral-artery]]** — P1 segments connect the basilar to the PCoAs
- **[[posterior-communicating-artery]]** (bilateral) — connect the ICA to the PCA, forming the lateral posterior boundary
- **[[vertebral-artery]]** — paired arteries merge to form the basilar

## Configuration and Variants

A "complete" and symmetric circle is found in only ~20–25% of individuals. Common variants include:
- **Fetal PCA** — PCoA remains the dominant supply to the PCA territory (the P1 is hypoplastic)
- **Hypoplastic A1** — one A1 is significantly smaller, the contralateral ACA supplies both hemispheres via the ACoA
- **Absent or hypoplastic PCoA** — disrupts anterior-posterior communication
- **Infundibular widening** at the PCoA origin (~6%) — may mimic aneurysm

## Functional Significance

The circle provides collateral flow in the event of:
- Carotid occlusion — flow crosses via ACoA from contralateral ICA
- Vertebrobasilar insufficiency — flow reverses through PCoAs from the ICA
- Proximal vessel sacrifice during surgery — circle maintains perfusion if variant anatomy permits

## Aneurysm Sites

The circle of Willis and its branches are the most common locations for intracranial aneurysms:
- [[anterior-communicating-artery]] complex — 25–30% of aneurysms
- [[internal-carotid-artery]] (PCoA origin, bifurcation) — 25–30%
- [[middle-cerebral-artery]] bifurcation — 20%
- Basilar apex — 5–10%

## Surgical Exposure

The anterior circle is exposed via the [[pterional-approach]], working through the [[sylvian-fissure]] and opticocarotid triangle. The posterior circle (basilar apex) is accessed through the same approach by deepening the exposure between the ICA and [[optic-tract]], or via subtemporal routes.

Key landmarks:
- **Opticocarotid triangle** — between ICA, A1, and [[optic-nerve]]; provides access to PCoA and basilar apex
- **[[lamina-terminalis]]** — thin membrane posterior to the ACoA; can be opened for CSF drainage during [[aneurysm-surgery]]
