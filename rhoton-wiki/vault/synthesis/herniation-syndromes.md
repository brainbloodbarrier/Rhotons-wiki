# Herniation Syndromes

Brain herniation occurs when a mass lesion or diffuse swelling forces brain tissue through rigid dural or bony openings. Understanding herniation anatomy requires knowledge of the [[tentorial-incisura]], [[foramen-magnum]], falx cerebri, and the neurovascular structures compressed at each site. Rhoton's anatomical descriptions of these openings directly inform the clinical recognition and surgical treatment of herniation.

## Types

### 1. Uncal (Lateral Transtentorial) Herniation

The most common and clinically important herniation. The medial uncus of the temporal lobe displaces medially over the tentorial free edge into the [[tentorial-incisura]].

**Compressed structures (sequential):**
1. **[[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]]** — compressed between the uncus and the tentorial edge or [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] → ipsilateral fixed dilated pupil (earliest sign, parasympathetics on the outer surface compressed first)
2. **Cerebral peduncle** ([[brainstem]]) — ipsilateral compression → contralateral hemiparesis (Kernohan's notch = contralateral peduncle compressed against opposite tentorial edge → ipsilateral hemiparesis, a false localizing sign)
3. **[[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]]** — compressed against the tentorial edge → occipital lobe infarction (contralateral homonymous hemianopia)
4. **Midbrain** — progressive compression → loss of consciousness, decerebrate posturing
5. **Posterior communicating and anterior choroidal arteries** — stretched → additional ischemia

**Anatomical basis:** The [[tentorial-incisura]] is ~30 mm wide; the midbrain fills most of it. The uncus (part of the [[hippocampus|parahippocampal gyrus]]) is the most medial temporal structure, directly above the free edge. [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] courses in the middle incisural space between the PCA and [[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]], making it the first structure compressed.

### 2. Central (Descending Transtentorial) Herniation

Bilateral downward displacement of both hemispheres through the [[tentorial-incisura]]. Both temporal lobes descend symmetrically.

**Compressed structures:**
- Bilateral [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] → bilateral pupil dilation
- [[basilar-artery]] perforators stretched → midbrain ischemia → [[perforating-arteries|thalamoperforating]] territory infarction
- [[brainstem]] compressed → progressive rostral-to-caudal deterioration (diencephalic → midbrain → pontine → medullary stages)
- Duret hemorrhages — secondary brainstem hemorrhages from perforator stretching

### 3. Tonsillar (Foramen Magnum) Herniation

The cerebellar tonsils descend through the [[foramen-magnum]], compressing the medulla.

**Compressed structures:**
- **Medulla** ([[brainstem]]) — respiratory and cardiovascular centers → **respiratory arrest** (most immediately lethal herniation)
- **[[vertebral-artery]]** and [[pica|PICA]] — compressed between the tonsils and the foramen rim → medullary ischemia
- **[[lower-cranial-nerves|CN XI]]** (spinal accessory) — the only cranial nerve traversing the foramen magnum
- **Posterior spinal arteries** — compressed → spinal cord ischemia

**Anatomical basis:** The tonsils sit directly above the posterior edge of the [[foramen-magnum]]. The cisterna magna ([[basal-cisterns]]) is obliterated. Chiari I malformation (tonsils >5 mm below the foramen) represents chronic tonsillar herniation.

### 4. Subfalcine (Cingulate) Herniation

The cingulate gyrus herniates under the falx cerebri, crossing the midline.

**Compressed structures:**
- **[[anterior-cerebral-artery|ACA]]** (pericallosal and callosomarginal arteries) — compressed against the falx → medial frontal infarction (contralateral leg weakness)
- **[[corpus-callosum]]** — compressed
- **Foramen of Monro** — may be obstructed → ipsilateral hydrocephalus

**Anatomical basis:** The falx cerebri has a free inferior edge above the [[corpus-callosum]]. The [[anterior-cerebral-artery|ACA]] branches course in the interhemispheric fissure ([[interhemispheric-approach]] corridor) and are trapped against the rigid falx.

### 5. Ascending (Upward) Herniation

The [[cerebellum]] (superior vermis and hemisphere) herniates upward through the [[tentorial-incisura]] from a posterior fossa mass.

**Compressed structures:**
- **[[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]]** — compressed against the tentorial edge → superior cerebellar infarction
- **[[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]]** — compressed
- **Cerebral aqueduct** — compressed by upward displacement of the vermis → acute obstructive hydrocephalus
- **[[vein-of-galen]]** complex — may be compressed → deep venous congestion
- **[[trochlear-nerve|CN IV]]** — may be stretched at the tentorial edge

### 6. Transcalvarial (External) Herniation

Brain tissue herniates through a skull defect (post-craniectomy, fracture). The only herniation that may be therapeutic (decompressive craniectomy deliberately allows controlled external herniation to reduce ICP).

## Herniation at Each Anatomical Bottleneck

| Bottleneck | Herniation type | Key structure compressed | Lethal mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[tentorial-incisura]] | Uncal, central, ascending | [[oculomotor-nerve\|CN III]], [[brainstem]], [[posterior-cerebral-artery\|PCA]] | Brainstem compression |
| [[foramen-magnum]] | Tonsillar | Medulla, [[vertebral-artery]], [[pica\|PICA]] | Respiratory arrest |
| Falx cerebri | Subfalcine | [[anterior-cerebral-artery\|ACA]], [[corpus-callosum]] | ACA territory infarction |
| Skull defect | Transcalvarial | Cortex (therapeutic or traumatic) | Secondary injury |

## Vascular Consequences

Herniation causes both **compression** and **stretching** of arteries:
- **PCA compression** → occipital infarction (uncal herniation)
- **ACA compression** → medial frontal infarction (subfalcine)
- **[[perforating-arteries|Perforator stretching]]** → Duret hemorrhages in brainstem (central herniation)
- **[[vertebral-artery]]/[[pica|PICA]] compression** → medullary infarction (tonsillar)
- **[[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]] compression** → cerebellar infarction (ascending)

## Surgical Treatment

| Herniation | Emergency intervention |
|---|---|
| Uncal | Decompressive craniectomy, hematoma evacuation, temporal lobectomy |
| Central | Bilateral decompressive craniectomy, CSF drainage |
| Tonsillar | Posterior fossa decompression, EVD for hydrocephalus |
| Subfalcine | Treat mass lesion; falcotomy (rare) |
| Ascending | Posterior fossa decompression, EVD |

## Relations

- [[tentorial-incisura]] — the principal herniation bottleneck (uncal, central, ascending)
- [[foramen-magnum]] — tonsillar herniation site
- [[brainstem]] — compressed in all major herniation types
- [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] — first nerve compressed in uncal herniation — see [[cranial-nerves-overview]]
- [[posterior-cerebral-artery]], [[anterior-cerebral-artery]], [[superior-cerebellar-artery]] — compressed by herniating tissue
- [[perforating-arteries]] — stretched, causing Duret hemorrhages
- [[vertebral-artery]] and [[pica]] — compressed in tonsillar herniation
- [[cerebellum]] (tonsils) — the herniating structure in tonsillar herniation
- [[hippocampus]] (uncus, parahippocampal gyrus) — the herniating structure in uncal herniation
- [[corpus-callosum]] — compressed in subfalcine herniation
- [[vein-of-galen]] — at risk in ascending herniation
