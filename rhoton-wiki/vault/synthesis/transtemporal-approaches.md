---Transtemporal Approaches

All approaches directed through the [[temporal-bone]] to reach the [[posterior-fossa]], [[clivus]], and petroclival region. Selection depends on hearing status, tumor size, target location, and the acceptable extent of temporal bone removal.

## Approach Taxonomy

### Through the Middle Fossa Floor

Directed through the superior surface of the [[temporal-bone]] by elevating the [[cerebral-lobes|temporal lobe]]:

1. **[[middle-fossa-approach]]** — Limited IAC exposure from above. Hearing preservation for small intracanalicular schwannomas. Requires identification of the arcuate eminence, [[greater-petrosal-nerve]] hiatus, and tegmen landmarks.

2. **[[anterior-petrosectomy]]** (Kawase) — Drilling medial to the [[internal-auditory-canal]] through the [[petrous-apex]] (Kawase triangle) to expose the upper anterior posterior fossa and lateral [[clivus]]. Bounded by [[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]], [[greater-petrosal-nerve]], [[internal-auditory-canal]], and petrous [[internal-carotid-artery]].

3. **Extended middle fossa** — Resection extends lateral to the IAC to include the semicircular canals, vestibule, roof of the mastoid antrum and tympanic cavity, and posterior face of the [[temporal-bone]]. Sacrifices hearing for wider petroclival access.

4. **Subtemporal preauricular infratemporal** — Combines the middle fossa exposure with exposure of the [[infratemporal-fossa]] and, as needed, the petrous carotid, [[petrous-apex]], [[pterygopalatine-fossa]], and [[orbit]]. Used for extensive lesions crossing the middle fossa floor.

### Through the Mastoid (Presigmoid)

Directed through the mastoid portion anterior to the [[sigmoid-sinus]], with progressively more bone removal:

1. **Minimal mastoidectomy** — Exposes only enough presigmoid dura to open anterior to the sigmoid sinus without exposing the labyrinth. Most conservative variant.

2. **Retrolabyrinthine** — Exposes the bony capsule of the labyrinth without violating it. Preserves both hearing and vestibular function. Access to the [[cerebellopontine-cistern|cerebellopontine angle]] is limited.

3. **Partial labyrinthectomy** — Removal of one or more semicircular canals. Sacrifices vestibular function but may preserve cochlear function depending on extent.

4. **Translabyrinthine** — Resection of all semicircular canals and vestibule. Sacrifices hearing. Provides direct access to the [[internal-auditory-canal]] and [[facial-nerve]]/[[vestibulocochlear-nerve]] at the fundus. Primary approach for large vestibular schwannomas with non-serviceable hearing.

5. **Transcochlear** — Removal of the entire labyrinth including the cochlea and possibly the [[petrous-apex]]. Maximum exposure of the petroclival region. Requires [[facial-nerve]] rerouting (anterior transposition). Sacrifices all hearing and vestibular function.

### Combined Supra- and Infratentorial Presigmoid

Any of the transmastoid variants can be combined with division of the [[tentorium-cerebelli]] to create a supra- and infratentorial exposure:

- **[[combined-petrosal-approach]]** — [[anterior-petrosectomy]] (Kawase) + [[posterior-petrosectomy]] (transmastoid presigmoid). Full petroclival exposure from the [[sellar-region]] to the [[foramen-magnum]]. The most extensive temporal bone approach.

### Postauricular Transtemporal

Allows lesions involving the mastoid, tympanic cavity, [[petrous-apex]], and [[jugular-foramen]] to be followed in multiple directions:
- **Posteriorly** → connects to [[retrosigmoid-approach]] and [[far-lateral-approach]] exposures
- **Anteriorly** → extends to [[infratemporal-fossa]], [[pterygopalatine-fossa]], middle fossa, lateral maxilla, and [[orbit]]

## Approach Selection Matrix

| Target | Hearing preserved | Hearing sacrificed |
|---|---|---|
| IAC (small tumor) | [[middle-fossa-approach]] | Translabyrinthine |
| IAC (large tumor) | [[retrosigmoid-approach]] | Translabyrinthine |
| Upper clivus | [[anterior-petrosectomy]] | Extended middle fossa |
| Petroclival meningioma | Retrolabyrinthine + tentorial division | Transcochlear |
| Full petroclival | [[combined-petrosal-approach]] | [[combined-petrosal-approach]] + transcochlear |
| Jugular foramen tumor | Retrolabyrinthine / [[far-lateral-approach]] | Postauricular transtemporal |
| Petrous apex lesion | [[anterior-petrosectomy]] | Transcochlear |

## Critical Structures at Risk

### [[facial-nerve]]
The intratemporal course has three segments, each exposed during different approaches:
- **Labyrinthine segment** — shortest, narrowest; between IAC fundus and geniculate ganglion; at risk during middle fossa and translabyrinthine approaches
- **Tympanic segment** — from geniculate ganglion across the medial wall of the tympanic cavity; located at the level of the lateral semicircular canal; Spine of Henle → 1.5 cm depth = tympanic CN VII
- **Mastoid segment** — descends to the stylomastoid foramen; at risk during all mastoidectomy approaches

### Petrous [[internal-carotid-artery]]
Courses through the carotid canal medial to the tympanic part of the [[temporal-bone]]. At risk during extended middle fossa, infratemporal fossa, and transcochlear approaches. The vertical and horizontal segments of the petrous ICA define the anterior boundary of safe drilling.

### Inner Ear
The cochlea, vestibule, and semicircular canals form an ascending hierarchy of sacrifice:
- Retrolabyrinthine: all preserved
- Partial labyrinthectomy: canal(s) sacrificed
- Translabyrinthine: vestibular function sacrificed
- Transcochlear: all auditory and vestibular function sacrificed

## Surface Landmarks for Approach Planning

| Landmark | Corresponds to | Approach relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Spine of Henle | Lateral canal, tympanic CN VII (1.5 cm deep) | All mastoid approaches |
| Suprameatal triangle | Mastoid antrum (1.5 cm deep) | Mastoidectomy entry |
| Asterion | Transverse-sigmoid junction (lower half) | Retrosigmoid, presigmoid |
| Supramastoid crest + squamous suture | Lateral petrous ridge, middle fossa floor | Middle fossa approach |
| Arcuate eminence | Superior semicircular canal | Middle fossa approach, anterior petrosectomy |
| Tegmen | Epitympanic area | Middle fossa approach |
