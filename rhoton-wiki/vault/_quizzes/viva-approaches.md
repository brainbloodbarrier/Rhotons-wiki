# Viva Questions — Surgical [[surgical-approaches-overview|Approach Selection]]

*Given a lesion, select the approach. Each answer references approach pages, [[basal-cisterns|cisterns]], and structures at risk.*

---

### Q1: Vestibular schwannoma, 2.5 cm, serviceable hearing

**Answer:** [[retrosigmoid-approach]]. Craniotomy posterior to [[sigmoid-sinus]], inferior to [[transverse-sinus]]. Enter [[cerebellopontine-cistern]], identify [[superior-petrosal-vein]] (may need sacrifice), find [[facial-nerve|CN VII]]/[[vestibulocochlear-nerve|CN VIII]] at [[internal-auditory-canal|IAC]] porus. Drill posterior IAC wall. Monitor BAEP for hearing. Alternative: [[middle-fossa-approach]] if <1.5 cm and intracanalicular only.

---

### Q2: Basilar apex aneurysm, high-riding

**Answer:** [[orbitozygomatic-approach]] (low-angle trajectory). Follow [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] → [[posterior-communicating-artery|PCoA]] → [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] P1 through [[crural-cistern]] to [[interpeduncular-cistern]]. [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] divides the field. [[thalamoperforating-arteries]] behind the neck — dissect free before clipping. Open [[lamina-terminalis]] for CSF drainage. Standard [[pterional-approach]] if basilar tip at [[posterior-clinoid-process|dorsum sellae]] level.

---

### Q3: Fourth ventricle ependymoma in a child

**Answer:** [[telovelar-approach]]. Midline suboccipital craniotomy. Follow [[pica]] through the [[cerebellomedullary-fissure]] (tela choroidea + inferior medullary velum). Opens the [[fourth-ventricle]] floor without splitting the vermis. Preserve [[pica]] branches. Identify [[facial-nerve|facial colliculus]] and [[brainstem]] safe entry zones ([[brainstem|suprafacial/infrafacial triangles]]). Avoid the [[fourth-ventricle]] floor — tumor is debulked from center outward.

---

### Q4: Petroclival meningioma extending above and below tentorium

**Answer:** [[combined-petrosal-approach]]. [[anterior-petrosectomy]] (Kawase triangle: [[greater-petrosal-nerve|GSPN]], [[petrous-apex]], [[trigeminal-nerve|V3]] — drill to expose [[clivus|lateral clivus]]) + [[posterior-petrosectomy]] (retrolabyrinthine or translabyrinthine based on hearing) + [[tentorium-cerebelli|tentorial division]]. Provides widest petroclival exposure from [[sellar-region]] to [[foramen-magnum]]. Preserves or sacrifices hearing depending on petrosectomy variant chosen.

---

### Q5: Craniopharyngioma, suprasellar, no hydrocephalus

**Answer:** Two options depending on size and extension:
1. **[[endoscopic-endonasal-approach]]** — [[transsphenoidal-approach|transsphenoidal]] extended to [[chiasmatic-cistern]]. Reconstruct with [[sphenopalatine-artery|nasoseptal flap]]. Best for midline, infrachiasmatic tumors.
2. **[[pterional-approach]]** — for laterally extending tumors. Access [[chiasmatic-cistern]], open [[lamina-terminalis]] for CSF drainage, dissect tumor from [[optic-chiasm]], [[pituitary-gland|stalk]], and [[superior-hypophyseal-artery|superior hypophyseal perforators]].

---

### Q6: Trigeminal neuralgia refractory to medication

**Answer:** [[retrosigmoid-approach]] for microvascular decompression (MVD). Enter [[cerebellopontine-cistern]]. [[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]] root entry zone at lateral pons — offending vessel usually [[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]] (75-80%). Interpose Teflon between artery and REZ. [[superior-petrosal-vein]] may need to be divided if obscuring the field. See [[neurovascular-compression-syndromes]] for complete offending vessel map.

---

### Q7: [[pineal-region]] tumor with obstructive hydrocephalus

**Answer:** Two-stage:
1. **EVD or endoscopic third ventriculostomy** (ETV) — through [[lamina-terminalis]] or [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|foramen of Monro]] to relieve hydrocephalus
2. **[[supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach]]** — sitting or prone position. [[cerebellum]] falls with gravity. Enter [[quadrigeminal-cistern]]. [[vein-of-galen]] and [[internal-cerebral-veins]] are the obstacles — retract, never sacrifice. [[trochlear-nerve|CN IV]] visible at lateral midbrain.

Alternative: [[interhemispheric-approach]] occipital transtentorial for tumors extending above the [[vein-of-galen]].

---

### Q8: Foramen magnum meningioma, anterolateral

**Answer:** [[far-lateral-approach]]. Lateral suboccipital craniotomy + condylar resection (posterior 1/3 of [[occipital-condyle|occipital condyle]]). Identify [[vertebral-artery]] V3 segment in the [[suboccipital-triangle]] (between obliquus capitis superior/inferior and rectus capitis posterior major). Follow V4 intradurally. Tumor attached to [[foramen-magnum]] anterolateral dura. Preserve [[anterior-spinal-artery]], [[pica]], and [[lower-cranial-nerves|CN IX-XII]].

---

### Q9: Insular glioma, left hemisphere, dominant

**Answer:** [[pterional-approach]] with wide [[sylvian-fissure]] splitting (transsylvian). [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]] M2 branches course over the [[insula]]. [[lenticulostriate-arteries]] and [[internal-capsule]] form the medial resection limit. Subcortical stimulation mapping identifies [[internal-capsule|corticospinal tract]] at 2-5 mA. Awake craniotomy for language mapping (dominant hemisphere). Preserve [[bridging-veins|sylvian veins]].

---

### Q10: Anterior communicating artery aneurysm, ruptured

**Answer:** [[pterional-approach]] (contralateral to dominant A1). Open [[sylvian-fissure]] → carotid cistern → [[chiasmatic-cistern]]. Identify [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] → [[anterior-cerebral-artery|A1]] → [[anterior-communicating-artery|ACoA]] complex. Open [[lamina-terminalis]] for CSF drainage. Identify both A2s and [[recurrent-artery-of-heubner|Heubner]] before clipping. [[perforating-arteries|Hypothalamic perforators]] arise from posterior ACoA surface — clip blades must not occlude them. If A1 is symmetric, [[interhemispheric-approach]] is an alternative.
