# Viva Questions — Neurovascular Anatomy

*Multi-hop oral exam questions synthesized from the Rhoton Wiki. Each answer traces across 3-5 wiki pages.*

---

### Q1: Trace CN VI from its nucleus to the orbit

**Answer:** The [[abducens-nerve|abducens nucleus]] lies in the dorsal pons (floor of the [[fourth-ventricle]]). CN VI exits the [[brainstem]] at the pontomedullary junction, ascends through the [[prepontine-cistern]] alongside the [[basilar-artery]], pierces the dura at the [[clivus|Dorello's canal]] below the [[petrous-apex]], enters the [[cavernous-sinus]] where it courses lateral to the [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] (the only CN inside the sinus, not in the wall), then exits through the [[orbit|superior orbital fissure]] within the annulus of Zinn to innervate the [[extraocular-muscles|lateral rectus]].

### Q2: Describe the arterial supply of the internal capsule

**Answer:** The [[internal-capsule]] receives supply from three arterial systems:
1. **Anterior limb** — [[recurrent-artery-of-heubner]] (from [[anterior-cerebral-artery|ACA]] A1-A2) and medial [[lenticulostriate-arteries]] (from [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]] M1)
2. **Genu** — direct perforators from the [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] and medial [[lenticulostriate-arteries]]
3. **Posterior limb (anterior)** — lateral [[lenticulostriate-arteries]] from [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]] M1
4. **Posterior limb (posterior)** — [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] from ICA
5. **Retrolenticular** — [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] and [[posterior-choroidal-arteries]]

All are end-arteries. [[anterior-choroidal-artery|AChA]] occlusion → AChA syndrome (hemiplegia + hemianesthesia + hemianopia).

### Q3: List the [[basal-cisterns|cisterns]] traversed during a pterional approach to the basilar apex

**Answer:** The [[pterional-approach]] traverses cisterns in sequence:
1. **[[sylvian-fissure|Sylvian cistern]]** — opened first; releases CSF; [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]] M1-M2 exposed
2. **Carotid cistern** — [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] supraclinoid segment; opticocarotid triangle opened
3. **[[chiasmatic-cistern]]** — [[optic-chiasm]], [[anterior-cerebral-artery|A1]] segments, [[lamina-terminalis]] (opened for CSF drainage)
4. **[[crural-cistern]]** — follows ICA → [[posterior-communicating-artery|PCoA]] → [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] P1 around the peduncle
5. **[[interpeduncular-cistern]]** — [[basilar-artery]] apex exposed between the [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCAs]]; [[thalamoperforating-arteries]] visible behind the bifurcation; [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] marks the superior-inferior division

### Q4: What are the boundaries and contents of the cavernous sinus?

**Answer:** The [[cavernous-sinus]] is a dural venous space lateral to the [[sellar-region|sella turcica]]:
- **Lateral wall (superficial to deep):** [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]], [[trochlear-nerve|CN IV]], [[trigeminal-nerve|V1]], [[trigeminal-nerve|V2]]
- **Inside the sinus:** [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] (cavernous segment C3) and [[abducens-nerve|CN VI]] (only CN inside, not in wall)
- **Medial wall:** [[pituitary-gland|pituitary]] lateral surface, [[sellar-region|sella]] dura
- **Surgical triangles:** 7 windows defined by CN boundaries — [[cavernous-sinus-triangles|clinoidal, oculomotor, supratrochlear, infratrochlear (Parkinson), anterolateral (Mullan), anteromedial, posterolateral (Glasscock)]]

### Q5: Describe the venous drainage from the thalamus to the internal jugular vein

**Answer:** [[thalamus|Thalamic]] veins → [[internal-cerebral-veins|ICV]] (via thalamostriate vein at the venous angle, [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|foramen of Monro]]) → courses through [[velum-interpositum]] → joins [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal]] at the [[quadrigeminal-cistern]] → forms [[vein-of-galen]] → drains into the **straight sinus** → **torcular Herophili** → [[transverse-sinus]] → [[sigmoid-sinus]] → **jugular bulb** ([[jugular-foramen]]) → **internal jugular vein**.

Alternative pathway: thalamic surface veins → [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal]] directly → [[vein-of-galen]] → same downstream.

### Q6: What perforators are at risk during MCA bifurcation aneurysm clipping?

**Answer:** During [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]] bifurcation [[aneurysm-surgery|aneurysm clipping]] via the [[pterional-approach]]:
- **[[lenticulostriate-arteries]]** (lateral group) — arise from M1 proximal to the bifurcation; supply [[basal-ganglia|putamen]], [[internal-capsule|posterior limb of internal capsule]]; injury → contralateral hemiplegia
- **Early temporal branches** — may arise from the aneurysm neck; must be dissected free before clipping
- **[[perforating-arteries|Rule]]:** Dissect all perforators free from the neck BEFORE placing the clip. Perforators arise from the dorsal (hidden) surface of M1.

### Q7: Compare the retrosigmoid and middle fossa approaches to the IAC

**Answer:**
| Feature | [[retrosigmoid-approach]] | [[middle-fossa-approach]] |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Posterior → anterior | Superior → inferior |
| Bone removed | Retrosigmoid craniotomy | [[temporal-bone|Tegmen]] elevation |
| IAC exposure | Posterior wall drilled; full length | Roof drilled; fundus to porus |
| Hearing preservation | Good (BAEP monitoring) | Best (<2 cm tumor) |
| CN VII relation | CN VII anterior in IAC | Bill's bar separates VII from VIII |
| Key risk | [[superior-petrosal-vein]] sacrifice | [[greater-petrosal-nerve|GSPN]] and [[facial-nerve|geniculate ganglion]] injury |
| Tumor size | Any | <1.5-2 cm intracanalicular |
| CN at risk | [[facial-nerve|VII]], [[vestibulocochlear-nerve|VIII]], V | [[facial-nerve|VII]] (geniculate), [[greater-petrosal-nerve|GSPN]] |

### Q8: What is the Percheron artery variant and its clinical significance?

**Answer:** The Percheron artery is a variant of the [[thalamoperforating-arteries]] where a **single trunk arises from one P1 segment** of the [[posterior-cerebral-artery]] and supplies **both thalami** bilaterally. Normally, each P1 gives separate thalamoperforating branches to the ipsilateral [[thalamus]].

**Clinical significance:** Occlusion of the Percheron artery (e.g., during [[basilar-artery]] apex [[aneurysm-surgery]] or cardioembolic stroke) → **bilateral thalamic infarction** → coma, vertical gaze palsy, memory loss, akinetic mutism. On MRI: bilateral symmetric thalamic diffusion restriction.

### Q9: Describe [[herniation-syndromes|uncal herniation]] step by step

**Answer:** Expanding supratentorial mass → [[uncus]] displaces medially through the [[tentorial-incisura]]:
1. **[[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] compression** → ipsilateral fixed dilated pupil (parasympathetics on nerve surface compressed first)
2. **[[brainstem|Cerebral peduncle]] compression** → contralateral hemiparesis
3. **Kernohan's notch** → contralateral peduncle compressed against opposite [[tentorium-cerebelli|tentorial]] edge → ipsilateral hemiparesis (false localizing sign)
4. **[[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] compression** → trapped against [[tentorium-cerebelli|tentorium]] → occipital infarction → contralateral hemianopia
5. **Duret hemorrhage** → [[brainstem]] [[perforating-arteries|perforator]] stretch/tear → pontine hemorrhage (irreversible)

The [[crural-cistern]] and [[ambient-cistern]] are obliterated sequentially as the [[uncus]] herniates.

### Q10: What are the safe entry zones of the brainstem?

**Answer:** The [[brainstem]] safe entry zones are areas where entering the brainstem produces minimal neurological deficit:
1. **Anterior mesencephalic zone** — between the [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] rootlets on the [[brainstem|cerebral peduncle]]
2. **Lateral mesencephalic zone** — between [[brainstem|substantia nigra]] and medial lemniscus
3. **Peritrigeminal zone** — lateral to [[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]] entry on the pons
4. **Suprafacial triangle** — above the [[facial-nerve|facial colliculus]] on the [[fourth-ventricle]] floor
5. **Infrafacial triangle** — below the facial colliculus, above the [[lower-cranial-nerves|hypoglossal triangle]]
6. **Median sulcus of 4V** — midline raphe of the [[fourth-ventricle]] floor
7. **Lateral medullary zone** — posterior to the [[lower-cranial-nerves|olivary]] prominence
8. **Posterior median fissure** — dorsal midline of the medulla

Approached via [[retrosigmoid-approach]], [[telovelar-approach]], [[subtemporal-approach]], or [[supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach]] depending on the zone.
