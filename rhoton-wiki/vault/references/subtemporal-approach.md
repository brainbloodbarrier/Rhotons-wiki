# Subtemporal Approach

The subtemporal approach exposes the **middle incisural space** of the [[tentorial-incisura]] by elevating the [[cerebral-lobes|temporal lobe]] from the floor of the middle cranial fossa. It is a workhorse corridor for lesions of the medial temporal region, the [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]], [[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]], [[basilar-artery]] apex, and the upper [[posterior-fossa]].

## Indications

- **Basilar apex aneurysms** (particularly low bifurcation)
- Meningiomas arising from [[meckels-cave|Meckel's cave]], [[petrous-apex]], or anterior tentorial edge
- [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] and [[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]] aneurysms in the middle incisural space
- [[trigeminal-nerve|Trigeminal nerve]] neuromas and microvascular decompression for trigeminal neuralgia
- Temporal lobe gliomas and thalamic tumors extending medially
- Arteriovenous malformations of the medial temporal lobe
- Amygdalohippocampectomy and temporal lobectomy for epilepsy
- Lesions impacted in the [[tentorial-incisura]]

## Technique

### Positioning and craniotomy
- Temporal craniotomy with the lower margin at the [[cranial-base]] (floor of middle fossa)
- Placing the bone flap at the skull base minimizes brain retraction
- The temporal lobe is elevated to expose the cisterns around the incisura

### Key steps
1. **Temporal lobe elevation** — gentle retraction upward; avoid occluding [[bridging-veins]], especially the **vein of Labbé** (hemorrhage, venous infarction, and edema are the principal complications)
2. **Tentorial division** — frequently performed to increase exposure or decompress the [[brainstem]] when mass lesions are impacted in the incisura
3. **Cisternal dissection** — opening the [[ambient-cistern]] and [[crural-cistern]] exposes the PCA, SCA, and [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]]

### Tentorial section
The [[tentorium-cerebelli|tentorium]] may be divided to combine the subtemporal exposure with a suboccipital craniectomy, providing access to the prepontine and cerebellopontine cisterns. **Critical:** The [[trochlear-nerve|trochlear nerve (CN IV)]] enters the anterior edge of the tentorium and is at risk when dividing the free edge. It is the cranial nerve most frequently injured in the middle incisural space — so thin and friable that gentle retraction may rupture it.

## Exposed Structures

### Arteries
- [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] — passes through the middle incisural space above [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]]
- [[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]] — passes below CN III, bifurcates into rostral and caudal trunks at the lateral cerebral peduncle
- [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] — enters the crural cistern between the peduncle and [[uncus]]
- [[basilar-artery]] apex — visible by depressing the PCA
- Medial and lateral [[posterior-choroidal-arteries]]

### Nerves
- [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] — between the PCA (above) and SCA (below)
- [[trochlear-nerve|CN IV]] — courses with SCA branches around the brainstem; most vulnerable nerve
- [[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]] — posterior root exposed in the infratentorial part of the middle incisural space

### Veins
- Vein of Labbé (must be preserved)
- [[cerebral-veins|Basal vein of Rosenthal]] — courses through the ambient cistern
- Bridging veins from temporal lobe to transverse/[[sigmoid-sinus]]

## Variants and Combinations

### Subtemporal + anterior petrosectomy ([[anterior-petrosectomy|Kawase approach]])
Drilling the petrous apex between the [[trigeminal-nerve]] and [[internal-auditory-canal|internal acoustic meatus]] exposes the lateral [[clivus]] and provides access to the [[prepontine-cistern]], [[basilar-artery]], and [[abducens-nerve|CN VI]].

### Subtemporal + suboccipital (combined supratentorial-infratentorial)
The tentorium and [[transverse-sinus]] may be divided to create a combined corridor for large petroclival lesions extending both above and below the tentorium.

### Transventricular variant
A cortical incision in the nondominant inferior or middle temporal gyrus reaches the temporal horn and [[choroidal-fissure]], opening into the upper middle incisural space. On the dominant hemisphere, the medial occipitotemporal gyrus incision minimizes visual and speech deficits.

## Complications

- **Temporal lobe retraction injury** — hemorrhage, edema, venous infarction
- **Vein of Labbé occlusion** — venous infarction of the temporal lobe
- **[[trochlear-nerve|CN IV]] palsy** — from tentorial division or retraction
- **Visual field deficit** — from Meyer's loop injury or PCA branch compromise

## Relations

- Exposes the middle incisural space of the [[tentorial-incisura]]
- Access to [[posterior-cerebral-artery]], [[superior-cerebellar-artery]], [[basilar-artery]] apex
- [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]], [[trochlear-nerve|CN IV]], [[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]] exposed
- Combinable with [[pterional-approach]] (frontotemporal extension)
- Combinable with [[retrosigmoid-approach]] (via tentorial section)
- Exposes [[basal-cisterns]] (ambient, crural, interpeduncular cisterns)
- Related to [[cavernous-sinus]] and [[sellar-region]] anteriorly
- [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] visible in the crural cistern
