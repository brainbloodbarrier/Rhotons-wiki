# Combined Petrosal Approach

The combined petrosal approach unites an [[anterior-petrosectomy]] (Kawase) with a [[posterior-petrosectomy]] (retrolabyrinthine, translabyrinthine, or transcochlear) and [[tentorium-cerebelli|tentorial division]], providing the widest possible exposure of the petroclival region from the [[sellar-region]] to the [[foramen-magnum]].

## Rationale

Large petroclival lesions (meningiomas, chordomas, chondrosarcomas) extend across the [[clivus]] from the posterior [[anterior-clinoid-process|clinoid process]] to the jugular tubercle, involving the middle and posterior fossae. No single approach provides adequate exposure of the entire petroclival surface. The combined petrosal approach solves this by:

- Removing bone from **both sides** of the petrous ridge
- Dividing the [[tentorium-cerebelli]] to connect the supra- and infratentorial exposures
- Minimizing [[brainstem]] retraction by approaching the lesion from two convergent angles

## Components

### 1. Temporal Craniotomy
A frontotemporal craniotomy exposes the middle fossa floor and the subtemporal dura.

### 2. [[anterior-petrosectomy]] (Kawase)
- Drill the [[petrous-apex]] at the Kawase triangle (see [[cavernous-sinus-triangles]])
- Exposes the **lateral [[clivus]]** and [[posterior-fossa]] dura from above
- Limits: [[internal-carotid-artery|petrous ICA]] (lateral), cochlea (posterolateral), [[facial-nerve|geniculate ganglion]]

### 3. [[posterior-petrosectomy]]
Depending on hearing preservation goals:

| Variant | Hearing | Structures Sacrificed |
|---|---|---|
| **Retrolabyrinthine** | Preserved | Mastoid air cells only |
| **Translabyrinthine** | Sacrificed | Semicircular canals, vestibule |
| **Transcochlear** | Sacrificed | Cochlea, labyrinth; [[facial-nerve]] transposed |

### 4. [[tentorium-cerebelli|Tentorial Division]]
The tentorium is divided **posterior to the entry point of CN IV** to connect the supratentorial (Kawase) and infratentorial (posterior petrosectomy) exposures into a single operative corridor.

## Exposure

The combined approach provides a panoramic view of:

| Structure | Visibility |
|---|---|
| [[basilar-artery]] (mid to upper) | Full length along the clivus |
| [[brainstem]] (pons, upper medulla) | Anterolateral surface |
| [[abducens-nerve\|CN VI]] | Entire course from brainstem to [[cavernous-sinus]] |
| [[trigeminal-nerve\|CN V]] | Root entry zone and [[meckels-cave]] |
| [[facial-nerve\|CN VII]], [[vestibulocochlear-nerve\|CN VIII]] | IAC to brainstem |
| [[clivus]] (petroclival surface) | Upper and middle clivus |
| [[posterior-cerebral-artery]], [[superior-cerebellar-artery]] | At tentorial level |
| [[internal-carotid-artery]] (petrous, cavernous) | Within carotid canal |

## Cranial Nerve Risks

| Nerve | Risk Mechanism |
|---|---|
| [[trochlear-nerve\|CN IV]] | Tentorial division must preserve its entry point |
| [[facial-nerve\|CN VII]] | Transposed in transcochlear variant; at risk in translabyrinthine |
| [[vestibulocochlear-nerve\|CN VIII]] | Hearing sacrificed in translabyrinthine/transcochlear |
| [[abducens-nerve\|CN VI]] | Draped over petroclival tumors; displaced by lesion |
| [[trigeminal-nerve\|CN V]] | Tumor may encase V at Meckel's cave |

## Comparison with Single Approaches

| Feature | Kawase Alone | Posterior Petrosectomy Alone | Combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clival exposure | Upper-lateral | Lower-lateral | Full petroclival |
| Brainstem retraction | Moderate | Moderate | Minimal |
| Hearing preservation | Yes | Retrolabyrinthine: yes | Depends on posterior variant |
| Operative time | Shorter | Shorter | Longest |
| Complexity | Moderate | Moderate | High |

## Indications

- **Large petroclival meningiomas** extending from posterior clinoid to jugular tubercle
- **Chordomas** of the petroclival region
- **Chondrosarcomas** at the petroclival synchondrosis
- **Epidermoids** extending across the [[tentorial-incisura|tentorial hiatus]]
- **Basilar trunk aneurysms** (mid-basilar)
