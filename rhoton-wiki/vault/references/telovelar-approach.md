# Telovelar Approach

The telovelar approach accesses the [[fourth-ventricle]] through the cerebellomedullary fissure by opening the tela choroidea and inferior medullary velum — the inferior half of the ventricular roof — without splitting the vermis of the [[cerebellum]]. It follows the [[pica|PICA]] (telovelotonsillar segment) as the natural surgical guide into the fissure.

## Principle

The cerebellomedullary fissure is a natural cleft between the [[cerebellum]] (tonsils, inferior vermis) and the medulla ([[brainstem]]). The [[pica|PICA]] courses through this fissure in its telovelotonsillar segment, making hairpin turns between the tonsil and the inferior medullary velum/tela choroidea. Opening the tela choroidea and velum exposes the entire floor of the [[fourth-ventricle]] without vermian incision, avoiding the cerebellar mutism associated with transvermian approaches.

## Indications

- [[fourth-ventricle]] tumors (ependymomas, medulloblastomas, subependymomas)
- [[choroid-plexus]] papillomas of the fourth ventricle
- Brainstem cavernous malformations accessible from the ventricular floor
- Fourth ventricle cysts
- Biopsy of floor lesions

## Technique

### Positioning and exposure
1. Prone or lateral position (park-bench)
2. Midline suboccipital craniectomy extending to the [[foramen-magnum]]
3. C1 laminectomy (optional, improves inferior exposure)
4. Y-shaped dural opening

### Key steps
1. **Identify the cerebellar tonsils** — the tonsils sit above the posterior rim of the [[foramen-magnum]], flanking the cisterna magna
2. **Identify the [[pica|PICA]]** — follow the artery from the [[vertebral-artery]] origin around the medulla into the cerebellomedullary fissure
3. **Separate the tonsils** — gently retract the tonsils laterally from the medulla; the PICA's caudal loop appears between the inferior tonsil pole and the medulla
4. **Enter the cerebellomedullary fissure** — follow the PICA cranially as it ascends between the tonsil and the inferior medullary velum (telovelotonsillar segment)
5. **Open the tela choroidea** — the thin arachnoidal-pial membrane stretching from the medulla to the cerebellum; opening it exposes the [[fourth-ventricle]] cavity
6. **Open the inferior medullary velum** — the thin white matter sheet between the cerebellar nodule and flocculus; opening it completes the exposure of the ventricular floor
7. **Tumor resection** — the entire floor (rhomboid fossa) is now visible, from the aqueduct above to the obex below

### PICA as surgical guide
The PICA's telovelotonsillar segment is the roadmap:
- **Caudal loop** — at the inferior tonsil pole, marks the initial entry into the fissure
- **Cranial (supratonsillar) loop** — wraps around the rostral tonsil pole near the fastigium
- Choroidal branches arise from this segment to supply the [[choroid-plexus]] of the fourth ventricle

## Exposed Structures

### Ventricular floor
- **Facial colliculus** — prominence where CN VII loops around [[abducens-nerve|CN VI]] nucleus (DO NOT resect)
- **Hypoglossal triangle** — [[lower-cranial-nerves|CN XII]] nucleus
- **Vagal triangle** — [[lower-cranial-nerves|CN X]] nucleus
- **Stria medullaris** — the transverse fiber bundle dividing the floor into pontine (upper) and medullary (lower) halves
- **Median sulcus** — midline safe entry zone for intrinsic [[brainstem]] lesions
- **Obex** — the caudal apex of the fourth ventricle at the calamus scriptorius

### Surrounding structures
- [[pica|PICA]] (telovelotonsillar and choroidal branches)
- [[cerebellum]] (tonsils, inferior vermis — retracted, not split)
- [[brainstem]] (medulla — the ventricular floor)
- [[choroid-plexus]] — attached to the tela choroidea
- Foramen of Magendie — opened to access the cisterna magna

## Advantages over Transvermian Approach

| Feature | Telovelar | Transvermian |
|---|---|---|
| Vermian incision | None | Required |
| Cerebellar mutism risk | Minimal | Significant (especially children) |
| Floor visualization | Complete (aqueduct → obex) | Limited by vermian edges |
| PICA preservation | Follows PICA as guide | PICA at risk from blind splitting |
| Recovery | Faster | Slower (vermian dysfunction) |

## Complications

- **[[pica|PICA]] injury** — the artery's loops are intimately embedded in the fissure; meticulous dissection required
- **Cranial nerve nuclei injury** — the facial colliculus and vagal/hypoglossal triangles on the floor must be identified and preserved
- **CSF leak** — through the craniectomy or foramen magnum exposure
- **Pseudomeningocele** — CSF collection under the skin
- **Lower cranial nerve dysfunction** — if [[lower-cranial-nerves|CN IX-XII]] rootlets are injured near the foramen of Luschka
