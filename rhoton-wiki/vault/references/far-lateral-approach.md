# Far Lateral Approach

The far-lateral approach extends a standard suboccipital craniotomy laterally to provide ventrolateral access to the [[brainstem]], lower [[clivus]], and [[foramen-magnum]] region without significant brainstem retraction. The basic exposure does not include condylar removal but provides the platform for three named extensions.

## Indications

- [[foramen-magnum]] meningiomas (ventral/ventrolateral)
- Lower clival lesions (chordomas, chondrosarcomas)
- [[vertebral-artery]] aneurysms (intradural V4 segment)
- Lower brainstem cavernous malformations
- [[craniocervical-junction]] pathology
- Lesions extending to the [[jugular-foramen]] (via paracondylar extension)

## Three Extensions

### Transcondylar
Directed **through** the [[occipital-condyle]] or atlanto-occipital joint. Drilling the posterior condyle increases the ventral approach angle, providing access to the lower [[clivus]] and premedullary area. The posterior third of the condyle can be removed without exposing the hypoglossal nerve in its canal; removing more than half risks [[craniocervical-junction]] instability requiring fusion.

### Supracondylar
Directed **above** the [[occipital-condyle]], through the area between the condyle and the [[jugular-foramen]]. Accesses the region of the hypoglossal canal and jugular tubercle — the bony prominence that often blocks visualization of the pontomedullary junction and middle-lower clivus junction. Drilling below the hypoglossal canal exposes the lateral clivus; drilling above it exposes the jugular tubercle.

### Paracondylar
Directed **lateral** to the [[occipital-condyle]], through the jugular process of the occipital bone. Accesses the posterior margin of the [[jugular-foramen]], the jugular bulb (sigmoid portion), and the posterior aspect of the [[facial-nerve]] canal and mastoid lateral to the jugular foramen.

## Three Stages

### 1. Muscular Stage
Horseshoe scalp flap from midline (~5 cm below external occipital protuberance) laterally above the superior nuchal line to the mastoid, then down anterior to the sternocleidomastoid to ~5 cm below the mastoid tip.

**Muscle layers (superficial to deep):**
1. Sternocleidomastoid + trapezius (posterior triangle margins)
2. Splenius capitis (attached just below superior nuchal line)
3. Longissimus capitis (exposes [[occipital-artery]] — may pass superficial or deep to it)
4. Semispinalis capitis (reflected medially → exposes the [[suboccipital-triangle]])

**[[suboccipital-triangle]]** — the critical surgical landmark, bounded by:
- **Superomedially**: rectus capitis posterior major
- **Superolaterally**: superior oblique
- **Inferolaterally**: inferior oblique

The triangle floor contains the posterior atlanto-occipital membrane and posterior arch of C1. Within the triangle: [[vertebral-artery]] (V3 segment) and C1 nerve, both in the groove on the upper surface of the posterior arch of C1. The vertebral venous plexus surrounding the artery must be obliterated for exposure.

**Rectus capitis lateralis** — a short muscle from the C1 transverse process to the jugular process of the occipital bone. Because it attaches at the posterior edge of the [[jugular-foramen]], it serves as a landmark for locating the jugular foramen and the [[facial-nerve]] exit at the stylomastoid foramen.

### 2. Extradural Stage
- Suboccipital craniectomy or craniotomy
- Removal of at least half of the posterior arch of C1
- Extent of [[occipital-condyle]] drilling determines which extension is used
- Landmarks: hypoglossal canal (above middle third of condyle), jugular process, jugular tubercle

### 3. Intradural Stage
Relationships of:
- [[vertebral-artery]] (V4 intradural segment) and its branches
- [[pica]] origin (usually from V4 near the foramen magnum)
- [[lower-cranial-nerves|CN IX, X, XI]] and [[lower-cranial-nerves|CN XII]] (hypoglossal nerve exiting its canal)
- Upper cervical nerves (C1, C2)
- [[dentate-ligament]] — divides the intradural space into anterior and posterior compartments

## Vertebral Artery (V3 Segment)

The V3 segment courses between the C1 transverse process and the dural entrance:
- Exits the C1 transverse foramen → courses medially behind the atlanto-occipital joint → lies in the groove on the posterior arch of C1
- The C1 nerve passes on the lower surface of the artery, between artery and posterior arch
- The posterior atlanto-occipital membrane may ossify, forming a bony canal around the artery
- V3 gives rise to muscular branches (some may need division for transposition) and the posterior meningeal artery

## Occipital Condyle Anatomy

- Ovoid, convex articular surface facing downward and laterally → articulates with C1 superior facet
- Average longest axis: 21 mm
- **Hypoglossal canal**: intracranial end ~5 mm above the junction of posterior and middle thirds of the condyle (~8 mm from posterior condylar edge); passes anterolateral at ~45 degrees from midsagittal plane
- **Safe drilling zone**: posterior third of condyle can be removed without entering the hypoglossal canal
- **Condylar canal** (when present): transmits the posterior condylar emissary vein connecting the vertebral venous plexus with the [[sigmoid-sinus]] proximal to the jugular bulb

## Extension Selection

| Extension | Drilling target | Access gained |
|---|---|---|
| Transcondylar | Posterior occipital condyle | Lower clivus, premedullary area |
| Supracondylar (below XII canal) | Below hypoglossal canal | Lateral clivus edge |
| Supracondylar (above XII canal) | Jugular tubercle | Pontomedullary junction, mid-lower clivus |
| Paracondylar | Jugular process | Posterior jugular foramen, jugular bulb |

## Surgical Risks

- [[vertebral-artery]] injury (V3 segment, venous plexus hemorrhage)
- [[lower-cranial-nerves|CN IX-XII]] deficits → dysphagia, dysphonia, tongue atrophy
- Occipitocervical instability (if >50% condyle removed → consider fusion)
- CSF leak (dural closure over craniectomy defect)
- [[occipital-artery]] injury during muscular dissection
