# Far Lateral Approach

The far lateral approach provides access to the ventrolateral [[brainstem]], lower [[clivus]], and foramen magnum region by extending a standard suboccipital craniotomy laterally with partial condylar resection. It avoids the need for significant brainstem retraction.

## Indications

- Foramen magnum meningiomas (ventral/ventrolateral)
- Lower clival lesions
- Vertebral artery aneurysms (intradural segment)
- Lower brainstem cavernous malformations
- [[craniocervical-junction]] pathology

## Key Structures Exposed

- Vertebral artery (V3-V4 segments)
- Lower brainstem (medulla oblongata)
- Cranial nerves IX, X, XI, XII
- Foramen magnum
- Cerebellar tonsils and PICA
- Lower clivus

## Technique

1. Hockey-stick or lazy-S skin incision
2. Suboccipital craniotomy extended to foramen magnum
3. Partial condylectomy (posterior 1/3 of occipital condyle) — increases ventral exposure angle
4. C1 laminectomy if needed
5. Vertebral artery identification and mobilization (V3 segment between C1/C2)

## Surgical Risks

- Vertebral artery injury (V3 segment)
- Lower cranial nerve deficits (CN IX-XII) → dysphagia, dysphonia
- Occipitocervical instability (if >50% condyle removed)
- CSF leak

## Relations

- Provides ventrolateral access to [[posterior-fossa]] at [[foramen-magnum]]
- [[vertebral-artery]] (V3-V4) is the critical vascular structure
- [[lower-cranial-nerves|CN IX-XII]] cross the surgical corridor
- [[pica]] origin and course near the surgical field
- Vertebrobasilar junction exposes lower [[basilar-artery]]
- Related to the [[tentorial-incisura]] (posterior incisural space) superiorly
- [[suboccipital-triangle]] landmarks guide [[vertebral-artery]] V3 identification
- Complementary to [[retrosigmoid-approach]] (lateral) and [[subtemporal-approach]] (superior)
