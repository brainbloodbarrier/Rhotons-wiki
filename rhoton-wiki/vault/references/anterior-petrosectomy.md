# Anterior Petrosectomy (Kawase Approach)

The anterior petrosectomy extends the [[subtemporal-approach]] by drilling the [[petrous-apex]] of the [[temporal-bone]] — the **Kawase triangle** — to expose the lateral clivus, [[prepontine-cistern]], [[basilar-artery]], and [[abducens-nerve|CN VI]]. It bridges the middle and posterior cranial fossae without a [[posterior-fossa]] craniotomy.

## Indications

- Petroclival meningiomas
- Upper and middle clival chordomas
- [[basilar-artery]] trunk aneurysms (mid-basilar)
- [[trigeminal-nerve|Trigeminal]] schwannomas extending into the posterior fossa
- Epidermoids of the petroclival region
- [[brainstem]] cavernous malformations (anterolateral midbrain/pons)

## Kawase Triangle (Posteromedial Triangle)

The Kawase triangle defines the safe zone for petrous apex drilling on the middle fossa floor:

- **Anterolateral margin** — [[greater-petrosal-nerve]] (from the geniculate ganglion of [[facial-nerve|CN VII]])
- **Medial margin** — lateral edge of the [[trigeminal-nerve|trigeminal nerve]] (posterior root entering [[meckels-cave]])
- **Posterior margin** — line connecting the hiatus of the greater petrosal nerve (hiatus fallopii) to the dural ostium of [[meckels-cave]]
- **Deep landmark** — the petrous [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] crosses the anterior apex of the triangle
- **Lateral limit** — the cochlea (must not be violated to preserve hearing)

### Critical structures to preserve
- **Cochlea** — located in the lateral apex of the triangle; drilling here causes sensorineural deafness
- **Petrous ICA** — crosses the anterior margin; injury is catastrophic
- **Greater petrosal nerve** — marks the anterolateral boundary; its injury causes dry eye (loss of lacrimation)

## Technique

1. [[subtemporal-approach]] craniotomy — [[cerebral-lobes|temporal lobe]] elevation from middle fossa floor
2. Identify the **greater petrosal nerve** coursing toward the hiatus fallopii
3. Identify the **arcuate eminence** (superior semicircular canal landmark)
4. Identify the **[[trigeminal-nerve|trigeminal nerve]]** entering [[meckels-cave]]
5. Drill the Kawase triangle bone between these landmarks — remove petrous apex **medial to the cochlea and behind the petrous ICA**
6. Open the posterior fossa dura → exposes the prepontine cistern, [[basilar-artery]], and [[abducens-nerve|CN VI]]
7. Optionally section the [[tentorium-cerebelli|tentorium]] for additional exposure

### Angular relationship
The long axis of the [[internal-auditory-canal|internal acoustic meatus]] is ~61° behind the greater petrosal nerve and ~37° medial to the arcuate eminence — these angles guide safe drilling toward the IAC fundus.

## Exposed Structures

### After petrous apex removal
- **Prepontine cistern** ([[basal-cisterns]]) — the [[basilar-artery]] on the ventral pons
- **[[abducens-nerve|CN VI]]** — ascending on the clivus, entering Dorello's canal
- **[[aica|AICA]]** — arising from the lower [[basilar-artery]]
- **Lateral clivus** — the [[clivus|clival bone]] surface
- **[[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]]** posterior root — above the drilling zone

### With tentorial section
- [[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]] and [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] in the [[tentorial-incisura]]
- [[trochlear-nerve|CN IV]] at the tentorial edge (at risk during section)
- [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] in the [[anterior-incisural-space|anterior incisural space]]

## Combination Approaches

### Kawase + subtemporal
The standard combination. Temporal lobe elevation + petrous apex drilling. Provides access to the entire petroclival region from the tentorial edge to the [[foramen-magnum]].

### Kawase + retrosigmoid (combined petrosal)
Adds a [[retrosigmoid-approach]] posterior fossa craniotomy. The tentorium is divided, creating a combined supra-infratentorial presigmoid corridor. Maximum exposure of the petroclival region.

### Kawase + orbitozygomatic
Adds the [[orbitozygomatic-approach]] for additional anterior and inferior angulation toward the basilar apex.

## Complications

- **Hearing loss** — cochlear injury during drilling (most common)
- **[[facial-nerve]] injury** — greater petrosal nerve or labyrinthine segment
- **Petrous ICA injury** — bleeding from the carotid canal
- **[[abducens-nerve|CN VI]] palsy** — from manipulation in Dorello's canal
- **[[trochlear-nerve|CN IV]] palsy** — from tentorial section
- **CSF leak** — through opened mastoid air cells or middle ear
- **Temporal lobe retraction injury**

## Relations

- Extension of [[subtemporal-approach]] with petrous apex drilling
- Exposes [[basilar-artery]] and [[abducens-nerve|CN VI]] through the clivus
- [[temporal-bone]] (petrous apex = Kawase triangle)
- [[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]] and [[meckels-cave]] at the medial boundary
- [[facial-nerve|CN VII]] (greater petrosal nerve) at the anterolateral boundary
- [[internal-carotid-artery]] (petrous segment) at the anterior apex
- [[aica|AICA]] visible in the prepontine cistern after drilling
- Cochlea limits lateral drilling — preserving hearing
- Complementary to [[retrosigmoid-approach]] (combined petrosal)
- See [[surgical-approaches-overview]] for petroclival approach selection
