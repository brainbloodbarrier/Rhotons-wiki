# Retrosigmoid Approach

The retrosigmoid approach provides access to the [[cerebellopontine-cistern]] and lateral [[posterior-fossa]] through a craniotomy behind the sigmoid sinus. It is the standard approach for vestibular schwannomas, trigeminal neuralgia (microvascular decompression), and other CP angle lesions.

## Indications

- Vestibular schwannomas (acoustic neuromas)
- Microvascular decompression for trigeminal neuralgia ([[superior-cerebellar-artery]] compressing CN V) and hemifacial spasm
- CP angle meningiomas and epidermoids
- CN V–XI schwannomas
- Endolymphatic sac tumors

## Key Structures Exposed

- **[[cerebellopontine-cistern]]** — primary surgical field
- Cranial nerves V, VI, VII, VIII, IX, X, XI
- **[[superior-cerebellar-artery]]** — above CN V
- **AICA** — near CN VII/VIII complex
- **PICA** — inferior field
- Superior petrosal vein (vein of Dandy)
- Petrosal cerebellar surface
- Internal auditory canal (after drilling posterior wall)

## Surgical Risks

- Hearing loss (CN VIII injury)
- Facial nerve injury (CN VII)
- Superior petrosal vein sacrifice → risk of venous infarction
- CSF leak (most common complication)
- Cerebellar retraction injury

## Relations

- Provides access to [[cerebellopontine-cistern]]
- Craniotomy positioned behind sigmoid sinus, below transverse sinus
- [[posterior-fossa-veins]] (superior petrosal vein) cross the surgical corridor
- Deep structures: brainstem, cerebellum, fourth ventricle
