# Middle Fossa Approach

The middle fossa approach elevates the [[cerebral-lobes|temporal lobe]] from the floor of the middle cranial fossa to access the internal acoustic canal (IAC), [[petrous-apex]], and the superior surface of the [[temporal-bone|petrous bone]] from above. It is the only approach that provides direct access to the IAC fundus while preserving both hearing and facial nerve function.

## Indications

- Small intracanalicular vestibular schwannomas with serviceable hearing
- [[facial-nerve|Facial nerve]] decompression (for Bell's palsy or trauma)
- Repair of superior semicircular canal dehiscence
- Tegmen defects with CSF leak or encephalocele
- Small petrous apex lesions (cholesterol granuloma, epidermoid)
- [[trigeminal-nerve|Trigeminal nerve]] exploration at [[meckels-cave]]

## Technique

### Positioning and craniotomy
1. Temporal craniotomy centered over the zygomatic root
2. Temporal lobe elevated extradurally from the middle fossa floor
3. Floor landmarks identified systematically

### Key landmarks on the middle fossa floor
- **Arcuate eminence** — overlies the superior semicircular canal; guides orientation
- **[[greater-petrosal-nerve]]** — courses anterolateral from the geniculate ganglion toward the foramen lacerum; exits through the hiatus fallopii
- **Tegmen tympani** — thin bone roofing the middle ear and mastoid antrum
- **Petrous [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]]** — the carotid canal creates a prominence anterolateral in the floor
- **[[trigeminal-nerve|V3]]** at foramen ovale — anterolateral landmark
- **[[middle-meningeal-artery]]** at foramen spinosum — anterolateral landmark

### IAC unroofing
The IAC is located using angular relationships:
- **Greater petrosal nerve to IAC axis** — the IAC long axis is ~61° posterior to the greater petrosal nerve
- **Arcuate eminence to IAC axis** — the IAC is ~37° medial to the superior semicircular canal axis

Drilling proceeds medially to laterally, following the superior petrosal surface toward the IAC fundus. The bone is thinned until the dura of the IAC is identified (blue-line technique).

### Fundus landmarks
- **Vertical crest (Bill's bar)** — separates the [[facial-nerve|facial nerve]] (anterosuperior) from the superior vestibular nerve (posterosuperior); the most critical landmark for facial nerve identification
- **Transverse crest** — divides the fundus into superior and inferior halves

## Exposed Structures

- **[[facial-nerve|CN VII]]** — labyrinthine segment, geniculate ganglion, tympanic segment origin; greater petrosal nerve
- **[[vestibulocochlear-nerve|CN VIII]]** — superior and inferior vestibular nerves at the IAC fundus
- **Cochlea** — anteroinferior to the IAC fundus; must NOT be violated
- **Superior semicircular canal** — under the arcuate eminence
- **Petrous [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]]** — anterior to the cochlea
- **[[trigeminal-nerve]]** and [[meckels-cave]] — medial exposure possible
- **Greater petrosal nerve** — from the geniculate ganglion to the hiatus

## Comparison with Other IAC Approaches

| Feature | Middle fossa | [[retrosigmoid-approach]] | Translabyrinthine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hearing preservation | Yes | Yes | No (sacrifices labyrinth) |
| Facial nerve at fundus | Directly visualized | Requires drilling | Directly visualized |
| Best tumor size | ≤1.5 cm intracanalicular | Any size | Large tumors, no hearing |
| [[posterior-fossa]] entry | No | Yes | Yes |
| Temporal lobe retraction | Yes | No | No |
| Approach angle to IAC | Superior | Posterior | Lateral |

## Complications

- **Hearing loss** — cochlear injury from drilling or heat transmission
- **[[facial-nerve]] injury** — especially at the geniculate ganglion or labyrinthine segment
- **Temporal lobe retraction injury** — edema, contusion, seizures
- **CSF leak** — through opened mastoid air cells or tegmen
- **Epidural hematoma** — from middle meningeal artery injury
- **[[abducens-nerve|CN VI]] palsy** — from temporal lobe retraction stretching CN VI at the petrous apex

## Relations

- Approaches the [[temporal-bone]] (petrous surface) from the middle cranial fossa
- [[facial-nerve|CN VII]] identified at the geniculate ganglion and IAC fundus
- [[vestibulocochlear-nerve|CN VIII]] at the IAC — target for schwannoma removal
- [[internal-carotid-artery]] (petrous segment) anterolateral in the floor
- [[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]] and [[meckels-cave]] medially
- Greater petrosal nerve → connects to [[facial-nerve|CN VII]] geniculate ganglion
- Complementary to [[retrosigmoid-approach]] (posterior) and translabyrinthine (lateral)
- [[anterior-petrosectomy]] (Kawase) extends this approach medially to the [[clivus]]
