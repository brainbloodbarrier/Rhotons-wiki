# Orbitozygomatic Approach

The orbitozygomatic approach is an extension of the [[pterional-approach]] that removes the supraorbital rim and zygomatic arch (one-piece or two-piece osteotomy), providing a lower and wider angle of access to the skull base. It reduces brain retraction by increasing the surgical trajectory angle, particularly for deep midline targets like the [[basilar-artery]] apex and upper clivus.

## Indications

- **[[basilar-artery]] apex aneurysms** — especially low bifurcation where the standard [[pterional-approach]] cannot reach
- Large [[sellar-region]] tumors with suprasellar and parasellar extension
- [[cavernous-sinus]] lesions requiring combined intra/extradural exposure
- Craniopharyngiomas with extensive suprasellar extension
- [[tentorial-incisura]] meningiomas
- Upper clival lesions
- Complex [[anterior-communicating-artery|ACoA]] aneurysms projecting posteriorly

## Technique

### One-piece variant
The frontotemporal craniotomy, supraorbital bar, and zygomatic arch are removed as a single bone flap.

### Two-piece variant
1. Standard [[pterional-approach]] frontotemporal craniotomy
2. Separate orbitozygomatic osteotomy (supraorbital rim + lateral orbital wall + zygomatic arch)

### Key bone removal
- **Supraorbital rim** — the bar of bone between the frontal and orbital roofs; its removal allows downward tilting of the surgical view
- **Lateral orbital wall** — the greater wing of the [[sphenoid-bone]] forming the posterior orbit
- **Zygomatic arch** — its removal allows the temporalis muscle to fall inferiorly, widening the corridor
- **Anterior clinoid process** — optional additional drilling for paraclinoid lesions

## Advantage: Angle of Attack

The key benefit is geometric — removing the orbital rim and zygomatic arch opens a **steeper upward angle** toward the basilar apex and interpeduncular cistern:

| Approach | Angle to basilar apex | Brain retraction |
|---|---|---|
| Standard [[pterional-approach]] | ~15–20° | Moderate frontal/temporal |
| Orbitozygomatic | ~30–40° | Minimal (bone removal replaces retraction) |
| [[subtemporal-approach]] | Direct lateral | Temporal lobe elevation |

## Exposed Structures

### Through the anterior incisural space
- [[basilar-artery]] apex and bifurcation
- [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] P1 and [[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]] origins
- [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] between PCA and SCA
- [[posterior-communicating-artery|PCoA]] and [[anterior-choroidal-artery]]
- [[optic-nerve]] and [[optic-chiasm]]
- Interpeduncular cistern ([[basal-cisterns]])
- [[perforating-arteries|Thalamoperforating arteries]] from P1

### Skull base structures
- [[internal-carotid-artery]] (supraclinoid and clinoid segments)
- [[cavernous-sinus]] (with anterior clinoidectomy)
- [[sphenoid-bone]] (greater and lesser wings, anterior clinoid process)
- [[orbit]] (roof and lateral wall)

## Complications

- **Cosmetic deformity** — temporal wasting (temporalis muscle atrophy), enophthalmos
- **Frontal branch of [[facial-nerve]]** — at risk in the skin flap; courses within the superficial temporal fascia
- **[[optic-nerve]] injury** — during orbital roof removal or anterior clinoidectomy
- **CSF leak** — through opened frontal sinus or orbital roof
- **Periorbital edema** — temporary, resolves in days

## Relations

- Extension of [[pterional-approach]] with orbital rim and zygomatic removal
- Accesses [[basilar-artery]] apex through [[basal-cisterns]] (interpeduncular cistern)
- [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]], [[optic-nerve|CN II]], [[optic-chiasm]] in the surgical field
- [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]], [[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]], [[posterior-communicating-artery|PCoA]] exposed
- [[perforating-arteries]] (thalamoperforating) at risk during basilar apex surgery
- [[sphenoid-bone]] (lesser and greater wings) partially removed
- [[orbit]] (roof and lateral wall) exposed
- [[cavernous-sinus]] accessible with anterior clinoidectomy
- [[sylvian-fissure]] splitting combined with the bone work
- Complementary to [[subtemporal-approach]] for different basilar bifurcation heights
- See [[surgical-approaches-overview]] for approach selection
