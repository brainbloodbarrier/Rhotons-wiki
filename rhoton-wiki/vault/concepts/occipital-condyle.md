# Occipital Condyle

The occipital condyles are ovoid structures located along the lateral margin of the anterior half of the [[foramen-magnum]]. Their articular surfaces are convex, face downward and laterally, and articulate with the superior facet of C1 (the [[craniocervical-junction|atlanto-occipital joint]]). Average length of the longest axis: 21 mm.

## Relationship to Hypoglossal Canal

The [[hypoglossal-canal]] is located above the middle third of the occipital condyle:
- **Intracranial end:** ~5 mm above the junction of the posterior and middle thirds, ~8 mm from the posterior condyle edge
- **Extracranial end:** ~5 mm above the junction of the anterior and middle thirds
- The canal is directed from posterior to anterior and medial to lateral, passing forward approximately 45 degrees from the midsagittal plane

> The portion of the occipital condyle that can be removed without exposing the [[lower-cranial-nerves|hypoglossal nerve]] in the hypoglossal canal corresponds to the posterior third of the condyle. (Rhoton 2023, p. 1402)

## Surgical Extensions of the Far-lateral Approach

The [[far-lateral-approach]] uses the condyle as the key decision point for 3 extensions:

- **Transcondylar:** directed through the condyle or [[craniocervical-junction|atlanto-occipital joint]] → access to lower [[clivus]] and premedullary area
- **Supracondylar:** directed above the condyle, below the [[hypoglossal-canal]] → access to lateral clivus and jugular tubercle
- **Paracondylar:** directed lateral to the condyle through the jugular process → access to posterior [[jugular-foramen]]

## Adjacent Structures

- The jugular process of the occipital bone extends laterally from the posterior half of the condyle to form the posterior margin of the [[jugular-foramen]]
- The condylar fossa (posterior to the condyle) frequently contains the condylar canal transmitting the posterior condylar emissary vein connecting the [[vertebral-artery|vertebral venous plexus]] with the [[dural-venous-sinuses|sigmoid sinus]]
- The [[suboccipital-triangle]] lies posterolateral to the condyle
