# Superior Cerebellar Peduncle

The **superior cerebellar peduncle (SCP)**, also called the **brachium conjunctivum**, is the principal **efferent** pathway of the cerebellum and the most medial of the three [[cerebellar-peduncles]]. Each SCP leaves the superomedial cerebellum, forms part of the roof and lateral wall of the superior [[fourth-ventricle]], enters the [[midbrain]] tegmentum, and **decussates in the lower midbrain** before terminating on the contralateral [[red-nucleus]] and ventral lateral (VL) nucleus of the [[thalamus]]. Together the two peduncles frame the superior medullary velum of the ventricular roof and bound the cerebellomesencephalic fissure along which the [[supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach]] is developed.

## Course and Relations

| Segment | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Cerebellar origin** | White matter of the superomedial cerebellum converges from the dentate nucleus region |
| **Intraventricular wall** | Forms the lateral walls and, with the superior medullary velum, the roof of the superior [[fourth-ventricle]] |
| **Isthmic segment** | Ascends into the midbrain tegmentum just caudal to the inferior colliculus, lateral to the central tegmental tract |
| **Decussation** | Fibers cross in the **lower midbrain tegmentum**, at the level of the inferior colliculus |
| **Termination** | Contralateral [[red-nucleus]] (magnocellular and parvocellular) and VL nucleus of the [[thalamus]] |

On the external surface of the [[brainstem]] the SCP frames the superolateral wall of the cerebellomesencephalic fissure, a key working corridor for approaches to the pineal region, quadrigeminal plate, and superior [[fourth-ventricle]].

## Fiber Content

1. **Dentato-rubro-thalamic tract (DRTT)** — the principal cerebellar efferent; dentate nucleus → contralateral [[red-nucleus]] parvocellular portion → VL [[thalamus]] → primary motor cortex. The DRTT is the preferred target of MR-guided focused ultrasound thalamotomy for essential tremor.
2. **Dentato-thalamic fibers** — bypass the red nucleus and project directly to the VL thalamus.
3. **Dentato-rubral fibers** — terminate on the magnocellular [[red-nucleus]] for rubrospinal output (vestigial in humans).
4. **Spinocerebellar afferents (ventral tract)** — the only major afferent component, entering the SCP in the caudal segment.

## Surgical Relevance

- **[[supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach]]** — the paired SCPs and the superior medullary velum between them form the floor of the working corridor to the pineal region and the roof of the upper [[fourth-ventricle]]. The SCPs must not be transgressed; lesions are removed through the cerebellomesencephalic fissure between the two peduncles.
- **Telovelar approach** — opening the tela choroidea and inferior medullary velum preserves the SCP but requires awareness of its course along the superolateral ventricular wall.
- **DRTT targeting** — stereotactic ablation (radiofrequency, gamma knife, focused ultrasound) or DBS of the VIM nucleus for tremor traverses the DRTT; tractography of the SCP guides trajectory.
- **Brainstem cavernomas** — lesions abutting the superior [[fourth-ventricle]] wall are accessed via the **supracerebellar peri-trigeminal** or **lateral mesencephalic** safe entry zones, which straddle the SCP.
- **Superior cerebellar artery (SCA) relations** — the [[superior-cerebellar-artery]] loops around the [[brainstem]] just below the SCP, a constant relationship exploited in micro-vascular decompression of the trigeminal nerve and in SCA aneurysm surgery.

## Clinical Significance

- **Claude syndrome** — unilateral lesion involving the [[red-nucleus]] and the decussating SCP fibers produces contralateral ataxia and ipsilateral CN III palsy.
- **Holmes tremor ("rubral tremor")** — low-frequency, large-amplitude rest-postural-kinetic tremor from lesions of the SCP, [[red-nucleus]], or DRTT, often after [[brainstem]] stroke or hemorrhage.
- **Multiple system atrophy (MSA-C)** — T2 hyperintensity and atrophy of the SCP (the "hot cross bun" sign with pontine involvement) is a diagnostic imaging feature.
- **Progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP)** — SCP atrophy is a specific MRI marker distinguishing PSP from Parkinson's disease.
- **Wernekinck commissure syndrome** — bilateral ataxia from a single lesion at the SCP decussation in the caudal [[midbrain]].

## Relations

- Medial-most of the [[cerebellar-peduncles]]; framed laterally by the middle cerebellar peduncle
- Forms the roof-lateral wall of the superior [[fourth-ventricle]]
- Decussates in the caudal [[midbrain]] tegmentum
- Terminates on contralateral [[red-nucleus]] and VL [[thalamus]]
- Superficially related to the [[superior-cerebellar-artery]] and the cerebellomesencephalic fissure
