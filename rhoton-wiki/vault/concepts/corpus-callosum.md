# Corpus Callosum

The corpus callosum is the largest white matter commissure in the brain, connecting the two cerebral hemispheres across the interhemispheric fissure. It is the principal landmark for the [[interhemispheric-approach]] and the roof of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|lateral ventricles]].

## Parts (anterior to posterior)

### Rostrum
The thin, recurved portion that projects posteroinferiorly from the genu to blend with the [[lamina-terminalis]] and anterior commissure. Located above the [[optic-chiasm]].

### Genu
The anterior curved portion. The [[anterior-cerebral-artery|ACA]] (A3 precallosal segment) curves around the genu. The forceps minor (frontal) fibers radiate anteriorly from the genu.

### Body
The central portion forming the roof of the lateral ventricle body. The **pericallosal artery** courses in the callosal sulcus on the superior surface. The [[fornix]] is attached to the undersurface of the body via the [[septum-pellucidum]].

### Splenium
The thick, rounded posterior end. The forceps major (occipital) fibers radiate posteriorly. The [[internal-cerebral-veins]] and [[vein-of-galen]] pass beneath the splenium. Related to the [[posterior-incisural-space|posterior incisural space]] of the [[tentorial-incisura]] and the [[pineal-region]].

## Vascular Relations

### Arterial
- **Pericallosal artery** — terminal branch of the [[anterior-cerebral-artery]], courses in the callosal sulcus along the entire superior surface
- **Callosomarginal artery** — courses in or near the cingulate sulcus, paralleling the pericallosal above the cingulate gyrus
- **Posterior pericallosal (splenial) artery** — from the [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] (P3 segment), supplies the splenium. Creates an anastomosis between [[anterior-circulation]] and [[posterior-circulation]] at the splenium

### Venous
- **Callosal veins** — drain the corpus callosum surface
- Internal cerebral veins course beneath the splenium → join the vein of Galen
- [[cerebral-veins]] from the medial hemispheric surface cross the callosum to reach the [[dural-venous-sinuses|superior sagittal sinus]]

## Surgical Significance

### Transcallosal approach ([[interhemispheric-approach]])
The body of the corpus callosum is divided (callosotomy, typically 2–2.5 cm) to access the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|lateral ventricle]] body and the foramen of Monro. Through the ventricle, the third ventricle is reached via transchoroidal or interforniceal routes. Excessive callosotomy causes **disconnection syndrome** (alien hand, apraxia).

### Posterior transcallosal approach
Division of the splenium accesses the pineal region, posterior third ventricle, and atrium. Alternative to the [[supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach]] for lesions above the vein of Galen.

### Callosal gliomas
Butterfly gliomas cross the corpus callosum between hemispheres via callosal fibers. The pericallosal arteries are displaced and may be encased.

### Callosotomy for epilepsy
Anterior two-thirds callosotomy disconnects bihemispheric seizure spread (drop attacks). Performed via anterior [[interhemispheric-approach]].

### Landmark for ventricular localization
The callosum is the key landmark for entering the ventricle during transcortical or transcallosal approaches — its white color contrasts with the ependymal surface of the ventricle below.

## Relations

- Roof of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|lateral ventricles]]
- [[anterior-cerebral-artery]] (pericallosal, callosomarginal) courses on its surface — see [[anterior-circulation]]
- [[posterior-cerebral-artery]] (splenial branch) supplies the splenium — see [[posterior-circulation]]
- [[cerebral-veins]] (internal cerebral veins, vein of Galen) course beneath the splenium
- Separated from the cingulate gyrus by the callosal sulcus
- Fornix attached to undersurface — pathway from [[hippocampus]] ([[cerebrum]]) to mamillary bodies
- Septum pellucidum spans between callosum and fornix, separating the lateral ventricles
- Genu related to [[optic-chiasm]] and lamina terminalis anteriorly
- Splenium related to [[tentorial-incisura]] (posterior incisural space) and pineal
- Accessed via [[interhemispheric-approach]] — see [[surgical-approaches-overview]]
- Part of the [[cerebrum]] white matter system
