# Insula

The insula (island of Reil) is a deep cortical lobe hidden within the [[sylvian-fissure]], entirely covered by the frontal, parietal, and temporal opercula. It is the cortical territory most intimately related to the [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]] and is a critical landmark in sylvian fissure surgery, insular glioma resection, and epilepsy surgery.

## Surface Anatomy

The insula is roughly triangular, with its apex (limen insulae) directed anteroinferiorly:

- **Limen insulae** — the anteroinferior apex where the insular cortex meets the anterior perforated substance; the [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]] M1 segment bifurcates at or near the limen
- **Central insular sulcus** — divides the insula into an anterior part (short gyri) and a posterior part (long gyri)
- **Short gyri (3–4)** — anterior to the central sulcus, covered by the frontal operculum
- **Long gyri (2)** — posterior to the central sulcus, covered by the frontoparietal and temporal opercula
- **Circular sulcus** — the circumferential sulcus separating the insula from the surrounding opercula; the MCA branches exit from beneath the opercula at this sulcus

## Vascular Relations

### Middle cerebral artery ([[middle-cerebral-artery]])
The insula is the territory most intimately related to the MCA:
- **M1 (sphenoidal)** — courses laterally in the sphenoidal compartment of the [[sylvian-fissure]] toward the limen insulae
- **M2 (insular)** — branches course on the insular surface within the operculoinsular compartment; this is the most surgically accessible MCA segment during sylvian fissure splitting
- **M3 (opercular)** — branches course along the inner opercular surface before emerging as M4 cortical branches

### Lenticulostriate arteries
Arise from the M1 segment and pass superiorly through the anterior perforated substance (at the limen insulae) to supply the basal ganglia and internal capsule. These critical perforators must be preserved during insular surgery — their injury causes contralateral hemiplegia.

### Venous drainage
- **Superficial sylvian vein** — courses in the sylvian fissure along the insular surface; drains to the [[dural-venous-sinuses|sphenoparietal sinus]] or [[cavernous-sinus]]
- **Deep insular veins** — drain medially toward the [[cerebral-veins|basal vein of Rosenthal]]

## Deep Relations

The insula overlies critical deep structures separated by the **extreme capsule**, **claustrum**, and **external capsule**:
- **Putamen** — immediately deep to the external capsule
- **Globus pallidus** — medial to the putamen
- **Internal capsule** — the white matter corridor carrying corticospinal and thalamocortical fibers; its posterior limb is supplied by lenticulostriate arteries and the [[anterior-choroidal-artery]]
- **[[thalamus]]** — medial to the internal capsule

## Surgical Significance

### Sylvian fissure splitting ([[pterional-approach]])
The insula is exposed by splitting the [[sylvian-fissure]] and retracting the opercula. The M2 branches coursing on the insular surface are the first arteries encountered. The arachnoid of the sylvian cistern must be carefully opened to avoid avulsing bridging veins.

### Insular gliomas
Low-grade gliomas frequently arise in the insula. Resection requires navigating between the M2 branches on the insular surface and the lenticulostriate perforators arising from M1 at the limen insulae. The **posterior insular point** (junction of the circular sulcus with the posterior Sylvian point) marks the limit of safe posterior resection.

### Epilepsy surgery
The insula is increasingly recognized as an epileptogenic zone, particularly in drug-resistant focal epilepsy. Depth electrodes (stereo-EEG) target the insular cortex for mapping.

### Transsylvian approach
The operative corridor through the [[sylvian-fissure]] accesses the insula, the [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]] bifurcation, and deep structures (basal ganglia, internal capsule). Used for insular tumors, MCA aneurysms, and deep hemorrhages.

## Relations

- Hidden within the [[sylvian-fissure]], covered by opercula of [[cerebrum]]
- [[middle-cerebral-artery]] (M1-M3) courses on and around the insular surface — see [[anterior-circulation]]
- Lenticulostriate arteries arise at the limen insulae from M1
- Deep to the insula: putamen, globus pallidus, internal capsule, [[thalamus]]
- [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] supplies adjacent structures (internal capsule, optic tract)
- Superficial sylvian vein drains along the insular surface to [[dural-venous-sinuses]]
- Accessed via [[pterional-approach]] (sylvian fissure splitting)
- See [[surgical-approaches-overview]] for approach selection
