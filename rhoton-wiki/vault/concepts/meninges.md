# Meninges

The meninges are the three-layered membrane system (dura mater, arachnoid, pia mater) surrounding the brain and spinal cord. In Rhoton's microsurgical anatomy, the meninges define surgical corridors: the dura is the first layer opened in every craniotomy, the arachnoid membranes compartmentalize the [[basal-cisterns]], and the pia mater forms the boundary between the subarachnoid space and the brain parenchyma.

## Dura Mater

The outermost, thickest meningeal layer. Composed of two fused layers (periosteal and meningeal) that separate at specific locations to form the [[dural-venous-sinuses]].

### Dural folds
The meningeal layer forms inward reflections that partition the intracranial cavity:

- **Falx cerebri** — midline vertical partition between the cerebral hemispheres; attached to the crista galli anteriorly and the internal occipital protuberance posteriorly; contains the [[dural-venous-sinuses|superior sagittal sinus]] (superior margin), inferior sagittal sinus (inferior free edge), and straight sinus (junction with tentorium)
- **Tentorium cerebelli** — horizontal tent separating the [[cerebrum]] from the [[cerebellum]] and [[posterior-fossa]]; the free edge forms the [[tentorial-incisura]] around the [[brainstem]]; attached laterally to the petrous ridge of the [[temporal-bone]]; contains the transverse sinus in its attached margin
- **Falx cerebelli** — small midline fold below the tentorium between the cerebellar hemispheres; contains the occipital sinus
- **Diaphragma sellae** — small fold roofing the [[sellar-region]] over the [[pituitary-gland]]; perforated by the infundibulum

### Dural innervation
- Supratentorial dura — [[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]] (meningeal branches of V1, V2, V3)
- Posterior fossa dura — [[lower-cranial-nerves|CN X]] (vagus meningeal branch), C1-C3 meningeal branches
- Middle meningeal artery supplies the dura of the middle fossa

## Arachnoid Membrane

The middle meningeal layer — a thin, avascular membrane that bridges over sulci and fissures, creating the subarachnoid space filled with CSF.

### Outer arachnoid
Lies against the inner surface of the dura. At specific locations, the arachnoid extends inward to form membranes that compartmentalize the subarachnoid space into the [[basal-cisterns]].

### Liliequist's membrane
The most important arachnoid membrane in microsurgery. Arises from the arachnoid resting on the dorsum sellae and splits into:
- **Diencephalic membrane** — a complete membrane attaching to the mamillary bodies; separates the chiasmatic and interpeduncular cisterns
- **Mesencephalic membrane** — attaches at the midbrain-pons junction; forms an incomplete wall between the interpeduncular and prepontine cisterns with an opening through which the [[basilar-artery]] ascends

Opening Liliequist's membrane releases CSF from the interpeduncular cistern, providing brain relaxation during [[pterional-approach]] surgery.

### Arachnoid cuffs
The arachnoid forms sleeves around cranial nerves as they exit the brain:
- The [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] arachnoid cuff is the convergence point for multiple membranes (medial/lateral carotid, mesencephalic, diencephalic, pontomesencephalic membranes)
- These cuffs must be opened during microvascular decompression to expose the nerve root entry zone

### Cisternal membranes ([[basal-cisterns]])
Arachnoid membranes define the boundaries between cisterns:
- **Anterior pontine membrane** — between prepontine and [[cerebellopontine-cistern]]
- **Lateral pontomesencephalic membrane** — between ambient and [[cerebellopontine-cistern|cerebellopontine cisterns]]
- **Lateral pontomedullary membrane** — between cerebellomedullary and [[cerebellopontine-cistern]]
- **Medial pontomedullary membrane** — between premedullary and prepontine cisterns

## Pia Mater

The innermost meningeal layer — a thin, vascular membrane that closely adheres to the brain surface, dipping into every sulcus and fissure.

### Surgical significance
- **Pial boundary** — tumor-brain interface. Well-defined pial planes allow safe dissection in meningiomas and extra-axial tumors
- **Pial arteries** — cortical branches of [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]], [[anterior-cerebral-artery|ACA]], [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] course in the subpial space
- **Tela choroidea** — double layer of pia forming the base of the [[choroid-plexus]]; roofs the third ventricle ([[lateral-and-third-ventricles]]) and the inferior half of the [[fourth-ventricle]]
- **Velum interpositum** — the space between the two pia layers of the tela choroidea in the third ventricle roof; contains the internal cerebral veins ([[vein-of-galen]])

## Surgical Significance

### Dural opening
The dura is opened in every craniotomy as the first step to accessing the brain. Dural opening technique (C-shaped, cruciate, or linear) depends on the approach. The dura is reapproximated (primary closure or graft) to prevent CSF leak.

### Arachnoid dissection
Sharp arachnoid dissection is the foundation of microsurgical technique. Opening cisterns releases CSF (brain relaxation) and exposes arteries, veins, and cranial nerves within protective arachnoid planes. Rhoton's work defined these cisterns and membranes.

### Meningiomas
Tumors arising from meningothelial cells of the arachnoid. Attached to the dura with a dural tail. May arise from any dural surface — convexity, falx, tentorium, skull base, [[sphenoid-bone]] (sphenoid wing meningiomas), [[sellar-region]] (tuberculum sellae), [[foramen-magnum]], etc.

## Relations

- Dura contains [[dural-venous-sinuses]] (superior sagittal, transverse, sigmoid, straight, cavernous, petrosal)
- Tentorium divides supratentorial ([[cerebrum]]) from infratentorial ([[posterior-fossa]], [[cerebellum]])
- [[tentorial-incisura]] — the opening in the tentorium around the [[brainstem]]
- Arachnoid membranes define [[basal-cisterns]] — the surgical corridors
- Liliequist's membrane between chiasmatic and interpeduncular cisterns — opened in [[pterional-approach]]
- Tela choroidea contains [[vein-of-galen]] (internal cerebral veins) and attaches [[choroid-plexus]]
- Pia covers all cortical surfaces of [[cerebrum]], [[cerebellum]], [[brainstem]]
- Falx cerebri related to [[interhemispheric-approach]] and [[herniation-syndromes|subfalcine herniation]]
- Diaphragma sellae roofs the [[pituitary-gland]] in the [[sellar-region]]
- See [[surgical-approaches-overview]] — dural opening is step one in all approaches
