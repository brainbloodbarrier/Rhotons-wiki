# Pulvinar

The **pulvinar** is the most voluminous nucleus of the human [[thalamus]], forming its expansive posterior pole that overhangs the midbrain tectum and dominates the [[pineal-region]]. Its name (Latin "cushion") describes the swollen, pillow-shaped prominence that projects backward from the body of the thalamus over the superior and medial surfaces of the midbrain. Surgically the pulvinar is one of the most accessible but most heavily surrounded deep-brain structures: its surfaces contribute to the walls of the [[quadrigeminal-cistern]], the [[ambient-cistern]], the trigone of the lateral ventricle, and the [[choroidal-fissure]] — making it a constant feature of pineal, atrial, and tectal surgery.

## Subdivisions

The pulvinar is classically divided into four principal subnuclei:

| Subnucleus | Location | Principal connections |
|------------|----------|----------------------|
| **Anterior (oral) pulvinar** | Anterior pole, adjoining the ventral posterolateral nucleus | Parietal association cortex |
| **Medial pulvinar** | Medial, adjacent to the centromedian nucleus | Prefrontal, cingulate, and temporal cortex — higher attention |
| **Lateral pulvinar** | Over the medial geniculate body | Parieto-temporo-occipital association areas |
| **Inferior pulvinar** | Under the lateral geniculate body | Extrastriate visual cortex (V2/V4/MT) — visual attention |

## Surfaces and Relations

The pulvinar is the keystone of the posterior thalamus and displays five working surfaces relevant to surgery:

- **Superior surface** — floor of the ventricular body and trigone, covered by [[choroid-plexus]] of the glomus.
- **Medial surface** — faces the [[quadrigeminal-cistern]], superior colliculus, and the roof of the third ventricle; separated from the opposite pulvinar by the pineal recess.
- **Inferior surface** — the "pulvinar surface" overhanging the midbrain; roof of the [[ambient-cistern]] and [[crural-cistern|crural]] extensions.
- **Lateral surface** — the medial wall of the atrium/trigone of the lateral ventricle.
- **Posterior pole (pulvinar body)** — free surface projecting into the [[quadrigeminal-cistern]], visible through all posterior approaches.

Immediate vascular neighbors are the **medial** and **lateral posterior choroidal arteries** (branches of the [[posterior-cerebral-artery]]), the [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal]] coursing along the crural/ambient cistern beneath the pulvinar, and the [[vein-of-galen]] complex above it.

## Connections

- **Cortico-pulvinar-cortical loops** — reciprocal connections with parietal, temporal, and occipital association cortex make the pulvinar the largest thalamic relay for **higher-order visual attention** and cross-modal integration.
- **Tectopulvinar pathway** — superior colliculus → inferior pulvinar → extrastriate cortex; a subcortical bypass around the [[optic-radiation]] that mediates **blindsight**.
- **Amygdalo-pulvinar connections** — medial pulvinar to amygdala; implicated in emotional salience.
- **No primary sensory input** — unlike the lateral geniculate, the pulvinar receives no direct retinal afferents.

## Surgical Relevance

- **Posterior (occipital-transtentorial, Poppen) approach** — the pulvinar forms the medial wall of the working corridor to the pineal region; the lateral pulvinar must not be retracted.
- **Supracerebellar-infratentorial (Krause) approach** — the inferior pulvinar surface forms the roof of the exposure; the pineal gland is removed from under the apposed pulvinar poles.
- **Transcallosal-interforniceal and posterior transcallosal approaches** — enter the atrium between the pulvinar medially and the [[choroidal-fissure]] laterally.
- **Atrial / trigonal approaches (parietal, intraparietal sulcus, temporal)** — the lateral pulvinar is the medial wall of the trigone; atrial AVMs and meningiomas are dissected off this surface with awareness of the lateral posterior choroidal artery.
- **Pulvinar thalamotomy** — an older target for intractable pain (de-afferentation) and, more recently, interest in visual-attention modulation.

## Clinical Significance

- **Pulvinar sign (MRI)** — bilateral symmetric T2/FLAIR pulvinar hyperintensity is a cardinal imaging feature of **variant Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease**; a similar appearance is seen in Fabry disease and anti-Ma2 encephalitis.
- **Hemineglect and visual attention** — lesions of the lateral/inferior pulvinar produce contralateral visual neglect without a hemianopia.
- **Blindsight** — patients with extensive striate cortex lesions may retain rudimentary visual localization through the tectopulvinar route.
- **Pineal-region and tectal tumors** — pulvinar invasion limits resectability; infiltration posteriorly into the pulvinar typically mandates biopsy and radiation rather than resection.
- **Thalamic pain (Dejerine-Roussy variant)** — occasional contribution from posterior thalamic / pulvinar infarction.

## Relations

- Posterior pole of the [[thalamus]], overhanging the midbrain tectum
- Medial wall of the trigone of the lateral ventricle and forms the floor of the ventricular body
- Faces the [[quadrigeminal-cistern]], [[ambient-cistern]], [[crural-cistern]] across its three external surfaces
- Flanked by the [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal]] (inferior) and [[vein-of-galen]] tributaries (superior)
- Supplied by the medial and lateral posterior choroidal branches of the [[posterior-cerebral-artery]]
