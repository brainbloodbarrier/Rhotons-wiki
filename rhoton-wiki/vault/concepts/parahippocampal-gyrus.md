# Parahippocampal Gyrus

The parahippocampal gyrus (PHG) is the longitudinal gyrus on the medial surface of the temporal lobe that extends from the [[uncus]] anteriorly to the isthmus of the cingulate gyrus posteriorly. It forms the roof of the **collateral sulcus**, the floor of the hippocampal formation, and the inferior wall of the choroidal fissure along its anterior portion. The PHG is central to several anatomic systems — memory, medial-temporal epilepsy surgery, and transtemporal cisternal approaches.

## Surface Anatomy

### Boundaries

- **Superior (medial):** hippocampal sulcus; separates PHG from the [[hippocampus]]
- **Inferior (lateral):** collateral sulcus; separates PHG from the fusiform (occipitotemporal lateral) gyrus
- **Anterior:** merges with the [[uncus]] at the rhinal sulcus — the uncus "caps" the anterior PHG
- **Posterior:** continues as the isthmus of the cingulate gyrus, connecting with the lingual gyrus

### Subdivisions

| Region | Cytoarchitecture | Function |
|--------|------------------|----------|
| **Entorhinal cortex (BA 28, 34)** | Anterior PHG + uncus | Main cortical input–output of hippocampus (memory) |
| **Perirhinal cortex (BA 35, 36)** | Just lateral, around rhinal sulcus | Item recognition, semantic memory |
| **Parahippocampal place area** | Posterior PHG | Scene/spatial recognition |
| **Presubiculum / parasubiculum** | Medial edge, below hippocampus | Memory circuitry |

## Relationships

### Medial / Cisternal

The PHG forms the **inferolateral wall** of the following cisterns (all part of [[basal-cisterns]]):

- **[[crural-cistern]]** — between uncus and cerebral peduncle (anterior)
- **[[ambient-cistern]]** — between PHG and cerebral peduncle (middle)
- **Quadrigeminal cistern** — posterior extension, near isthmus

### Vascular

- **Posterior cerebral artery (P2 segment)** — hooks around the cerebral peduncle and courses over the medial PHG within the ambient cistern; supplies PHG cortical branches (anterior, middle, posterior temporal arteries)
- **Anterior choroidal artery** — cisternal segment passes in the crural cistern adjacent to the anterior PHG
- **Basal vein of Rosenthal** — runs in the ambient cistern alongside PHG

### Ventricular

- The **temporal horn of the lateral ventricle** lies deep to the PHG; its medial wall is formed by the hippocampus, with the PHG just below
- The **[[choroidal-fissure]]** (taenia fimbria–taenia fornicis) forms the superomedial boundary — critical surgical entry point

## Functional Relevance

- **Memory (declarative/episodic):** the entorhinal cortex is the gateway for hippocampal memory circuits — bilateral injury causes severe anterograde amnesia (classic H.M. lesion territory)
- **Epilepsy:** mesial temporal sclerosis (MTS) affects hippocampus + entorhinal PHG; selective amygdalohippocampectomy spares lateral temporal neocortex
- **Visual recognition:** parahippocampal place area contributes to scene memory; lesions cause topographagnosia

## Surgical Relevance

### Selective Amygdalohippocampectomy

- **Transsylvian–transinsular** (Yaşargil): corridor through sylvian fissure, then inferior insular point, into temporal horn; the PHG is approached from within the ventricle
- **Subtemporal** (trans-parahippocampal): retraction of the temporal lobe exposes the medial PHG; incision in the collateral sulcus opens the temporal horn
- **Transcortical through middle/inferior temporal gyrus**: crosses temporal white matter into the temporal horn and PHG

### Anterior Temporal Lobectomy

- **En bloc resection** of anterior ~4–4.5 cm of temporal lobe (dominant) or 5–6 cm (non-dominant)
- PHG is resected back to the level of the midbrain; care is taken not to violate the pia of the ambient cistern (P2 injury risk)
- The **superior temporal gyrus** is the anatomic "ceiling" — PHG and hippocampus are resected beneath

### Transparahippocampal Approach

- A PHG "pial window" in the medial fusiform region enters the ambient and quadrigeminal cisterns for:
  - P2–P3 [[posterior-cerebral-artery]] aneurysms
  - Pineal-region tumors (posterior-supracerebellar alternatives)
  - Tentorial-incisura meningiomas

## Operative Pitfalls

- **CN III injury**: trajectory passes over the edge of the tentorial incisura — oculomotor nerve is adherent to the posterior petroclinoid ligament
- **P2 segment avulsion**: posterior cerebral artery is subpial and tightly adherent to PHG
- **Vein of Labbé sacrifice** (during non-dominant temporal lobectomy): can cause venous infarction of posterior temporal and parietal cortex
- **Visual field deficit**: Meyer's loop (optic radiations) crosses superolateral to the temporal horn — over-aggressive lateral resection produces superior quadrantanopia
