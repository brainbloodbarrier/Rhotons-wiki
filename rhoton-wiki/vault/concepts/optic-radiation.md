# Optic Radiation

The optic radiation (geniculocalcarine tract) is the final relay of the visual pathway, projecting from the [[thalamus|lateral geniculate body]] to the primary visual cortex along the calcarine fissure. Its course through the [[temporal-bone|temporal]] and parietal lobes makes it vulnerable during surgery for [[hippocampus|mesial temporal]] pathology, insular tumors, and deep [[white-matter-tracts|white matter]] lesions.

## Anatomy

### Origin and Termination
- Arises from the **lateral geniculate nucleus** (LGN) of the [[thalamus]]
- Terminates in the **primary visual cortex** (V1, area 17) along the calcarine fissure of the [[cerebral-lobes|occipital lobe]]
- Carries retinotopic organization throughout its course

### Three Bundles
1. **Anterior bundle (Meyer's loop)** — carries inferior retinal fibers (superior visual field); loops forward into the [[cerebral-lobes|temporal lobe]] roof of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|temporal horn]] before curving posteriorly
2. **Central bundle** — carries macular fibers; courses directly lateral to the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|atrium]] of the lateral ventricle
3. **Posterior bundle** — carries superior retinal fibers (inferior visual field); courses through the [[cerebral-lobes|parietal lobe]] to the upper calcarine lip

### Meyer's Loop
- The anterior extent of Meyer's loop is the most surgically critical landmark
- Tip lies **approximately 25-30 mm posterior to the temporal pole** (range 22-37 mm)
- Courses over the roof of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|temporal horn]]
- Lies lateral to the [[hippocampus|hippocampal tail]] and temporal horn
- Injury produces a **contralateral superior quadrantanopia** ("pie in the sky" defect)

## Relationships

### Lateral
- [[insula|Insular cortex]] and extreme/external capsule lie lateral to the radiation at its anterior extent
- Sagittal stratum of [[white-matter-tracts|association fibers]] intermingles with radiation fibers

### Medial
- [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|Lateral ventricle]] — temporal horn (anterior), atrium and occipital horn (posterior)
- [[choroid-plexus]] and [[hippocampus]] lie medial in the temporal horn region

### Vascular Supply
- Anterior portion: [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] and [[middle-cerebral-artery|lateral lenticulostriate]] branches
- Central portion: deep branches of [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]]
- Posterior portion: [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] calcarine and parieto-occipital branches

## Surgical Significance

### Temporal Lobe Surgery
- **Anterior temporal lobectomy** — resection posterior to 4.5 cm from temporal pole (dominant) or 5.5 cm (non-dominant) minimizes Meyer's loop injury
- **Amygdalohippocampectomy** — the [[hippocampus|transsylvian]] or subtemporal selective approach avoids the radiation better than anterior lobectomy
- **Temporal horn access** — the radiation forms the roof; entry through the inferior temporal sulcus is safest

### Insular Surgery
- The radiation lies deep to the [[insula|posterior insula]]
- [[sylvian-fissure|Transsylvian]] approaches to insular gliomas risk the posterior radiation
- Subcortical stimulation mapping identifies the radiation intraoperatively

### Approaches at Risk
- [[pterional-approach]] and [[orbitozygomatic-approach]] — temporal extension
- [[subtemporal-approach]] — deep lateral trajectory
- Transcortical approaches to the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|atrium]]

## Visual Field Defects by Location

| Lesion site | Deficit |
|---|---|
| Meyer's loop (temporal) | Contralateral superior quadrantanopia |
| Central bundle (parietal) | Contralateral inferior quadrantanopia |
| Complete radiation | Contralateral homonymous hemianopia |
| Calcarine cortex | Macular sparing hemianopia |
