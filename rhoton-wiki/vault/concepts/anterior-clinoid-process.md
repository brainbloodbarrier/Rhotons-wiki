# Anterior Clinoid Process

The anterior clinoid process (ACP) is a bony projection at the medial end of the lesser wing of the [[sphenoid-bone]], forming a critical landmark for the paraclinoid [[internal-carotid-artery]], [[optic-nerve]], and [[cavernous-sinus]]. Its removal (clinoidectomy) is essential for exposing the clinoid segment of the ICA and for clipping paraclinoid aneurysms.

## Osseous Anatomy

The ACP is located at the medial terminus of the sphenoid ridge, which projects into the [[sylvian-fissure]] to separate the frontal and temporal lobes. Key bony relationships:

- **Optic strut** — the bridge of bone connecting the ACP inferiorly to the sphenoid body, separating the lateral margin of the optic canal from the medial edge of the superior orbital fissure
- **Lesser sphenoid wing** — extends laterally from the ACP as the sphenoid ridge
- **Middle clinoid process** — situated lateral to the tuberculum sellae; an inconstant bony projection that may form a caroticoclinoid ring with the ACP
- **Posterior clinoid process** — located at the superolateral margin of the dorsum sellae

## Dural Relationships

### Upper Dural Ring
The dura extending medially from the **top** of the ACP forms the upper dural ring around the [[internal-carotid-artery]]. This ring creates a tight collar around the ICA and marks the transition from the clinoid segment to the supraclinoid (subarachnoid) ICA.

### Lower Dural Ring
The dura extending medially from the **lower margin** of the ACP forms the lower dural ring, marking the inferior boundary of the clinoid segment. This layer separates the clinoid process from the upper surface of the [[oculomotor-nerve]].

### Falciform Process
A thin fold of dura extending medially from the ACP to the tuberculum sellae, covering the [[optic-nerve]] immediately proximal to the optic foramen. Division of the falciform process is a key step in mobilizing the optic nerve during paraclinoid surgery.

### Carotid Cave
A narrow depression in the dura at the anteromedial aspect of the ICA where the upper dural ring hugs the artery. The cave extends a variable distance below the upper ring and is a site for carotid cave aneurysms — distinct from clinoid segment aneurysms.

## The Clinoid Segment of the ICA

The clinoid segment is the portion of the [[internal-carotid-artery]] situated medial to the ACP, between the upper and lower dural rings. It is exposed by removing the anterior clinoid process (clinoidectomy). This segment represents the junction of the intracavernous and subarachnoid portions of the ICA.

- The [[ophthalmic-artery]] most commonly arises anterior to the tip of the ACP, approximately 5 mm medial to the clinoid process
- Superior hypophyseal arteries arise distal to the ophthalmic origin

## Clinoidectomy

Removal of the ACP is performed to expose the clinoid segment of the ICA for paraclinoid aneurysm surgery or for tumor removal in the paraclinoid region.

### Indications
- Paraclinoid (ophthalmic, superior hypophyseal, carotid cave) aneurysm clipping
- Clinoidal [[meninges|meningiomas]]
- Proximal control of the ICA
- Decompression of the [[optic-nerve]] in the optic canal

### Technique Considerations
- The optic strut must be drilled to fully mobilize the optic nerve
- The upper and lower dural rings are divided to mobilize the ICA
- Pneumatization of the ACP (from the sphenoid sinus) occurs in some patients, requiring sealing to prevent CSF leak
- The [[oculomotor-nerve]] courses immediately beneath the lower dural ring
