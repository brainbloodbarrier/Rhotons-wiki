---Cerebral Veins

The cerebral veins are divided into a **superficial group** draining cortical surfaces and a **deep group** draining deep white and gray matter. Unlike the arteries, the veins are often more adherent to the brain and provide more accurate localizing information on imaging. Sacrifice of major venous trunks can cause hemiplegia, coma, or death despite extensive anastomoses.

## Superficial Veins

The superficial veins collect into four groups of [[bridging-veins]]:

### Superior Sagittal Group
Drains into the superior sagittal sinus. Collects from the superior medial and lateral surfaces of frontal, parietal, and occipital lobes. Each bridging vein has a 1–2 cm free segment in the subdural space — vulnerable to tearing in traumatic subdural hematoma.

### Sphenoidal Group
Drains into sphenoparietal or [[cavernous-sinus]]. Formed by superficial sylvian veins draining the areas adjoining the [[sylvian-fissure]].

### Tentorial Group
Drains into tentorial sinuses or transverse/superior petrosal sinuses. Collects from the lateral temporal and basal temporal/occipital surfaces. Includes temporobasal and occipitobasal veins.

### Falcine Group
Empties into the inferior sagittal or straight sinus. Includes cortical veins reaching the internal cerebral and great veins.

## Deep Veins

The [[deep-venous-system]] drains the deep gray and white matter through channels coursing in the ventricular walls and [[basal-cisterns]]:

- **[[internal-cerebral-veins]]** (paired) — course in the roof of the third ventricle; formed by the junction of the thalamostriate and septal veins at the venous angle (behind the foramen of Monro)
- **Basal veins (of Rosenthal)** — encircle the midbrain through the crural and ambient cisterns; drain the medial [[cerebral-lobes|temporal lobe]], [[basal-ganglia]], and [[insula]]
- **Great vein (of Galen)** — formed by the junction of the paired internal cerebral veins; drains into the straight sinus at the tentorial apex

## Surgical Relevance

- Bridging veins over the central sulcus must be preserved during parasagittal approaches
- The vein of Labbe (inferior anastomotic vein) is critical to preserve during temporal lobe surgery — its sacrifice causes venous infarction
- The deep venous system (internal cerebral veins, [[vein-of-galen]]) poses the major obstacle to [[pineal-region]] approaches
- Subtemporal approaches require awareness of bridging veins below the posterior temporal lobe
- Ventricular veins provide larger landmarks than arteries within dilated [[lateral-and-third-ventricles]] (hydrocephalus)
- The [[pterional-approach]] exposes superficial sylvian veins in the [[sylvian-fissure]]
- See also [[posterior-fossa-veins]] for the infratentorial venous system

## Relations

- Superficial veins drain into [[dural-venous-sinuses|dural sinuses]] (superior sagittal, transverse, [[cavernous-sinus]])
- Deep veins drain into internal cerebral veins → great vein of Galen → straight sinus
- [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal|Basal vein]] courses alongside [[posterior-cerebral-artery]] in the [[ambient-cistern]]
- Related to ventricular anatomy (see ventricles chapter)
