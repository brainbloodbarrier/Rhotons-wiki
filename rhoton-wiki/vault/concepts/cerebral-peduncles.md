# Cerebral Peduncles

The cerebral peduncles (crura cerebri, basis pedunculi) are the paired ventrolateral columns of the **midbrain** that diverge from the pons to enter the cerebral hemispheres. They carry the major descending motor projections from cortex and are the anatomic floor of multiple surgical corridors around the tentorial incisura. Each peduncle is a roughly triangular wedge between the interpeduncular fossa (medial) and the uncus/parahippocampal gyrus (lateral).

## Gross Anatomy

### Position

- Emerge from the pontomesencephalic junction
- Course superiorly, laterally, and slightly posteriorly to enter the base of the cerebral hemisphere
- Disappear from surface view at the optic tract

### Shape and Surfaces

- **Medial surface**: bounds the interpeduncular fossa; grooved by CN III (oculomotor sulcus)
- **Lateral surface**: bounds the crural and ambient cisterns; covered by the [[parahippocampal-gyrus]]
- **Dorsal border**: transition to midbrain tegmentum (posterior to substantia nigra)
- **Ventral apex**: rounded anterior prominence — often called the "crural shoulder"

## Internal Composition (From Medial → Lateral)

The peduncle is arranged **somatotopically** — a principle exploited in functional neurosurgery:

| Region | Fibers |
|--------|--------|
| **Medial 1/5** | Frontopontine fibers (Arnold's bundle) |
| **Middle 3/5** | **Corticospinal tract** — face (most medial) → arm → trunk → leg (most lateral) |
| **Lateral 1/5** | Temporopontine, parieto-occipito-pontine fibers (Türck's bundle) |
| **Corticobulbar** | Interspersed with upper corticospinal fibers (face area) |

Dorsal to the peduncle proper lies the **substantia nigra** (pars compacta and pars reticulata), separated from the peduncle by a sulcus. Dorsal to the nigra is the midbrain tegmentum containing the red nucleus and the oculomotor nuclei.

## Vascular Relationships

- **Posterior cerebral artery (PCA)** — the P1 segment emerges between the peduncles at the interpeduncular fossa; P2 segment hooks around the lateral peduncle in the crural cistern then enters the ambient cistern
- **Superior cerebellar artery (SCA)** — courses below CN III, around the inferior peduncle surface in the pontomesencephalic cistern
- **Basilar artery** — terminates between the peduncles at the interpeduncular fossa ([[basilar-artery]] bifurcation)
- **Perforators to the peduncle**: arise from P1, posterior communicating, and anterior choroidal arteries — infarction produces Weber, Benedikt, or Claude syndromes depending on territory
- **Medial posterior choroidal artery** runs along the dorsal peduncle in the ambient cistern

## Cranial Nerve Relationships

- **CN III (oculomotor)** — emerges from the medial surface of the peduncle into the interpeduncular cistern, runs between SCA (below) and PCA (above) to enter the cavernous sinus roof; see [[oculomotor-nerve]]
- **CN IV (trochlear)** — emerges dorsally below the inferior colliculus, wraps around the peduncle's posterior aspect in the ambient cistern
- **CN II (optic tract)** — overrides the superior peduncle lateral-to-medial as it approaches the LGN

## Clinical and Surgical Syndromes

### Midbrain Stroke Syndromes

| Syndrome | Lesion | Features |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **Weber** | Ventral peduncle + CN III fascicle | Ipsilateral CN III palsy + contralateral hemiparesis |
| **Benedikt** | Tegmentum incl. red nucleus + CN III | Weber + contralateral tremor/ataxia |
| **Claude** | Tegmentum + SCN + red nucleus (not peduncle) | CN III palsy + ipsilateral ataxia |
| **Parinaud** | Dorsal midbrain (not peduncle) | Upgaze paresis, light-near dissociation |

### Uncal Herniation

- The uncus/medial [[parahippocampal-gyrus]] compresses the **lateral cerebral peduncle**
- Produces an ipsilateral corticospinal-tract injury → **contralateral hemiparesis** classically, but can produce **ipsilateral ("Kernohan's notch")** hemiparesis from contralateral peduncle impingement on the tentorial edge

## Surgical Relevance

### Transcrural / Peduncular Approaches

- **Cavernous malformations of the peduncle**: approached via a pial incision in the **anterolateral sulcus** (between the lateral peduncle surface and the substantia nigra) — the "safe entry zone" that minimizes corticospinal injury
- **Anterior mesencephalic cavernoma**: via orbitozygomatic transsylvian-transcrural corridor
- **Ambient-cistern lesions** (P2 aneurysms, tentorial meningiomas): exposed over the lateral peduncle surface via subtemporal or anterior-temporal corridors

### Anterior Temporal Lobectomy

- Posterior-medial resection border is defined by the visible **cerebral peduncle** through the ambient-cistern pia
- Do not violate the pia — P2 [[posterior-cerebral-artery]] lies directly on the peduncle surface

### [[tentorial-incisura]] Surgery

- The cerebral peduncle is the floor of the posterior incisural space
- Pineal-region tumors often displace peduncle laterally; approach via supracerebellar-infratentorial or occipital-transtentorial routes must avoid peduncle compression

## Safe Entry Zones

Summarized in functional neurosurgery literature:

| Zone | Location | Target |
|------|----------|--------|
| **Perioculomotor** | Medial peduncle at CN III exit | Medial mesencephalic cavernoma |
| **Anterolateral sulcus** | Between lateral peduncle and substantia nigra | Lateral peduncular cavernoma |
| **Lateral mesencephalic sulcus** | Between peduncle (anterior) and tegmentum (posterior) | Deep mesencephalic lesions |
