# Thalamus

The thalamus is the central relay station of the brain, located at the core of the [[cerebrum]] between the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|lateral ventricles]] and the third ventricle. Each lateral ventricle wraps around it in a C-shape. The thalamus receives virtually all sensory, motor, and limbic information and relays it to the cortex. Its vascular supply — the thalamoperforating and [[thalamogeniculate-arteries]] — arises from the [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] and represents one of the most critical perforator systems in neurosurgery.

## Surface Relations to the Ventricles

The thalamus is the dominant structure in the walls of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|lateral ventricle]]:
- **Superior surface** — forms the floor of the body of the lateral ventricle
- **Posterior surface (pulvinar)** — forms the anterior wall of the atrium
- **Inferior surface** — situated at the medial edge of the roof of the temporal horn
- **Medial surface** — forms the lateral wall of the third ventricle

The [[basal-ganglia|caudate nucleus]] wraps around the thalamus: superolateral in the body, posterolateral in the atrium, and inferolateral in the temporal horn. The fornix, another C-shaped structure, courses between the caudate and thalamus along the **choroidal fissure**.

## Choroidal Fissure

The choroidal fissure is the cleft between the fornix and the thalamus through which the [[choroid-plexus]] attaches. It extends along the entire C-shape of the lateral ventricle. Opening the choroidal fissure is a key step in the **transchoroidal approach** to the third ventricle — it provides access without cortical incision and without splitting the fornix.

The [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] enters the ventricle through the choroidal fissure at the **inferior choroidal point** (posterior to the head of the [[hippocampus]]).

## Vascular Supply

### [[thalamoperforating-arteries]]
Arise predominantly from the P1 segment of the [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]]. An average of 4 (up to 12) branches arise from the superior and posterior P1 surfaces, course superiorly, and terminate in the [[interpeduncular-cistern|interpeduncular fossa]], posterior perforated substance, cerebral peduncle, mamillary bodies, and posterior midbrain. The largest P1 branch is a thalamoperforating artery in 42% of hemispheres. These arteries also arise from the upper centimeter of the [[basilar-artery]] (average 8 branches).

### Thalamogeniculate arteries
Arise from the P2 segment of the [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] (approximately equally from P2A and P2P). Pass upward through the lateral and medial geniculate bodies to reach the thalamus and posterior limb of the internal capsule.

### [[posterior-choroidal-arteries]]
- **Medial posterior choroidal (MPChA)** — arises from P2A, courses through the [[ambient-cistern]] to the roof of the third ventricle and [[pineal-region]]
- **Lateral posterior choroidal (LPChA)** — arises from P2P, courses through the choroidal fissure around the pulvinar to the choroid plexus in the temporal horn and atrium

### Anterior supply
The thalamotuberal (premamillary) artery from the [[posterior-communicating-artery|PCoA]] supplies the anterior thalamus. The [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] supplies the lateral geniculate body and parts of the posterior limb of the internal capsule.

## Surgical Significance

### Thalamic tumors
Intrinsic thalamic gliomas are among the most challenging surgical targets. Approaches include:
- **Transcortical-transventricular** — through the lateral ventricle body
- **Transcallosal-transchoroidal** — via [[interhemispheric-approach]], opening the choroidal fissure
- **[[subtemporal-approach]]** — for posterolateral thalamic lesions extending into the ambient cistern
- **[[supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach]]** — for posteroinferior thalamic/pulvinar lesions

### Perforator preservation
The thalamoperforating arteries are the principal constraint during [[basilar-artery]] apex [[aneurysm-surgery]] via the [[pterional-approach]]. Injury causes thalamic infarction → contralateral sensory loss, memory deficits, altered consciousness.

### Deep brain stimulation
The ventral intermediate nucleus (Vim) of the thalamus is the target for DBS in essential tremor. The anterior nucleus is targeted for epilepsy.

## Relations

- Central structure of the [[cerebrum]], surrounded by [[lateral-and-third-ventricles]]
- Supplied by [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] (thalamoperforating, thalamogeniculate, posterior choroidal) — see [[posterior-circulation]]
- [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] supplies lateral geniculate body — see [[anterior-circulation]]
- [[posterior-communicating-artery]] gives thalamotuberal branches
- [[basilar-artery]] apex [[perforating-arteries|perforators]] overlap with thalamoperforating territory
- [[choroidal-fissure]] separates thalamus from [[fornix]] — transchoroidal surgical corridor
- [[internal-capsule]] — posterior limb lies lateral to thalamus, supplied by thalamogeniculate arteries
- [[optic-radiation]] — retrolenticular capsule carries geniculocalcarine fibers from LGN
- Pulvinar related to the [[tentorial-incisura]] ([[posterior-incisural-space|posterior incisural space]])
- [[optic-chiasm]] → [[optic-tract]] → lateral geniculate body (thalamic relay for vision)
- Accessible via [[interhemispheric-approach]], [[subtemporal-approach]], [[supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach]]
- See [[cranial-nerves-overview]] — thalamic relay for all sensory pathways
- Related to [[brainstem]] (diencephalon is the rostral brainstem transition)
