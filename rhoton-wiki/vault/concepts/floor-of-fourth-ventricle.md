# Floor of the Fourth Ventricle

The **floor of the fourth ventricle** is the rhomboid (diamond-shaped) dorsal surface of the pons and medulla, covered by a thin ependymal layer and stretched between the [[superior-cerebellar-peduncle|superior]] and inferior cerebellar peduncles as they converge on the cerebellum. It is one of the densest neural surfaces in the human nervous system — every square millimeter lies over a cranial nerve nucleus or a long tract — and is therefore the anatomical template on which all **brainstem safe entry zones** are defined. Surgical exposure is achieved through the [[telovelar-approach]] via the [[cerebellomedullary-fissure]].

## Topography

The rhomboid fossa is divided into a **superior (pontine) triangle** and an **inferior (medullary) triangle** by the **striae medullares**, bundles of transverse arcuate fibers running laterally into the [[cerebellum]] via the inferior cerebellar peduncle. A **median sulcus** bisects the floor longitudinally; on each side the following landmarks lie in a consistent order from medial to lateral and rostral to caudal:

| Landmark | Level | Underlying structure |
|----------|-------|---------------------|
| **Medial eminence** | Pons | Deep to it lies the paramedian tegmental motor column |
| **Facial colliculus** | Mid-pons | [[abducens-nerve|CN VI]] nucleus + overlying genu of the [[facial-nerve|CN VII]] fibers |
| **Sulcus limitans** | Full length | Developmental motor/sensory boundary |
| **Locus coeruleus** (pigmented spot) | Rostral pons | Noradrenergic arousal nucleus |
| **Vestibular area** | Pontomedullary junction, lateral | Vestibular nuclei |
| **Hypoglossal trigone** | Medulla, paramedian | Hypoglossal (CN XII) nucleus |
| **Vagal trigone (ala cinerea)** | Medulla, lateral to XII | Dorsal motor nucleus of [[vagus-nerve|CN X]] |
| **Area postrema** | Inferior apex, at the obex | Chemoreceptor trigger zone |
| **Obex** | Caudal apex | Junction of the ventricle with the central canal |

## Surgical Safe Entry Zones

Trans-floor access to intrinsic brainstem lesions uses four classical zones that avoid the critical cranial-nerve nuclei and long tracts:

1. **Suprafacial triangle** — between the [[facial-nerve|facial colliculus]] (inferior border), the [[superior-cerebellar-peduncle]] (lateral), and the median sulcus (medial). Used for upper dorsolateral pontine cavernomas.
2. **Infrafacial triangle** — between the facial colliculus (superior), the striae medullares (inferior), and the sulcus limitans (lateral). Used for lower pontine and tegmental cavernomas; risks include [[abducens-nerve|CN VI]] palsy and gaze paresis.
3. **Median pontine (supracollicular) zone** — a longitudinal midline raphe incision between the medial eminences, reserved for strictly midline lesions.
4. **Area acustica / peri-trigeminal zone** — lateral entries for ventrolateral pontine lesions; respect the [[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]] root and its deep nuclei.

Intraoperative **electrical mapping** of the floor (facial colliculus → evoked facial EMG; hypoglossal trigone → tongue EMG; vagal trigone → vocalis EMG) defines each nucleus before incision, as the ependymal surface alone does not reliably localize them.

## Approaches and Exposure

- **[[telovelar-approach]]** — the workhorse for floor exposure; opens the [[cerebellomedullary-fissure]] without splitting the vermis, providing access from the obex to the cerebral aqueduct.
- **Trans-tonsillar / supracerebellar infratentorial variants** — supplement telovelar exposure for the superior recess and obex, respectively.
- **Cerebellomesencephalic corridor** — the [[superior-cerebellar-peduncle]] bounds the lateral recess of the upper floor; combined exposure reaches the rostral apex at the [[cerebral-aqueduct]].

## Clinical Significance

- **Cavernous malformations of the brainstem** — surgical removal is guided by the "two-point method" referenced to the floor's safe entry zones and the lesion's most superficial emergence on imaging.
- **Ependymomas and medulloblastomas** — floor invasion by midline posterior fossa tumors mandates subtotal resection at the colliculus to preserve [[facial-nerve]] function; postoperative deficits localize to specific landmarks.
- **Floor-hemorrhage syndromes** — strokes of the pontine tegmentum produce the "one-and-a-half" syndrome (horizontal gaze palsy + INO) when the abducens nucleus and adjacent MLF in the facial colliculus are involved.
- **Area postrema syndrome** — intractable vomiting and hiccups; a cardinal presentation of neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD).

## Relations

- Dorsal surface of the pons (superior triangle) and medulla (inferior triangle)
- Bounded rostrally by the [[cerebral-aqueduct]] and caudally by the obex
- Roof above = [[superior-cerebellar-peduncle|superior medullary velum]] + [[cerebellum|cerebellum/tela]] + inferior medullary velum
- Flanked laterally by the three [[brainstem]] peduncles that converge on the [[cerebellum]]
- Ependymal covering only — no pial intervening layer between the ventricle and the nuclei beneath
