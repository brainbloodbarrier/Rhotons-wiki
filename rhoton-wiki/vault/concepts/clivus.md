# Clivus

The clivus is the sloping midline bony surface extending from the dorsum sellae to the anterior margin of the [[foramen-magnum]]. It forms the central axis of the posterior [[cranial-base]], separating the nasopharynx anteriorly from the [[brainstem]] and [[basilar-artery]] posteriorly.

## Osseous Composition

The clivus is formed by two bones joined at the spheno-occipital synchondrosis:

| Segment | Bone | Relations |
|---|---|---|
| **Upper clivus** | Body of [[sphenoid-bone]] | Continuous with dorsum sellae, posterior clinoid processes; [[sellar-region]] above |
| **Lower clivus** | Basal part of occipital bone | Slopes to [[foramen-magnum]]; [[occipital-condyle|occipital condyles]] laterally |

The spheno-occipital synchondrosis fuses between ages 12–20 and is a common site for chordomas.

## Relationships

### Anterior (Exocranial)
- **Nasopharynx** — the nasopharyngeal roof rests against the upper clivus and floor of the [[paranasal-sinuses|sphenoid sinus]]
- **Longus capitis muscle** — attaches to the lower clivus, separating it from the nasopharyngeal wall
- **Pharyngobasilar fascia** — anchors pharyngeal musculature to the clival periosteum

### Posterior (Endocranial)
- **[[basilar-artery]]** — ascends along the clival dura to bifurcate at the upper clivus into the [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCAs]]
- **[[abducens-nerve]]** — pierces the clival dura to enter Dorello's canal along the petroclival fissure
- **[[brainstem]]** — pons lies directly posterior, separated by the prepontine [[basal-cisterns|cistern]]
- **Basilar venous plexus** — sits on the clival dura between the layers of the [[meninges]], connecting the [[cavernous-sinus|cavernous sinuses]] and inferior petrosal sinuses

### Lateral
- **Petroclival fissure** — junction of the [[petrous-apex]] of the [[temporal-bone]] with the clivus; the inferior petrosal sinus courses along this fissure
- **[[meckels-cave]]** — trigeminal ganglion housing, lateral to the upper clivus
- **Petrous apex** — interposed between the greater sphenoid wing and the clival part of the occipital bone

## Surgical Approaches to the Clivus

### Anterior (Ventral) Approaches

| Approach | Target | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| [[transsphenoidal-approach]] (extended endonasal) | Upper and middle clivus | Through [[paranasal-sinuses|sphenoid sinus]]; direct midline access |
| Transoral/transpalatal | Lower clivus, [[craniocervical-junction]] | Pharyngeal route; limited lateral access |
| Transmaxillary | Middle and lower clivus | Maxillary antrostomy with clivus exposure |

### Lateral Approaches

| Approach | Target | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| [[anterior-petrosectomy]] (Kawase) | Petroclival region, upper clivus | Petrous apex drilling exposes lateral clivus |
| [[posterior-petrosectomy]] | Petroclival region | Retrolabyrinthine/translabyrinthine access |
| Combined petrosal | Full petroclival exposure | Anterior + posterior petrosectomy |
| [[subtemporal-approach]] | Upper clivus laterally | Tentorial division for lateral access |

### Posterior Approaches

| Approach | Target | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| [[retrosigmoid-approach]] | Lateral clival dura | CP angle corridor, limited clival view |
| [[far-lateral-approach]] | Lower clivus, [[foramen-magnum]] | Condylar drilling extends clival exposure |

## Clival Bone Thickness

The clivus is one of the thickest bones of the skull base. The thickest bone is found at the clivus and tuberculum sellae. This thickness is surgically relevant during drilling for endonasal and transoral approaches.

## Clinical Significance

### Clival Tumors
- **Chordomas** — arise from notochordal remnants in the spheno-occipital synchondrosis; the most characteristic clival tumor
- **Meningiomas** — petroclival meningiomas arise from the dura of the petroclival fissure; among the most challenging skull base tumors
- **Chondrosarcomas** — arise from the petroclival synchondrosis cartilage

### Vascular Relations
The [[basilar-artery]] and its [[perforating-arteries|perforating branches]] lie directly on the clival dura. Drilling through the clivus during endonasal approaches risks injury to the basilar artery, and the basilar venous plexus causes significant bleeding during clival bone removal.

## Connections

- Superior: dorsum sellae → [[sellar-region]] → [[sphenoid-bone]]
- Inferior: [[foramen-magnum]] → occipital condyles
- Lateral: petroclival fissure → [[temporal-bone|petrous apex]] → [[meckels-cave]]
- Posterior: [[basilar-artery]], [[brainstem]], prepontine [[basal-cisterns|cistern]]
- Anterior: [[paranasal-sinuses|sphenoid sinus]], nasopharynx
- Venous: basilar plexus → [[cavernous-sinus]], [[dural-venous-sinuses|inferior petrosal sinus]]
