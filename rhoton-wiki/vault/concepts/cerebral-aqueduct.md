# Cerebral Aqueduct

The cerebral aqueduct (aqueduct of Sylvius) is the narrow channel within the [[brainstem|midbrain]] connecting the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|third ventricle]] to the [[fourth-ventricle]]. It is the most constricted segment of the ventricular system and the most common site of obstructive hydrocephalus.

## Anatomy

### Dimensions
- **Length**: ~15 mm
- **Diameter**: ~1–2 mm (narrowest point of the entire ventricular system)
- **Shape**: triangular in cross-section superiorly, oval inferiorly

### Location within the Midbrain
The aqueduct passes through the center of the [[brainstem|midbrain]], surrounded by the **periaqueductal gray matter** (central gray):

- **Anterior (ventral)**: tegmentum of the midbrain, containing the oculomotor and trochlear nuclei
- **Posterior (dorsal)**: tectal plate (quadrigeminal plate) — superior and inferior colliculi
- **Lateral**: periaqueductal gray → reticular formation

### Openings
- **Superior**: opens into the posterior part of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|third ventricle]], just below the posterior commissure
- **Inferior**: opens into the superior recess of the [[fourth-ventricle]], at the level of the superior medullary velum

## Surrounding Structures

### Tectal Plate (Posterior)
The quadrigeminal plate forms the roof over the aqueduct:
- **Superior colliculi** — visual reflex centers
- **Inferior colliculi** — auditory relay stations
- The [[trochlear-nerve|CN IV]] exits dorsally just below the inferior colliculus

### Periaqueductal Gray (Lateral/Circumferential)
The periaqueductal gray matter surrounding the aqueduct contains:
- Pain modulation circuits (endogenous analgesia system)
- Autonomic regulation nuclei
- [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III nucleus]] (Edinger-Westphal nucleus included) — ventral to the superior aqueduct
- [[trochlear-nerve|CN IV nucleus]] — ventral to the inferior aqueduct

### Tegmentum (Anterior)
The midbrain tegmentum anterior to the aqueduct contains:
- Red nucleus
- Medial longitudinal fasciculus
- Decussation of the superior cerebellar peduncle

## Clinical Significance

### Aqueductal Stenosis
Narrowing or obstruction of the aqueduct produces **obstructive (non-communicating) hydrocephalus**, with dilatation of the lateral and third ventricles while the [[fourth-ventricle]] remains normal-sized. Causes include:

- **Congenital**: aqueductal web, forking, or gliosis
- **[[pineal-region]] tumors**: compress the tectal plate, occluding the aqueduct from behind
- **Tectal gliomas**: intrinsic brainstem tumors expanding the tectal plate
- **[[vein-of-galen]] malformations**: may compress the aqueduct

### Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy (ETV)
In aqueductal stenosis, ETV creates an alternative CSF pathway by fenestrating the floor of the third ventricle (at the [[lamina-terminalis]] or tuber cinereum), bypassing the obstructed aqueduct entirely.

### Tectal Plate Surgery
Approaches to tectal lesions must respect the aqueduct:
- [[supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach]] — accesses the tectal plate from below
- The [[pineal-region]] lies just above and behind the aqueductal outlet

## Connections

- Connects: [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|third ventricle]] (superior) to [[fourth-ventricle]] (inferior)
- Within: [[brainstem|midbrain]]
- Posterior: tectal plate (superior/inferior colliculi)
- Anterior: midbrain tegmentum
- Surrounded by: periaqueductal gray
- CN nuclei adjacent: [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]], [[trochlear-nerve|CN IV]]
- Compressed by: [[pineal-region]] tumors, tectal gliomas
- Bypassed by: endoscopic third ventriculostomy via [[lamina-terminalis]]
