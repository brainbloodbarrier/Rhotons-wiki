# Orbit

The orbit is a complexly organized group of neural, vascular, muscular, ligamentous, and osseous structures that opens onto the face to collect binocular visual information. Nearly all bones forming the anterior and middle cranial base contribute to its walls. It communicates posteriorly with the anterior and middle cranial fossae and inferiorly with the pterygopalatine and infratemporal fossae.

## Osseous Walls

Seven bones form the orbit: frontal, zygomatic, sphenoid, lacrimal, ethmoid, palatine, and maxilla. Key features:
- **Roof** — orbital plate of frontal bone; separates orbit from anterior cranial fossa
- **Lateral wall** — greater wing of sphenoid and zygomatic bone; thickest wall
- **Floor** — maxilla, zygomatic, palatine; roof of maxillary sinus
- **Medial wall** — lamina papyracea of ethmoid (paper-thin); separates orbit from ethmoid sinuses

## Key Foramina

- **Optic canal** — transmits optic nerve and ophthalmic artery; passes through lesser wing of sphenoid
- **Superior orbital fissure** — between greater and lesser sphenoid wings; transmits CN III, IV, V1, VI, ophthalmic veins
- **Inferior orbital fissure** — between maxilla and greater sphenoid wing; transmits infraorbital nerve and vein

## Annular Tendon (of Zinn)

A ring of connective tissue at the orbital apex from which all four rectus muscles arise. It partially surrounds the optic canal and superior orbital fissure, dividing structures entering the orbit into those passing through the annulus (optic nerve, ophthalmic artery, CN III inferior division, CN VI, nasociliary nerve) and those passing outside (CN IV, frontal nerve, lacrimal nerve, superior ophthalmic vein).

## Vascular

- **Ophthalmic artery** — first branch of [[internal-carotid-artery]] (C4); enters through optic canal below the optic nerve; supplies globe, extraocular muscles, eyelids
- **Superior ophthalmic vein** — drains into [[cavernous-sinus]] via superior orbital fissure
- **Inferior ophthalmic vein** — drains into [[cavernous-sinus]] or pterygoid plexus

## Surgical Approaches

- **Transcranial (supraorbital/pterional)** — through superior and lateral walls for lesions at the orbital apex, optic canal, superior orbital fissure
- **Lateral orbitotomy** — through lateral wall for intraconal lesions
- **Anterior (transconjunctival/transcutaneous)** — for anterior orbital lesions
- **Endoscopic endonasal** — through medial wall via ethmoid sinuses for medial orbital lesions

## Relations

- Optic canal connects to middle cranial fossa (contains optic nerve, ophthalmic artery from [[internal-carotid-artery]])
- Superior orbital fissure connects to [[cavernous-sinus]] (CN III, IV, V1, VI)
- Medial wall separates from ethmoid sinuses (paper-thin — fracture risk)
- Roof separates from frontal lobe and anterior cranial fossa
