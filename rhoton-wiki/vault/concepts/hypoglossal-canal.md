# Hypoglossal Canal

The hypoglossal canal transmits the [[lower-cranial-nerves|hypoglossal nerve (CN XII)]] through the [[cranial-base]] above the [[occipital-condyle]]. It is a critical surgical landmark in the [[far-lateral-approach]] and its extensions.

## Anatomy

- Located above the middle third of the [[occipital-condyle]]
- Directed from posterior to anterior and medial to lateral, approximately 45 degrees from the midsagittal plane
- **Intracranial end:** ~5 mm above the junction of the posterior and middle thirds of the condyle
- **Extracranial end:** ~5 mm above the junction of the anterior and middle thirds

> A probe inserted through the hypoglossal canal passes forward approximately 45 degrees from the midsagittal plane in an anterolateral direction. (Rhoton 2023, p. 1402)

## Surgical Significance

The hypoglossal canal is the critical boundary for safe condylar drilling:
- The **posterior third** of the condyle can be drilled without entering the canal
- Drilling anterior to this boundary risks CN XII injury
- **Supracondylar drilling** below the canal accesses the lateral edge of the [[clivus]]
- **Supracondylar drilling** above the canal accesses the jugular tubercle, which often blocks visualization of the pontomedullary junction

## Relations

- Above: jugular tubercle (projects upward, blocks [[posterior-fossa]] view during [[far-lateral-approach]])
- Lateral: [[jugular-foramen]] and jugular process of occipital bone
- Medial: [[foramen-magnum]] and medial condylar surface
- The condylar canal (transmitting the posterior condylar emissary vein) passes above and usually does not communicate with the hypoglossal canal
