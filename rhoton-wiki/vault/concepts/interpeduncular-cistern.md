# Interpeduncular Cistern

The interpeduncular cistern (interpeduncular fossa) is the midline CSF space between the two [[brainstem|cerebral peduncles]], bounded anteriorly by the [[liliequist-membrane]] and posteriorly by the posterior perforated substance. It is the most critical cistern in aneurysm surgery — the [[basilar-artery]] apex, [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] origin, [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] P1 segments, and [[thalamoperforating-arteries]] all converge here. Virtually every approach to the basilar apex must traverse this cistern.

## Anatomy

### Boundaries
- **Anterior** — [[liliequist-membrane]] (diencephalic leaf) separating it from the prepontine cistern
- **Posterior** — posterior perforated substance (floor of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|third ventricle]])
- **Lateral** — [[brainstem|cerebral peduncles]] (medial surfaces)
- **Superior** — mammillary bodies of the [[hypothalamus]], tuber cinereum
- **Inferior** — dorsum sellae, upper pons

### Shape
- Diamond-shaped in axial section, with the cerebral peduncles forming the lateral points
- The fossa narrows superiorly at the mammillary bodies and inferiorly at the pontomesencephalic junction

## Contents

### Arteries
- **[[basilar-artery]] apex** — the bifurcation into bilateral [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCAs]] sits within or just above the cistern
- **[[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] P1 segments** — course laterally from the basilar apex through the cistern
- **[[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]]** — arises from the basilar just below the PCA origin; courses around the peduncle
- **[[posterior-communicating-artery|PCoA]]** — courses posterolaterally from the [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] to join the P1; its premamillary branch penetrates the floor of the cistern
- **[[thalamoperforating-arteries]]** — arise from the P1 and upper basilar; penetrate the posterior perforated substance to supply the [[thalamus]] and [[brainstem|midbrain]]
- **Percheron artery** — variant single trunk from one P1 supplying bilateral thalami (occlusion → bilateral thalamic infarction)

### Nerves
- **[[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]]** — exits the midbrain at the medial surface of the cerebral peduncle, courses through the cistern between the PCA and SCA → the "PCA-SCA sandwich"
- The [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] origin is the key landmark for the interpeduncular cistern

### Veins
- **Peduncular veins** — small veins draining the cerebral peduncle surface
- **Interpeduncular vein** — drains into the [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal]]

## Surgical Significance

### Basilar Apex Aneurysm Surgery
- The most challenging aneurysm location in neurosurgery
- The interpeduncular cistern is the target; the [[thalamoperforating-arteries]] behind the aneurysm are the principal surgical constraint
- **[[pterional-approach]]** — standard; the [[posterior-communicating-artery|PCoA]] and [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] are followed to the basilar apex; the opticocarotid triangle or carotid-oculomotor triangle provides the surgical window
- **[[orbitozygomatic-approach]]** — low-angle trajectory for high-riding basilar apices
- **[[subtemporal-approach]]** — lateral access through the [[ambient-cistern|crural cistern]] to the interpeduncular fossa

### Perforator Preservation
- The [[thalamoperforating-arteries]] (3-12 branches) arise from the posterior surface of P1 and basilar apex
- They are **behind** the aneurysm and adherent to the neck — the most common cause of morbidity in basilar apex surgery
- Temporary clipping of the basilar trunk must be brief (<5 min) to avoid bilateral perforator ischemia

### [[meninges|Liliequist Membrane]]
- The [[liliequist-membrane]] must be opened to access the interpeduncular cistern from the prechiasmatic/prepontine space
- The membrane has two leaves: diencephalic (between mammillary bodies and dorsum sellae) and mesencephalic
- Endoscopic third ventriculostomy perforates the floor of the third ventricle into this cistern

### CN III Relationships
- [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] is the principal nerve at risk during interpeduncular cistern surgery
- It divides the space into a superior compartment (PCA, basilar apex) and inferior compartment (SCA, pons)
- [[posterior-communicating-artery|PCoA]] aneurysms compress CN III from above → CN III palsy is the classic presentation
