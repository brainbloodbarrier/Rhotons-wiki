# Fornix

The fornix is the principal efferent pathway of the [[hippocampus]], arching beneath the [[corpus-callosum]] to connect the mesial [[cerebral-lobes|temporal lobe]] with the [[hypothalamus]], septal nuclei, and [[thalamus]]. It forms a critical surgical landmark at the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|foramen of Monro]] and along the [[choroid-plexus|choroidal fissure]], and its disruption can produce permanent memory deficits.

## Anatomy

### Parts
1. **Fimbria** — hippocampal surface origin; the white matter edge along the [[hippocampus|hippocampal]] body
2. **Crus (crus fornicis)** — arches posterosuperiorly beneath the splenium of the [[corpus-callosum]]
3. **Commissure (hippocampal commissure)** — transverse fibers connecting the two crura beneath the splenium
4. **Body** — paired columns fused in the midline, lying in the floor of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|body of the lateral ventricle]] and roof of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|third ventricle]]
5. **Columns** — descend at the anterior margin of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|foramen of Monro]], forming its anterior boundary

### Foramen of Monro Relationship
- The fornical column forms the **anterior boundary** of the foramen of Monro
- The [[thalamus]] forms the posterior boundary
- The [[choroid-plexus]] attaches along the thalamochoroidal (posterior) edge
- The [[internal-cerebral-veins|thalamostriate vein]] enters the [[internal-cerebral-veins|ICV]] at the venous angle just posterior to the foramen

### [[velum-interpositum]]
- The body of the fornix forms the **floor** of the velum interpositum cistern
- The [[internal-cerebral-veins]] course within this cistern above the [[thalamus|roof of the third ventricle]]
- Surgical access via the transchoroidal or interforniceal approach

## Relationships

### Superior
- [[corpus-callosum]] — the fornix is suspended from the undersurface of the callosal body by the septum pellucidum

### Inferior
- [[thalamus]] — the body of the fornix lies above the thalamic surface, separated by the velum interpositum
- [[hypothalamus]] — the columns terminate in the mammillary bodies via the postcommissural fornix

### Lateral
- [[choroid-plexus|Choroidal fissure]] — the fimbria and crus form the hippocampal lip of the [[choroidal-fissure]]
- [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|Lateral ventricle]] — the fornix forms part of the medial wall (body) and floor (atrium)

### Vascular Supply
- Body and columns: branches from [[anterior-cerebral-artery|ACA]] (pericallosal) and [[anterior-choroidal-artery]]
- Crus and fimbria: [[posterior-choroidal-arteries|lateral posterior choroidal artery]] and [[anterior-choroidal-artery]]

## Surgical Significance

### Transchoroidal Approach
- Access to the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|third ventricle]] via the choroidal fissure between the fornix and [[thalamus]]
- The fornix is retracted laterally; the [[choroid-plexus]] is reflected medially
- Preserves forniceal integrity better than the transforaminal or interforniceal route

### Interforniceal Approach
- Midline split between the two fornical bodies to access the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|third ventricle]]
- Risk of bilateral forniceal injury with devastating memory loss
- The raphe between the bodies may be absent in up to 30% of cases, making safe separation impossible

### Subchoroidal Approach
- Entry through the tenia fornicis to access the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|temporal horn]] and [[ambient-cistern]]
- [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] and [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal]] encountered in the choroidal fissure

### Colloid Cyst Surgery
- Colloid cysts arise at the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|foramen of Monro]] between the fornical columns
- Transcortical-transventricular or endoscopic approach
- Bilateral forniceal stretching by the cyst may already compromise memory preoperatively

### Memory Risk
- Unilateral forniceal injury (dominant side) may produce verbal memory deficits
- Bilateral injury produces severe anterograde amnesia
- Intraoperative monitoring of the fornix is critical during third ventricle and pineal region surgery
