# Posterior Clinoid Process

The posterior clinoid process (PCP) is a short bony projection at the superolateral corner of the **dorsum sellae**, paired across the midline. Together with the dorsum it forms the posterior wall of the sella turcica and the anterior margin of the interpeduncular cistern. The PCP is the surgical gatekeeper to the basilar apex: its removal (posterior clinoidectomy) opens the interpeduncular space for [[basilar-artery]] bifurcation aneurysms and for transcavernous exposures of the upper clivus.

## Osseous Anatomy

- Paired projections on the **superolateral dorsum sellae**, medial to the petrous apex
- Continuous inferiorly with the **clivus** (upper third)
- Variable in size — from a blunt knob to a true pointed process; occasionally fused with the [[anterior-clinoid-process|anterior clinoid]] via a caroticoclinoid ligament ossification
- Related medially to the **posterior hypophyseal fossa** and the posterior intercavernous sinus

## Ligamentous Relationships

The PCP is the anchor point of three key dural folds that organize the posterior [[cavernous-sinus]] and tentorial region:

| Ligament | Runs From | Runs To |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| **Anterior petroclinoid ligament** | PCP | [[petrous-apex]] (anterior) — forms superior border of Parkinson's triangle |
| **Posterior petroclinoid ligament** | PCP | Petrous apex (posterior) — free edge of the tentorial incisura above CN IV |
| **Interclinoid ligament** | PCP | [[anterior-clinoid-process]] ipsilateral — often ossifies |

The oculomotor nerve (CN III) pierces the oculomotor triangle just **lateral** to the PCP, between the anterior and posterior petroclinoid ligaments.

## Relationships to Neural Structures

- **Posteromedial:** the [[basilar-artery]] and its bifurcation lie in the interpeduncular cistern — the height of the bifurcation relative to the PCP determines surgical accessibility
- **Lateral:** CN III runs from the interpeduncular fossa between the petroclinoid ligaments into the roof of the [[cavernous-sinus]]
- **Superior:** [[posterior-cerebral-artery]] (P1–P2 junction), superior cerebellar artery, and the anterior thalamoperforating branches
- **Inferior:** the [[clivus]] and the upper pons
- **Anterior:** the pituitary gland, diaphragma sellae, and posterior intercavernous sinus
- **Medial:** the dorsum sellae and contralateral PCP

## Basilar-Apex Height Classification

- **High-riding** — bifurcation >5 mm above PCP: difficult subtemporal, favorable transsylvian
- **Normal** — within ±5 mm of PCP: pterional / orbitozygomatic favorable
- **Low-lying** — >5 mm below PCP: often requires PCP removal or combined skull-base approach

## Surgical Relevance

### Posterior Clinoidectomy

Removal of the PCP is a key enabling step for:

- **[[basilar-artery]] bifurcation aneurysm** clipping — increases the depth of exposure of the interpeduncular cistern
- **Upper clival chordoma / chondrosarcoma** — adds a "window" to the clival dura
- **Pituitary macroadenoma with retrosellar extension** — transcranial or extended transsphenoidal approaches
- **Transcavernous / transsellar** approach to the upper clivus (Dolenc)

### Approaches that Use PCP Removal

- **Extradural anterior clinoidectomy + intradural posterior clinoidectomy** via pterional or orbitozygomatic craniotomy
- **Posterior petrosectomy** combined with PCP drilling for petroclival meningioma
- **Endoscopic endonasal transdorsum sellae** — drills the PCP from below; exposes interpeduncular cistern for basilar-apex access without brain retraction

### Operative Pitfalls

- **CN III injury** — the nerve lies at the base of the oculomotor triangle just lateral to the PCP
- **Posterior thalamoperforators** — the small vessels arising from the P1 and basilar apex lie within mm of the PCP; devascularization causes thalamic or brainstem infarction
- **Posterior intercavernous sinus bleeding** — usually controlled with hemostatic agents; pack carefully to avoid CN III or CN VI injury
- **Dorsum sellae fracture propagation** — careful micro-drill technique with copious irrigation prevents thermal injury

## Anatomic Variants

- **Caroticoclinoid foramen** (ossified caroticoclinoid ligament) — connects PCP to ipsilateral [[anterior-clinoid-process]], limits ICA mobilization
- **Bridged sella** — both interclinoid and caroticoclinoid ligaments ossified
- **Absent PCP** — rare; dorsum sellae terminates without a defined process
- **Hypertrophic PCP** — seen in acromegaly; impinges on optic apparatus
