# Internal Auditory Canal

The internal auditory canal (IAC) is a bony channel within the [[temporal-bone|petrous bone]] that transmits the [[facial-nerve|facial (CN VII)]] and [[vestibulocochlear-nerve|vestibulocochlear (CN VIII)]] nerves, the [[labyrinthine-artery]], and the nervus intermedius from the [[posterior-fossa]] to the inner ear. It is the primary surgical target in vestibular schwannoma surgery and hearing preservation approaches.

## Anatomy

### Dimensions
- **Length**: 8–12 mm (average ~10 mm)
- **Diameter**: 3–8 mm
- **Orientation**: directed laterally and slightly anteriorly from the posterior surface of the [[petrous-apex|petrous bone]]

### Porus (Medial Opening)
The porus acusticus internus opens into the [[cerebellopontine-cistern]] on the posterior surface of the petrous bone. It is located:
- Approximately at the junction of the middle and posterior thirds of the petrous surface
- Posterior and inferior to the [[trigeminal-nerve|trigeminal nerve]] impression
- Above the [[jugular-foramen]]
- Below the [[dural-venous-sinuses|superior petrosal sinus]]

### Fundus (Lateral End)
The lateral end of the IAC is divided into four quadrants by two crests:

| Landmark | Divides |
|---|---|
| **Transverse crest (crista falciformis)** | Superior from inferior quadrants |
| **Vertical crest (Bill's bar)** | Anterior from posterior in the superior half |

#### Four Quadrants of the IAC Fundus

| Quadrant | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Anterosuperior** | [[facial-nerve]] (labyrinthine segment entrance) |
| **Anteroinferior** | Cochlear nerve |
| **Posterosuperior** | Superior vestibular nerve |
| **Posteroinferior** | Inferior vestibular nerve |

**Bill's bar** (vertical crest) is the most important surgical landmark at the IAC fundus — it separates the [[facial-nerve]] anteriorly from the superior vestibular nerve posteriorly.

## Neurovascular Contents

| Structure | Course | Function |
|---|---|---|
| [[facial-nerve]] (CN VII) | Anterosuperior → labyrinthine segment → geniculate ganglion | Motor to face, taste, lacrimation |
| Nervus intermedius | Between CN VII and CN VIII | Sensory/parasympathetic component of VII |
| Superior vestibular nerve | Posterosuperior quadrant | Utricle, superior/lateral semicircular canals |
| Inferior vestibular nerve | Posteroinferior quadrant | Saccule, posterior semicircular canal |
| Cochlear nerve | Anteroinferior quadrant | Hearing |
| [[labyrinthine-artery]] | Courses with CN VII/VIII | Sole arterial supply to cochlea and labyrinth |

## Surgical Approaches

### [[retrosigmoid-approach]]
- Accesses the IAC **porus** from the posterior fossa
- The posterior wall of the IAC is drilled to expose the canal contents
- **Hearing preservation** possible — cochlear nerve visualized
- Risk: [[superior-petrosal-vein|vein of Dandy]] at the porus

### [[middle-fossa-approach]]
- Accesses the IAC from **above** (middle fossa floor)
- The roof of the IAC is unroofed by drilling the petrous bone
- **Bill's bar** is the critical landmark separating the facial nerve from the vestibular nerve
- Best for small intracanalicular tumors with hearing preservation
- Risk: [[facial-nerve|geniculate ganglion]], cochlea (anterior to IAC fundus)

### [[posterior-petrosectomy]] (Translabyrinthine)
- Accesses the IAC through the mastoid and labyrinth
- Sacrifices hearing (labyrinth destroyed)
- Provides **360-degree exposure** of the IAC
- Least risk to the [[facial-nerve]] — early identification at the fundus

## Vestibular Schwannoma

Vestibular schwannomas (acoustic neuromas) arise from the Schwann cells of the [[vestibulocochlear-nerve|vestibular nerve]], most commonly the superior vestibular nerve, within the IAC. As they grow:

1. **Intracanalicular stage** — confined to the IAC; hearing loss, tinnitus
2. **Cisternal extension** — expands medially into the [[cerebellopontine-cistern]]
3. **Brainstem compression** — large tumors compress the [[brainstem]], [[cerebellum]], and adjacent cranial nerves

The relationship of the tumor to the [[facial-nerve]] determines surgical difficulty — CN VII is typically draped over the anterior-superior surface of the tumor.

## Connections

- Within: [[temporal-bone]] (petrous pyramid)
- Opens into: [[cerebellopontine-cistern]] (porus)
- Contents: [[facial-nerve]], [[vestibulocochlear-nerve]], [[labyrinthine-artery]]
- Landmark: Bill's bar (vertical crest at fundus)
- Adjacent: cochlea (anterolateral), vestibule (lateral), [[petrous-apex]] (medial)
- Approached via: [[retrosigmoid-approach]], [[middle-fossa-approach]], [[posterior-petrosectomy]]
- Tumor: vestibular schwannoma ([[vestibulocochlear-nerve]])
