# Caudate Nucleus

The **caudate nucleus** is the dorsal component of the striatum and the most consistent intraventricular landmark of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|lateral ventricle]]. It is a long, C-shaped grey matter mass that wraps around the [[thalamus]] along the concavity of the ventricle: the **head** bulges into the floor of the frontal horn, the **body** runs in the floor of the ventricular body flanking the [[thalamus]], and the **tail** curves downward and forward along the roof of the temporal horn to terminate near the [[amygdala]]. Together with the [[putamen]] it forms the **striatum**, the main input station of the [[basal-ganglia]].

## Parts and Ventricular Relations

| Part | Ventricular relation | Key surface |
|------|----------------------|-------------|
| **Head** | Floor and lateral wall of the frontal horn | Broad, convex bulge medial to the [[internal-capsule|anterior limb of the internal capsule]] |
| **Body** | Floor of the body of the lateral ventricle, lateral to the [[thalamus]] | Separated from the thalamus by the **stria terminalis** and the **thalamostriate vein** |
| **Tail** | Roof of the temporal horn | Follows the optic radiation into the temporal lobe; ends at the [[amygdala]] |

The **caudothalamic groove** between the caudate body and [[thalamus]] contains the stria terminalis and the **thalamostriate vein**, a critical surgical landmark for transcallosal-transforaminal entry into the third ventricle.

## Connections

- **Cortico-striatal input** — diffuse projections from all neocortical areas, especially frontal and parietal association cortex, terminate on medium spiny neurons of the caudate.
- **Nigrostriatal input** — dopaminergic fibers from the [[substantia-nigra|substantia nigra pars compacta]] modulate direct and indirect pathways.
- **Striato-pallidal output** — GABAergic projections to the globus pallidus (internal and external segments) and the [[substantia-nigra|SNr]] close the basal-ganglia–thalamocortical loop.
- **Thalamostriatal input** — intralaminar thalamic nuclei (centromedian, parafascicular) project back onto the caudate.

## Surgical Relevance

- **Transcallosal-transforaminal approach** — the head of the caudate is the lateral wall of the [[foramen-of-monro]] corridor; the taenia thalami and the **thalamostriate vein** along the caudothalamic groove define the boundary not to be crossed when opening the choroidal fissure.
- **Transcortical-transventricular approach** — a corticotomy in the middle frontal gyrus enters the frontal horn directly onto the head of the caudate, used for colloid cysts and intraventricular tumors.
- **Sub-choroidal and trans-choroidal routes to the third ventricle** — exploit the plane between the caudate body laterally and the [[thalamus]] medially by opening the **taenia fornicis** or **taenia thalami**, respectively.
- **Temporal horn approaches** — the tail of the caudate marks the superolateral roof of the temporal horn and must be preserved during amygdalohippocampectomy and selective mesial resections.
- **Basal ganglia hemorrhage** — caudate head bleeds are the smallest of the striatal hematomas, often rupture into the frontal horn, and typically have a favorable prognosis after endoscopic evacuation.

## Clinical Significance

- **Huntington disease** — selective loss of medium spiny neurons in the caudate and [[putamen]] produces the characteristic **box-car ventricle** sign on CT (loss of the caudate head bulge).
- **Caudate stroke syndromes** — infarcts in the recurrent artery of Heubner and lenticulostriate territory produce abulia, executive dysfunction, and contralateral motor neglect without dense hemiparesis.
- **OCD and Tourette circuitry** — functional imaging implicates caudate head hyperactivity; DBS of the ventral striatum modulates these circuits.
- **Parkinson's disease** — nigrostriatal dopamine loss affects the caudate less severely than the [[putamen]], but cognitive symptoms correlate with caudate involvement.
