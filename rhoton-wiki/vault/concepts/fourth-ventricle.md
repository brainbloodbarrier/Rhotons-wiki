# Fourth Ventricle

The fourth ventricle is a broad, tent-shaped midline cavity situated between the [[brainstem]] (pons and medulla) anteriorly and the [[cerebellum]] posteriorly. It is the CSF conduit between the third ventricle (via the [[cerebral-aqueduct]]) and the subarachnoid space (via the foramina of Magendie and Luschka).

## Boundaries

### Floor (rhomboid fossa)
The dorsal surface of the pons (upper half) and medulla (lower half). Contains the motor and sensory nuclei of cranial nerves V–XII. The **facial colliculus** — a rounded elevation produced by CN VII fibers looping over the [[abducens-nerve|CN VI]] nucleus — is a critical landmark on the floor.

### Roof
Composed of two parts:
- **Superior half** — the superior medullary velum (thin white sheet between the superior [[cerebellar-peduncles]]) and the lingula of the vermis
- **Inferior half** — the inferior medullary velum, tela choroidea (a double layer of pia-arachnoid), and the nodule of the vermis. The [[choroid-plexus]] attaches to the tela choroidea

### Lateral walls
Formed by the three cerebellar peduncles (superior, middle, inferior) converging toward the ventricular cavity.

## Communications

| Opening | Location | Connects to |
|---|---|---|
| Cerebral aqueduct | Rostral apex | [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|Third ventricle]] |
| Foramen of Magendie (median) | Caudal apex (fastigium) | Cisterna magna ([[basal-cisterns]]) |
| Foramina of Luschka (paired) | Lateral recesses | [[cerebellopontine-cistern]] |

The **lateral recesses** extend laterally from the ventricle around the [[brainstem]], ending at the foramina of Luschka. The choroid plexus projects through each foramen of Luschka into the cerebellopontine angle, sitting posterior to the [[lower-cranial-nerves|glossopharyngeal and vagus nerves]].

## Choroid Plexus

The fourth ventricle choroid plexus is T-shaped:
- **Vertical limb** — courses along the midline from the nodule to the fastigium
- **Horizontal limbs** — extend into each lateral recess and project through the foramina of Luschka

The choroid plexus is supplied by the [[pica|PICA]] (inferior choroidal branches from the telovelotonsillar segment) and the [[aica|AICA]] (choroidal branches from the flocculopeduncular segment).

## Surgical Approaches

### [[telovelar-approach]]
The primary microsurgical corridor. Opens the [[cerebellomedullary-fissure]] between the cerebellar tonsil and the inferior medullary velum/tela choroidea, following the [[pica|PICA]] (telovelotonsillar segment). Provides access to the ventricular floor **without splitting the vermis**.

**Technique (Rhoton):**
1. Open tela choroidea from foramen of Magendie to telovelar junction — exposes full floor, entire ventricular cavity except possibly fastigium, superolateral recess, and superior half of roof
2. If more exposure needed, open inferior medullary velum (paper-thin, semi-translucent) — accesses fastigium, superolateral recess, and superior roof
3. Extend telar opening laterally toward foramen of Luschka to open lateral recess and peduncular surfaces

**Critical landmarks during telovelar approach:**
- **Dentate nuclei** — located just rostral to the superior pole of the tonsils, wrapped around the superolateral recess near the lateral edges of the inferior medullary velum (dentate tubercles)
- **Cerebellar peduncles** — all three converge on the lateral wall/roof: superior peduncle vulnerable in upper roof lesions, inferior peduncle in lateral recess lesions, middle peduncle in CPA-facing lesions
- **[[pica|PICA]]** — exposed in the telovelar approach; occlusion distal to medullary branches causes labyrinthitis-like syndrome (rotatory dizziness, nausea, gait ataxia) without medullary infarction

### Midline suboccipital (transvermian)
Direct splitting of the inferior vermis. Largely replaced by the telovelar approach due to cerebellar mutism risk.

### Lateral recess approach
Via the [[retrosigmoid-approach]] — provides access to tumors extending into the lateral recess and foramen of Luschka, closely related to [[aica|AICA]] and [[facial-nerve|CN VII]]/[[vestibulocochlear-nerve|CN VIII]].

## Neural Injury Consequences

| Structure | Injury Effect |
|---|---|
| **Vermis (uvula, nodule, flocculus)** | Truncal ataxia, staggering gait, head oscillation — no limb ataxia. Nystagmus in all gaze directions |
| **Cerebellar hemisphere (unilateral)** | Ipsilateral limb ataxia, hypotonia, adiadochokinesia — often transient |
| **Dentate nucleus** | More severe, enduring ataxia + intention tremor with voluntary movement |
| **Superior cerebellar peduncle** | Severe ipsilateral intention tremor, dysmetria, decomposition of movement |
| **Inferior cerebellar peduncle** | Equilibrium disturbance like flocculonodular ablation — truncal ataxia, staggering gait |
| **Floor manipulation** | Intraop: BP decrease, apnea, respiratory rate increase. Postop: diplopia, dysphagia, poor cough → GI bleed, aspiration pneumonia |
| **Vermis splitting (children)** | Risk of **cerebellar mutism** — transient loss of speech output with preserved comprehension ± oropharyngeal apraxia |

## Pathology

- **Ependymomas** — arise from the ventricular floor; most common fourth ventricle tumor in children; adherent to the floor (CN nuclei at risk)
- **Medulloblastomas** — arise from the roof (superior medullary velum or nodule); fill the ventricle
- **Choroid plexus papillomas** — arise from the plexus; may protrude through the foramina of Luschka
- **Obstructive hydrocephalus** — fourth ventricle tumors block the aqueduct or foramina

## Relations

- Floor formed by the dorsal [[brainstem]] ([[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]]–XII nuclei)
- Roof formed by the [[cerebellum]] (velum, tela, nodule)
- [[pica|PICA]] intimately related — courses through the cerebellomedullary fissure along the roof
- [[aica|AICA]] related via lateral recess and choroid plexus
- [[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]] related via the superior medullary velum
- Communicates with [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|third ventricle]] via aqueduct
- Opens into [[basal-cisterns]] (cisterna magna, [[cerebellopontine-cistern]])
- [[lower-cranial-nerves|CN IX, X]] posterior to the choroid plexus at the foramen of Luschka
- Exposed via telovelar approach, [[retrosigmoid-approach]] (lateral recess)
- Part of the [[posterior-fossa]] ventricular system
