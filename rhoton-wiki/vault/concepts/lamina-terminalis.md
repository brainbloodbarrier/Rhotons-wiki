# Lamina Terminalis

The lamina terminalis is a thin membrane forming the anterior wall of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|third ventricle]], extending from the upper surface of the [[optic-chiasm]] to the rostrum of the [[corpus-callosum]]. It is the embryological anterior boundary of the neural tube and a critical surgical landmark for accessing the third ventricle and draining CSF in cases of hydrocephalus.

## Anatomical Boundaries

- **Inferior**: upper surface of the [[optic-chiasm]]
- **Superior**: anterior commissure → rostrum of the [[corpus-callosum]]
- **Lateral**: continuous with the walls of the third ventricle
- **Anterior**: cistern of the lamina terminalis (part of the [[basal-cisterns]])
- **Posterior**: cavity of the third ventricle

## Cistern of the Lamina Terminalis

The cistern of the lamina terminalis is the subarachnoid space anterior to the lamina, containing:

- **[[anterior-cerebral-artery]] (A2 segments)** — ascend in front of the lamina terminalis, one typically lying in the concavity of the other
- **[[anterior-communicating-artery]]** — located at or just above the level of the lamina
- **[[recurrent-artery-of-heubner]]** — passes upward between the A2 and the lamina toward the [[anterior-perforated-substance]]
- **[[perforating-arteries]]** — small branches from the ACA and ACoA enter the brain through the lamina terminalis region, supplying the anterior diencephalon and [[hypothalamus]]

## Relationship to the ACA

The [[anterior-cerebral-artery]] A2 (infracallosal) segment begins at the [[anterior-communicating-artery|ACoA]], passes anterior to the lamina terminalis, and terminates at the junction of the rostrum and genu of the [[corpus-callosum]]. Above the lamina, the paired ACAs make a smooth curve around the genu. Their junction with the ACoA is usually above the chiasm (70%) rather than above the optic nerves (30%).

## Perforating Branches

Small perforating arteries arising from the [[anterior-communicating-artery|ACoA]] and [[anterior-cerebral-artery|A2 segment]] pass through the lamina terminalis region to supply:

- **Anterior hypothalamus**
- **Lamina terminalis itself**
- **Anterior diencephalon**
- **Suprachiasmatic area**
- **Dorsal [[optic-chiasm]]**

These perforators are at risk during ACoA [[aneurysm-surgery]] and during opening of the lamina terminalis.

## Surgical Significance

### Lamina Terminalis Opening
Opening the lamina terminalis is a standard neurosurgical maneuver for:

- **CSF drainage** during aneurysm surgery (reduces brain tension)
- **Third ventricle access** — provides a natural midline corridor to the third ventricle without parenchymal transgression
- **Hydrocephalus management** — third ventriculostomy via the lamina terminalis

### Approach Context

| Approach | Lamina Terminalis Access |
|---|---|
| [[pterional-approach]] | Accessed via the carotid-chiasmatic corridor after sylvian fissure opening |
| [[interhemispheric-approach]] | Directly visualized in the anterior interhemispheric fissure |
| [[orbitozygomatic-approach]] | Enhanced low-angle view of the lamina and [[chiasmatic-cistern]] |

### ACoA Aneurysm Surgery
The lamina terminalis is a key landmark during [[anterior-communicating-artery|ACoA]] aneurysm clipping. The ACoA complex sits at or just above the lamina. Opening the lamina provides:

- CSF release and brain relaxation
- Improved visualization of the ACoA complex
- Access to contralateral A1 for proximal control

The [[recurrent-artery-of-heubner]] courses in close proximity, ascending between the A2 and the lamina, and must be preserved.

## Adjacent Structures

The paraterminal gyrus and subcallosal area lie on the medial hemispheric surface just anterior and lateral to the lamina. The [[sylvian-fissure]] and anterior interhemispheric fissure provide corridors to access the subcallosal area in front of the lamina terminalis.
