# Velum Interpositum

The velum interpositum is a triangular cistern (tela choroidea of the third ventricle) located between the [[fornix]] body inferiorly and the [[corpus-callosum]] splenium superiorly. It contains the [[internal-cerebral-veins]], [[posterior-choroidal-arteries|medial posterior choroidal arteries]], and the choroidal vasculature supplying the [[choroid-plexus]] of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|lateral ventricle]] body and [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|third ventricle]]. The cistern is a key surgical corridor for transchoroidal and supracerebellar-transtentorial approaches to the [[pineal-region]] and posterior third ventricle.

## Anatomy

### Boundaries
- **Floor** — body of the [[fornix]] (bilaterally) and the superior surface of the [[thalamus]] (roof of the third ventricle)
- **Roof** — undersurface of the [[corpus-callosum]] (splenium and posterior body)
- **Apex (anterior)** — narrows to the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|foramen of Monro]], where the [[internal-cerebral-veins]] originate at the venous angle
- **Base (posterior)** — opens into the [[quadrigeminal-cistern]] at the [[pineal-region]]; the [[internal-cerebral-veins]] exit here to join the [[vein-of-galen]]
- **Lateral walls** — the tenia of the [[fornix]] and the [[choroidal-fissure]] on each side

### Shape
- Triangular in sagittal section: narrow anteriorly at the foramen of Monro, widening posteriorly toward the [[vein-of-galen|galenic confluence]]
- Width: 1-15 mm (highly variable)
- In some patients, the cistern is obliterated (the two tela layers fuse) — this limits surgical utility

### Tela Choroidea
- The velum interpositum is formed by a **double layer of pia-arachnoid** (tela choroidea)
- The upper layer lies beneath the [[fornix]]
- The lower layer lies above the [[thalamus|thalamic roof]]
- The [[choroid-plexus]] of the third ventricle hangs from the lower layer
- The [[choroid-plexus]] of the lateral ventricle body attaches along the [[choroidal-fissure]]

## Contents

### Veins
- **[[internal-cerebral-veins]]** — the paired ICVs course posteriorly through the full length of the cistern, from the venous angle to the [[vein-of-galen]]
- **Medial atrial veins** — drain the medial atrial wall into the ICVs
- **Direct thalamic veins** — small tributaries from the [[thalamus]] superior surface

### Arteries
- **[[posterior-choroidal-arteries|Medial posterior choroidal arteries (MPChA)]]** — course from the [[ambient-cistern]] through the velum interpositum to supply the [[choroid-plexus]] of the third ventricle and thalamic roof
- **[[posterior-choroidal-arteries|Lateral posterior choroidal arteries (LPChA)]]** — course through the [[choroidal-fissure]] at the lateral margin

## Surgical Significance

### Transcallosal-Transvelum Interpositum Approach
- Via [[interhemispheric-approach]] → callosotomy → open the velum interpositum from above
- Provides access to the posterior third ventricle and [[pineal-region]]
- The [[internal-cerebral-veins]] must be preserved — they are the principal obstacle
- The ICVs can usually be retracted laterally (one at a time) to expose the midline

### Transchoroidal Approach
- Opening the [[choroidal-fissure]] enters the velum interpositum from the lateral ventricle
- The [[fornix]] lip is separated from the [[thalamus]] lip
- Provides wide access to the third ventricle roof, [[hypothalamus]], and velum interpositum cistern

### Pineal Region Surgery
- The velum interpositum is the superior approach corridor to the [[pineal-region]]
- Alternative to the [[supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach]] (inferior corridor)
- The [[vein-of-galen]] and [[internal-cerebral-veins]] are the critical venous obstacles in both corridors

### Colloid Cysts
- Large colloid cysts at the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|foramen of Monro]] may extend into the anterior velum interpositum
- Transcallosal or endoscopic approach through the foramen
