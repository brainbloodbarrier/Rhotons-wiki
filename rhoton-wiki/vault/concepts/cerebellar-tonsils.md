# Cerebellar Tonsils

The **cerebellar tonsils** are paired ovoid lobules of the inferior vermian surface of the [[cerebellum]] that hang over the posterior [[foramen-magnum]] and cap the dorsal [[fourth-ventricle|fourth-ventricular]] outlet. Each tonsil is a discrete, easily mobilized lobule attached to the rest of the cerebellum only by a narrow **tonsillar peduncle** on its anterosuperior surface — a feature exploited surgically, because the tonsil can be elevated or transected without disrupting the remaining cerebellar circuitry. Tonsillar anatomy drives the geometry of the [[telovelar-approach]], the [[far-lateral-approach]], and the definition of **Chiari I malformation**.

## Anatomy and Surfaces

Each tonsil is an oblong mass measuring approximately 20–28 mm craniocaudally, 20 mm transversely, and 10–15 mm in its anteroposterior dimension. Four surfaces are surgically defined:

| Surface | Relation |
|---------|----------|
| **Posterior (dorsal) surface** | Superficial; visible on opening the [[cerebellomedullary-fissure]] |
| **Medial surface** | Faces the opposite tonsil across the vallecula; continuous with the uvula of the vermis by a thin **velum** |
| **Lateral (biventral) surface** | Abuts the biventral lobule and, at its anterolateral corner, the PICA loop |
| **Anterior surface** | Faces the posterior medulla and the dorsal wall of the cisterna magna; the **supratonsillar cleft** separates tonsil from the inferior medullary velum |

The **tonsillar peduncle** at the anterosuperior corner is the only parenchymal attachment; by dissecting free the avascular arachnoid of the cerebellomedullary fissure, the tonsil can be displaced laterally without resection, opening a direct corridor to the [[fourth-ventricle]].

## Vascular Relations

- **[[PICA|Posterior inferior cerebellar artery (PICA)]]** — the tonsillomedullary and telovelotonsillar segments loop along the tonsillar surface; the **caudal loop** lies at the inferior pole, the **cranial loop** between tonsil and vermis. PICA perforators supply the medulla and must be preserved during tonsillar mobilization.
- **Tonsillohemispheric and inferior vermian arteries** — terminal PICA branches coursing on the posterior tonsil surface.
- **Inferior vermian veins** — drain into the vein of the cerebellomedullary fissure and ultimately the torcular.

## Surgical Relevance

- **[[telovelar-approach]]** — the tonsil is elevated off the posterior medulla by dissecting the **cerebellomedullary fissure**, the **tela choroidea**, and the **inferior medullary velum**, providing a vermis-sparing route from the obex to the cerebral aqueduct through the floor of the [[fourth-ventricle]].
- **[[far-lateral-approach]]** — a bilateral supra-inferior tonsillar displacement widens the corridor to the vertebrobasilar junction and the ventrolateral medulla; the tonsil is retracted superomedially, not resected.
- **Tonsillar resection** — occasionally performed for Chiari decompression (intradural variant), fourth-ventricular tumors invading the tonsillar peduncle, or safe working space in the telovelar corridor; the tonsil is functionally expendable on one side.
- **Tonsillobiventral fissure** — plane of PICA exposure during aneurysm surgery at the tonsillomedullary segment.
- **Midline suboccipital craniotomy** — the tonsils are the first parenchymal structures encountered on opening the cisterna magna.

## Clinical Significance — Herniation and Chiari

- **Tonsillar herniation (coning)** — downward displacement of the tonsils through the [[foramen-magnum]] from raised supratentorial pressure; compresses the medulla and produces Cushing's triad, apnea, and death if unrelieved.
- **Chiari I malformation** — congenital descent of the tonsils >5 mm below the [[foramen-magnum]] plane (McRae line); classically produces cough-headache, central cord syringomyelia, and lower cranial nerve symptoms. Treated by posterior fossa decompression ± duraplasty ± tonsillar reduction.
- **Chiari II (Arnold-Chiari)** — broader descent of tonsils, vermis, and medulla through the [[foramen-magnum]], associated with myelomeningocele and hydrocephalus.
- **Acquired tonsillar descent** — from chronic intracranial hypotension (CSF leak) or chronic hydrocephalus; imaging mimic of Chiari I.

## Relations

- Hang over the posterior medulla and [[foramen-magnum]], separated from them by the cisterna magna
- Medial surface apposes the opposite tonsil across the midline vallecula
- Superior attachment: tonsillar peduncle to the inferior vermis (uvula, nodulus zone)
- Covered by the [[PICA]] tonsillomedullary and telovelotonsillar loops
- Overlie the [[cerebellomedullary-fissure]] and, through it, the roof of the [[fourth-ventricle]]
