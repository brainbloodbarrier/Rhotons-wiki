# Falx Cerebri

The falx cerebri is a large sickle-shaped dural fold that descends vertically in the midline longitudinal fissure between the two [[cerebrum|cerebral hemispheres]]. It extends from the crista galli anteriorly to the internal occipital protuberance posteriorly, where it merges with the [[tentorium-cerebelli]].

## Anatomy

### Attachments
- **Superior**: attached to the inner surface of the calvaria along the midline, from the crista galli (ethmoid bone) to the internal occipital protuberance
- **Inferior (free edge)**: arches above the [[corpus-callosum]], curving from the crista galli posteriorly over the genu, body, and splenium
- **Posterior**: continuous with the upper surface of the [[tentorium-cerebelli]] at the falcotentorial junction

### Free Edge
The inferior free edge of the falx is concave and hangs above the [[corpus-callosum]]. The gap between the free edge and the corpus callosum allows communication between the two hemispheric compartments — this is the route through which **subfalcine herniation** occurs.

The [[anterior-cerebral-artery]] (pericallosal arteries) courses in the pericallosal cistern just above the corpus callosum, beneath the inferior free edge of the falx.

## Venous Sinuses

The falx contains or borders three major [[dural-venous-sinuses]]:

| Sinus | Location | Drainage |
|---|---|---|
| **Superior sagittal sinus** | Upper attached margin of the falx | Receives [[bridging-veins]] from the superior sagittal group; drains to torcular Herophili |
| **Inferior sagittal sinus** | Lower free edge of the falx | Receives medial hemispheric veins; drains into the straight sinus |
| **Straight sinus** | Junction of falx and [[tentorium-cerebelli]] | Formed by the union of inferior sagittal sinus and [[vein-of-galen]] |

## Surgical Significance

### [[interhemispheric-approach]]
The falx is the medial boundary during interhemispheric approaches. The surgeon works lateral to the falx in the longitudinal fissure. Key considerations:

- **[[bridging-veins]]** crossing from the cortical surface to the superior sagittal sinus must be preserved when possible
- **Vein-free zone** — an area devoid of bridging veins commonly exists just anterior to the coronal suture, the preferred craniotomy site
- The [[anterior-cerebral-artery|pericallosal arteries]] course beneath the free edge

### Falcine Meningiomas
Meningiomas arising from the falx (falcine meningiomas) are distinct from parasagittal meningiomas (which arise from the dura adjacent to the superior sagittal sinus). Falcine meningiomas may:

- Grow bilaterally between the hemispheres
- Invade the inferior sagittal sinus
- Compress the pericallosal arteries

### Subfalcine Herniation
In subfalcine (cingulate) [[herniation-syndromes|herniation]], the cingulate gyrus herniates under the free edge of the falx, compressing:

- **[[anterior-cerebral-artery]]** (pericallosal artery) — producing ischemia of the medial frontal and parietal lobes
- **Contralateral hemisphere** — secondary compression

### Parasagittal Surgery
Operations near the superior sagittal sinus require understanding the relationship between the falx, the sinus, and the draining [[bridging-veins]]. The sinus may be sacrificed in its anterior third (anterior to the coronal suture) where collateral drainage is adequate, but occlusion of the middle or posterior thirds risks severe venous infarction.

## Connections

- Separates: right and left cerebral hemispheres in the longitudinal fissure
- Contains: superior sagittal sinus (superior margin), inferior sagittal sinus (inferior margin)
- Joins: [[tentorium-cerebelli]] at falcotentorial junction → straight sinus
- Below free edge: [[corpus-callosum]], [[anterior-cerebral-artery|pericallosal arteries]]
- Herniation: subfalcine [[herniation-syndromes]]
- Part of: [[meninges|dura mater]]
