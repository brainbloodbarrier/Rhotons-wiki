# Posterior Incisural Space

The posterior incisural space is located between the posterior surface of the [[brainstem]] and the tentorial apex. It corresponds to the region of the [[pineal-region|pineal gland]] and the [[cerebral-veins|vein of Galen]], and opens into the [[quadrigeminal-cistern]] and the cerebellomesencephalic fissure.

## Neural Relationships

The anterior wall is formed by the posterior midbrain surface (the [[tectum]] and tegmentum). The superior wall is the splenium of the [[corpus-callosum]]. The posterior wall is formed by the tentorial apex where the [[falx-cerebri]] joins the [[tentorium-cerebelli]].

> The posterior incisural space contains the convergence of the internal cerebral and basal veins and many of their tributaries on the vein of Galen. (Rhoton 2023, p. 1262)

## Venous Relationships

The venous anatomy is extremely complex in this space:

- The [[cerebral-veins|vein of Galen]] receives the [[cerebral-veins|internal cerebral veins]] and [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal|basal veins of Rosenthal]]
- The [[cerebral-veins|straight sinus]] begins at the tentorial apex where it receives the vein of Galen
- Multiple tributaries converge here: the precentral cerebellar vein, the posterior mesencephalic vein, and superior vermian veins

## Arterial Relationships

- The [[posterior-cerebral-artery]] P3-P4 segments
- The [[superior-cerebellar-artery]] (SCA) wraps around to reach this space
- Perforating arteries to the colliculi and posterior thalamus

## Surgical Relevance

Accessed via the [[supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach]] (Krause approach) for [[pineal-region]] tumors, tectal gliomas, and vein of Galen malformations. The [[interhemispheric-approach|posterior interhemispheric approach]] provides access from above.
