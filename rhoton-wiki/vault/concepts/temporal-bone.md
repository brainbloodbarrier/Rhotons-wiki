# Temporal Bone

The temporal bone is one of the most anatomically complex cranial bones, [[CONTRIBUTING]] to the middle and posterior cranial fossae, housing the auditory and vestibular organs, and transmitting critical neurovascular structures. Its petrous portion forms the boundary between the middle and posterior fossae and is the target of multiple skull base approaches.

## Parts

### Squamous part
Thin, flat plate forming the lateral skull wall. The zygomatic process projects anteriorly to form the zygomatic arch. The [[middle-meningeal-artery]] grooves its internal surface. Related to the [[pterional-approach]] and [[orbitozygomatic-approach|orbitozygomatic craniotomy]].

### Petrous part
The dense, pyramid-shaped core of the temporal bone, oriented anteromedially. Its long axis points toward the [[sellar-region|dorsum sellae]]:
- **Anterior (middle fossa) surface** — forms the posterior part of the middle cranial fossa floor; contains the arcuate eminence (over the superior semicircular canal), tegmen tympani (roof of the middle ear and mastoid antrum), and the hiatus for the [[greater-petrosal-nerve]]
- **Posterior ([[posterior-fossa]]) surface** — faces the [[cerebellopontine-cistern]]; contains the **[[internal-auditory-canal|internal acoustic meatus]]** (IAM/IAC) transmitting [[facial-nerve|CN VII]], [[vestibulocochlear-nerve|CN VIII]], the [[labyrinthine-artery]], and the nervus intermedius
- **Inferior surface** — contains the carotid canal (entry of the [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] into the skull), jugular fossa (roof of the [[jugular-foramen]]), and stylomastoid foramen ([[facial-nerve|CN VII]] exit)

### Mastoid part
The posterior portion containing the mastoid air cells. The **[[sigmoid-sinus]]** ([[dural-venous-sinuses]]) grooves its inner surface. The [[retrosigmoid-approach]] craniotomy is placed behind the sigmoid sinus and below the [[transverse-sinus]] on the mastoid bone.

### Tympanic part
The small plate forming the anterior and inferior walls of the external auditory canal.

## Internal Acoustic Meatus

The IAM is a canal ~10 mm long in the posterior petrous surface, transmitting:
- **[[facial-nerve]]** (CN VII) — anterosuperior compartment
- **[[vestibulocochlear-nerve]]** (CN VIII) — posterosuperior (superior vestibular), posteroinferior (inferior vestibular), anteroinferior (cochlear)
- **Labyrinthine artery** — usually from [[aica]]
- **Nervus intermedius** — sensory/parasympathetic root of CN VII

The **transverse crest** (crista falciformis) divides the fundus into superior and inferior halves. The **vertical crest** (Bill's bar) separates the facial nerve from the superior vestibular nerve — the critical surgical landmark in vestibular schwannoma surgery.

## [[petrous-apex]]

The anteromedial tip of the petrous pyramid, between the [[cavernous-sinus]] and the posterior fossa. The [[abducens-nerve|CN VI]] crosses it to enter Dorello's canal. The [[trigeminal-nerve|trigeminal nerve]] posterior root enters [[meckels-cave|Meckel's cave]] above the petrous apex. The superior petrosal sinus ([[dural-venous-sinuses]]) runs along the petrous ridge.

## Jugular Foramen

Located at the posterior border of the petrous bone, between the temporal and occipital bones. Divided by intrajugular processes into:
- **Petrous part** — [[lower-cranial-nerves|CN IX]], inferior petrosal sinus
- **Intrajugular part** — [[lower-cranial-nerves|CN X, XI]]
- **Sigmoid part** — sigmoid sinus → internal jugular vein

## Surgical Approaches

### Retrosigmoid ([[retrosigmoid-approach]])
Craniotomy behind the sigmoid sinus on the mastoid portion. Accesses the [[cerebellopontine-cistern]], CN V–XI, [[aica]], vestibular schwannomas.

### Translabyrinthine
Through the mastoid air cells and labyrinth (sacrifices hearing). Provides direct access to the IAC and [[facial-nerve|CN VII]]/[[vestibulocochlear-nerve|CN VIII]]. Used for large vestibular schwannomas when hearing is already lost.

### Middle fossa
Elevation of the [[cerebral-lobes|temporal lobe]] from the petrous anterior surface. Accesses the IAC from above without entering the posterior fossa. Used for small intracanalicular vestibular schwannomas with serviceable hearing.

### Anterior petrosectomy (Kawase)
Extension of the [[subtemporal-approach]]. Drilling the petrous apex between the [[trigeminal-nerve]] and IAC exposes the lateral [[clivus]], [[prepontine-cistern]], [[basilar-artery]], and [[abducens-nerve|CN VI]].

### [[posterior-petrosectomy]] (retrolabyrinthine/presigmoid)
Removal of mastoid bone behind the labyrinth and anterior to the sigmoid sinus. Accesses the cerebellopontine angle and petroclival region while preserving hearing.

## Relations

- Forms the boundary between middle and posterior cranial fossae ([[cranial-base]])
- Petrous surface faces the [[cerebellopontine-cistern]] posteriorly
- IAC transmits [[facial-nerve|CN VII]] and [[vestibulocochlear-nerve|CN VIII]] — see [[cranial-nerves-overview]]
- [[aica]] meatal loop enters or approaches the IAC — see [[posterior-circulation]]
- Sigmoid sinus ([[dural-venous-sinuses]]) grooves the mastoid
- Superior petrosal sinus along the petrous ridge — borders [[cavernous-sinus]]
- Jugular foramen transmits [[lower-cranial-nerves|CN IX-XI]] and sigmoid sinus
- [[internal-carotid-artery]] enters the skull through the carotid canal
- [[abducens-nerve|CN VI]] crosses the petrous apex (Dorello's canal)
- [[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]] enters Meckel's cave above the petrous apex
- Related to [[sphenoid-bone]] anteriorly (sphenopetrosal suture)
- Related to [[foramen-magnum]] region inferiorly
- See [[surgical-approaches-overview]] for approach selection
