# Red Nucleus

The **red nucleus** (nucleus ruber) is an ovoid, iron-rich nucleus in the rostral mesencephalic tegmentum at the level of the superior colliculus. It is the midbrain waystation of the **dentato-rubro-thalamic tract (DRTT)**, one of the most surgically consequential white-matter pathways in functional neurosurgery. In [[microsurgical-anatomy-principles|Rhoton-style microsurgical anatomy]], the red nucleus is a principal internal landmark for locating the perimesencephalic safe-entry zones and for interpreting brainstem cavernoma geography.

## Location and Boundaries

| Plane | Relation |
|-------|----------|
| **Rostrocaudal level** | Superior colliculus; extends from the subthalamic region into the rostral midbrain tegmentum |
| **Medial** | Periaqueductal gray and the [[cerebral-aqueduct]] |
| **Lateral** | Medial lemniscus and substantia nigra |
| **Anterior** | Substantia nigra and the [[cerebral-peduncles]] (basis pedunculi) |
| **Posterior** | Decussation of the superior cerebellar peduncle; fibers of the DRTT |
| **Superior** | Subthalamic region, including the subthalamic nucleus |

## Connections

1. **Dentato-rubro-thalamic tract (DRTT)** — the crown jewel. Fibers from the dentate nucleus ascend through the [[cerebellar-peduncles|superior cerebellar peduncle]], decussate in the caudal midbrain tegmentum, pass **through or around** the red nucleus, and ascend to the ventral intermediate (VIM) nucleus of the [[thalamus]]. Tremor suppression by thalamic DBS and MR-guided focused ultrasound targets this pathway.
2. **Rubrospinal tract** — from the magnocellular part (small in humans), descends in the lateral tegmentum to the contralateral spinal cord; vestigial motor function in primates.
3. **Central tegmental tract** — links the parvocellular red nucleus to the inferior olive via the Guillain–Mollaret triangle; disruption produces palatal myoclonus.
4. **Cortico-rubral fibers** — from the motor and premotor cortices via the internal capsule.

## Surgical Relevance

- **Safe entry zones (SEZ) of the [[midbrain]]** — the perimesencephalic SEZs are defined with reference to the red nucleus:
  - **Supracollicular SEZ** (Cavalcanti) — above the superior colliculus, medial to the red nucleus
  - **Lateral mesencephalic sulcus** — between the red nucleus and the lateral surface of the peduncle
  - **Perioculomotor SEZ** — antero-medial to the red nucleus, between CN III rootlets
- **DBS targets** — the VIM, subthalamic nucleus (STN), and zona incerta all lie in close topographic relationship to the red nucleus; the red nucleus is a critical internal fiducial on susceptibility-weighted MRI for DBS atlas registration.
- **Brainstem cavernoma surgery** — cavernomas abutting the red nucleus require mapping of the DRTT to preserve tremor-suppression and coordination pathways; surgical corridors include the [[subtemporal-approach]] and supracerebellar routes.
- **MR-guided focused ultrasound thalamotomy** — uses the DRTT passage through/around the red nucleus as an anatomical constraint for lesioning the VIM.

## Clinical Significance

- **Benedikt syndrome** — lesion of the red nucleus and CN III fascicle: ipsilateral oculomotor palsy with contralateral tremor, ataxia, and choreoathetosis.
- **Claude syndrome** — involvement of the red nucleus with sparing of the peduncle: ipsilateral CN III palsy and contralateral cerebellar ataxia (no hemiparesis).
- **Palatal myoclonus** — disruption of the Guillain–Mollaret triangle (red nucleus → inferior olive → dentate nucleus → superior cerebellar peduncle → red nucleus) produces rhythmic palatal movements and olivary hypertrophy.
- **Holmes tremor (rubral tremor)** — large-amplitude rest, postural, and kinetic tremor from lesions near the red nucleus disrupting the DRTT.
