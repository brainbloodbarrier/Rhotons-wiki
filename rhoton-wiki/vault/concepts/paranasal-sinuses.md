# Paranasal Sinuses

The paranasal sinuses are air-filled cavities within the bones of the [[cranial-base]] and face that communicate with the nasal cavity. In neurosurgery, they are principally important as the surgical corridor for the [[transsphenoidal-approach]] and endoscopic endonasal approaches, and as landmarks for anterior skull base surgery.

## Sphenoid Sinus

The most neurosurgically important sinus — the gateway to the [[sellar-region]].

### Pneumatization types
- **Conchal** (~3%) — thick bone anterior to the sella; minimal pneumatization
- **Presellar** (~10%) — pneumatization extends to the tuberculum sellae
- **Sellar** (~85%) — pneumatization extends beneath and behind the sella; the most favorable for [[transsphenoidal-approach]]
- **Lateral recess** — extension into the greater wing of the [[sphenoid-bone]] or pterygoid process

### Landmarks on sinus walls
- **Sellar floor** — the thin bone (often dehiscent) over the [[pituitary-gland]]
- **Carotid prominences** — bilateral protrusions of the [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] (C3 cavernous segment) on the posterolateral walls
- **[[optic-canal]] prominences** — bilateral protrusions of the [[optic-nerve|CN II]] on the superolateral walls
- **Opticocarotid recess** — the depression between the carotid and optic prominences; lateral limit of safe bone removal
- **Planum sphenoidale** — the flat bone above the sinus, between the optic canals; floor of the anterior cranial fossa
- **[[clivus]]** — the posterior slope below the sellar floor, toward the [[foramen-magnum]]
- **Sinus septum** — often deviated; may insert on the ICA prominence (critical to identify preoperatively)

### Ostia
The paired sphenoid ostia open into the sphenoethmoidal recesses of the nasal cavity, located above and behind the superior turbinate.

## Ethmoid Sinuses

A labyrinth of 6–12 air cells between the [[orbit]] medially and the nasal cavity:
- **Anterior ethmoid cells** — drain into the middle meatus (hiatus semilunaris)
- **Posterior ethmoid cells** — drain into the superior meatus
- **Lamina papyracea** — paper-thin medial orbital wall separating ethmoid cells from the [[orbit]]
- **Cribriform plate** — the roof of the ethmoid labyrinth; floor of the anterior cranial fossa; transmits olfactory nerve fibers ([[olfactory-nerve|CN I]])

### Surgical landmarks
- **Anterior ethmoidal artery** — from the [[ophthalmic-artery]]; crosses the ethmoid roof in the anterior ethmoidal canal. Marks the level of the cribriform plate (anterior skull base floor). Its injury causes retrobulbar hematoma.
- **[[ethmoidal-arteries|Posterior ethmoidal artery]]** — from the [[ophthalmic-artery]]; crosses the posterior ethmoid. Located ~12 mm anterior to the optic canal.

## Maxillary Sinus

The largest paranasal sinus, located below the [[orbit]] floor:
- **Roof** — the orbital floor; contains the infraorbital nerve and artery
- **Medial wall** — the lateral nasal wall; the sinus opens into the middle meatus via the hiatus semilunaris
- **Floor** — above the upper molar teeth roots
- **Posterior wall** — separates the sinus from the [[infratemporal-fossa]] and [[pterygopalatine-fossa]]

## Frontal Sinus

Located above the orbital roofs and nasal bridge. Drains into the middle meatus via the frontonasal duct. Variable size and asymmetry. Related to the anterior cranial fossa dura — fracture or surgery risks CSF leak.

## Surgical Significance

### Transsphenoidal approach ([[transsphenoidal-approach]])
The nasal cavity → sphenoid sinus → sellar floor corridor is the standard route to the [[pituitary-gland]]. The sphenoid ostia are widened, the intersinus septum removed, and the sellar floor opened. The carotid and optic prominences define the lateral safe limits.

### Extended endonasal approaches
Beyond the sella, the endoscopic endonasal corridor extends to:
- **Planum/tuberculum** — for meningiomas of the anterior skull base
- **Clivus** — for chordomas (upper, middle, lower clival access)
- **Cribriform plate** — for esthesioneuroblastomas (olfactory groove)
- **Pterygopalatine/[[infratemporal-fossa]]** — for lateral extensions

### Anterior skull base surgery
The cribriform plate (ethmoid roof) is the thinnest part of the anterior cranial fossa floor. Endoscopic repair of CSF leaks targets this area. The anterior ethmoidal artery ([[ophthalmic-artery]]) is the key landmark.

### CSF rhinorrhea
Post-traumatic or spontaneous CSF leaks most commonly occur through the cribriform plate or sphenoid sinus. Endoscopic repair has replaced most craniotomy-based repairs.

## Relations

- [[sphenoid-bone]] contains the sphenoid sinus — houses the [[pituitary-gland]] above
- [[internal-carotid-artery]] and [[optic-nerve]] create prominences on sphenoid sinus walls
- [[cavernous-sinus]] lateral to the sphenoid sinus
- [[ophthalmic-artery]] (ethmoidal branches) crosses the ethmoid sinuses
- [[orbit]] separated from ethmoid sinuses by the lamina papyracea
- [[cranial-base]] (anterior fossa floor = cribriform plate + planum sphenoidale)
- Corridor for [[transsphenoidal-approach]] — see [[surgical-approaches-overview]]
- [[infratemporal-fossa]] behind the maxillary sinus posterior wall
