# Pineal Region

The pineal region is a deep midline area centered on the pineal gland at the posterior wall of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|third ventricle]], within the quadrigeminal cistern. It is one of the most surgically challenging regions due to its depth and the convergence of critical venous and arterial structures.

## Pineal Gland

The pineal gland (epiphysis cerebri) is a small neuroendocrine structure attached to the posterior wall of the third ventricle by the pineal stalk, which contains the habenular and posterior commissures. Key relationships:

- **Superior**: splenium of the [[corpus-callosum]]
- **Inferior**: quadrigeminal plate (superior and inferior colliculi) of the [[brainstem|midbrain]]
- **Anterior**: posterior commissure, third ventricle
- **Lateral**: pulvinar of the [[thalamus]]

The pineal gland sits at the junction of the posterior incisural space and serves as a surface landmark for the posterior [[thalamus]] margin — the foramen of Monro at one end and the pineal at the other approximate the deep anteroposterior extent of the thalamus.

## Quadrigeminal Cistern

The quadrigeminal cistern (cistern of the great cerebral vein) surrounds the pineal region and contains:

### Venous Structures
- **[[vein-of-galen]]** — forms at the union of the [[internal-cerebral-veins]] above/posterior to the pineal body
- **[[internal-cerebral-veins]]** — exit the velum interpositum above the pineal, following the concave curve of the splenium
- **[[basal-vein-of-rosenthal]]** — terminal segments enter laterally to join the vein of Galen
- **Superior vermian vein** — ascends from the [[cerebellum]] to join the vein of Galen
- **Tectal veins** — drain the superior and inferior colliculi

### Arterial Structures
- **[[posterior-cerebral-artery]] (P3 segment)** — branches course through the quadrigeminal cistern
- **[[posterior-choroidal-arteries]]** — medial posterior choroidal artery encircles the brainstem and turns forward beside the pineal to reach the roof of the third ventricle
- **[[superior-cerebellar-artery]]** — branches ascend from the cerebellomesencephalic fissure

### Neural Structures
- **[[trochlear-nerve]]** — CN IV exits dorsally from the midbrain below the inferior colliculus, coursing through the ambient cistern around the brainstem

## Posterior Incisural Space

The pineal region corresponds to the **posterior incisural space** of the [[tentorial-incisura]] — the area behind the [[brainstem]] between the splenium above and the cerebellum below. The veins in this space drain into the [[internal-cerebral-veins]], [[vein-of-galen]], or [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal|basal veins]].

## Surgical Approaches

### [[supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach]]
The primary approach to the pineal region. The surgeon works between the superior cerebellar surface and the tentorium to reach the quadrigeminal cistern from below. The [[vein-of-galen]] and its tributaries define the superior limit of safe dissection. Variants include midline, paramedian, and extreme-lateral positions.

### Occipital Transtentorial
An approach through the occipital lobe, dividing the tentorium to reach the pineal from above and lateral. Provides access to the superior and lateral pineal region, but requires retraction of the occipital lobe and risks injury to the visual cortex.

### [[interhemispheric-approach]] (Posterior Transcallosal)
A posterior variant entering through the splenium of the [[corpus-callosum]] to access the pineal from above. The [[internal-cerebral-veins]] course in the velum interpositum just below the fornix and must be carefully preserved.

### Approach Selection

| Approach | Best For | Key Obstacle |
|---|---|---|
| [[supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach]] | Inferior/posterior pineal | Bridging veins to tentorium |
| Occipital transtentorial | Superior/lateral pineal | Visual cortex retraction |
| Posterior transcallosal | Superior pineal, third ventricle | [[internal-cerebral-veins]], [[vein-of-galen]] |

## Venous Obstacle

The deep venous confluence is the principal surgical obstacle in the pineal region. The [[vein-of-galen]], [[internal-cerebral-veins]], and terminal [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal|basal veins]] all converge here. Sacrifice of these veins can cause severe venous infarction of the [[thalamus]], [[basal-ganglia]], and deep white matter, with risk of hemiplegia, coma, and death.

## Clinical Significance

### Pineal Tumors
- **Germinomas** — most common pineal tumor; radiosensitive
- **Pineocytomas/pineoblastomas** — arise from pineal parenchyma
- **Teratomas** — germ cell tumors with mixed elements
- **Meningiomas** — may arise from the falcotentorial junction

### Hydrocephalus
Pineal tumors frequently compress the cerebral aqueduct, producing obstructive hydrocephalus. The [[fourth-ventricle]] and lateral ventricles dilate while the aqueduct is compressed between the tumor and the tectal plate.
