# Mammillary Bodies

The **mammillary bodies** are paired, spherical nuclei of the posterior [[hypothalamus]] that project from the ventral surface of the diencephalon as two small white protuberances in the floor of the [[interpeduncular-cistern]]. They are the terminal relay of the [[fornix]], the origin of the mammillothalamic tract (Vicq d'Azyr bundle), and a defining landmark of the Papez circuit of memory. On the basal surface of the brain, they are a classic orientation landmark during transbasal, orbitozygomatic, and third-ventricular approaches.

## Anatomy and Boundaries

| Boundary | Structure |
|----------|-----------|
| **Anterior** | Tuber cinereum and infundibulum (pituitary stalk) |
| **Posterior** | Posterior perforated substance, entering the [[interpeduncular-cistern]] |
| **Lateral** | [[cerebral-peduncles]] |
| **Superior** | Third ventricle floor and the [[hypothalamus]] proper |
| **Inferior** | Interpeduncular fossa and [[basilar-artery]] apex at distance |
| **Medial** | Midline — two bodies separated by a narrow median sulcus |

Each body contains a medial and a lateral mammillary nucleus, with the medial receiving the dominant fornix input.

## Connections

1. **Input — Fornix** — the principal afferent pathway: hippocampal formation → fornix → mammillary body. The postcommissural fornix terminates primarily in the medial mammillary nucleus.
2. **Output — Mammillothalamic tract (of Vicq d'Azyr)** — ascends to the anterior nucleus of the [[thalamus]], which then projects to the cingulate cortex — completing the Papez circuit.
3. **Mammillotegmental tract** — descends to the midbrain tegmental nuclei (Gudden's nuclei) — implicated in arousal and visceral integration.
4. **Reciprocal connections** with the brainstem tegmentum via the dorsal longitudinal fasciculus.

## Surgical Relevance

- **[[interpeduncular-cistern]] dissection** — the mammillary bodies form the anterior wall of this cistern and are the first midline landmark encountered when approaching the [[basilar-artery]] apex via a [[pterional-approach]] or orbitozygomatic route.
- **Transbasal and transcallosal–interforniceal approaches** — during anterior third-ventricle exposure, identifying the mammillary bodies in the floor orients the surgeon with respect to the aqueductal recess and tuber cinereum.
- **Endoscopic third ventriculostomy (ETV)** — the fenestration is made in the tuber cinereum **anterior** to the mammillary bodies and **posterior** to the infundibular recess; misidentification risks basilar artery injury.
- **Craniopharyngioma surgery** — retrochiasmatic craniopharyngiomas can displace or invade the mammillary bodies and the tuberoinfundibular hypothalamus; preservation protects memory and autonomic function.

## Clinical Significance

- **Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome** — thiamine deficiency produces hemorrhagic necrosis of the mammillary bodies and the periaqueductal gray, with anterograde amnesia, confabulation, ocular findings, and ataxia. MRI shows T2 hyperintensity and later atrophy of the mammillary bodies.
- **Papez circuit and memory** — bilateral mammillary body lesions (surgical, vascular, or nutritional) produce profound anterograde amnesia via disconnection from the anterior [[thalamus]] and cingulate.
- **Thalamic infarcts of the anterior nucleus** — lesions of the mammillothalamic tract produce similar memory deficits.
- **Imaging marker** — mammillary body volume on MRI is a biomarker in Alzheimer disease, chronic alcoholism, and temporal lobe epilepsy.

## Relations

- Ventral surface landmark between the infundibulum (anterior) and the [[interpeduncular-cistern]] (posterior)
- Immediately rostral to the posterior perforated substance and the origins of the [[thalamoperforating-arteries]]
- Medial to the [[cerebral-peduncles]]
- Below the floor of the third ventricle and the [[hypothalamus]]
- Terminus of the [[fornix]] columns
