# Cerebrum

The cerebrum is the largest part of the brain. The paired cerebral hemispheres are separated by the longitudinal fissure, interconnected by the corpus callosum, and merged with the diencephalon to establish continuity with the brainstem and spinal cord. They encase the lateral and third ventricles.

## Hemispheric Organization

Each hemisphere has:
- **3 surfaces** — lateral, medial, basal
- **3 margins** — superior, inferior, medial
- **3 poles** — frontal, temporal, occipital
- **3 white matter fiber types** — projection, commissural, association
- **5 lobes** — frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital, and the hidden insula

The greatest transverse diameter is across the parietal lobes. The most important surgical landmarks are the three borders, three poles, the [[sylvian-fissure]], interhemispheric fissure, and central sulcus.

## Deep Structure Localization

Understanding the surface projection of deep structures is critical for surgical planning — a "see-through x-ray" knowledge:

| Deep Structure | Surface Landmark |
|---|---|
| Frontal horn | Deep to inferior frontal gyrus |
| Atrium (lateral ventricle) | Deep to supramarginal gyrus |
| Temporal horn | Deep to middle temporal gyrus |
| Foramen of Monro (cranial) | ~2 cm above pterion, behind lower 1/3 coronal suture |
| Foramen of Monro (cortical) | Deep to pars opercularis of inferior frontal gyrus |
| Foramen of Monro (insular) | Deep to central part of 2nd short insular gyrus |
| Pineal | Level of posterior middle temporal gyrus |
| Thalamus | Deep to lower pre/postcentral gyri and superior temporal sulcus |

## The Falx and Interhemispheric Fissure

The longitudinal fissure contains the falx cerebri — a sickle-shaped dural process separating the hemispheres. The anterior falx is narrower than posterior, leaving a wide space anteriorly between its free margin and corpus callosum. The anterior cingulate gyrus is below the free margin and can shift across midline (subfalcine herniation). The middle and posterior parts have progressively less freedom.

## Surgical Corridors

The supratentorial area provides natural pathways to deep lesions:

- **[[sylvian-fissure]]** — most frequent pathway; accesses insula, basal ganglia, uncus, anterior cranial fossa, [[circle-of-willis]], [[internal-carotid-artery]] and branches, upper basilar artery
- **Interhemispheric fissure** — accesses subcallosal area, anterior third ventricle, corpus callosum lesions, pineal region (posterior approach has no bridging veins)
- **Subtemporal corridor** — below temporal lobe base; accesses tentorial incisura from uncus to ambient cistern junction
- **Transcortical/transcallosal** — through cortex or corpus callosum to lateral ventricle and thalamus
- **Subfrontal** — below orbital surface to cribriform plate, optic nerves, chiasmatic cisterns

## Lobes

- **Frontal** — anterior to central sulcus, above [[sylvian-fissure]]; contains motor cortex, Broca's area
- **Parietal** — between central sulcus and parieto-occipital sulcus; sensory cortex, supramarginal and angular gyri
- **Temporal** — below [[sylvian-fissure]]; contains auditory cortex, hippocampus, amygdala
- **Occipital** — posterior to parieto-occipital sulcus; visual cortex along calcarine fissure
- **Insula** — hidden deep within [[sylvian-fissure]]; covered by frontal, parietal, and temporal opercula

## Relations

- Separated from posterior fossa by tentorium cerebelli
- Vascular supply: [[internal-carotid-artery]] → [[anterior-cerebral-artery]] + [[middle-cerebral-artery]] (anterior circulation); [[posterior-cerebral-artery]] (posterior circulation)
- Venous drainage: superficial and deep venous systems → dural sinuses
- Contains lateral ventricles (see ventricles chapter)
