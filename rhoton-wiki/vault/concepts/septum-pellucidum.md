# Septum Pellucidum

The septum pellucidum is a thin, translucent double membrane of glia and ependyma separating the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|two lateral ventricles]] in the midline. It stretches between the [[corpus-callosum]] (above) and the [[fornix]] body (below). The space between the two septal leaves — the **cavum septum pellucidum** — is present in fetal life and persists in ~15% of adults. The septum pellucidum is a critical landmark during [[interhemispheric-approach|transcallosal]] approaches to the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|lateral ventricle]].

## Anatomy

### Boundaries
- **Superior** — attached to the undersurface of the [[corpus-callosum]] body
- **Inferior** — attached to the superior surface of the [[fornix]] body
- **Anterior** — genu of the [[corpus-callosum]]
- **Posterior** — splenium of the [[corpus-callosum]] (narrows to a point at the columns of the [[fornix]])

### Cavum Septum Pellucidum
- Space between the two septal leaves
- Present in all fetuses; obliterates after birth in ~85%
- Persistent cavum is a normal variant; incidental finding on MRI
- **Cavum vergae** — posterior extension of the cavum behind the [[fornix|fornical columns]] into the trigone region

### Structure
- Two thin membranes of glial tissue covered by ependyma
- Contains no cortical neurons — not true brain tissue
- Small septal veins drain to the [[internal-cerebral-veins|thalamostriate vein]]

## Surgical Significance

### Transcallosal Approach
- During [[interhemispheric-approach|interhemispheric transcallosal]] approaches, the [[corpus-callosum]] is opened in the midline
- The septum pellucidum is the first structure seen after callosotomy — confirms midline entry
- Opening the septum provides access to the contralateral [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|lateral ventricle]]
- If a cavum is present, it may be entered instead of the ventricle — the surgeon must recognize this and open the cavum wall to reach the ventricular system

### Colloid Cyst Surgery
- Colloid cysts arise at the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|foramen of Monro]] between the [[fornix|fornical columns]]
- Transcallosal approach traverses the septum pellucidum to access the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|lateral ventricle]] body, then follows the septum inferiorly to the foramen of Monro

### Landmark for Midline
- The septum pellucidum confirms midline orientation during intraventricular surgery
- Absent or fenestrated septum → common in hydrocephalus (pressure erosion)
- Deviation of septum → mass effect from one lateral ventricle (indicator of midline shift)
