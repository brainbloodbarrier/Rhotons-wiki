# Temporal Lobe

The temporal lobe is the inferolateral lobe of the cerebrum, occupying the middle cranial fossa and bounded superiorly by the [[sylvian-fissure]]. It is the cortical home of the auditory system, mesial memory circuitry, and the dominant-hemisphere receptive-language network. The temporal lobe is the single most surgically operated cerebral lobe — epilepsy, vascular (MCA, PCA), and skull-base (cavernous sinus, tentorial-incisura) surgery all require its mastery.

## Boundaries

| Boundary | Structure |
|----------|-----------|
| **Superior** | [[sylvian-fissure]] separating temporal from frontal + parietal |
| **Anterior** | Temporal pole, anterior to sphenoid ridge |
| **Medial** | [[uncus]], [[parahippocampal-gyrus]], and the [[basal-cisterns]] around the midbrain |
| **Posterior** | Parieto-occipital transition (preoccipital notch to top of sylvian fissure line) |
| **Inferior** | Temporal base on middle cranial fossa floor |
| **Lateral** | Convexity cortex under temporal squama of skull |

## Surface Anatomy

### Lateral Surface (Convexity)

- **Superior temporal gyrus (STG)**: between sylvian fissure (superior) and superior temporal sulcus
- **Middle temporal gyrus (MTG)**: between superior and inferior temporal sulci
- **Inferior temporal gyrus (ITG)**: between inferior temporal sulcus and inferolateral edge

### Inferior Surface (Basal)

- **Fusiform gyrus (occipitotemporal lateral)**: between inferior temporal sulcus (lateral) and collateral sulcus (medial)
- **Lingual gyrus (occipitotemporal medial, posterior)**: continues posteriorly into occipital lobe

### Medial Surface

- **[[uncus]]**: anterior hook overlying the CN III and cerebral peduncle
- **[[parahippocampal-gyrus]]**: extends posteriorly to the isthmus of cingulate gyrus
- **[[hippocampus]]**: medial floor of the temporal horn, superomedial to parahippocampal gyrus

### Opercular / Insular Surface

- The **temporal operculum** forms the inferior bank of the sylvian fissure
- Lifting the operculum exposes the **[[insula]]** — the central operculum of the middle cerebral artery

## Cortical Functional Areas

| Area | Location | Function |
|------|----------|----------|
| **Primary auditory (Heschl, BA 41/42)** | Transverse gyri on superior bank of STG inside the sylvian fissure | Audition |
| **Wernicke area (BA 22 posterior STG)** | Posterior STG + temporo-parietal junction | Receptive language (dominant) |
| **Entorhinal cortex (BA 28/34)** | Medial: uncus + anterior PHG | Memory input to hippocampus |
| **Fusiform face area (FFA)** | Mid fusiform gyrus | Face recognition |
| **Parahippocampal place area** | Posterior PHG | Scene/place recognition |

## Ventricular and Vascular Relationships

### Temporal Horn

- The temporal horn of the lateral ventricle extends through the temporal lobe
- **Roof**: tail of the caudate and stria terminalis
- **Medial wall**: [[hippocampus]]
- **Floor**: collateral eminence (overlying collateral sulcus)
- **Anterior tip**: amygdala

### Vascular

- **Middle cerebral artery (M2–M4)**: insular + opercular branches supplying most of the lateral convexity
- **Anterior choroidal artery**: deep cisternal branches to uncus and parahippocampal gyrus; intraventricular segment along choroidal fissure to hippocampus
- **[[posterior-cerebral-artery]] (P2–P4)**: supplies inferior/medial surfaces through anterior, middle, and posterior temporal arteries
- **Vein of Labbé**: drains lateral temporal cortex into transverse sinus — its sacrifice is a principal cause of venous infarction in temporal surgery
- **Basal vein of Rosenthal**: drains medial temporal lobe toward vein of Galen

## Surgical Relevance

### Temporal Lobe Epilepsy Surgery

- **Anterior temporal lobectomy (ATL)**: en bloc 4–4.5 cm dominant / 5–6 cm non-dominant temporal resection with hippocampectomy
- **Selective amygdalohippocampectomy** (transsylvian, subtemporal, or transcortical): for mesial temporal sclerosis
- **Meyer's loop** (optic radiations) crosses superolateral to the temporal horn — over-aggressive lateral resection produces superior quadrantanopia

### Vascular

- **MCA bifurcation aneurysms**: approached via transsylvian split; temporal operculum retracted
- **PCA (P2/P3) aneurysms**: subtemporal or transtemporal-ambient corridors
- **Temporal cavernoma and AVM**: lesion-guided resections; functional mapping if dominant

### Skull-Base

- **Subtemporal approach** to petroclival region: temporal lobe retraction over the middle fossa floor — vein of Labbé preservation is critical
- **Transtemporal approaches** (transpetrosal variants): drilling through the temporal bone below temporal lobe
- **Temporal base meningiomas** (sphenoid-wing, middle-fossa): temporal-lobe retraction and mobilization

### Tumor

- **Gliomas** (especially in dominant temporal cortex) require awake mapping — Wernicke and basal temporal language areas
- **Metastases** favor gray-white junction on lateral convexity
- **Primary CNS lymphoma** has a predilection for deep temporal / periventricular sites

## Dominant vs Non-Dominant

- **Dominant (typically left)**: Wernicke, auditory language, naming; functional mapping essential for any non-marginal resection
- **Non-dominant (typically right)**: visuospatial, facial recognition (FFA), music processing; more tolerant of lobectomy
- Handedness and fMRI language lateralization must be documented preoperatively
