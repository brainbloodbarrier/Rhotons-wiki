# Internal Capsule

The internal capsule is a compact white matter lamina carrying virtually all cortical projection fibers — motor, sensory, and visual — between the [[cerebral-lobes|cerebral cortex]] and the [[brainstem]]/spinal cord. Bounded medially by the [[thalamus]] and caudate, and laterally by the [[basal-ganglia|lentiform nucleus]], it is the most eloquent deep structure in the hemisphere and the principal territory at risk from [[lenticulostriate-arteries|lenticulostriate]] hemorrhage and infarction.

## Anatomy

### Parts (axial section)
1. **Anterior limb** — between caudate head and [[basal-ganglia|putamen/globus pallidus]]
   - Frontopontine tract, anterior thalamic radiation
   - Supplied by [[recurrent-artery-of-heubner|Heubner]] and medial [[lenticulostriate-arteries]]
2. **Genu** — the bend at the level of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|foramen of Monro]]
   - Corticobulbar fibers (face/tongue motor)
   - Supplied by direct [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] perforators and medial [[lenticulostriate-arteries]]
3. **Posterior limb** — between [[thalamus]] and [[basal-ganglia|lentiform nucleus]]
   - **Corticospinal tract** (somatotopic: arm anterior, leg posterior)
   - Sensory (thalamocortical) fibers posteriorly
   - Supplied by lateral [[lenticulostriate-arteries]] (anterior part) and [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] (posterior part)
4. **Retrolenticular part** — posterior to lentiform nucleus
   - [[optic-radiation]] fibers
   - Supplied by [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] and [[posterior-choroidal-arteries|posterior choroidal]]
5. **Sublenticular part** — inferior to lentiform nucleus
   - Auditory radiation, temporal corticopontine fibers
   - Supplied by [[anterior-choroidal-artery]]

### Three-Dimensional Course
- Fibers converge from the corona radiata superiorly into the compact capsule
- Below the capsule, fibers enter the [[brainstem|cerebral peduncle]] of the midbrain
- The capsule forms an inverted V (or chevron) on axial imaging

## Vascular Supply

| Region | Arterial supply |
|---|---|
| Anterior limb | [[recurrent-artery-of-heubner]], medial [[lenticulostriate-arteries]] |
| Genu | [[internal-carotid-artery]] perforators, medial [[lenticulostriate-arteries]] |
| Posterior limb (anterior) | Lateral [[lenticulostriate-arteries]] from [[middle-cerebral-artery|M1]] |
| Posterior limb (posterior) | [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] |
| Retrolenticular | [[anterior-choroidal-artery]], [[posterior-choroidal-arteries]] |
| Sublenticular | [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] |

- The [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] supply to the posterior limb is the single most critical perforator territory — occlusion produces contralateral hemiplegia, hemianesthesia, and hemianopia (the "AChA syndrome")
- [[lenticulostriate-arteries]] are end-arteries with no anastomoses — occlusion produces lacunar infarcts

## Surgical Significance

### Hypertensive Hemorrhage
- The most common site of hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage is the [[basal-ganglia|putamen]], with hematoma extending into and destroying the internal capsule
- Lateral [[lenticulostriate-arteries|lenticulostriate]] rupture is the usual cause
- Produces contralateral hemiplegia acutely

### Insular/Basal Ganglia Surgery
- The internal capsule forms the medial limit of safe resection for [[insula|insular gliomas]]
- Subcortical stimulation mapping at 2-5 mA identifies the corticospinal tract
- The [[sylvian-fissure|transsylvian]] approach keeps the dissection lateral to the capsule

### Aneurysm Surgery
- [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]] aneurysm clipping must preserve [[lenticulostriate-arteries]] to protect the anterior capsule
- [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] must be identified and preserved during [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] bifurcation and [[posterior-communicating-artery|PCoA]] aneurysm surgery
- [[anterior-communicating-artery|ACoA]] aneurysm — [[recurrent-artery-of-heubner]] supplies the anterior limb

### Deep Brain Stimulation
- DBS electrodes for the subthalamic nucleus or [[basal-ganglia|globus pallidus internus]] traverse the internal capsule
- Stimulation spread to capsular fibers produces tonic muscle contractions — used to confirm proximity

## Functional Localization

| Tract | Capsular location | Deficit if injured |
|---|---|---|
| Corticobulbar | Genu | Contralateral face/tongue weakness |
| Corticospinal (arm) | Posterior limb (anterior) | Contralateral arm hemiparesis |
| Corticospinal (leg) | Posterior limb (posterior) | Contralateral leg hemiparesis |
| Thalamocortical sensory | Posterior limb (posterior) | Contralateral hemianesthesia |
| Optic radiation | Retrolenticular | Contralateral hemianopia |
