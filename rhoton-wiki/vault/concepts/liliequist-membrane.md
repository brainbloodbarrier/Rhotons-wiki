# Liliequist Membrane

The Liliequist membrane is an arachnoid sheet spanning the [[interpeduncular-cistern]] between the dorsum sellae and the anterior surface of the [[brainstem|midbrain]]. It separates the chiasmatic/carotid cisterns (above) from the interpeduncular cistern (below) and is a critical surgical landmark during approaches to the basilar apex, [[sellar-region]], and [[lamina-terminalis]].

## Anatomy

### Components
The membrane is classically described as having two leaves:

| Leaf | Attachment | Separates |
|---|---|---|
| **Diencephalic (anterior) leaf** | From dorsum sellae/posterior clinoid processes to mamillary bodies | [[chiasmatic-cistern]] above from interpeduncular cistern below |
| **Mesencephalic (posterior) leaf** | From mamillary bodies to anterior midbrain surface (cerebral peduncles) | Interpeduncular cistern from [[prepontine-cistern]] |

### Variations
The membrane varies from a robust, nearly impermeable sheet to a fenestrated, incomplete web. Its thickness and completeness affect CSF circulation between the suprasellar and [[posterior-fossa]] cisterns.

## Cisternal Relationships

The Liliequist membrane is the boundary between several [[basal-cisterns]]:

```
                    Chiasmatic cistern
                          |
            ── Diencephalic leaf ──
                          |
               Interpeduncular cistern
                          |
            ── Mesencephalic leaf ──
                          |
                  Prepontine cistern
```

- **Above (anterior)**: chiasmatic cistern containing the [[optic-chiasm]], [[internal-carotid-artery]] bifurcation, A1 segments of [[anterior-cerebral-artery]]
- **Between leaves**: interpeduncular cistern containing the [[basilar-artery]] apex, [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]], [[posterior-cerebral-artery]] P1, [[thalamoperforating-arteries]]
- **Below (posterior)**: prepontine cistern containing the [[basilar-artery]] trunk, [[abducens-nerve|CN VI]]

## Neurovascular Relations

The membrane is pierced by or lies adjacent to:

- **[[basilar-artery]] bifurcation** — at or just behind the diencephalic leaf
- **[[oculomotor-nerve]]** — CN III passes through the membrane laterally
- **[[posterior-cerebral-artery]] (P1)** — courses above the membrane after the basilar bifurcation
- **[[superior-cerebellar-artery]]** — courses below or through the membrane
- **[[thalamoperforating-arteries]]** — ascend through the posterior perforated substance above the membrane
- **[[posterior-communicating-artery]]** — courses above the diencephalic leaf

## Surgical Significance

### Basilar Apex Aneurysm Surgery
During [[pterional-approach]] or [[orbitozygomatic-approach]] for basilar apex aneurysms:

1. The [[sylvian-fissure]] is opened
2. The carotid-oculomotor triangle is entered
3. **The Liliequist membrane must be opened** to expose the basilar bifurcation and P1 segments
4. Opening the membrane releases CSF, providing brain relaxation
5. The [[thalamoperforating-arteries]] are visualized behind the membrane

### Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy
During ETV via the [[lamina-terminalis]] or tuber cinereum, the Liliequist membrane below the third ventricle floor must be fenestrated to establish communication between the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|ventricular system]] and the prepontine cistern.

### Transsphenoidal/Endonasal Surgery
In extended [[transsphenoidal-approach]] and [[endoscopic-endonasal-approach]], the membrane is encountered when working posterior to the sellar region toward the interpeduncular cistern. Its identification confirms entry into the correct cisternal plane.

### CSF Dynamics
An intact Liliequist membrane can:
- Compartmentalize CSF flow between the supratentorial and infratentorial cisterns
- Contribute to **non-communicating hydrocephalus** in some cases (membrane acts as a valve)
- Affect the distribution of subarachnoid hemorrhage
