# Bridging Veins

Bridging veins are the superficial cortical veins that traverse the subdural space — a free segment of 1–2 cm — from their bed in the pia-arachnoid to their entry point into the [[dural-venous-sinuses]]. They are a principal surgical obstacle in craniotomy approaches and a major source of subdural hematomas when torn.

## Four Drainage Groups

The superficial [[cerebral-veins]] collect into four groups of bridging veins based on their target sinus:

### 1. Superior Sagittal Group
- **Drains into**: superior sagittal sinus
- **Territory**: superior parts of medial and lateral surfaces of the frontal, parietal, and occipital lobes; anterior orbital surface of frontal lobe
- **Key feature**: commonly an area **devoid of bridging veins** just in front of the coronal suture — Rhoton's preferred site for the [[interhemispheric-approach|transcallosal approach]] (craniotomy flap placed one-third behind and two-thirds in front of the coronal suture)
- **Includes**: anterior, middle, and posterior frontal veins; central, postcentral, and parietal veins

### 2. Sphenoidal Group
- **Drains into**: sphenoparietal or [[cavernous-sinus]]
- **Territory**: frontal, temporal, and parietal lobes adjoining the [[sylvian-fissure]]
- **Formed by**: terminal ends of the superficial sylvian veins and occasionally the deep sylvian veins
- **Less commonly**: drains into sphenobasal or sphenopetrosal sinuses

### 3. Tentorial Group
- **Drains into**: tentorial sinuses, transverse sinus, [[dural-venous-sinuses|superior petrosal sinus]]
- **Territory**: lateral surface of temporal lobe; basal surface of temporal and occipital lobes
- **Includes**: temporobasal veins, occipitobasal veins, and descending veins including the **vein of Labbé**
- **Key feature**: these veins converge on the preoccipital notch

### 4. Falcine Group
- **Drains into**: inferior sagittal sinus, straight sinus, or their tributaries
- **Territory**: medial hemispheric surfaces
- **Includes**: cortical veins reaching the straight sinus via the [[internal-cerebral-veins]], [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal|basal veins]], or [[vein-of-galen]]

## Vein of Labbé

The vein of Labbé (inferior anastomotic vein) is the most important named bridging vein. It courses across the lateral temporal surface from the [[sylvian-fissure]] to the transverse sinus. It is the dominant venous drainage of the temporal lobe in many individuals.

- **Surgical risk**: temporal lobe elevation during the [[subtemporal-approach]], [[middle-fossa-approach]], or temporal lobectomy may stretch or avulse the vein of Labbé
- **Sacrifice**: can produce temporal lobe venous infarction with hemorrhagic transformation

## Vein of Trolard

The vein of Trolard (superior anastomotic vein) courses from the [[sylvian-fissure]] superiorly to the superior sagittal sinus across the lateral convexity. When large, it drains the majority of the lateral frontal and parietal surfaces.

## Subdural Segment

Each bridging vein has a **free subdural segment** (1–2 cm) between its exit from the pia-arachnoid and its entry into the sinus. This segment is:

- Vulnerable to tearing during brain shift (trauma → subdural hematoma)
- At risk during cortical retraction in craniotomy approaches
- Oriented obliquely, entering the sinuses at an acute angle against the direction of blood flow

## Surgical Significance

### Craniotomy Planning
The number and distribution of bridging veins vary considerably between patients. In limited surgical exposures, it is difficult to assess how significant the anastomotic channels are. Sacrificing a single large bridging vein that drains an entire lobe is far more likely to produce venous infarction than sacrificing a smaller vein in a territory served by multiple parallel channels.

### Approach-Specific Risks

| Approach | Bridging Vein Risk |
|---|---|
| [[interhemispheric-approach]] | Superior sagittal group; coronal suture landmark for vein-free zone |
| [[pterional-approach]] | Sphenoidal group (superficial sylvian veins) |
| [[subtemporal-approach]] | Tentorial group, vein of Labbé |
| [[middle-fossa-approach]] | Vein of Labbé, temporal bridging veins |
| [[supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach]] | Tentorial group (cerebellar bridging veins to tentorium) |
| Parasagittal craniotomy | Superior sagittal group; central and postcentral veins |

### Venous Infarction
Sacrifice of bridging veins can produce **venous infarction** with hemorrhagic transformation. The risk depends on:
- Size and dominance of the sacrificed vein
- Adequacy of anastomotic drainage
- Whether the vein drains a large territory as a single trunk
