# Internal Carotid Artery

The internal carotid artery (ICA) is one of the principal arteries supplying the brain. Its supraclinoid portion (C4) is a common site of intracranial aneurysms, and its branches are frequently stretched, displaced, or encased by [[cranial-base]] tumors.

## Segments

The ICA is divided into four segments:

- **C1 (cervical)** — from the common carotid bifurcation to the external orifice of the carotid canal
- **C2 (petrous)** — courses within the carotid canal, ends where the artery enters the [[cavernous-sinus]]
- **C3 (cavernous)** — courses within the [[cavernous-sinus]], ends where the artery passes through the dural roof
- **C4 (supraclinoid)** — enters the subarachnoid space and terminates at the bifurcation into the [[anterior-cerebral-artery]] (ACA) and [[middle-cerebral-artery]] (MCA)

The C3 and C4 segments together form the **carotid siphon**, an S-shaped configuration visible on lateral view: the lower half (intracavernous) is convex anteriorly, the upper half (supraclinoid) is convex posteriorly.

## C4 Course and Relations

The C4 segment emerges from the dural roof of the [[cavernous-sinus]], passes along the medial side of the [[anterior-clinoid-process]] and below the optic nerve. It courses posterior, superior, and slightly lateral to reach the lateral side of the [[optic-chiasm]], then bifurcates below the [[anterior-perforated-substance]] at the medial end of the [[sylvian-fissure]].

## Branches (Prebifurcation)

The C4 gives rise to five named branches before its terminal bifurcation:

1. **[[ophthalmic-artery]]** — difficult to expose due to short intradural length beneath the optic nerve
2. **[[posterior-communicating-artery]]** (PCoA) — directed posteromedially above the oculomotor nerve toward the [[interpeduncular-cistern|interpeduncular fossa]]
3. **[[anterior-choroidal-artery]]** (AChA) — arises from the posterior wall of C4, passes laterally around the cerebral peduncle into the temporal horn
4. **[[superior-hypophyseal-artery]]** — supplies the [[pituitary-gland|pituitary]] stalk and adjacent structures
5. **[[perforating-arteries]]** — small branches to surrounding neural structures

During surgical exposure, the AChA is typically seen before the PCoA despite arising more distally, due to its more lateral origin on the C4 wall and its lateral course.

## Surgical Exposure

The intradural exposure of C4 and the anterior [[circle-of-willis]] is directed along the ipsilateral [[sphenoid-bone|sphenoid]] ridge or orbital roof to the anterior clinoid process. The approach proceeds proximal-to-distal, beginning with the ophthalmic segment and working toward the bifurcation.

Key operative relationships:
- The [[posterior-communicating-artery]] is visible through the **opticocarotid triangle** (between the ICA, optic nerve, and A1)
- The [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] ascends on the medial side of the [[uncus]]
- The bifurcation lies below the anterior perforated substance

## Clinical Significance

- **Aneurysms** at the ICA bifurcation, PCoA origin, and ophthalmic segment are among the most common intracranial aneurysms
- **Cranial base tumors** (sphenoid ridge meningiomas, pituitary adenomas) frequently encase or displace the ICA
- Agenesis or aplasia of the ICA is rare

## Relations

- Principal vessel of the [[anterior-circulation]]
- Bifurcates into [[anterior-cerebral-artery]] and [[middle-cerebral-artery]]
- Gives rise to [[posterior-communicating-artery]], [[anterior-choroidal-artery]], ophthalmic artery
- C3 segment traverses the [[cavernous-sinus]] — see [[cavernous-sinus-contents]]
- [[optic-nerve|CN II]] medial in the [[optic-canal]]; [[optic-chiasm]] above
- [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]] related at the PCoA origin
- Completes the [[circle-of-willis]] laterally
- Exposed by [[pterional-approach]], [[transsphenoidal-approach]], [[interhemispheric-approach]]
