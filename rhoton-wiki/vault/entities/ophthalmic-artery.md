# Ophthalmic Artery

The ophthalmic artery is the first major intradural branch of the [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] (C4 supraclinoid segment). It supplies the [[orbit]], [[optic-nerve|optic nerve]], retina, and adjacent structures. Its origin marks the transition from the intracavernous to the supraclinoid ICA and is a critical landmark in anterior skull base surgery.

## Origin and Course

1. **Origin** — arises from the medial or superior surface of the supraclinoid [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]], just distal to the upper dural ring, beneath the anterior clinoid process
2. **Optic canal** — enters the canal inferolateral to the [[optic-nerve]], traveling within the optic sheath
3. **Orbital entry** — enters the [[orbit]] on the lateral side of the optic nerve, then crosses medially above the nerve (90% of cases) or below it (10%)
4. **Orbital course** — courses along the medial orbital wall between the superior oblique and medial rectus muscles, giving off its terminal branches

## Branches

### Ocular branches
- **Central retinal artery** — enters the optic nerve ~12 mm behind the globe; sole supply to the inner retina. Occlusion causes permanent blindness
- **Short and long posterior ciliary arteries** — supply the choroid and outer retina
- **Anterior ciliary arteries** — via the rectus muscles

### Orbital branches
- **Lacrimal artery** — to the lacrimal gland and upper eyelid; gives the recurrent meningeal artery that anastomoses with the middle meningeal artery
- **Muscular branches** — to extraocular muscles
- **Supraorbital artery** — exits supraorbital foramen to the forehead
- **Posterior and anterior ethmoidal arteries** — pass through the ethmoidal foramina in the medial orbital wall to supply the ethmoid sinuses, nasal cavity, and dura of the anterior cranial fossa. The anterior ethmoidal artery is a landmark for the frontoethmoidal suture line

### Meningeal branch
- **Recurrent meningeal artery** — passes back through the superior orbital fissure to supply the dura of the anterior cranial fossa; located anterior to the [[optic-nerve]] in the optic canal

## Surgical Significance

### Anterior clinoidectomy
Removing the anterior clinoid process unroofs the optic canal and exposes the ophthalmic artery origin from the ICA. Critical for paraclinoid [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] aneurysm surgery and optic nerve decompression.

### Pterional approach ([[pterional-approach]])
The ophthalmic artery origin is visible at the ICA just below the anterior clinoid. The artery enters the optic canal with the [[optic-nerve]] — both must be preserved during dissection around the opticocarotid triangle.

### Transsphenoidal approach ([[transsphenoidal-approach]])
The ophthalmic artery is at risk when the sphenoid sinus is broadly pneumatized laterally toward the optic canal. Preoperative CT must define the relationship of the sphenoid sinus septae to the optic canal and ICA prominences.

### Ethmoidal arteries and anterior skull base
The anterior ethmoidal artery is the key landmark during endoscopic endonasal approaches — it marks the level of the anterior skull base floor (cribriform plate). Its injury during sinus surgery can cause orbital hematoma.

### Bypass surgery
The ophthalmic artery (superficial temporal artery → ophthalmic artery bypass) is occasionally used for revascularization when the ICA must be sacrificed.

## Relations

- First intradural branch of [[internal-carotid-artery]] — see [[anterior-circulation]]
- Enters [[orbit]] through optic canal with [[optic-nerve|CN II]]
- Crosses above the [[optic-nerve]] in most cases
- Related to [[cavernous-sinus]] (origin at clinoid transition)
- Ethmoidal branches related to [[cranial-base]] (anterior fossa floor)
- Recurrent meningeal branch anastomoses with middle meningeal artery
- Exposed by [[pterional-approach]], anterior clinoidectomy
- See [[circle-of-willis]] — ICA branches proximal to bifurcation
- Central retinal artery = sole inner retinal supply (no collateral)
