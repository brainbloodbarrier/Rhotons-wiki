# Sigmoid Sinus

The sigmoid sinus is an S-shaped [[dural-venous-sinuses|dural venous sinus]] that descends from the transverse-sigmoid junction on the inner surface of the mastoid portion of the [[temporal-bone]] to the [[jugular-foramen]], where it becomes the jugular bulb. It is a critical landmark for [[posterior-fossa]] craniotomy placement and a boundary for multiple surgical approaches.

## Course

| Segment | Location | Landmark |
|---|---|---|
| **Transverse-sigmoid junction** | Posterior surface of petrous/mastoid bone | Asterion (junction of lambdoid, occipitomastoid, parietomastoid sutures) |
| **Descending (vertical) segment** | Sigmoid groove in the mastoid bone | Behind the mastoid antrum |
| **Inferior turn** | Curves medially toward jugular foramen | At the posterior margin of the [[jugular-foramen]] |
| **Jugular bulb** | Dilated terminus within the jugular foramen | Transitions to internal jugular vein |

## Surface Landmarks

- **Asterion** — most commonly overlies the lower half of the transverse-sigmoid junction; the key surface landmark for posterior fossa craniotomy
- **Mastoid emissary vein foramen** — a small foramen in the mastoid bone connecting the sigmoid sinus to the occipital veins; its presence during drilling signals proximity to the sinus
- **Digastric groove** — the inferior limit of the sigmoid sinus corresponds to the digastric groove on the mastoid tip

## Tributaries

| Tributary | Source |
|---|---|
| **[[transverse-sinus]]** | Main inflow; drains the [[vein-of-galen]] → straight sinus and superior sagittal sinus via torcular |
| **[[superior-petrosal-vein]]** | Drains the [[cerebellopontine-cistern]]; enters at the transverse-sigmoid junction |
| **Superior petrosal sinus** | Drains the [[cavernous-sinus]]; enters along the [[tentorium-cerebelli|tentorial]] attachment at the petrous ridge |
| **Mastoid emissary vein** | Connects to extracranial occipital veins |
| **Posterior condylar emissary vein** | Connects to the vertebral venous plexus |
| **Posterior fossa [[bridging-veins]]** | Cerebellar surface drainage |

## Surgical Significance

### [[retrosigmoid-approach]]
The sigmoid sinus is the **anterior boundary** of the retrosigmoid craniotomy. The bone flap is placed just posterior to the sigmoid sinus, and the dura is opened behind it to access the [[cerebellopontine-cistern]].

- **Sinus identification** is confirmed by the blue discoloration of the dura or by the mastoid emissary vein during drilling
- Injury to the sigmoid sinus causes significant venous bleeding and risks air embolism

### [[posterior-petrosectomy]]
The retrolabyrinthine variant works in the presigmoid space (Trautmann's triangle) — between the sigmoid sinus posteriorly and the labyrinth anteriorly. The translabyrinthine variant requires the sigmoid sinus to be skeletonized and retracted.

### [[far-lateral-approach]]
The inferior sigmoid sinus and its transition to the jugular bulb are encountered during far-lateral approaches that extend anteriorly toward the [[jugular-foramen]].

### Craniotomy Placement
The sigmoid sinus must be identified during any posterior fossa craniotomy to avoid inadvertent sinus injury:

| Approach | Sigmoid Sinus Role |
|---|---|
| [[retrosigmoid-approach]] | Anterior boundary of exposure |
| [[posterior-petrosectomy]] | Posterior boundary of presigmoid space |
| [[far-lateral-approach]] | Inferior extent at jugular bulb |
| [[middle-fossa-approach]] | Superior petrosal sinus marks superior limit |

## Dominance

The right transverse-sigmoid system is dominant in ~60% of individuals. In cases of unilateral dominance, the dominant sigmoid sinus must be preserved — its occlusion can cause severe venous hypertension and venous infarction.

## Clinical Significance

### Dural Arteriovenous Fistulae
The transverse-sigmoid junction is the most common site for intracranial dAVFs. Feeders include the [[occipital-artery]], [[middle-meningeal-artery]], and [[ascending-pharyngeal-artery]].

### Thrombosis
Sigmoid sinus thrombosis causes increased intracranial pressure, headache, and may extend to the jugular bulb. Often associated with mastoiditis (Lemierre syndrome variant).
