# Anterior Cerebral Artery

The anterior cerebral artery (ACA) is the smaller of the two terminal branches of the [[internal-carotid-artery]]. It arises at the medial end of the [[sylvian-fissure]], lateral to the [[optic-chiasm]] and below the [[anterior-perforated-substance]].

## Course

The ACA courses anteromedially above the [[optic-nerve]] or chiasm and below the medial olfactory stria to enter the interhemispheric fissure. Near the fissure entrance, it is joined to the opposite ACA by the [[anterior-communicating-artery]] (ACoA). It then ascends in front of the [[lamina-terminalis]], curves around the genu of the [[corpus-callosum]], and passes backward above the corpus callosum in the pericallosal cistern.

The paired ACAs are typically not side by side as they ascend — rather, one lies in the concavity of the other. The ACA terminates as a fine vessel around the splenium of the corpus callosum, ending in the [[choroid-plexus]] of the roof of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|third ventricle]].

## Segments

The ACA is divided at the ACoA into proximal and distal parts:

- **A1 (precommunicating)** — from ICA bifurcation to the ACoA. Length: 7.2–18.0 mm (avg 12.7 mm). Courses above the optic chiasm (70%) or optic nerves (30%)
- **A2 (infracallosal)** — begins at the ACoA, passes anterior to the lamina terminalis
- **A3 (precallosal)** — curves around the genu of the corpus callosum
- **A4 (supracallosal)** — courses above the body of the corpus callosum
- **A5 (posterocallosal)** — terminal segment around the splenium

## A1 Segment and the Anterior Communicating Artery

The A1 courses above the [[optic-chiasm]] or optic nerves to join the [[anterior-communicating-artery]]. The ACoA length is usually 2–3 mm (range 0.3–7.0 mm). A normal ACA-ACoA complex connects A1 segments of nearly equal size, allowing cross-circulation between the two carotid arteries through the anterior [[circle-of-willis]].

The **[[recurrent-artery-of-heubner]]** is the largest perforating branch of the ACA. It typically arises from the A1–A2 junction or proximal A2, courses laterally above the A1, and enters the anterior perforated substance to supply the head of the [[basal-ganglia|caudate nucleus]], anterior limb of the [[internal-capsule]], and anterior part of the putamen.

## Surgical Relevance

Branches of the distal ACA are exposed in approaches to:
- Sellar and chiasmatic regions
- Third and lateral ventricles
- [[falx-cerebri|Falx]] and parasagittal areas
- Medial parieto-occipital and pineal regions

The A1 and ACoA complex is one of the most common sites of intracranial aneurysms. During [[pterional-approach]] surgery, the ACoA is exposed through the opticocarotid triangle between the [[internal-carotid-artery]], A1, and optic nerve.

## Relations

- Arises from the [[internal-carotid-artery]] bifurcation
- Connected to contralateral ACA via [[anterior-communicating-artery]]
- Courses above the [[optic-chiasm]]
- Enters the interhemispheric fissure alongside the opposite ACA
- Terminal branches anastomose with [[posterior-cerebral-artery]] around the splenium
