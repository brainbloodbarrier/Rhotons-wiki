# Recurrent Artery of Heubner

The recurrent artery of Heubner is the largest named [[perforating-arteries|perforating artery]] of the [[anterior-circulation]]. It arises from the proximal [[anterior-cerebral-artery|ACA]] A2 segment (or A1-A2 junction) and courses **recurrently** (backward) along A1 to enter the anteromedial [[anterior-perforated-substance]]. It is the perforator most frequently injured during [[anterior-communicating-artery|ACoA]] [[aneurysm-surgery]].

## Origin

- Most commonly arises from the A2 segment within 4 mm of the ACoA (60–78% of hemispheres)
- May arise from A1 (14%) or from the ACoA itself (8%)
- Present bilaterally in virtually all brains; may be duplicated in 10–15%
- Diameter: 0.5–1.5 mm (average ~1 mm) — the largest of the ACA perforators

## Course

1. **Arises from A2** — on the inferior or posterior surface, just distal to the [[anterior-communicating-artery|ACoA]]
2. **Courses recurrently** — turns back along A1, running parallel and adjacent to it (may lie above, below, or behind A1)
3. **Enters the [[anterior-perforated-substance]]** — at the anteromedial portion, near the olfactory trigone
4. **Penetrates upward** — through the anterior perforated substance into the [[basal-ganglia]]

The recurrent course makes the Heubner artery easy to confuse with a cortical branch or to inadvertently incorporate into an aneurysm clip.

## Supply Territory

- **Caudate head** — the primary territory (largest supply)
- **Anterior putamen**
- **Anterior limb of the [[internal-capsule]]** ([[basal-ganglia]])
- **Nucleus accumbens**
- **Anterior part of the globus pallidus** (variable)
- **Subcallosal area and septal nuclei** (variable)

## Surgical Significance

### ACoA aneurysm surgery ([[pterional-approach]])
The Heubner artery is the perforator most frequently injured in ACoA aneurysm clipping:
- It courses parallel to A1, making it appear as a small cortical branch that can be ignored
- It may be adherent to the aneurysm fundus or neck
- The clip blades may kink, occlude, or shear the Heubner if its course is not identified before clip application
- **Preservation protocol:** identify the Heubner bilaterally before placing the clip; after clipping, confirm flow (ICG angiography, micro-Doppler)

### Interhemispheric approach ([[interhemispheric-approach]])
In the anterior interhemispheric approach for ACoA aneurysms, the Heubner artery is identified along the A1-A2 junction in the interhemispheric fissure.

### Clinical deficit from occlusion
- **Contralateral face and arm weakness** (more than leg — caudate head and anterior internal capsule territory)
- **Behavioral changes** — abulia, personality changes (caudate head dysfunction)
- **Dysarthria** — from caudate/putaminal involvement
- The pattern differs from cortical ACA territory infarction (which causes leg weakness)

## Differentiation from Other ACA Perforators

| Perforator | Origin | Territory | Risk setting |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Heubner** | ACA A2 (or A1-A2) | Caudate head, anterior internal capsule | [[anterior-communicating-artery|ACoA]] aneurysm |
| Medial [[lenticulostriate-arteries|lenticulostriates]] | ACA A1 | Medial caudate, anterior internal capsule | A1 manipulation |
| [[anterior-communicating-artery|ACoA]] perforators | ACoA | [[hypothalamus]], [[fornix]], anterior commissure | ACoA aneurysm (memory deficits) |
| Frontopolar artery | ACA A2 | Frontal pole cortex | NOT a perforator — cortical branch |

## Relations

- Arises from [[anterior-cerebral-artery|ACA]] A2 near the [[anterior-communicating-artery|ACoA]] — see [[anterior-circulation]]
- Courses recurrently along ACA A1
- Enters the [[anterior-perforated-substance]] anteromedially
- Supplies [[basal-ganglia]] (caudate head, anterior putamen, anterior internal capsule)
- At risk in [[pterional-approach]] (ACoA aneurysm) and [[interhemispheric-approach]]
- Part of the [[perforating-arteries]] system
- Parallels the [[lenticulostriate-arteries]] territory (lateral lenticulostriates from [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]])
- Related to [[olfactory-nerve]] (olfactory trigone near the entry point)
- Related to [[circle-of-willis]] (ACoA complex)
- See [[surgical-approaches-overview]]
