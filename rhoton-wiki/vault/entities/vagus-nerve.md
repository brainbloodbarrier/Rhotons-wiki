# Vagus Nerve (CN X)

The **vagus nerve** is the middle member of the lower cranial nerve triad (CN IX–X–XI) and by far the most physiologically complex cranial nerve. It is the chief parasympathetic outflow to the thorax and abdomen, the branchial-motor supply to the pharynx and larynx, and the afferent limb of numerous visceral reflexes. In cranial surgery CN X is the functionally dominant nerve of the [[jugular-foramen]] — injury produces vocal cord paralysis, dysphagia, and hemodynamic instability.

## Brainstem Origin and Course

1. **Nuclear column** — four medullary nuclei: dorsal motor nucleus of X (parasympathetic preganglionics), nucleus ambiguus (branchial motor to pharynx, larynx, palate), solitary nucleus (visceral sensation and taste from epiglottis), and spinal trigeminal nucleus (somatic afferents from external auditory canal via Arnold's nerve).
2. **Rootlets** — emerge from the **postolivary sulcus** of the medulla in series with [[glossopharyngeal-nerve|CN IX]] above and the cranial root of CN XI below, forming a continuous rootlet stack.
3. **Cisternal segment** — crosses the lateral [[cerebellomedullary-fissure]] toward the jugular foramen, closely related to the [[pica|PICA]] caudal and tonsillohemispheric loops; vascular compression of the root exit zone is the substrate of refractory hypertension and, rarely, vagoglossopharyngeal neuralgia.
4. **Jugular foramen** — exits through the **pars vascularis** (posterolateral compartment) together with CN XI and the jugular bulb; separated from the pars nervosa (CN IX) by the intrajugular dural septum.
5. **Extracranial segment** — descends within the carotid sheath between the internal carotid artery and internal jugular vein, gives off the superior laryngeal and recurrent laryngeal branches, and continues to the thoracic and abdominal viscera.

## Surgical Anatomy

- **[[retrosigmoid-approach]]** — CN X sits between [[glossopharyngeal-nerve|CN IX]] and CN XI in the lower cranial nerve triangle; tumor resection requires gentle superomedial retraction to avoid traction injury and postoperative dysphagia.
- **[[far-lateral-approach]]** — the nerve is the lateral boundary of the lateral medullary cistern; identification before drilling the jugular tubercle prevents injury during foramen magnum meningioma resection.
- **[[jugular-foramen]] tumors** — schwannomas and paragangliomas classically enlarge the pars vascularis; preoperative vocal cord assessment is mandatory, and intraoperative EMG monitoring of the vocalis muscle via endotracheal electrodes guides dissection.
- **Brainstem cavernomas** — the **postolivary sulcus** is the safe entry zone (SEZ) for lateral medullary cavernomas entered via the [[far-lateral-approach]]; drilling the jugular tubercle widens the working angle.

## Clinical Significance

- **Recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy** — isolated hoarseness with vocal cord paralysis; the **left** nerve loops around the aortic arch (longer course, more vulnerable) and the **right** around the subclavian artery.
- **Vernet syndrome** — jugular foramen lesion producing CN IX/X/XI deficit: dysphagia, dysphonia, trapezius weakness.
- **Vasovagal syncope and autonomic storm** — unintended CN X stimulation during jugular foramen or carotid-body surgery can trigger profound bradycardia and hypotension.
- **Vagus nerve stimulator (VNS)** — chronic cervical stimulation is an adjunctive therapy for refractory epilepsy and depression.
- **Gag reflex efferent limb** — CN IX afferent → CN X efferent; absent gag reflex localizes to the medulla or skull base.

## Relations

- Caudal to [[glossopharyngeal-nerve]] and rostral to the spinal rootlets of the accessory nerve along the postolivary row
- Below [[vestibulocochlear-nerve|CN VIII]] and [[facial-nerve|CN VII]] in the [[cerebellopontine-cistern]]
- Lateral to the [[vertebral-artery]] intradural segment and medial to the sigmoid sinus
- Traverses the lateral medullary cistern in the [[posterior-fossa]]
