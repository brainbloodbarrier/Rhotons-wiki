# Superficial Temporal Artery

The superficial temporal artery (STA) is the smaller terminal branch of the [[external-carotid-artery]], coursing over the zygomatic root anterior to the ear to supply the lateral scalp and temporal region. It is the most commonly used **donor vessel** for extracranial-intracranial (EC-IC) bypass to the [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]] and the first artery encountered during the [[pterional-approach]], [[orbitozygomatic-approach]], and all frontotemporal craniotomies. Its preservation or intentional harvest is a key early decision in every lateral [[cranial-base|skull base]] procedure.

## Anatomy

### Course
1. **Origin** — from the ECA bifurcation within the parotid gland, posterior to the mandibular condyle
2. **Preauricular segment** — crosses the zygomatic root superficial to the temporalis fascia, 1-1.5 cm anterior to the tragus; palpable pulse
3. **Bifurcation** — divides into frontal and parietal branches approximately 2-3 cm above the zygomatic arch

### Branches
- **Frontal branch** — courses anterosuperiorly across the forehead; used as the primary bypass donor (larger caliber, ~1.5-2 mm)
- **Parietal branch** — courses posterosuperiorly over the parietal scalp; secondary bypass option
- **Middle temporal artery** — perforates the temporalis fascia to supply the temporalis muscle; encountered during interfascial dissection
- **Transverse facial artery** — courses anteriorly below the zygomatic arch (may arise from ECA before STA)
- **Zygomatico-orbital artery** — courses along the superior zygomatic arch toward the [[orbit]]

### Relationship to Facial Nerve
- The **frontal (temporal) branch of [[facial-nerve|CN VII]]** courses within or just deep to the superficial temporal fascia (temporoparietal fascia)
- The STA frontal branch and the CN VII frontal branch travel in close proximity over the zygomatic arch
- The interfascial dissection technique protects CN VII during [[pterional-approach|pterional]] and [[orbitozygomatic-approach|orbitozygomatic]] craniotomies by staying between the two leaves of the temporalis fascia

## Surgical Significance

### EC-IC Bypass (STA-MCA)
- The **STA-MCA bypass** is the standard low-flow revascularization procedure
- The STA frontal branch (donor) is anastomosed end-to-side to a cortical [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]] branch (M4)
- Indications: moyamoya disease, complex aneurysms requiring parent vessel sacrifice, refractory hemodynamic ischemia
- The STA must be **harvested with a fascial cuff** to prevent spasm and intimal damage
- Flow: 15-25 mL/min (low-flow bypass); sufficient for cortical territory but not the entire MCA distribution
- For high-flow bypass, the [[maxillary-artery]] or radial artery graft to MCA is used instead

### Pterional and Frontotemporal Craniotomies
- The STA is the first artery encountered in the skin incision
- **If bypass is planned**, the STA is carefully dissected free with a cuff of galea and preserved
- **If bypass is not planned**, the STA branches are typically divided with the scalp flap
- Preoperative Doppler mapping identifies the STA course and bifurcation

### Craniotomy Flap Vascularity
- The STA is the dominant blood supply to the frontotemporal scalp flap
- Injury to both STA branches risks flap necrosis, especially in patients with prior surgery or radiation
- In redo craniotomies, the STA pedicle of the scalp flap must be identified and preserved

### Scalp Reconstruction
- STA-based rotational flaps are used for scalp defect coverage
- The parietal branch can supply a large parietal/occipital flap
