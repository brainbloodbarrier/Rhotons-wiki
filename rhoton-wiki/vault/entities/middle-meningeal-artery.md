# Middle Meningeal Artery

The middle meningeal artery (MMA) is the largest meningeal artery, supplying the dura mater ([[meninges]]) of the middle cranial fossa and adjacent convexity. It enters the skull through the foramen spinosum ([[skull-base-foramina]]) of the [[sphenoid-bone]] greater wing and courses on the inner surface of the [[temporal-bone]] squama. Its epidural position and groove in the temporal bone make it the artery most commonly involved in **epidural hematomas** from temporal skull fractures.

## Origin and Course

### Extracranial
Arises from the first part of the [[maxillary-artery]] (terminal branch of the [[external-carotid-artery|external carotid]]) in the [[infratemporal-fossa]]. Ascends between the sphenomandibular ligament and lateral pterygoid muscle to reach the foramen spinosum.

### Intracranial
1. Enters through the **foramen spinosum** ([[sphenoid-bone]] greater wing)
2. Courses laterally in the **epidural space** (between the periosteal dura and inner table of the skull)
3. Grooves the inner surface of the temporal squama — the groove is the key landmark for the artery's position in the middle fossa
4. Divides into **anterior (frontal)** and **posterior (parietal)** branches at the pterion (junction of frontal, parietal, temporal, and sphenoid bones)

### Branches
- **Anterior (frontal) branch** — larger; courses toward the vertex along the coronal suture region; the branch most commonly torn in pterional fractures → epidural hematoma
- **Posterior (parietal) branch** — courses posterosuperiorly
- **Petrosal branch** — enters the hiatus of the [[greater-petrosal-nerve]] to supply the geniculate ganglion region of [[facial-nerve|CN VII]] (anastomosis with the stylomastoid branch of the posterior auricular artery)
- **Superior tympanic artery** — to the middle ear
- **Orbital branch** — enters the [[orbit]] through the superior orbital fissure (anastomosis with [[ophthalmic-artery]] recurrent meningeal branch)

## Surgical Significance

### Epidural hematoma
Rupture of the MMA (usually the anterior branch) from a temporal bone fracture at the pterion is the classic cause of epidural hematoma. The lucid interval (temporary improvement before deterioration) occurs as the arterial hemorrhage progressively compresses the [[cerebral-lobes|temporal lobe]] → uncal [[herniation-syndromes|herniation]].

### Pterional approach ([[pterional-approach]])
The MMA is encountered during frontotemporal [[surgical-approaches-overview|craniotomy]] — it may need to be coagulated and divided where it crosses the surgical exposure. The artery grooves the bone at the pterion region.

### Middle fossa approach ([[middle-fossa-approach]])
The MMA enters the middle fossa through the foramen spinosum at the anterolateral floor — a landmark for the Glasscock triangle in the [[infratemporal-fossa]]. It is identified and coagulated when elevating the dura from the middle fossa floor.

### Anterior petrosectomy ([[anterior-petrosectomy]])
The foramen spinosum (MMA entry) is an anterolateral landmark in the middle fossa floor, near foramen ovale ([[trigeminal-nerve|V3]]). It helps orient the surgeon to the middle fossa anatomy.

### Dural arteriovenous fistula (dAVF) treatment
The MMA is the most common feeding artery in convexity and transverse-sigmoid dAVFs. Transarterial embolization via the MMA is a primary treatment for these lesions.

### Meningioma supply
Convexity and sphenoid wing [[meninges|meningiomas]] receive their principal blood supply from MMA branches. Preoperative embolization of the MMA reduces intraoperative bleeding.

## Relations

- Branch of the maxillary artery from the [[infratemporal-fossa]]
- Enters through foramen spinosum ([[sphenoid-bone]], [[skull-base-foramina]])
- Courses on the inner [[temporal-bone]] squama (epidural)
- Divides at the pterion — landmark for [[pterional-approach]] craniotomy
- Anastomoses with [[ophthalmic-artery]] (recurrent meningeal branch) at the [[orbit]]
- Petrosal branch supplies [[facial-nerve|CN VII]] geniculate region
- Supplies the [[meninges]] (dura mater) of the middle fossa and convexity
- At risk in [[pterional-approach]], [[middle-fossa-approach]], [[orbitozygomatic-approach]]
- Rupture causes epidural hematoma → [[herniation-syndromes|uncal herniation]]
- [[pterygopalatine-fossa]] (maxillary artery origin) is the extracranial source
- See [[cranial-base]] and [[dural-venous-sinuses]] for related meningeal anatomy
