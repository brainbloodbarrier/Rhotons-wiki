# Superior Petrosal Vein

The superior petrosal vein (vein of Dandy) is the most important bridging vein of the [[posterior-fossa]], draining the anterior (petrosal) group of [[posterior-fossa-veins]] into the [[dural-venous-sinuses|superior petrosal sinus]]. It converges near the [[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]] root entry zone and crosses the operative corridor in the [[retrosigmoid-approach]], making it the most surgically critical vein in cerebellopontine angle surgery.

## Anatomy

### Formation
Formed by the confluence of tributaries near the [[trigeminal-nerve|trigeminal nerve]] posterior root:
- **Transverse pontine vein** — from the ventral pons
- **Vein of the middle cerebellar peduncle** — from the lateral [[cerebellum]] (petrosal surface)
- **Vein of the cerebellopontine fissure** — from the fissure between [[cerebellum]] and pons
- **Pontotrigeminal vein** — from the region around CN V
- **Common floccular vein** — from the flocculus near [[facial-nerve|CN VII]]/[[vestibulocochlear-nerve|CN VIII]]

### Course
The tributaries unite into 1–3 trunks that course superiorly to drain into the **[[dural-venous-sinuses|superior petrosal sinus]]** at the petrous ridge of the [[temporal-bone]], near [[meckels-cave]].

### Variations
- May consist of a single large trunk or 2–3 smaller veins
- The point of entry into the superior petrosal sinus is variable — medial, lateral, or at the level of CN V porus

## Surgical Significance

### Retrosigmoid approach ([[retrosigmoid-approach]])
The superior petrosal vein crosses the surgical corridor between the [[cerebellum]] and the [[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]] root. During microvascular decompression for trigeminal neuralgia:
- The vein may **block access** to the CN V root entry zone where the [[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]] compresses the nerve
- **Preservation is preferred** — sacrifice can cause cerebellar venous infarction (petrosal surface)
- When sacrifice is unavoidable, it should be coagulated and divided **at the sinus entry point** (not at the brainstem origin) to preserve collateral drainage
- Multiple tributaries are safer to sacrifice than a single dominant trunk

### Vestibular schwannoma surgery
The superior petrosal vein is encountered in the superior part of the exposure during [[retrosigmoid-approach]] tumor resection. Large tumors may displace or stretch the vein.

### Venous infarction risk
Sacrifice of a dominant superior petrosal vein without adequate collateral drainage causes **hemorrhagic venous infarction** of the petrosal cerebellar surface and lateral [[brainstem]] — a potentially devastating complication with cerebellar swelling, brainstem compression, and hydrocephalus.

## Relations

- Drains into [[dural-venous-sinuses|superior petrosal sinus]] at the [[temporal-bone]] petrous ridge
- Converges near [[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]] posterior root and [[meckels-cave]]
- Part of the anterior (petrosal) group of [[posterior-fossa-veins]]
- Drains the petrosal surface of the [[cerebellum]]
- Related to [[cerebellopontine-cistern]] — the veins course through it
- [[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]] courses nearby (above CN V, below the vein)
- [[aica]] and [[labyrinthine-artery]] in the lower part of the exposure
- Crosses the corridor of the [[retrosigmoid-approach]]
- Complementary venous drainage to [[vein-of-galen]] (superior/galenic group)
- See [[cranial-nerves-overview]] and [[surgical-approaches-overview]]
