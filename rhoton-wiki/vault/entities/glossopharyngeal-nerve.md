# Glossopharyngeal Nerve (CN IX)

The **glossopharyngeal nerve** is the most rostral of the [[lower-cranial-nerves]], carrying mixed motor, sensory, and parasympathetic fibers. It is the rostral neighbor of the vagus (CN X) and accessory (CN XI) nerves, with which it shares the lower cranial nerve rootlet complex and the [[jugular-foramen]]. CN IX is the key landmark of the middle neurovascular complex of the [[posterior-fossa]] and a primary actor in glossopharyngeal neuralgia, Collet–Sicard syndrome, and the Vernet syndrome of jugular foramen lesions.

## Brainstem Origin and Course

1. **Nucleus column** — four nuclei in the medulla: nucleus ambiguus (motor to stylopharyngeus), inferior salivatory nucleus (parasympathetic to parotid via otic ganglion), solitary nucleus (taste and carotid body/sinus afferents), and spinal trigeminal nucleus (somatic sensation).
2. **Rootlets** — emerge from the **postolivary sulcus** of the medulla, dorsal to the olive, as the highest of the postolivary rootlet row (CN IX, X, XI from rostral to caudal).
3. **Cisternal segment** — traverses the lateral [[cerebellomedullary-fissure]] toward the jugular foramen, crossing beneath the flocculus and above [[pica|PICA]] (whose choroidal and tonsillohemispheric loops are highly variable here).
4. **Jugular foramen** — exits through the **pars nervosa** (anteromedial compartment) together with the inferior petrosal sinus; separated by a dural septum from CN X/XI and the jugular bulb in the pars vascularis.
5. **Extracranial segment** — descends between the internal carotid artery and internal jugular vein, contributes Jacobson's nerve to the middle-ear tympanic plexus, and terminates in the posterior third of the tongue and the stylopharyngeus.

## Surgical Anatomy

- **[[retrosigmoid-approach]]** — CN IX is the rostral boundary of the lower-cranial-nerve triangle encountered during MVD for glossopharyngeal neuralgia and during resection of vestibular schwannomas extending inferiorly.
- **[[far-lateral-approach]]** — the nerve lies above and anterior to the vertebral artery's intradural segment; careful preservation during foramen magnum meningioma resection prevents postoperative dysphagia.
- **[[jugular-foramen]]** exposure — the pars nervosa must be identified before drilling the jugular tubercle; Jacobson's nerve is the endoscopic landmark in infralabyrinthine approaches.
- **Glossopharyngeal neuralgia** — vascular compression at the root exit zone (most often by [[pica|PICA]]) produces lancinating throat and ear pain triggered by swallowing; microvascular decompression is the definitive treatment.

## Clinical Significance

- **Vernet syndrome** — isolated CN IX/X/XI palsy from a jugular foramen lesion (schwannoma, paraganglioma) — dysphagia, vocal cord paralysis, trapezius weakness.
- **Collet–Sicard syndrome** — CN IX–XII palsy from a skull-base or high-cervical process.
- **Carotid sinus reflex** — CN IX afferents from the carotid sinus mediate the syncope triggered by manipulation of the [[internal-carotid-artery]] bifurcation.
- **Gag reflex** — CN IX afferent limb (CN X efferent); loss is a bedside sign of brainstem or skull-base pathology.

## Relations

- Rostral to the vagus (CN X) and accessory (CN XI) nerves along the postolivary row; together they form the CN IX–X–XI rootlet complex of the [[lower-cranial-nerves]]
- Below the [[vestibulocochlear-nerve|CN VIII]] and [[facial-nerve|CN VII]] complex in the [[cerebellopontine-cistern]]
- Medial to the sigmoid sinus and lateral to the [[vertebral-artery]] intradural segment
- Traverses the [[posterior-fossa]] cisterns along the lateral medullary cistern
