# Anterior Spinal Artery

The anterior spinal artery (ASA) is formed by the union of paired branches from the distal [[vertebral-artery]] (V4 segment), near the vertebrobasilar junction. It descends as a single midline trunk in the anterior median fissure of the spinal cord and medulla, providing the principal arterial supply to the anterior two-thirds of the spinal cord.

## Origin

- Arises from the medial surface of each [[vertebral-artery]] near the vertebrobasilar junction
- The paired anterior spinal arteries unite in the midline to form a single trunk
- The origin is often **asymmetric** — one vertebral artery contribution is larger than the other, predominantly from the left [[vertebral-artery]]
- The union occurs at or just above the level of the [[foramen-magnum]]

## Course

1. **Paired origins** — from the medial V4 segments of the [[vertebral-artery|vertebral arteries]]
2. **Descend and unite** — merge into a single trunk at or near the anterior surface of the cervicomedullary junction
3. **Anterior median fissure** — the single trunk descends along the entire length of the spinal cord in the midline groove between the medullary pyramids (brainstem) and the anterior funiculi (spinal cord)
4. **Reinforced segmentally** — receives radiculomedullary arteries at various spinal levels; the largest is the artery of Adamkiewicz (T9-T12 level)

## Supply Territory

### At the foramen magnum level
- **Medullary pyramids** — corticospinal tracts crossing at the pyramidal decussation
- **Median anterior medullary surface** — the anterior column of the medulla

### Along the spinal cord
- **Anterior two-thirds** of the spinal cord (via sulcal/central arteries entering the anterior median fissure):
  - Anterior horns (motor neurons)
  - Lateral corticospinal tracts
  - Spinothalamic tracts
  - Anterior funiculi

The **posterior one-third** is supplied separately by the paired posterior spinal arteries.

## Surgical Significance

### Far-lateral approach ([[far-lateral-approach]])
The ASA is visible on the anterior surface of the medulla after opening the dura at the [[foramen-magnum]]. It marks the midline and must be preserved during dissection of ventral foramen magnum lesions.

### Foramen magnum meningiomas
Ventral meningiomas at the [[foramen-magnum]] are intimately related to the ASA. The artery may be displaced, stretched, or encased by the tumor. Sacrifice causes anterior spinal artery syndrome.

### Vertebrobasilar junction surgery
The ASA origin from the vertebral arteries is visible near the vertebrobasilar junction — exposed during [[far-lateral-approach]] or transclival approaches.

### Anterior spinal artery syndrome
Occlusion causes infarction of the anterior two-thirds of the spinal cord:
- **Motor paralysis** (bilateral — anterior horn + corticospinal tracts)
- **Bilateral loss of pain and temperature** (spinothalamic tracts)
- **Preserved proprioception and vibration** (posterior columns supplied by posterior spinal arteries)
- **Bladder dysfunction** (autonomic fibers in the lateral columns)

Causes include aortic surgery, aortic dissection, [[vertebral-artery]] dissection, and foramen magnum surgery.

## Relations

- Arises from [[vertebral-artery]] V4 segments near the vertebrobasilar junction — see [[posterior-circulation]]
- Descends in the anterior median fissure of the [[brainstem]] (medulla) and spinal cord
- Related to the [[foramen-magnum]] (cervicomedullary junction)
- [[basilar-artery]] formed just above the ASA origin by union of the vertebral arteries
- Posterior spinal arteries (also from [[vertebral-artery]]) supply the posterior cord
- [[pica]] origin from the [[vertebral-artery]] is near the ASA origin
- [[lower-cranial-nerves|CN XII]] rootlets emerge between the pyramid and olive, adjacent to the ASA territory
- Exposed by [[far-lateral-approach]] and [[transsphenoidal-approach|transclival]] approaches
- Related to [[meninges]] (pia mater on the cord surface)
- See [[perforating-arteries]] — ASA gives sulcal perforators to the anterior cord
