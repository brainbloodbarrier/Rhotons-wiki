# Superior Hypophyseal Artery

The superior hypophyseal arteries are a group of 1–5 (average 2) small perforating branches arising from the ophthalmic segment (C4) of the [[internal-carotid-artery]]. They supply the [[pituitary-gland|pituitary stalk]], anterior lobe, [[optic-chiasm]], [[optic-nerve|optic nerves]], and floor of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|third ventricle]].

## Origin and Course

- **Origin**: posteromedial, medial, or posterior surface of the C4 ophthalmic segment of the [[internal-carotid-artery]], just distal to the [[ophthalmic-artery]] origin
- **Course**: pass medially below the [[optic-chiasm]] to reach the tuber cinereum and pituitary stalk
- **Number**: 1–5 per hemisphere (average 2); the largest is traditionally called "the superior hypophyseal artery"

### Infundibular Arteries
A separate group of small arteries originating from the [[posterior-communicating-artery]] and distributed to the infundibulum. Present in approximately 25% of hemispheres (1–2 arteries); the remainder have none.

## Circuminfundibular Anastomosis

The superior hypophyseal and infundibular arteries intermingle to form a fine anastomotic plexus around the pituitary stalk — the **circuminfundibular anastomosis**. This plexus gives rise to:

### Descending Arteries
- **Short-stalk arteries** — penetrate the infundibulum, form capillaries leading to sinusoids running down the stalk (portal circulation)
- **Superficial arteries** — course inferiorly on the outside of the stalk in the subarachnoid space, then penetrate the anterior lobe

### Ascending Arteries
- Supply the tuber cinereum, median eminence, and inferior surface of the [[optic-chiasm]]

## Supply Territories

| Territory | Source |
|---|---|
| Pituitary stalk | Short-stalk arteries via portal sinusoids |
| Anterior pituitary lobe | Superficial stalk arteries, portal vessels |
| [[optic-chiasm]] (inferior surface) | Ascending branches |
| [[optic-nerve]] (proximal portions) | Small branches from circuminfundibular plexus |
| Tuber cinereum / median eminence | Ascending arteries |
| Floor of third ventricle | Ascending branches |

The **posterior pituitary lobe** is supplied separately by the inferior hypophyseal artery, a branch of the meningohypophyseal trunk from the intracavernous [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]].

## Aneurysm Surgery

### Superior Hypophyseal Aneurysms
Aneurysms arise on the **medially convex curve** of the ICA lateral to the pituitary stalk, at the distal edge of the superior hypophyseal artery origin. Key features:

- **Direction**: point medially into the space between the inferior surface of the [[optic-chiasm]] and the diaphragma sellae
- **Confusion**: often mistaken for intracavernous aneurysms on lateral angiograms because they project below the level of the [[anterior-clinoid-process]], but they are in the subarachnoid space
- **Perforating risk**: the superior hypophyseal arteries and ophthalmic segment perforators are stretched around the aneurysm neck
- **Approach**: [[pterional-approach]] or [[orbitozygomatic-approach]]; [[anterior-clinoid-process|clinoidectomy]] may be needed for proximal control

### Surgical Preservation
During any paraclinoid aneurysm surgery, the superior hypophyseal arteries must be preserved to avoid:

- **Pituitary dysfunction** — damage to the portal circulation
- **Visual loss** — ischemia of the [[optic-chiasm]] and [[optic-nerve|optic nerves]]

## Connections

- Origin: [[internal-carotid-artery]] (C4 ophthalmic segment)
- Supplies: [[pituitary-gland]], [[optic-chiasm]], [[optic-nerve]]
- Anastomoses with: infundibular arteries from [[posterior-communicating-artery]]
- Forms: circuminfundibular anastomosis (portal system)
- Adjacent to: [[anterior-clinoid-process]], [[sellar-region]], diaphragma sellae
- Aneurysm approach: [[pterional-approach]], [[orbitozygomatic-approach]]
