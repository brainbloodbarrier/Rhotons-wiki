# Superior Cerebellar Artery

The superior cerebellar artery (SCA) arises from the basilar artery near its apex, usually as a single trunk. It is the most rostral of the infratentorial arteries. It is exposed in approaches to the basilar apex, [[tentorial-incisura]], trigeminal nerve, cerebellopontine angle, pineal region, and upper cerebellum.

## Course

1. **Origin** — from basilar artery, near the apex (occasionally duplicate or from proximal [[posterior-cerebral-artery]])
2. **Proximal segment** — passes below the oculomotor nerve (CN III), medial to the tentorial free edge
3. **Lateral segment** — dips caudally, encircles brainstem near pontomesencephalic junction, passing below CN IV (trochlear) and above CN V (trigeminal)
4. **Cerebellomesencephalic fissure** — enters the fissure between cerebellum and midbrain; gives off **precerebellar arteries** to deep cerebellar white matter and dentate nucleus
5. **Cortical segments** — exits fissure and distributes to the tentorial cerebellar surface, passing under the tentorial edge

## Branching Pattern

Usually bifurcates into:
- **Rostral trunk** — supplies vermian and paravermian areas on the tentorial surface
- **Caudal trunk** — supplies the hemisphere

## Posterior Fossa Neurovascular Complexes

The SCA belongs to the **upper complex**, one of three in the [[posterior-fossa]]:

| Complex | Artery | Brainstem | Peduncle | Fissure | Surface | Cranial Nerves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upper | **SCA** | Midbrain | Superior | Cerebellomesencephalic | Tentorial | CN III, IV, V |
| Middle | AICA | Pons | Middle | Cerebellopontine | Petrosal | CN VI, VII, VIII |
| Lower | PICA | Medulla | Inferior | Cerebellomedullary | Suboccipital | CN IX, X, XI, XII |

## Cranial Nerve Relations

The SCA frequently contacts:
- **Oculomotor nerve (CN III)** — SCA passes below it (between PCA above and SCA below)
- **Trochlear nerve (CN IV)** — SCA passes below it in the ambient cistern
- **Trigeminal nerve (CN V)** — SCA passes above it; may cause trigeminal neuralgia via neurovascular compression

## Surgical Relevance

- **Microvascular decompression** for trigeminal neuralgia often involves mobilizing the SCA off CN V
- SCA aneurysms are rare but approached via subtemporal or [[pterional-approach]]
- The cerebellomesencephalic fissure provides a natural surgical corridor (telovelar approach from above)
- SCA branches are at risk during supracerebellar-infratentorial approach to pineal region

## Relations

- Arises from [[basilar-artery]] (near [[posterior-cerebral-artery]] origin)
- Courses between [[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]]/[[trochlear-nerve|CN IV]] above and [[trigeminal-nerve|CN V]] below
- Intimately related to [[tentorial-incisura]] and cerebellomesencephalic fissure
- Supplies tentorial cerebellar surface
- Part of the upper neurovascular complex of [[posterior-fossa]]
- Sister cerebellar arteries: [[aica]] (middle), [[pica]] (lower)
- [[vertebral-artery|Vertebral arteries]] join to form the [[basilar-artery]], which gives rise to the SCA
- Exposed via [[pterional-approach]], [[subtemporal-approach]], [[supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach]]
