# Lenticulostriate Arteries

The lenticulostriate arteries are a group of small perforating branches that arise from the [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]] (lateral group) and [[anterior-cerebral-artery|ACA]] (medial group), pass through the [[anterior-perforated-substance]], and supply the [[basal-ganglia]] and internal capsule. They are the **most surgically critical perforating arteries** in the anterior circulation — their injury during aneurysm clipping or tumor surgery causes immediate contralateral hemiplegia.

## Lateral Lenticulostriates

### Origin
Arise from the superior or posterosuperior surface of the [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]] M1 segment. Typically 6–12 arteries, forming a candelabra-like cluster.

### Course
Pass superiorly from M1 through the [[anterior-perforated-substance]] (lateral portion, near the limen of the [[insula]]). They are end-arteries with **no collateral supply** — each perforator supplies a unique territory.

### Territory
- **Putamen** — the largest supply territory
- **Globus pallidus** (lateral portion)
- **Body of the caudate nucleus** (superolateral portion)
- **Internal capsule** — genu and posterior limb ([[basal-ganglia]])
- **Corona radiata** (inferior portion)

### Surgical anatomy
- The lenticulostriates are often **hidden behind the M1 trunk** — the surgeon must rotate the M1 to see them before clip placement
- They may arise as single trunks that branch distally, or as individual fine arteries directly from M1
- Larger lenticulostriates (up to 1 mm diameter) may be visible on high-resolution angiography, but most are invisible on preoperative imaging
- They are tethered to the brain — they cannot be mobilized or rerouted

## Medial Lenticulostriates

### Origin
Arise from the [[anterior-cerebral-artery|ACA]] A1 segment.

### Course
Pass through the medial portion of the [[anterior-perforated-substance]].

### Territory
- **Caudate head** (medial portion)
- **Anterior limb of the internal capsule**
- **Anterior commissure** (partial)

## Recurrent Artery of Heubner

The largest named perforator of the anterior circulation. Technically a separate entity, but functionally part of the lenticulostriate group.

### Origin
Arises from proximal A2 of the [[anterior-cerebral-artery]] (or the A1-A2 junction). Courses back (recurrently) along A1 to enter the anteromedial [[anterior-perforated-substance]].

### Territory
- **Caudate head** — largest supply
- **Anterior putamen**
- **Anterior internal capsule** (anterior limb)
- **Nucleus accumbens**

### Surgical significance
The Heubner artery is the perforator most frequently injured in [[anterior-communicating-artery|ACoA]] aneurysm surgery. It courses parallel and adjacent to A1, making it easy to confuse with a cortical branch or to incorporate into the clip.

## Surgical Significance

### MCA aneurysm clipping ([[pterional-approach]])
The MCA bifurcation sits at the lateral edge of the [[anterior-perforated-substance]]. Lenticulostriates arise from M1 proximal to the bifurcation and pass upward into the brain **behind** the aneurysm. Before clip placement:
1. Rotate M1 to visualize all perforators arising from the posterior/superior surface
2. Ensure clip blades do not cross, kink, or incorporate any lenticulostriate
3. After clipping, confirm flow in lenticulostriates (micro-Doppler, ICG angiography)

### ICA bifurcation aneurysm
Lenticulostriates from M1 and medial lenticulostriates from A1 surround the ICA bifurcation. The Heubner artery courses near A1.

### Insular glioma surgery
Resection of [[insula|insular tumors]] through the [[sylvian-fissure]] encounters the lenticulostriates at the limen insulae. They define the **medial limit of safe resection** — resecting beyond them enters the internal capsule.

### Hypertensive hemorrhage
Rupture of lenticulostriate arteries is the most common cause of hypertensive putaminal hemorrhage — the most frequent type of spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage. The hemorrhage dissects through the putamen and often ruptures into the internal capsule.

## Clinical Syndromes

| Artery occluded | Deficit |
|---|---|
| Lateral lenticulostriates | Contralateral hemiplegia (internal capsule posterior limb) |
| Medial lenticulostriates | Contralateral arm/face weakness |
| Heubner artery | Contralateral face/arm weakness + behavioral changes (caudate head) |
| Multiple lenticulostriates | Dense hemiplegia + hemisensory loss |

## Relations

- Arise from [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]] M1 (lateral) and [[anterior-cerebral-artery|ACA]] A1 (medial) — see [[anterior-circulation]]
- Pass through [[anterior-perforated-substance]] (limen of [[insula]])
- Supply [[basal-ganglia]] (putamen, globus pallidus, caudate) and internal capsule
- Heubner artery from [[anterior-cerebral-artery|ACA]] A2 — at risk in [[anterior-communicating-artery|ACoA]] surgery
- End-arteries — no collateral, no redundancy — see [[perforating-arteries]]
- At risk in [[pterional-approach]] (MCA/ICA aneurysms), [[interhemispheric-approach]] (ACoA aneurysms)
- [[thalamus]] supplied by separate perforators (thalamoperforating from [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]])
- Rupture causes hypertensive putaminal hemorrhage
- See [[surgical-approaches-overview]] and [[circle-of-willis]]
