# Lower Cranial Nerves (CN IX–XII)

The lower cranial nerves — glossopharyngeal (IX), vagus (X), accessory (XI), and hypoglossal (XII) — arise from the medulla and are intimately related to the [[vertebral-artery]], [[pica]], [[posterior-fossa]], and the jugular foramen/foramen magnum complex. They are the principal neural structures at risk in surgery of the craniocervical junction and [[cerebellopontine-cistern|cerebellopontine angle]].

## CN IX — Glossopharyngeal Nerve

### Origin and course
Arises from the posterolateral medulla, dorsal to the inferior olive, in the **cerebellomedullary cistern**. The rootlets emerge in a linear series between the olive and the inferior cerebellar peduncle, rostral to the vagus rootlets.

### Jugular foramen
Passes through the **petrous part** (anteromedial compartment) of the jugular foramen, separated from CN X and XI by a dural septum and the intrajugular processes. Contains the superior and inferior (petrosal) ganglia within the foramen.

### Relations
- The choroid plexus projecting from the foramen of Luschka sits on the posterior surface of CN IX and X
- The [[pica]] enters the cerebellomedullary cistern by passing between the rootlets of CN IX, X, and XI
- The lateral pontomedullary membrane separates the cerebellomedullary from the [[cerebellopontine-cistern|cerebellopontine cistern]] at the level of CN IX

### Clinical
- **Glossopharyngeal neuralgia** — treated by microvascular decompression (usually [[pica]] or [[aica]] compression)
- Tumors of the jugular foramen (glomus jugulare, schwannomas) compress CN IX early

## CN X — Vagus Nerve

### Origin and course
Arises from the posterolateral medulla immediately caudal to CN IX, with 8–10 rootlets emerging dorsal to the olive. The rootlets converge to form a flat fascicle that crosses the cerebellomedullary cistern to reach the jugular foramen.

### Jugular foramen
Passes through the **intrajugular part** of the jugular foramen along the intrajugular processes, between the petrous (CN IX) and sigmoid (venous) compartments. Contains the jugular (superior) and nodose (inferior) ganglia.

### Relations
- The cranial part of the accessory nerve (rostral medullary rootlets) arises from the vagal nuclei and functionally represents inferior vagal rootlets
- Once inside the jugular foramen, the upper medullary rootlets of CN XI join either the vagus or accessory nerve
- The vagus continues through the carotid sheath (with internal jugular vein and internal carotid artery)

### Clinical
- Vagal schwannomas present at the jugular foramen
- Vocal cord paralysis from CN X injury during skull base surgery
- Vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) targets the cervical vagus

## CN XI — Accessory Nerve

### Origin and course
The most complex of the lower cranial nerves, with dual origin:

**Cranial part** — 3–6 small rootlets emerge from the lower medulla. The most rostral medullary rootlets are functionally inferior vagal rootlets (arise from vagal nuclei). The lower medullary rootlets join the spinal portion.

**Spinal part** — rootlets arise from the cervical spinal cord (C1–C7, most commonly C4–C6), situated midway between the ventral and dorsal rootlets. These unite to form a trunk (~1 mm diameter) that ascends through the foramen magnum between the dentate ligament and dorsal spinal roots, entering the [[posterior-fossa]] **behind** the [[vertebral-artery]].

### Foramen magnum transit
CN XI is the **only cranial nerve that traverses the foramen magnum**. The spinal trunk ascends from the cervical canal, passes behind both the [[pica]] and the [[vertebral-artery]], and courses anterior to the dentate ligament at upper levels but posterior to it at lower levels.

### Connections
All 50 accessory nerves studied had connections with dorsal roots of upper cervical nerves. The C1 dorsal root most commonly arose solely from the accessory nerve (28/50 cases) without a separate spinal cord contribution.

### Jugular foramen
Passes through the intrajugular part with CN X. The cranial part joins the vagus; the spinal part exits to innervate sternocleidomastoid and trapezius.

## CN XII — Hypoglossal Nerve

### Origin and course
Arises from the anterolateral medulla, between the pyramids and inferior olives, in the **premedullary cistern**. Multiple rootlets (typically 10–15) emerge in a linear series and converge toward the hypoglossal canal.

### Hypoglossal canal
Passes through the hypoglossal canal, which is situated above the occipital condyle in the condylar part of the occipital bone. The canal is directed forward and laterally from the [[posterior-fossa]]. It may be partially or completely divided by a bony septum (septated in 6% of skulls).

### Relations
- Rootlets pass **behind** the [[vertebral-artery]] — they are stretched over the posterior aspect of the artery at the foramen magnum level
- Some rootlets pass above and others below the [[pica]] origin
- One rootlet may loop upward around the PICA origin before descending to join the others
- The hypoglossal canal is located above the condyle and medial to the jugular foramen

### Clinical
- CN XII palsy (tongue deviation) — localizing sign for foramen magnum/condylar lesions
- At risk during the [[far-lateral-approach]], particularly with transcondylar extension
- Hypoglossal schwannomas may expand the hypoglossal canal

## Cisterns and Membranes

The lower cranial nerves traverse specific cisternal compartments ([[basal-cisterns]]):

| Nerve | Cistern of origin | Destination |
|---|---|---|
| CN IX, X, XI (cranial) | Cerebellomedullary cistern | Jugular foramen |
| CN XI (spinal) | Posterior spinal cistern → cerebellomedullary cistern | Foramen magnum → jugular foramen |
| CN XII | Premedullary cistern | Hypoglossal canal |

The **lateral pontomedullary membrane** separates the cerebellomedullary from the cerebellopontine cistern at the level of CN IX–XI. The [[pica]] passes dorsally between the rootlets of CN IX–XI to transition from premedullary to cerebellomedullary cisterns.

## Surgical Significance

### Far-lateral approach ([[far-lateral-approach]])
The primary corridor exposing all four lower cranial nerves. CN XI is identified ascending behind the [[vertebral-artery]] through the foramen magnum. CN XII rootlets are seen crossing behind the vertebral artery. CN IX and X are visible at the jugular foramen above.

### Retrosigmoid approach ([[retrosigmoid-approach]])
Exposes CN IX–XI at the lateral cerebellomedullary cistern. Used for microvascular decompression of CN IX (glossopharyngeal neuralgia) and tumor resection.

### Jugular foramen surgery
The jugular foramen is bordered posteriorly by the jugular process of the occipital bone and anteriorly by the jugular fossa of the petrous temporal bone. It is divided by the intrajugular processes into the petrous part (CN IX, inferior petrosal sinus), the intrajugular part (CN X, XI), and the sigmoid part (sigmoid sinus → internal jugular vein).

### Foramen magnum tumors
Meningiomas and schwannomas at the foramen magnum involve all four lower cranial nerves. The [[pica]] and [[vertebral-artery]] are inseparable from the nerve complex and must be preserved during dissection.

## Relations

- Arise from medulla in [[posterior-fossa]]
- Intimately related to [[vertebral-artery]] and [[pica]] at foramen magnum
- Course through [[basal-cisterns]] (cerebellomedullary, premedullary cisterns)
- CN IX–XI pass through jugular foramen; CN XII through hypoglossal canal
- CN XI traverses foramen magnum — only CN to do so
- Related to [[cerebellopontine-cistern]] and [[posterior-fossa-veins]]
- Exposed by [[far-lateral-approach]], [[retrosigmoid-approach]]
- Related to [[cranial-base]] (occipital condyle, jugular tubercle)
- The [[aica]] may course near CN IX–XI at the cerebellopontine angle
- [[basilar-artery]] perforators supply the medullary nuclei of these nerves
