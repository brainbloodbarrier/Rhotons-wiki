# Internal Cerebral Veins

The internal cerebral veins are paired deep venous channels that course through the velum interpositum in the roof of the [[lateral-and-third-ventricles|third ventricle]], collecting drainage from the ventricular walls, [[thalamus]], [[basal-ganglia]], and deep white matter before joining to form the [[vein-of-galen]].

## Formation and the Venous Angle

Each internal cerebral vein arises just behind the foramen of Monro at the confluence of its two principal tributaries:

- **Thalamostriate vein** — courses along the striothalamic sulcus between [[basal-ganglia|caudate nucleus]] and [[thalamus]], turns sharply posteriorly at the foramen of Monro
- **Superior choroidal vein** — runs forward on the [[choroid-plexus]] in the body of the lateral ventricle

The junction of the thalamostriate vein and the internal cerebral vein forms the **venous angle**, a landmark that approximates the position of the foramen of Monro on cerebral angiography (average 1.5 mm from the posterior edge of the foramen).

## Course

### Velum Interpositum
The internal cerebral veins course posteriorly through the **velum interpositum** — a potential space in the roof of the third ventricle between the fornix above and the thalami below. This space:

- Is widest posteriorly (from lower margin of splenium to upper margin of pineal)
- Tapers to a narrow apex just behind the foramen of Monro
- Is bounded by two layers of tela choroidea
- Contains the internal cerebral veins and the [[posterior-choroidal-arteries|medial posterior choroidal arteries]]

### Quadrigeminal Cistern
The paired internal cerebral veins exit the velum interpositum above the pineal body, enter the quadrigeminal cistern, and unite to form the [[vein-of-galen]].

## Tributaries

### From the Frontal Horn
- **Anterior septal veins** — cross the septum pellucidum toward the foramen of Monro
- **Anterior caudate veins** — course on the caudate head, terminate near the foramen of Monro in the thalamostriate or directly in the ICV

### From the Body
- **Thalamostriate vein** — principal lateral tributary; drains the striothalamic sulcus (present in ~90% of hemispheres)
- **Thalamocaudate vein** — courses medially across caudate/thalamus; inversely proportional in size to the thalamostriate
- **Posterior caudate veins** — drain the superolateral angle of the body
- **Posterior septal veins** — course across the septum pellucidum, pierce the fornix to enter the velum interpositum

### From the Atrium
- **Medial atrial veins** — drain the medial wall of the atrium
- **Lateral atrial veins** — may drain into the ICV or the [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal|basal vein]]

### From the Choroid Plexus
- **Superior choroidal vein** — the largest choroidal vein; runs forward on the plexus in the ventricular body
- **Inferior choroidal vein** — courses in the temporal horn; may drain into the [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal|basal vein]] instead

### Posterior Tributaries
- **Posterior pericallosal veins** — drain the posterior [[corpus-callosum]] and cingulate gyrus
- **Internal occipital veins** — drain the medial occipital lobe
- **Anterior calcarine veins** — drain into the posterior end of the ICV near the vein of Galen

## Drainage Territories

| Structure | Via |
|---|---|
| Caudate nucleus | Anterior caudate, thalamostriate veins |
| Thalamus | Thalamostriate, thalamocaudate, posterior thalamic veins |
| Internal capsule | Deep tributaries to thalamostriate |
| Septum pellucidum | Anterior and posterior septal veins |
| Fornix | Septal veins piercing the fornix |
| [[corpus-callosum]] | Posterior pericallosal veins |
| [[choroid-plexus]] | Superior and inferior choroidal veins |
| Deep white matter | Subependymal ventricular veins |

## Surgical Significance

### Transcallosal Approach
The [[interhemispheric-approach|transcallosal approach]] exposes the internal cerebral veins in the roof of the third ventricle after opening the velum interpositum. The tela choroidea between the fornix and the ICV must be opened carefully to access third ventricle tumors.

### Transchoroidal Approach
Opening the choroidal fissure between the fornix and thalamus exposes the velum interpositum containing the ICV and [[posterior-choroidal-arteries|medial posterior choroidal arteries]]. The ICV limits the superior extent of this exposure.

### Pineal Region
The terminal portions of the internal cerebral veins converge above the pineal to form the [[vein-of-galen]]. Both the [[supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach]] and occipital transtentorial approaches must respect this confluence. The [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal|basal veins]] join laterally.

### Venous Infarction Risk
Sacrifice of the internal cerebral vein can produce **bilateral thalamic venous infarction**, causing coma or death. Even unilateral injury may cause severe neurological deficit due to the critical structures drained.

- Adjacent to: [[posterior-choroidal-arteries|medial posterior choroidal arteries]], fornix
