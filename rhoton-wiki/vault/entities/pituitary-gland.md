# Pituitary Gland

The pituitary gland (hypophysis) is located below the center of the brain in the center of the [[cranial-base]], within the sella turcica of the [[sellar-region|sphenoid bone]]. It is the master endocrine gland, connected to the [[hypothalamus]] via the infundibulum (pituitary stalk). Its deep position — surrounded by critical neurovascular structures — makes surgical access challenging and historically drove the development of the [[transsphenoidal-approach]].

## Anatomy

### Anterior lobe (adenohypophysis)
The larger portion (~80% of the gland). Composed of hormone-producing cells: somatotrophs (GH), lactotrophs (prolactin), corticotrophs (ACTH), thyrotrophs (TSH), and gonadotrophs (LH/FSH). The anterior and posterior lobes form separate distinct nodules.

### Posterior lobe (neurohypophysis)
Stores and releases oxytocin and vasopressin (ADH), produced in hypothalamic nuclei and transported down the stalk via axonal transport.

### Infundibulum (pituitary stalk)
Connects the hypothalamus to the gland through the opening in the diaphragma sellae. Contains the hypothalamo-hypophyseal portal system and neurohypophyseal axons. Its preservation is critical during surgery to maintain endocrine function.

## Boundaries and Relations

### Superior
- **Diaphragma sellae** — dural fold roofing the sella with an opening for the stalk
- **[[optic-chiasm]]** and [[optic-nerve|optic nerves]] — directly above; compressed by suprasellar extension of adenomas → bitemporal hemianopia
- **[[anterior-communicating-artery]]** and [[anterior-cerebral-artery|ACA]] A1 — above the chiasm
- **Hypothalamus and third ventricle floor** ([[lateral-and-third-ventricles]])

### Lateral
- **[[cavernous-sinus]]** — on each side; the medial wall separates the gland from the sinus venous channels
- **[[internal-carotid-artery]]** (C3 cavernous segment) — lateral prominences in the [[sellar-region|sphenoid sinus]] wall; at risk in broadly pneumatized sinuses
- **[[oculomotor-nerve|CN III]], [[trochlear-nerve|CN IV]], [[trigeminal-nerve|V1]], [[abducens-nerve|CN VI]]** — in the [[cavernous-sinus]] lateral wall

### Inferior
- **Sellar floor** — thin bone (often dehiscent) separating the gland from the [[paranasal-sinuses|sphenoid sinus]]
- **[[sphenoid-bone|Sphenoid]] sinus** — the surgical corridor of the [[transsphenoidal-approach]]

### Posterior
- **Dorsum sellae** — posterior bony wall of the sella
- **[[basilar-artery]]** and [[brainstem]] — behind the dorsum sellae

## Vascular Supply

### Arterial
- **[[superior-hypophyseal-artery|Superior hypophyseal arteries]]** — arise from the medial side of the supraclinoid [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]]; supply the stalk, [[optic-chiasm]], and anterior lobe via the portal system
- **Inferior hypophyseal arteries** — arise from the meningohypophyseal trunk of the intracavernous ICA (C3 segment in the [[cavernous-sinus]]); supply the posterior lobe and capsule
- **Capsular arteries** — from the [[ophthalmic-artery|ophthalmic]] and superior hypophyseal systems

### Venous
- Drains via the cavernous sinus → [[dural-venous-sinuses|petrosal sinuses]] → internal jugular vein
- The intercavernous sinuses (anterior and posterior) connect bilateral [[cavernous-sinus|cavernous sinuses]] across the sellar floor

## Surgical Significance

### Transsphenoidal approach ([[transsphenoidal-approach]])
The preferred route for pituitary adenomas. The nasal cavity → sphenoid sinus → sellar floor pathway avoids brain retraction. The endoscopic endonasal variant has become the standard technique. Extended approaches reach suprasellar and clival lesions.

### Transcranial approach
Subfrontal or [[pterional-approach]] — used when the tumor extends significantly beyond the sella (large suprasellar component, lateral extension into the [[cavernous-sinus]], or dumbbell-shaped tumors with a narrow waist at the diaphragma).

### Pathology
- **Pituitary adenomas** — most common sellar lesion; microadenomas (<10 mm) or macroadenomas (≥10 mm); compress [[optic-chiasm]] superiorly and may invade [[cavernous-sinus]] laterally
- **Craniopharyngiomas** — arise from Rathke's pouch remnants; may be suprasellar, intrasellar, or both
- **Rathke's cleft cysts** — benign cysts between the anterior and posterior lobes
- **Pituitary apoplexy** — hemorrhage or infarction of an adenoma; surgical emergency if vision is acutely threatened
