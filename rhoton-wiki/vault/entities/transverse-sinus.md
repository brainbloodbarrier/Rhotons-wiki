# Transverse Sinus

The transverse sinus is a paired dural venous channel coursing along the attachment of the [[tentorium-cerebelli]] to the inner surface of the occipital bone, from the torcular Herophili (confluence of sinuses) to the transverse-sigmoid junction. It is the primary venous drainage conduit from the [[dural-venous-sinuses|superior sagittal sinus]] and [[vein-of-galen|straight sinus]] to the [[sigmoid-sinus]] and ultimately the internal jugular vein. Its position defines the inferior limit of supratentorial craniotomies and the superior limit of posterior fossa approaches.

## Anatomy

### Course
1. **Origin** — torcular Herophili (confluence of sinuses) at the internal occipital protuberance
2. **Course** — laterally along the tentorium-occipital bone attachment in the transverse sulcus
3. **Termination** — at the transverse-sigmoid junction, where it curves inferiorly to become the [[sigmoid-sinus]]

### Drainage Pattern
- The **right transverse sinus** is dominant in ~60% of individuals (receives the [[dural-venous-sinuses|superior sagittal sinus]])
- The **left transverse sinus** receives the [[vein-of-galen|straight sinus]] in most cases
- Significant asymmetry is common — one sinus may be hypoplastic or absent in 20-30%
- The torcular Herophili configuration is highly variable

### Tributaries
- **[[dural-venous-sinuses|Superior sagittal sinus]]** → torcular → usually right transverse
- **[[vein-of-galen|Straight sinus]]** → torcular → usually left transverse
- **Superior petrosal sinus** — drains the [[cavernous-sinus]] into the transverse-sigmoid junction
- **[[bridging-veins|Vein of Labbé]]** — the dominant inferior anastomotic vein of the temporal lobe drains into the transverse sinus (critical surgical landmark)
- **Tentorial veins** — drain the [[tentorium-cerebelli|tentorial]] surface
- **Inferior cerebral veins** — from the occipital and temporal lobes

### Dimensions
- Length: approximately 4-6 cm
- Diameter: 8-12 mm (dominant side), 4-8 mm (non-dominant)

## Relationships

### Superior
- Occipital lobe of the [[cerebral-lobes|cerebrum]] — rests on the tentorium above the sinus
- [[tentorium-cerebelli]] — the sinus is enclosed within the tentorial attachment to bone

### Inferior
- [[cerebellum]] — the superior cerebellar surface lies below the tentorium
- [[posterior-fossa]] — the transverse sinus marks the boundary between supratentorial and infratentorial compartments

### Medial
- Torcular Herophili — confluence with contralateral transverse, [[dural-venous-sinuses|superior sagittal]], and [[vein-of-galen|straight sinus]]

### Lateral
- [[sigmoid-sinus]] — continuation at the transverse-sigmoid junction
- Asterion — surface landmark for the transverse-sigmoid junction (junction of lambdoid, occipitomastoid, and parietomastoid sutures)

## Surgical Significance

### Craniotomy Planning
- The transverse sinus defines the **inferior limit** of occipital and parietal craniotomies
- The **asterion** marks the transverse-sigmoid junction — critical for positioning the [[retrosigmoid-approach]] and suboccipital craniotomies
- Pre-operative CT venography or MR venography is essential to assess dominance and asymmetry

### Posterior Fossa Surgery
- [[retrosigmoid-approach]] — craniotomy placed just inferior to the transverse-sigmoid junction
- Suboccipital craniotomy — superior limit is the transverse sinus
- Sinus injury during occipital burr holes → significant hemorrhage

### Vein of Labbé
- The [[bridging-veins|vein of Labbé]] drains into the transverse sinus and **cannot be sacrificed**
- [[subtemporal-approach]] and temporal craniotomies must preserve Labbé
- Temporal lobe retraction that stretches Labbé → venous infarction → hemorrhagic swelling

### Sinus Thrombosis
- Transverse sinus thrombosis → elevated intracranial venous pressure, pseudotumor cerebri pattern
- If the dominant sinus is thrombosed → venous hypertension, papilledema, venous infarction
- Endovascular thrombectomy or anticoagulation may be required

### Dural Arteriovenous Fistulae (dAVF)
- The transverse-sigmoid junction is the most common site of intracranial dAVFs
- Fed by [[occipital-artery]], [[middle-meningeal-artery]], and tentorial branches
- Cortical venous reflux determines hemorrhage risk (Borden/Cognard classification)
