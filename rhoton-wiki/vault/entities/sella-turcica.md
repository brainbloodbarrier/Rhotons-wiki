# Sella Turcica

The **sella turcica** ("Turkish saddle") is the midline bony depression of the superior surface of the [[sphenoid-bone]] that cradles the [[pituitary-gland]]. It is the central recess of the middle cranial base and the anatomical hub of sellar, parasellar, and suprasellar surgery. Every [[transsphenoidal-approach]] and modern [[endoscopic-endonasal-approach]] terminates at the floor of the sella, and each transcranial route — [[pterional-approach]], [[orbitozygomatic-approach]], subfrontal — uses its bony prominences as orientation.

## Bony Boundaries

| Wall | Structure |
|------|-----------|
| **Anterior** | **Tuberculum sellae** and **chiasmatic sulcus**; continuous with the planum sphenoidale; flanked by the [[anterior-clinoid-process|anterior clinoids]] |
| **Posterior** | **Dorsum sellae**, with the paired [[posterior-clinoid-process|posterior clinoids]] at its superior corners |
| **Floor** | Roof of the **sphenoid sinus** (variable pneumatization — conchal / presellar / sellar / postsellar) |
| **Lateral walls** | Medial walls of the paired [[cavernous-sinus]]; paraclinoidal and cavernous segments of the [[internal-carotid-artery]] lie immediately lateral |
| **Roof** | **Diaphragma sellae**, a dural fold pierced centrally by the pituitary stalk |

The distance between the medial walls of the two carotid sieges (the **intercarotid distance**) averages 17–20 mm at the sella and narrows to 12–14 mm at the level of the superior intercavernous sinus — a critical corridor width for endoscopic surgery.

## Contents

- **[[pituitary-gland|Anterior and posterior pituitary lobes]]** occupy the fossa, anchored above by the pituitary stalk piercing the diaphragma sellae.
- **Intercavernous sinuses** (anterior, posterior, inferior) — venous channels coursing through the dural walls of the sella, connecting the two [[cavernous-sinus]]es; their opening releases venous bleeding at sellar floor opening.
- **Arachnoid** extensions occasionally herniate through an incompetent diaphragma, forming a **partial empty sella**.

## Surgical Relevance

- **[[transsphenoidal-approach]] / [[endoscopic-endonasal-approach]]** — the sellar floor is opened centrally; the medial opticocarotid recess (OCR) and lateral OCR (junction of the sellar floor, paraclinoid ICA, and optic canal) are the cardinal extradural landmarks.
- **Extended endonasal exposures** — the tuberculum/planum is removed superiorly for tuberculum meningiomas and suprasellar craniopharyngiomas; the dorsum sellae and posterior clinoids are removed to expose the interpeduncular cistern and basilar apex.
- **Pterional / OZ transcranial exposures** — the [[anterior-clinoid-process]] is removed (intradural or extradural Dolenc) to unroof the paraclinoid ICA and optic nerve; the [[posterior-clinoid-process]] is removed for a posterior clinoidectomy that widens the interpeduncular window.
- **Intercavernous sinus control** — sellar-floor opening must account for the anterior intercavernous sinus running across the superior sellar dura; coagulation or clipping before durotomy is routine.
- **Knosp grading** of cavernous sinus invasion (grade 0–4) orients the surgeon to the resectability of a [[pituitary-gland|pituitary adenoma]] through the medial cavernous wall.

## Clinical Significance

- **Pituitary adenomas** — the dominant sellar pathology; macroadenomas expand the fossa, balloon the diaphragma, compress the [[optic-chiasm]] (bitemporal hemianopia), and may invade the [[cavernous-sinus]] laterally.
- **Empty sella syndrome** — CSF herniation through the diaphragma; primary (idiopathic) or secondary (post-surgery, post-radiation); often incidental.
- **Craniopharyngioma** — intrasellar/suprasellar mass often enlarging the sella asymmetrically, classically in children with hypothalamic-endocrine symptoms.
- **Rathke cleft cyst** — midline intrasellar cyst between pars distalis and pars nervosa.
- **Pituitary apoplexy** — sudden hemorrhage into an adenoma; sellar expansion produces acute headache, ophthalmoplegia (CN III through the medial cavernous wall), and panhypopituitarism.
- **Sellar deformations** — "J-shaped" sella in mucopolysaccharidoses; enlarged sella in chronic hydrocephalus with third ventricular herniation.

## Relations

- Centered on the body of the [[sphenoid-bone]], at the crossroads of the anterior and middle cranial fossae
- Flanked by the paired [[cavernous-sinus]] and paraclinoidal [[internal-carotid-artery]]
- Superiorly continuous with the [[optic-chiasm]] above the diaphragma, forming the [[chiasmatic-cistern]]
- Anteriorly bounded by the tuberculum and [[anterior-clinoid-process]]
- Posteriorly bounded by the dorsum and [[posterior-clinoid-process]]
- Floored by the sphenoid sinus, the principal working corridor of the [[endoscopic-endonasal-approach]]
