# Occipital Artery

The occipital artery is a posterior branch of the [[external-carotid-artery]] that courses through the posterior cervical musculature to supply the posterior scalp. It is a key landmark in the [[far-lateral-approach]] and [[suboccipital-triangle]] dissection, and serves as a potential donor vessel for extracranial-intracranial bypass procedures.

## Course

### Origin and Cervical Course
The occipital artery arises from the posterior external carotid artery in the upper neck, at approximately the level of the [[facial-nerve|facial nerve]] exit from the stylomastoid foramen. It courses posteriorly on the medial side of the posterior belly of the digastric muscle, passing deep to the sternocleidomastoid.

### Suboccipital Course
The artery ascends through the posterior cervical musculature:

- Passes **superficial or deep to the longissimus capitis** (variable)
- Courses **behind the superior oblique** and semispinalis capitis in the [[suboccipital-triangle]] region
- May occupy the **occipital groove** on the medial surface of the mastoid process of the [[temporal-bone]] when this groove is present
- Pierces the deep cervical fascia to reach the posterior scalp

### Scalp Distribution
After piercing the fascia between the sternocleidomastoid and trapezius near the superior nuchal line, the occipital artery ramifies in the posterior scalp, anastomosing with the posterior auricular and superficial temporal arteries.

## Branches

| Branch | Supply |
|---|---|
| **Muscular branches** | Posterior cervical muscles (splenius, semispinalis, obliques, rectus) |
| **Meningeal branches** | [[posterior-fossa]] dura via [[jugular-foramen]] and mastoid foramen |
| **Descending branch** | Deep cervical muscles; anastomoses with deep cervical artery (vertebral system) |
| **Scalp branches** | Posterior scalp |

### Meningeal Branches
Several meningeal branches of the occipital artery ascend to pass through the [[jugular-foramen]] and the mastoid foramen to supply the posterior fossa dura. These may hypertrophy as feeders in dural arteriovenous fistulae (dAVFs) of the transverse-sigmoid junction.

## Surgical Significance

### Far-Lateral Approach
The occipital artery is encountered early during the [[far-lateral-approach]] as the superficial and deep muscular layers are dissected. It is exposed passing behind the superior oblique and semispinalis capitis. The artery must be identified and preserved or controlled during the suboccipital muscle dissection.

### Suboccipital Triangle
In the [[suboccipital-triangle]], the occipital artery courses superficial to the triangle boundaries (superior oblique, inferior oblique, rectus capitis posterior major). The [[vertebral-artery]] courses in the depths of the triangle — the occipital artery is a superficial landmark on the way to the deeper vertebral artery.

### EC-IC Bypass
The occipital artery can serve as a **donor vessel** for extracranial-intracranial (EC-IC) bypass to the [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]] or [[pica|PICA]], particularly in cases of vertebrobasilar insufficiency or complex [[posterior-circulation]] aneurysms requiring flow replacement.

### Dural Arteriovenous Fistulae
Meningeal branches of the occipital artery are common feeders in dAVFs of the transverse-[[sigmoid-sinus]] region. The artery may be used for transarterial embolization access.

## Anastomoses

The occipital artery participates in several anastomotic networks:

- **With [[vertebral-artery]]** — via descending branch → deep cervical artery → vertebral muscular branches
- **With posterior auricular and superficial temporal arteries** — scalp anastomoses
- **With [[middle-meningeal-artery]]** — via meningeal branches through mastoid foramen

## Connections

- Origin: external carotid artery (posterior branch)
- Landmark for: [[far-lateral-approach]], [[suboccipital-triangle]]
- Meningeal supply to: posterior fossa dura via [[jugular-foramen]]
- Bypass donor for: [[pica]], [[posterior-cerebral-artery]]
- Anastomoses with: [[vertebral-artery]] (via deep cervical), [[middle-meningeal-artery]]
- Courses through: posterior cervical musculature, occipital groove of [[temporal-bone|mastoid]]
