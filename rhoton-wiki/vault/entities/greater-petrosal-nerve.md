# Greater Superficial Petrosal Nerve

The greater superficial petrosal nerve (GSPN) is a branch of the [[facial-nerve]] that carries parasympathetic fibers from the geniculate ganglion to the [[pterygopalatine-fossa]], where it synapses in the pterygopalatine ganglion to supply lacrimation and nasal mucosal secretion. On the middle fossa floor, it is one of the most important surgical landmarks for the [[middle-fossa-approach]], [[anterior-petrosectomy]], and localization of the petrous [[internal-carotid-artery]].

## Course

| Segment | Location | Relations |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin** | Geniculate ganglion of [[facial-nerve]] within [[temporal-bone]] | At the first genu of the facial nerve, where the labyrinthine segment turns into the tympanic segment |
| **Middle fossa floor** | Courses anterolaterally in a groove on the anterior surface of the petrous bone | Between the dura and periosteum, beneath the temporal lobe |
| **Foramen lacerum** | Enters the area of the foramen lacerum | Joins the deep petrosal nerve (sympathetic from ICA) to form the Vidian nerve |
| **Vidian (pterygoid) canal** | As the Vidian nerve, traverses the pterygoid canal | Through the [[sphenoid-bone]] to reach the [[pterygopalatine-fossa]] |
| **Pterygopalatine ganglion** | Synapses in the ganglion | Postganglionic fibers supply lacrimal gland, nasal/palatal mucosa |

## Surgical Landmark Function

### Locating the Geniculate Ganglion
The GSPN points directly back to the geniculate ganglion of the [[facial-nerve]]. Following the GSPN posterolaterally on the middle fossa floor leads to the first genu of the facial nerve — a critical step in the [[middle-fossa-approach]] for [[internal-auditory-canal]] unroofing.

### Locating the Petrous ICA
The GSPN courses **lateral to and approximately parallel with** the horizontal segment of the petrous [[internal-carotid-artery]]. The ICA lies medial and slightly inferior to the GSPN on the middle fossa floor. This relationship is used during the [[anterior-petrosectomy]] to avoid ICA injury during drilling.

### Defining the Kawase Triangle
The GSPN forms the **lateral boundary** of the Kawase (posteromedial) triangle in the [[cavernous-sinus-triangles]] system:
- Lateral: GSPN
- Anterior: V3 / foramen ovale
- Posterior: arcuate eminence / petrous ridge

## Middle Fossa Approach

During the [[middle-fossa-approach]]:

1. The temporal lobe dura is elevated from the middle fossa floor
2. The GSPN is identified coursing anterolaterally in its bony groove
3. The GSPN is followed **posterolaterally** to the geniculate ganglion
4. The [[internal-auditory-canal]] lies just posterior and medial to the geniculate ganglion
5. **Bill's bar** at the IAC fundus separates the facial nerve from the vestibular nerve

### Risks
- **Traction on the GSPN** can avulse the nerve from the geniculate ganglion → facial nerve injury
- **Drilling near the GSPN** can injure the petrous ICA medially or the cochlea posterolaterally

## Anterior Petrosectomy (Kawase)

During the [[anterior-petrosectomy]]:
- The GSPN defines the lateral safe limit for drilling the [[petrous-apex]]
- The petrous ICA lies medial to the GSPN
- Drilling lateral to the GSPN risks the cochlea and geniculate ganglion
- The GSPN must be gently elevated or preserved during Kawase triangle drilling

## Function

| Component | Function | Deficit if Damaged |
|---|---|---|
| Parasympathetic (secretomotor) | Lacrimation, nasal/palatal mucosal secretion | Dry eye (xerophthalmia), reduced nasal secretion |
| Taste (from palate) | Gustatory fibers from soft palate | Usually subclinical |

## Connections

- Branch of: [[facial-nerve]] (geniculate ganglion)
- Joins: deep petrosal nerve → Vidian nerve → [[pterygopalatine-fossa]]
- Landmark for: geniculate ganglion, petrous [[internal-carotid-artery]]
- Boundary of: Kawase triangle ([[cavernous-sinus-triangles]])
- Used in: [[middle-fossa-approach]], [[anterior-petrosectomy]]
- Courses on: middle fossa floor of [[temporal-bone]]
- Target: pterygopalatine ganglion (lacrimation, nasal secretion)
