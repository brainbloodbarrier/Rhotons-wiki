# Labyrinthine Artery

The labyrinthine artery (internal auditory artery) is a small end-artery that supplies the inner ear — cochlea, vestibular labyrinth, [[facial-nerve|CN VII]], and [[vestibulocochlear-nerve|CN VIII]]. It most commonly arises from the [[aica|AICA]] (85%) or directly from the [[basilar-artery]] (15%). It is the only arterial supply to the cochlea and vestibular apparatus — its occlusion causes **immediate, irreversible sensorineural deafness** and vestibular failure.

## Origin

- **From [[aica|AICA]]** (~85%) — arises from the lateral pontine or meatal segment, usually near the porus of the internal acoustic meatus ([[temporal-bone]])
- **From [[basilar-artery]]** (~15%) — arises directly as a separate branch
- **Rarely** from [[pica|PICA]] or [[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]]
- Diameter: 0.1–0.2 mm — one of the smallest named intracranial arteries

## Course

1. Enters the **internal acoustic meatus** (IAM) alongside [[facial-nerve|CN VII]] and [[vestibulocochlear-nerve|CN VIII]]
2. Courses within the meatus between the nerves
3. At the meatal fundus, divides into terminal branches:
   - **Common cochlear artery** → anterior inferior cerebellar artery (cochlear branch) + vestibulocochlear artery
   - **Anterior vestibular artery** → to the superior and lateral semicircular canals and utricle

## Supply Territory

| Branch | Structure | Consequence of occlusion |
|---|---|---|
| Cochlear branches | Cochlea (organ of Corti) | Sensorineural deafness |
| Anterior vestibular artery | Superior/lateral semicircular canals, utricle | Vertigo, disequilibrium |
| Posterior vestibular artery | Posterior semicircular canal, saccule | Vestibular failure |

**No collateral supply exists** — the labyrinthine artery is a true end-artery. There is no anastomosis with the middle ear circulation or any other source.

## Relation to the AICA Meatal Loop

The [[aica|AICA]] commonly forms a **meatal loop** — a hairpin turn that enters or approaches the porus of the internal acoustic meatus before turning back toward the [[cerebellum]]. The labyrinthine artery arises from this loop:
- **Type I** — AICA loop does not enter the meatus; labyrinthine artery arises at the porus
- **Type II** — AICA loop enters the meatus partially; labyrinthine artery arises within the meatus
- **Type III** — AICA loop extends to the meatal fundus; labyrinthine artery arises deep in the meatus

The meatal loop type determines the risk to the labyrinthine artery during vestibular schwannoma surgery.

## Surgical Significance

### Vestibular schwannoma surgery
The labyrinthine artery is at risk in all three surgical approaches:
- **[[retrosigmoid-approach]]** — the AICA meatal loop and labyrinthine artery are encountered at the porus when drilling the posterior wall of the IAC
- **[[middle-fossa-approach]]** — the artery is deep in the fundus, near the [[facial-nerve|CN VII]] labyrinthine segment
- **Translabyrinthine** — hearing is intentionally sacrificed, so the artery is not preserved

**Preservation of the labyrinthine artery is essential for hearing preservation.** Even transient vasospasm can cause permanent cochlear damage.

### AICA territory infarction
Occlusion of the AICA proximal to the labyrinthine artery origin causes:
- Sudden deafness (labyrinthine artery territory)
- Vertigo and nystagmus (vestibular loss)
- Facial weakness ([[facial-nerve|CN VII]] ischemia)
- Lateral pontine infarction (AICA perforators to [[brainstem]])
- Cerebellar infarction (petrosal surface of [[cerebellum]])

### Microvascular decompression
In hemifacial spasm surgery via [[retrosigmoid-approach]], the AICA loop near CN VII must be mobilized without compromising the labyrinthine artery branch.
