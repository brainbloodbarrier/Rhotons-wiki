# Middle Cerebral Artery

The middle cerebral artery (MCA) is the largest and most complex of the cerebral arteries. It arises as the larger terminal branch of the [[internal-carotid-artery]], with an origin diameter of 2.4–4.6 mm (avg 3.9 mm) — roughly twice that of the [[anterior-cerebral-artery]].

## Origin and Course

The MCA originates at the medial end of the [[sylvian-fissure]], lateral to the [[optic-chiasm]], below the [[anterior-perforated-substance]]. It courses laterally below the anterior perforated substance, parallel to and approximately 1 cm posterior to the [[sphenoid-bone|sphenoid]] ridge. As it passes beneath the anterior perforated substance, it gives off lenticulostriate [[perforating-arteries]]. It divides within the [[sylvian-fissure]] and turns sharply posterosuperiorly at the **genu** to reach the insular surface.

## Segments

- **M1 (sphenoidal)** — from origin to genu; courses laterally within the sphenoidal compartment of the [[sylvian-fissure]], roughly parallel to the sphenoid ridge. Subdivided into prebifurcation (single trunk) and postbifurcation parts. Bifurcation occurs proximal to genu in ~90% of hemispheres
- **M2 (insular)** — trunks on the insular surface; begins at genu where MCA passes over limen insulae, ends at circular sulcus. Greatest branching occurs here
- **M3 (opercular)** — from circular sulcus to sylvian fissure surface; branches course over frontal, parietal, and temporal opercula with characteristic 180-degree turns
- **M4 (cortical)** — branches on lateral convexity surface; supply most of the lateral and some basal cerebral surface

## Branching Patterns

The main trunk divides in one of three patterns:
- **Bifurcation** (78%) — into superior and inferior trunks (equal 18%, inferior dominant 32%, superior dominant 28%)
- **Trifurcation** (12%) — superior, middle, and inferior trunks
- **Multiple branches** (10%) — four or more trunks

## [[lenticulostriate-arteries]]

The perforating branches of the MCA are the **lenticulostriate arteries** — averaging 10 per hemisphere (range 1–21). They enter the anterior perforated substance and are divided into three groups:

- **Medial group** — present in ~50%; 1–5 branches near ICA bifurcation; arise from medial prebifurcation M1; supply lateral globus pallidus, superior anterior limb of [[internal-capsule]], anterosuperior caudate head
- **Intermediate group** — present in >90%; possesses at least one major artery arborizing into up to 30 branches before entering anterior perforated substance; average 3 parent arteries; arise almost exclusively from M1 or early branches
- **Lateral group** — present in nearly all hemispheres; average 5 per hemisphere dividing into up to 20 branches; originate from lateral M1 with S-shaped course; supply nearly entire anterior-to-posterior length of upper [[internal-capsule]] and body/head of caudate

**Critical relationship to MCA bifurcation:** ~30% of lateral lenticulostriates originate ≤2 mm from M1 bifurcation; ~70% originate ≤5 mm. Some arise directly at the bifurcation. This makes [[aneurysm-surgery|MCA aneurysm]] clip placement extremely hazardous to perforators. If bifurcation is early, more lenticulostriates arise from postbifurcation trunks (preferentially from inferior trunk).

## Cortical Territory

The MCA supplies most of the lateral cerebral surface, all insular and opercular surfaces, lateral orbital frontal surface, temporal pole, and lateral inferior temporal surface. The territory does NOT reach the frontal or occipital poles or the superior hemispheric margin — these belong to [[anterior-cerebral-artery|ACA]] and [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]].

The 12 cortical areas supplied by the MCA:
1. Orbitofrontal — lateral orbital surface
2. Prefrontal — anterior lateral [[cerebral-lobes|frontal lobe]]
3. Precentral (prerolandic) — precentral gyrus
4. Central (rolandic) — central sulcus region
5. Anterior parietal (postrolandic) — postcentral gyrus
6. Posterior parietal — superior/inferior parietal lobule
7. Angular — angular gyrus
8. Temporo-occipital — posterior temporal/occipital junction
9. Posterior temporal — posterior superior/middle temporal gyri
10. Middle temporal — mid-portion temporal lobe
11. Anterior temporal — anterior temporal lobe and temporal pole
12. Temporopolar — tip of temporal pole

The narrow peripheral strip on the lateral surface supplied by ACA/PCA is broadest in the superior frontal region and narrowest in the superior parietal area.

## Surgical Relevance

MCA branches are exposed in most supratentorial operations. Key considerations:
- The [[sylvian-fissure]] is the primary corridor for accessing MCA pathology
- Lenticulostriate arteries must be preserved during [[aneurysm-surgery]] at the MCA bifurcation
- The [[pterional-approach]] is the standard approach for MCA aneurysms
- Microsurgical bypass to the MCA is used to restore blood flow in occlusive disease

## Relations

- Arises from [[internal-carotid-artery]] bifurcation (lateral to [[anterior-cerebral-artery]])
- Courses within the [[sylvian-fissure]]
- Lenticulostriate branches supply [[basal-ganglia|basal ganglia and internal capsule]]
- Terminal branches supply lateral frontal, parietal, temporal, and occipital cortex
