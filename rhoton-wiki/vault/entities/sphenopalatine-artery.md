# Sphenopalatine Artery

The sphenopalatine artery is the terminal branch of the [[maxillary-artery]] (third segment), exiting the [[pterygopalatine-fossa]] through the sphenopalatine foramen to supply the posterior [[sellar-region|nasal cavity]], nasal [[septum-pellucidum|septum]], and lateral nasal wall. It is the principal arterial supply to the nasoseptal flap used in [[cranial-base|skull base]] reconstruction and the most common source of severe posterior epistaxis.

## Course

### Origin
The maxillary artery (internal maxillary), a terminal branch of the [[external-carotid-artery]], courses through the [[infratemporal-fossa]] and enters the [[pterygopalatine-fossa]] through the pterygomaxillary fissure. Within the pterygopalatine fossa, it gives rise to several branches before terminating as the sphenopalatine artery.

### Sphenopalatine Foramen
The sphenopalatine artery exits the [[pterygopalatine-fossa]] through the **sphenopalatine foramen**, which opens into the posterior nasal cavity at the junction of the superior and middle meatus, behind the posterior attachment of the middle turbinate.

### Nasal Cavity
After exiting the foramen, the artery divides into:

| Branch | Territory |
|---|---|
| **Posterior lateral nasal arteries** | Lateral nasal wall, middle and inferior turbinates, meati |
| **Posterior septal artery (nasopalatine)** | Posterior nasal septum; courses anteroinferiorly across the septum to the incisive canal |

## Nasoseptal Flap (Hadad-Bassagasteguy)

The **posterior septal branch** of the sphenopalatine artery is the pedicle of the **nasoseptal flap** — the workhorse vascularized flap for skull base reconstruction after [[endoscopic-endonasal-approach]] surgery:

- **Flap harvest**: the mucoperichondrium/mucoperiosteum of the nasal septum is elevated as a posteriorly pedicled flap based on the posterior septal artery
- **Reach**: covers defects from the cribriform plate to the [[clivus]] and from one [[optic-canal]] to the other
- **Success rate**: >95% for preventing CSF leak after endonasal skull base surgery
- **Critical step**: the pedicle must be preserved during the posterior septectomy — injury eliminates the flap option

## Surgical Significance

### Epistaxis
The sphenopalatine artery is the most common source of **posterior epistaxis** that fails conservative management. Surgical ligation via endoscopic approach at the sphenopalatine foramen is the definitive treatment.

### Endonasal Skull Base Surgery
During [[endoscopic-endonasal-approach]]:

- The nasoseptal flap must be **harvested early** before any posterior septectomy
- The sphenopalatine foramen is a landmark for the posterior nasal wall and the [[pterygopalatine-fossa]] behind it
- Transpterygoid extensions require dissecting through/behind the sphenopalatine artery territory

### Juvenile Nasopharyngeal Angiofibroma
These highly vascular tumors in the [[pterygopalatine-fossa]] receive primary supply from the maxillary artery branches including the sphenopalatine artery. Preoperative embolization typically targets this vessel.

## Anastomoses

| With | Via |
|---|---|
| [[ethmoidal-arteries]] (anterior/posterior) | Nasal septal and lateral wall arcades |
| Greater palatine artery | Through the incisive canal |
| Superior labial artery (facial artery) | Anterior nasal septum (Kiesselbach's plexus / Little's area) |

## Connections

- Terminal branch of: maxillary artery (external carotid)
- Exits through: sphenopalatine foramen from [[pterygopalatine-fossa]]
- Supplies: posterior nasal cavity (septum and lateral wall)
- Pedicle of: nasoseptal flap for [[endoscopic-endonasal-approach]] reconstruction
- Anastomoses with: [[ethmoidal-arteries]], greater palatine artery
- Clinical: posterior epistaxis (most common source), angiofibroma feeder
- Landmark for: [[pterygopalatine-fossa]], posterior nasal wall
