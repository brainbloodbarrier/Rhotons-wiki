# Posterior Spinal Artery

The paired posterior spinal arteries arise from the [[vertebral-artery]] (V3 or V4 segment) or occasionally from the [[pica]]. They descend along the posterolateral surface of the spinal cord, supplying the posterior one-third (dorsal columns — proprioception and vibration). At the [[foramen-magnum]], they are intimately related to the [[dentate-ligament]], [[lower-cranial-nerves|accessory nerve (CN XI)]], and the dural entry of the [[vertebral-artery]].

## Origin

- Usually arise from the **posteromedial surface** of the [[vertebral-artery]], just outside the dura (V3 segment) or from the initial intradural part (V4)
- May also arise from the [[pica]] (less common)
- The posterior spinal artery passes through the dura within the same **fibrous tunnel** as the [[vertebral-artery]] and the C1 nerve root — these three structures are bound together by dural bands at the dural foramen

## Course at the Foramen Magnum

1. Arises at or near the dural penetration site of the [[vertebral-artery]]
2. Courses medially **behind** the most rostral attachment of the dentate ligament
3. On reaching the lower medulla ([[brainstem]]), divides into **ascending** and **descending** branches:
   - **Ascending branch** — courses through the [[foramen-magnum]], supplies the restiform body ([[cerebellar-peduncles|inferior cerebellar peduncle]]), gracile and cuneate tubercles
   - **Descending branch** — courses along the posterolateral spinal cord, reinforced by segmental radiculopial arteries

## Supply Territory

- **Posterior one-third of the spinal cord:**
  - Gracile fasciculus (proprioception — lower extremities)
  - Cuneate fasciculus (proprioception — upper extremities)
  - Posterior horns (sensory relay)
- **Restiform body** (inferior cerebellar peduncle) — ascending branches
- The posterior columns have a **dual supply** from paired PSAs — more resistant to ischemia than the anterior cord ([[anterior-spinal-artery]] territory)

## Surgical Significance

### Far-lateral approach ([[far-lateral-approach]])
The posterior spinal artery is identified after opening the dura at the [[foramen-magnum]]:
- It courses along the posterolateral aspect of the spinal cord and medulla
- Passes **between** the dentate ligament (anterior) and the [[lower-cranial-nerves|accessory nerve (CN XI)]] (posterior)
- May be incorporated into the dural cuff around the [[vertebral-artery]] — care during dural opening to preserve it

### Suboccipital craniectomy
The posterior spinal artery gives ascending branches to the medulla that must be preserved during midline suboccipital approaches.

### Dural opening precaution
The posterior spinal artery may be embedded in the dura at the [[vertebral-artery]] entry point — the dura should be opened carefully to avoid transecting this artery.

## Relations

- Arises from [[vertebral-artery]] V3/V4 or [[pica]] — see [[posterior-circulation]]
- Courses at the [[foramen-magnum]] between dentate ligament and [[lower-cranial-nerves|CN XI]]
- Bound with [[vertebral-artery]] and C1 nerve root at the dural funnel — see [[suboccipital-triangle]]
- Supplies posterior spinal cord (complement to [[anterior-spinal-artery]] anteriorly)
- Related to [[brainstem]] (restiform body ascending branches)
- Exposed by [[far-lateral-approach]] and suboccipital craniectomy
- See [[perforating-arteries]] for spinal cord vascular supply
