# Cerebral Revascularization Overview

Cerebral bypass surgery restores, augments, or replaces blood flow to the brain when the native circulation is compromised by disease or requires deliberate sacrifice. Lawton's framework organizes all cerebral bypasses into **seven types** built from three fundamental [[microsurgical-anastomosis|anastomoses]] (end-to-side, side-to-side, end-to-end), analogous to his classification of the [[cerebrovascular-aneurysms-overview|seven aneurysms]] and [[cerebrovascular-avms-overview|seven AVMs]].

## Classification of Seven Bypasses

| # | Bypass Type | Anastomosis | Flow Category | Example |
|---|-------------|------------|---------------|---------|
| 1 | EC-IC bypass | End-to-side | Augmentation | [[superficial-temporal-artery\|STA]]-MCA, [[occipital-artery\|OA]]-PICA |
| 2 | IC-IC interposition graft | End-to-side (x2) | Replacement | [[external-carotid-artery\|ECA]]-[[radial-artery-graft\|RAG]]-MCA, [[internal-carotid-artery\|ICA]]-[[saphenous-vein-graft\|SVG]]-MCA |
| 3 | EC-IC interposition graft | End-to-side + End-to-end | Replacement | ECA-RAG-[[posterior-cerebral-artery\|PCA]], V3 [[vertebral-artery\|VA]]-RAG-[[posterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery\|PICA]] |
| 4 | Reimplantation | End-to-side | Replacement | Efferent branch reimplanted onto parent or graft |
| 5 | In-situ bypass | Side-to-side | Communication | PICA-PICA, [[superior-cerebellar-artery\|SCA]]-SCA, A3-A3 |
| 6 | Reanastomosis | End-to-end | Reconstruction | M1-M2 after [[mca-aneurysm\|MCA aneurysm]] excision |
| 7 | Combined bypass | Multiple | Variable | EC-IC + in-situ for complex giant aneurysms |

### Flow Categories

Bypasses serve three hemodynamic functions:

- **Augmentation (convergent)**: EC-IC bypass introduces new flow into the recipient territory without interrupting native supply. The [[superficial-temporal-artery|STA]]-MCA bypass is the prototype, creating a Y-shaped convergence of donor and recipient streams.
- **Replacement (convergent)**: Interpositional grafts using [[radial-artery-graft|radial artery]] or [[saphenous-vein-graft|saphenous vein]] replace flow that will be or has been lost by deliberate parent artery sacrifice. Two anastomoses are required (proximal and distal).
- **Communication**: In-situ side-to-side bypasses mimic the [[anterior-communicating-artery|anterior]] and [[posterior-communicating-artery|posterior communicating arteries]], creating a demand-driven connection between parallel arteries without governing flow direction.
- **Reconstruction**: End-to-end reanastomosis splices arterial ends after pathology excision, restoring original flow without adding a new input.

## Indications

### Moyamoya Disease

[[Moyamoya|Moyamoya disease]] is the most common indication. Progressive steno-occlusion of the supraclinoid [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] causes ischemic symptoms responsive to STA-MCA bypass (single or double-barrel), often combined with indirect techniques. Bilateral procedures are staged.

### Complex Aneurysms

Giant, fusiform, or thrombotic aneurysms that cannot be clipped directly may require parent artery sacrifice with bypass. Indications overlap with [[aneurysm-clipping-techniques|clipping techniques]] and [[temporary-clipping|temporary occlusion]]:

- Giant [[mca-aneurysm|MCA aneurysms]] -- trapping with STA-MCA bypass or M1-M2 reanastomosis
- Giant [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm|basilar bifurcation aneurysms]] -- STA-PCA or STA-SCA bypass before basilar occlusion
- [[pcoa-aneurysm|PCoA]] and [[ophthalmic-artery-aneurysm|ophthalmic artery]] giant aneurysms -- high-flow ECA-RAG-MCA interpositional bypass
- Fusiform [[pica-aneurysm|PICA aneurysms]] -- PICA-PICA in-situ bypass with aneurysm trapping
- [[pericallosal-artery-aneurysm|Pericallosal artery]] aneurysms -- A3-A3 in-situ bypass via [[anterior-interhemispheric-approach|interhemispheric approach]]

### Skull Base Tumors

Tumors encasing the ICA or vertebrobasilar arteries may require vessel sacrifice with preoperative bypass, particularly petroclival meningiomas and chordomas. The [[orbitozygomatic-approach|orbitozygomatic]] and [[far-lateral-approach|far lateral approaches]] provide the corridors.

### Atherosclerotic Occlusive Disease

A debated indication. The EC/IC Bypass Study (1985) showed no benefit in unselected patients, but ongoing trials target hemodynamically selected subgroups with documented perfusion failure.

## Technical Building Blocks

### Donor Arteries

| Donor | Source | Caliber (mm) | Bypass Use |
|-------|--------|--------------|------------|
| [[superficial-temporal-artery\|STA]] | [[external-carotid-artery\|ECA]] terminal | 1.93 | EC-IC low-flow bypass (most common) |
| [[occipital-artery\|OA]] | ECA | 2.05 | OA-PICA, OA-[[anterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery\|AICA]] |
| [[radial-artery-graft\|Radial artery]] | Forearm harvest | 2.5-3.0 | Intermediate-flow interpositional graft |
| [[saphenous-vein-graft\|Saphenous vein]] | Leg harvest | 3.5-4.5 | High-flow interpositional graft |

### Arteriotomies

Seven [[bypass-arteriotomy|arteriotomy types]] -- linear incision, fish-mouth, perpendicular transection, oblique transection, excision, transection bifurcotomy, and incision bifurcotomy -- are selected based on vessel caliber mismatch, anatomic location, and desired anastomotic geometry.

### Anastomoses

The three [[microsurgical-anastomosis|anastomotic techniques]] (end-to-side, side-to-side, end-to-end) are combined with arteriotomy selection and donor-recipient pairing to construct any bypass. The fish-mouthed end-to-side anastomosis is the workhorse, the intraluminal side-to-side is required for in-situ bypasses, and the circular end-to-end is the fastest but technically most demanding.

## Surgical Approaches for Bypass

| Approach | Bypass Target |
|----------|--------------|
| [[pterional-approach\|Pterional]] | STA-MCA, ICA-MCA interpositional |
| [[orbitozygomatic-approach\|Orbitozygomatic]] | STA-PCA, STA-SCA, deep interpositional |
| [[far-lateral-approach\|Far lateral]] | OA-PICA, VA-RAG-PICA, PICA-PICA in-situ |
| [[anterior-interhemispheric-approach\|Interhemispheric]] | A3-A3 in-situ, [[pericallosal-artery-aneurysm\|pericallosal]] reanastomosis |
| Subtemporal | STA-PCA via temporal base |

## Outcome Measures

Bypass patency is confirmed intraoperatively by ICG videoangiography, micro-Doppler insonation, and pulsation inspection. Long-term graft patency exceeds 95% for STA-MCA bypasses in experienced hands.
