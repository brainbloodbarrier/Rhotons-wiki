# Vascular Malformations — Overview

Cerebral vascular malformations are developmental lesions of the intracranial vasculature that, as a class, account for ~1–2% of strokes and are the **second-most-common cause of non-traumatic intracerebral hemorrhage in young adults** (after hypertension). The classical **McCormick taxonomy (1966)** divides them into four histologic categories — arteriovenous, cavernous, venous, and capillary — to which modern practice adds the arteriovenous-fistula families that lack a true nidus.

## Classification

| Class | Arterial input | Intervening tissue | Venous drainage | Hemorrhage risk | NCX entry |
|-------|----------------|--------------------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------|
| **AVM** (arteriovenous malformation) | Enlarged feeding arteries | Nidus — tangle of abnormal vessels without capillary bed | Enlarged draining veins | ~2–4 %/yr overall | [[cerebrovascular-avms-overview]] |
| **Cavernous malformation** | None (low-flow) | Sinusoidal caverns lined by endothelium, no intervening brain parenchyma | Small draining veins (often with DVA) | 0.5–1 %/yr sporadic | [[cavernous-malformation]] |
| **Developmental venous anomaly (DVA)** | Normal capillary drainage | Normal brain parenchyma | Dilated medullary veins converging into a "caput medusae" | Essentially 0 alone | — |
| **Capillary telangiectasia** | Normal capillaries | Normal brain parenchyma with scattered dilated capillaries | Small veins | Negligible | — |
| **Dural AVF** | Dural artery feeders | Arterialized dural sinus wall (no nidus) | Retrograde cortical/sinus drainage | Borden/Cognard-dependent | — |
| **Pial AVF** | Single pial feeder | Direct fistula (no nidus) | Single dilated vein | Moderate-high | — |
| **Vein-of-Galen malformation** | Choroidal + collicular feeders | Direct fistula | Dilated median vein of the prosencephalon | Neonatal heart failure vs. childhood hydrocephalus | — |

The critical distinction for surgical planning is whether a lesion contains a **nidus** (true AVM) or a **direct fistula** (AVF). Nidal lesions are graded by the [[spetzler-martin-grading]] system for resection risk.

## AVM subtypes by location

The NCX casebook carries per-location AVM entries. Each combines the general [[cerebrovascular-avms-overview]] surgical framework with location-specific corridor and eloquence considerations:

- [[frontal-avm]] — convexity dominant; hemisphere-related eloquence.
- [[temporal-avm]] — mesial temporal vs. lateral; language dominance matters.
- [[parieto-occipital-avm]] — visual cortex eloquence; deep venous drainage common.
- [[brainstem-avm]] — rarely resected; stereotactic [[spetzler-martin-grading]] surrogates.
- [[cerebellar-avm]] — midline vs. hemispheric; PICA/AICA/SCA territory.
- [[ventricular-avm]] — subependymal; choroidal feeders; deep venous drainage.
- [[deep-avm]] — basal ganglia/thalamus; typically non-surgical, SRS territory.

Common structural anatomy is indexed in [[avm-feeding-arteries]] and [[avm-draining-veins]]; the resection operative principles are consolidated in [[avm-resection-principles]].

## Hemorrhage risk — comparative

Annual first-hemorrhage rates, a common exam-and-consent anchor:

- AVM: ~2–4 %/yr (higher with deep drainage, deep location, prior hemorrhage).
- Cavernous malformation: ~0.5 %/yr sporadic, 1–2 %/yr after first bleed.
- DVA: ~0.2 %/yr — and usually only when an associated cavernoma bleeds.
- Dural AVF with cortical venous drainage (Borden II/III, Cognard ≥2a+b): 7–8 %/yr.

Hemorrhage risk — not size — drives most treatment decisions. A small eloquent-area AVM bleeds as readily as a large convexity one; what differs is *whether resection is safer than observation* — the central calculus of [[spetzler-martin-grading]].

## Treatment modalities

Three modalities, often combined:

1. **Microsurgical resection** — gold standard for Spetzler-Martin I–III AVMs and all symptomatic cavernomas; corridor depends on location.
2. **Stereotactic radiosurgery** — appropriate for small deep AVMs (nidus <3 cm) and residual after partial resection; 2–3 year latency to obliteration; no hemorrhage protection during latency.
3. **Endovascular embolization** — primarily adjunctive (pre-operative flow reduction, intranidal pedicle obliteration); curative in select small/pial AVF and dural AVF.

Multidisciplinary planning is standard; the casebook's bypass- and resection-related entries (e.g., [[ec-ic-bypass]], [[temporary-clipping]]) apply when a malformation shares inflow territory with a giant aneurysm or requires flow-replacement.
