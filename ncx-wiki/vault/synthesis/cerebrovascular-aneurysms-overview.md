# Cerebrovascular Aneurysms Overview

Intracranial aneurysms occur at branches or curves in cerebral arteries and can be found at more than 20 different locations along the [[circle-of-willis]] and its branches. Lawton's framework identifies **seven aneurysm types** that account for 75% of all aneurysms in his consecutive 12-year experience with 2500 cases. Proficiency in managing these seven types enables confident handling of the majority of aneurysms in practice.

## Foundational Tenets

Lawton structures aneurysm surgery around nine tenets. Four of these are represented by dedicated concept pages:

### [[subarachnoid-dissection|Subarachnoid Dissection]]
The subarachnoid space provides a navigable labyrinth through cisterns to deep targets. Every aneurysm resides in a specific cistern. Dissection follows arterial landmarks along their safe surfaces -- the inferior M1, superior ICA, inferior A1, and inferior P1. Three instruments cycle through the dissection: microscissors, bipolar forceps, and the Rhoton No. 6 dissector.

### [[temporary-clipping|Temporary Clipping]]
Enables final dissection by softening the aneurysm for aggressive manipulation in surgical blind spots. Single proximal clips suffice for [[mca-aneurysm|MCA]] and [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm|basilar bifurcation]] aneurysms; multiple clips needed when backfilling occurs (e.g., [[ophthalmic-artery-aneurysm|OphA]] from PCoA, [[acoa-aneurysm|ACoA]] from contralateral A1). Trapping with suction decompression reserved for thrombotic and giant aneurysms. Neurosurgeon speed is the most important factor during ischemia.

### [[aneurysm-clipping-techniques|Permanent Clipping]]
Guided by Rhoton's four rules on aneurysm morphology. Techniques range from simple single-clip application through multiple clipping (intersecting, stacked, overlapping), tandem clipping (Drake), tandem angled fenestrated clipping (Sugita), fenestration tubes, and full clip reconstruction after aneurysmotomy.

### [[intraoperative-rupture|Intraoperative Rupture]]
Occurs in 5-10% of cases regardless of experience level. The response has three dimensions: visceral (calm, clarity, confidence), technical (tamponade, proximal control, distal control, permanent clip), and cognitive (pattern recognition from accumulated operative experience). Paradoxically creates opportunity by permitting aggressive aneurysm mobilization.

## Cross-Transferable Knowledge

[[anterior-choroidal-artery|AChA]] aneurysms are anatomically similar to [[pcoa-aneurysm|PCoA aneurysms]] and are clipped with similar techniques. [[superior-cerebellar-artery|SCA]] aneurysms parallel [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm|basilar bifurcation aneurysms]]. Proficiency with the seven types therefore extends coverage to these related locations.
