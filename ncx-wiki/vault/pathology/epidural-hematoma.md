# Epidural Hematoma

An epidural hematoma (EDH) forms between the cranial inner table and the periosteal (outer) layer of the dura. Because the dura is firmly adherent to the skull at suture lines, the clot is typically bound by cranial sutures — giving the classic **biconvex / lenticular** shape on CT — but can readily cross the midline, unlike a subdural hematoma ([[subdural-hematoma]]). The blood source is usually arterial; the clinical tempo is correspondingly fast.

## Epidemiology

- Most common in young adults (15–40) with significant head trauma; rare in infants (adherent dura) and elderly (dura detaches with atrophy).
- Temporoparietal location dominates (~70%) — the thin squamous temporal bone fractures and tears the middle meningeal artery.
- Associated skull fracture in >85%; absence of fracture does not exclude EDH in children.

## Mechanism and source

| Site | Bleeding source |
|---|---|
| **Temporoparietal** (classic) | [[middle-meningeal-artery]] at the pterion |
| **Frontal** | Anterior division of MMA, sphenoparietal branch |
| **Posterior fossa** | Transverse / sigmoid sinus, occipital artery |
| **Vertex** | Superior sagittal sinus — venous, slower, tricky to diagnose |
| **Anterior (pre-dural)** | Diploic vein oozing across fracture line |

Venous and sinus EDH follows a slower course and may present days after injury.

## Classical triphasic course

1. **Initial LOC** from concussive force.
2. **Lucid interval** of minutes to hours as the clot accumulates.
3. **Rapid deterioration** — headache, vomiting, hemiparesis, ipsilateral pupil dilation (uncal herniation), ultimately coma and death.

Only ~50% of patients demonstrate the full triad; absence of lucid interval does not rule out EDH.

## Imaging

- **Non-contrast CT**: hyperdense, biconvex, does not cross suture lines, may cross midline. Swirl sign (hypodense region within hyperdense clot) indicates active hemorrhage.
- **Bone windows** confirm overlying fracture and trace the fracture line across the MMA groove.
- **CTA / DSA** occasionally useful for atypical locations or suspected sinus injury.
- **MRI** rarely needed acutely; differentiates subacute from chronic collections.

## Management

Every traumatic EDH demands rapid surgical evaluation. Non-operative management is acceptable only for small (<30 mL), thin (<15 mm), asymptomatic collections in a patient with GCS >14 and no focal deficit — and only with close neuro ICU monitoring.

### Bullock / BTF surgical thresholds

- Volume >30 mL regardless of GCS.
- Thickness >15 mm or midline shift >5 mm.
- GCS <9 with anisocoria — *emergent* surgery.

### Operative principles

- **Craniotomy** (not mere burr hole) — adequate exposure for hematoma removal and control of the bleeding artery.
- Flap centered over the clot; bone flap lifted carefully (clot often adheres to the inner table).
- Evacuate clot with suction, lift dura off skull inner table.
- Coagulate torn MMA and pack / bone-wax bleeding fracture line; for venous sinus sources, apply tamponade / patch graft — see [[intraoperative-rupture]] principles.
- Central dural tack-up sutures + tack-up sutures at flap periphery prevent recurrence.
- Routine dural opening is **not** required unless underlying subdural or intradural injury suspected; if opened, close watertight.
- [[intracranial-pressure]] monitoring postoperatively if GCS <9 or swelling observed intraoperatively.

## Special situations

- **Posterior fossa EDH** — small posterior fossa volume means modest clots are life-threatening; low threshold to operate; watch for brainstem compression and acute hydrocephalus.
- **Vertex EDH** — often missed on initial CT (axial cuts skim through); sagittal reconstructions mandatory; venous source from SSS complicates evacuation.
- **Delayed EDH** — may appear on repeat CT 6–24 h after initial injury; repeat imaging if GCS falls or clinical deterioration occurs.

## Outcomes

- With *rapid* surgical evacuation before herniation: mortality 5–10%; most survivors return to baseline.
- Once pupillary asymmetry / decerebrate posturing develops: mortality >40%.
- Outcome hinges on time from deterioration to evacuation — a paradigmatic neurosurgical emergency where minutes matter.
