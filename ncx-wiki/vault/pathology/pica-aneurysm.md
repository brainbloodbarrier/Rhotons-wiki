# PICA Aneurysm

Posterior inferior cerebellar artery (PICA) aneurysms arise at the PICA-vertebral artery convergence in the posterior fossa, requiring the [[far-lateral-approach]] and navigation through the lower cranial nerve lattice. PICA anatomy is among the most variable of any intracranial artery, and unusual morphology frequently calls for alternative techniques including bypass.

## Microsurgical Anatomy

### Vertebral Artery

The [vertebral-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/vertebral-artery.md) (VA) originates from the [[subclavian-artery]], ascends through the transverse foramina of C1-C6, swings behind the lateral mass of the atlas, passes anterior to the lateral [[atlanto-occipital-membrane]], and pierces the dura behind the [occipital-condyle](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/occipital-condyle.md). Intradurally, it passes over dorsal and ventral roots of C1, crosses anterior to the [dentate-ligament](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/dentate-ligament.md) and spinal [[accessory-nerve]], courses across the [[medullary-pyramid]], and joins the contralateral VA at or near the [[pontomedullary-sulcus]] to form the vertebrobasilar junction (VBJ).

### VA Branches

- **Posterior spinal artery**: first intracranial branch; courses behind the [dentate-ligament](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/dentate-ligament.md) with ascending/descending branches supplying the dorsal columns
- **[[Posterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery]]**: largest and most significant VA branch
- **Anterior spinal artery**: last branch proximal to the VBJ; forms a single midline artery supplying the pyramids, [[medial-lemniscus]], and [[hypoglossal-nerve]] nuclei

### PICA Segments

| Segment | Name | Course |
|---|---|---|
| p1 | Anterior medullary | PICA origin to medial edge of [[inferior-olive]] (past CN XII rootlets) |
| p2 | Lateral medullary | Olive to CN IX-XI rootlets at the lateral olive edge |
| p3 | Tonsillomedullary | Past CN IX-XI, descends to inferior tonsil (caudal loop), ascends along medial tonsil |
| p4 | Telovelotonsillar | Midpoint of medial tonsil ascent to [[tela-choroidea]] of [fourth-ventricle](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/fourth-ventricle.md) (cranial/supratonsillar loop) |
| p5 | Cortical | Emerges from tonsillobiventral fissure; medial trunk (vermian surface) and lateral trunk (hemispheric surface) |

### Lower Cranial Nerve Anatomy

The [[accessory-nerve]] (CN XI) traverses the entire far-lateral field from cervical rootlets through the [foramen-magnum](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/foramen-magnum.md) to the [jugular-foramen](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/jugular-foramen.md). It leads to CN IX and CN X. Key landmarks:

- **Retro-olivary sulcus**: origin of CN IX, X, XI rootlets (between lateral olive and posterolateral medulla)
- **Preolivary sulcus**: origin of CN XII rootlets (between medial olive and medullary pyramids)
- The VA always passes anterior to CN IX-XI but can split CN XII rootlets

## Anatomic Triangles

Three triangles through the lower cranial nerves define dissection routes:

| Triangle | Borders | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **Vagoaccessory** | CN X superiorly, CN XI laterally, medulla medially | Natural working window of [[far-lateral-approach]] |
| **Suprahypoglossal** | CN X, CN XI, CN XII (above XII) | Route for distal PICA origin aneurysms |
| **Infrahypoglossal** | CN XI, CN XII, medulla (below XII) | Route for proximal PICA origin aneurysms |

### Aneurysm Location by Medullary Zone

| Zone | Neural Obstacles | PICA Origin |
|---|---|---|
| Anterior medullary | CN X, XI, XII | Distal on VA |
| Lateral medullary | CN X, XI | Typical location |
| Tonsillomedullary | None (beyond nerve lattice) | Early PICA origin or distal aneurysm |

## Dissection Strategy

### Initial Steps

Preserve the arachnoid of the [[cisterna-magna]] until the microscope is in the field. Incise in the midline from vermis to cervical cord, then laterally under the tonsil.

### 6-Step Dissection

1. **Cut the [dentate-ligament](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/dentate-ligament.md)** at its foramen magnum origin and at C1 to access the VA anteriorly and widen the corridor. Prepare a segment of distal VA for proximal control. Distinguish the dentate ligament (pure white, fibrous) from CN XI (off-white, with perforating arteries, merges with cervical rootlets).
2. **Identify the PICA caudal loop** under the tonsil in the cerebellomedullary fissure. Retract the tonsil superiorly to widen the fissure; medullary and tonsillar surfaces separate easily.
3. **Converge on the PICA-VA junction** -- antegrade VA dissection and retrograde PICA dissection meet just below the aneurysm base.
4. **Identify the distal VA** medially -- it veers toward the VBJ; may require lateral traction on the aneurysm or medial traction on the medulla. Preserve small medullary perforators.
5. **Develop medullary-side neck plane** for one clip blade
6. **Develop clival-side neck plane** for the other blade

CN XII rootlets drape the VA perpendicular to the artery's axis like ladder rungs. They are workable but can settle in critical cleavage planes: the PICA axilla, over the PICA shoulder at the proximal neck, or in the crotch between the distal neck and distal VA.

## Clipping Technique

### Tandem Clipping (Most Common)

The far-lateral approach provides a view along the VA axis, aneurysms project superiorly, and the PICA often originates from the aneurysm base:

1. **Initial straight fenestrated clip**: blades parallel the efferent VA; fenestration encircles the PICA origin
2. **Stacked straight clip** over the fenestration: completes closure and generously reconstructs the PICA origin

Straight clips in tandem configuration can be maneuvered through lower cranial nerves even with deep anterior medullary zone aneurysms. Fenestrated clips can also transmit cranial nerves adherent to the neck.

### Simple Clipping

Used for small aneurysms and those located inferiorly in the vagoaccessory triangle or in the tonsillomedullary zone (superficial to cranial nerves). Multilobulated PICA aneurysms can be repaired by clipping each lobe as a distinct aneurysm.

## Bypass Techniques

The foramen magnum anatomy is excellent for bypass, with wide corridors, excellent visualization, and well-tolerated temporary occlusion:

| Technique | Indication | Method |
|---|---|---|
| **PICA-PICA in situ** | Efferent PICA loss from clipping | Side-to-side anastomosis of bilateral caudal loops |
| **PICA excision-reanastomosis** | Small/medium fusiform; distally located | End-to-end after trapping and excision |
| **PICA reimplantation** | PICA obliterated by clip; dysmorphic base | End-to-side onto proximal VA (accessible at foramen magnum) |
| **Radial artery graft** | Large/complex VA-PICA aneurysms | Extradural C1 VA to PICA; trap or proximally occlude aneurysm |
| **Occipital artery-PICA** | When intracranial options not feasible | Extracranial-intracranial bypass (tedious, often small donor) |

In situ bypasses are preferred: entirely intracranial, well-matched calibers, single anastomosis, not vulnerable to extracranial injury.

## Key Pearls

- PICA anatomy is the most variable of any intracranial artery -- origin can be anywhere along the VA trunk, even extradurally
- The [[accessory-nerve]] is the orienting landmark for the entire far-lateral field
- Aneurysm location relative to medullary zones (rather than PICA segments) better guides the dissection route
- Most PICA aneurysms project superiorly (Rhoton's third rule) with the PICA recurring toward the neurosurgeon
- The distal VA shoulders the clip tip -- must be clearly identified before permanent clipping
- An extended retrosigmoid approach may be preferable for distal VA aneurysms in the cerebellopontine angle
