# Herniation Syndromes

Brain herniation occurs when intracranial mass effect displaces brain tissue across dural boundaries (falx, tentorium, foramen magnum). Recognition of herniation patterns and their clinical stages is critical for timely neurosurgical intervention.

## Classification

| Type | Tissue displaced | Through | Key finding |
|------|-----------------|---------|-------------|
| [[uncal-herniation\|Uncal]] | Medial temporal (uncus, hippocampal gyrus) | Tentorial incisura (lateral) | Ipsilateral CN III palsy → dilated pupil |
| [[central-herniation\|Central]] | Diencephalon | Tentorial incisura (axial) | Progressive rostrocaudal deterioration |
| [[tonsillar-herniation\|Tonsillar]] | Cerebellar tonsils | Foramen magnum | Respiratory arrest (rapidly fatal) |
| Subfalcine | Cingulate gyrus | Under falx cerebri | Contralateral leg weakness, [ACA](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/anterior-cerebral-artery.md) infarct |
| Upward cerebellar | Cerebellar vermis | Above tentorium | [SCA](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/superior-cerebellar-artery.md) compression, hydrocephalus |
| External | Brain through cranial defect | Craniectomy site | Fungus cerebri |

## Uncal Herniation

Most common acute herniation — typically from rapidly expanding traumatic hematomas (lateral middle fossa or temporal lobe). The uncus and hippocampal gyrus are pushed over the tentorial edge, entrapping the [CN III](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/oculomotor-nerve.md) and directly compressing the midbrain. [PCA](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/posterior-cerebral-artery.md) may be occluded.

**Earliest consistent sign:** unilaterally dilating pupil (ipsilateral CN III compression). Impaired consciousness is NOT a reliable early sign.

### Stages

**Early CN III stage:**
- Pupil: ipsilateral dilation with sluggish light reflex
- Oculomotor: normal or dysconjugate doll's eyes
- Respiration: normal
- Motor: appropriate response to pain, contralateral Babinski

**Late CN III stage** (midbrain dysfunction imminent):
- Pupil: fully dilated, fixed ipsilaterally
- External oculomotor ophthalmoplegia (EOO)
- Consciousness: stupor → coma
- Motor: contralateral weakness (or ipsilateral — Kernohan's notch phenomenon, false localizing) → bilateral decerebration

**Midbrain—upper pons:** both pupils midposition (5-6 mm), fixed. From here, indistinguishable from central herniation.

## Central (Transtentorial) Herniation

More chronic progression than uncal — typically from frontal, parietal, or occipital tumors. The diencephalon is gradually forced through the tentorial incisura.

### Stages

**Diencephalic stage** (reversible with treatment):
- Consciousness: lethargy → agitation → stupor → coma
- Respiration: sighs, yawns, pauses → Cheyne-Stokes
- Pupils: small (1-3 mm), reactive
- Motor: bilateral Babinski, gegenhalten → decorticate posturing

**Midbrain—upper pons** (prognosis very poor, <5% good recovery):
- Pupils: midposition (3-5 mm), fixed
- Respiration: sustained tachypnea
- Motor: decorticate → bilateral decerebrate

**Lower pons—upper medulla:**
- Respiration: shallow, rapid (20-40/min)
- Motor: flaccid, bilateral Babinski

**Medullary (terminal):**
- Respiration: irregular, gasping → apnea
- Pupils: dilate widely with hypoxia

### Outcome

Series of 153 patients with central herniation signs: 9% good recovery, 18% functional outcome, 60% died. Better outcomes in age ≤17, anisocoria with non-flaccid motor. Bilateral fixed pupils: only 3.5% functional recovery.

## Tonsillar Herniation

Cerebellar tonsils cone through the foramen magnum, compressing the medulla → respiratory arrest. Usually rapidly fatal. Can occur with supra- or infratentorial masses, elevated [[intracranial-pressure\|ICP]], obstruction at foramen magnum (e.g., [[chiari-malformation\|Chiari malformation]]), or obstructive [[hydrocephalus]]. May be precipitated by lumbar puncture.

## Upward Cerebellar Herniation

Posterior fossa masses can push the cerebellar vermis above the tentorium, compressing the midbrain. May occlude [SCAs](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/superior-cerebellar-artery.md) → cerebellar infarction. Can compress the Sylvian aqueduct → [[hydrocephalus]]. May be exacerbated by ventriculostomy.

## CT/MRI Criteria

Impending uncal herniation: encroachment on lateral aspect of suprasellar cistern → loss of normal pentagonal shape. Once herniation occurs: brainstem displacement and flattening, compression of contralateral cerebral peduncle, midbrain rotation, contralateral [[hydrocephalus]].
