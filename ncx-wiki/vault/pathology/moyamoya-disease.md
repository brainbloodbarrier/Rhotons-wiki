# Moyamoya Disease

Moyamoya is a chronic, bilateral, progressive stenosis of the **supraclinoid internal carotid artery** and its immediate circle-of-Willis branches (A1, M1, proximal PCoA), accompanied by the development of a fragile basal collateral network from lenticulostriate, thalamoperforating, and choroidal perforators — the smoky angiographic appearance that names the disease. When bilateral and idiopathic it is **moyamoya disease**; when secondary to another condition (Down syndrome, sickle cell, NF1, post-radiation, atherosclerosis) it is **moyamoya syndrome**.

## Epidemiology

- Bimodal age distribution: peak in children 5–10 y, second peak in adults 30–50 y.
- East Asian predominance (Japan incidence ~0.5/100,000; US ~0.086/100,000 but rising with better recognition).
- Female:male ~2:1.
- Familial in ~10–15% (RNF213 susceptibility variant in East Asian cohorts).

## Pathophysiology

Progressive intimal hyperplasia of the distal ICA without atherosclerotic plaque or inflammatory infiltrate produces bilateral stenosis that is staged by the classical **Suzuki angiographic grading** (I–VI, from ICA narrowing → collateral bloom → collateral fadeout → ECA-pial collateralization). Chronic hemodynamic failure in the ICA territory recruits:

- **Lenticulostriate** and **thalamoperforator** hypertrophy → the smoky basal ganglia "moyamoya vessels."
- **Transdural ECA** collaterals from middle meningeal and STA (already partial before surgery).
- **Leptomeningeal PCA-to-MCA** pial-pial collaterals.

These fragile collaterals account for the characteristic adult-presentation hemorrhage (rupture of a dilated perforator or aneurysm at a stressed collateral branch point).

## Clinical presentation

| Age | Presentation | Mechanism |
|-----|--------------|-----------|
| Child (<15) | TIA or ischemic stroke, often hyperventilation-induced (crying, blowing, hot food) | Vasoconstrictive CO2 wash-out in a critically-balanced ICA territory |
| Child | Dystonia, chorea | Basal ganglia ischemia |
| Adult | Intracerebral or intraventricular hemorrhage | Rupture of dilated perforator / basal moyamoya vessel |
| Adult | Cognitive decline, headache, TIA | Chronic hemodynamic failure |

Pediatric cases generally present with ischemia; adult cases more often present with hemorrhage — a clinically useful dichotomy for consent and urgency.

## Diagnosis

- **MR / MRA**: "ivy sign" (leptomeningeal FLAIR hyperintensity from slow collateral flow), basal flow-voids from hypertrophied lenticulostriates, distal ICA narrowing.
- **DSA**: gold standard for Suzuki staging and surgical planning; confirms bilaterality and maps baseline ECA-pial collaterals.
- **Perfusion** (SPECT, xenon-CT, ASL, or CT perfusion with acetazolamide challenge): demonstrates exhausted vascular reserve — the physiologic indication for bypass.
- **Genetic testing**: RNF213 in East Asian cohorts.

## Treatment

Medical therapy alone (antiplatelet, calcium-channel blocker) does not halt progression. Surgical [[cerebral-revascularization-overview|revascularization]] is the cornerstone once ischemia or exhausted reserve is demonstrated:

### Direct bypass
- **STA-MCA bypass** (the classic [[ec-ic-bypass]]) — pedicled [[superficial-temporal-artery]] to an M4 MCA recipient. Provides immediate augmentation and is the gold-standard adult choice.
- [[radial-artery-graft]] or [[saphenous-vein-graft]] is rarely used in moyamoya — the native low-flow target territory would be oversupplied.

### Indirect bypass
- **Encephalo-duro-arterio-synangiosis (EDAS)**: STA laid over pial surface; new collaterals ingrow over 6–12 months.
- **Encephalo-myo-synangiosis (EMS)**: temporalis muscle placed onto brain surface for angiogenesis.
- **Encephalo-duro-arterio-myo-synangiosis (EDAMS)**: combined — preferred pediatric operation.
- **Burr holes**: simple pial synangiosis via multiple burr holes (useful for multi-territory disease).

### Combined
- Most adult centers perform **direct + indirect** at the same sitting: STA-MCA bypass plus a dural/temporalis onlay for delayed augmentation.

A [[pterional-approach]] craniotomy is the standard exposure for combined procedures (frontotemporal window allows STA recipient identification and a wide pial onlay). Children typically tolerate indirect procedures better than adults; adults benefit from the immediate protection of direct flow.

## Prognosis and outcomes

- Surgical revascularization reduces 5-year stroke risk from ~65% (medical) to <10% (surgical) in symptomatic patients.
- Hemorrhagic-onset adults: the JAM (Japan Adult Moyamoya) trial demonstrated reduction in recurrent hemorrhage with bypass.
- Progression halts in ~90% of operated hemispheres; the contralateral untreated hemisphere continues to progress and is usually also operated.
