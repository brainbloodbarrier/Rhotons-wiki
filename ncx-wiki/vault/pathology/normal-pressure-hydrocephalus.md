# Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus

Normal pressure hydrocephalus (NPH), or Hakim-Adams syndrome (1965), is one of the few treatable causes of dementia. Communicating [[hydrocephalus]] with normal opening pressure on lumbar puncture. Incidence ~5.5/100,000/year for idiopathic NPH (iNPH). Mean age >60 years, slight male predominance.

## Classification

| Type | Characteristics |
|------|----------------|
| **Idiopathic (iNPH)** | No identifiable cause, LP pressure <10-12 cm CSF |
| **Secondary NPH** | Post-SAH, post-meningitis, post-traumatic, post-radiation, post-surgery. LP ~20 cm CSF |

## Classic Triad

**"Wet, wacky, and wobbly"** — mnemonic for incontinence, dementia, gait disturbance.

### 1. Gait Disturbance (earliest, most responsive to treatment)
- Wide-based, short shuffling steps
- **Magnetic gait** — feet "glued to the floor"
- Difficulty initiating steps and turns (multistep turns)
- Retropulsion, frequent falls
- Appendicular ataxia characteristically **absent**

### 2. Dementia
- Primary: memory impairment + **bradyphrenia** (slowness of thought)
- Frontal-subcortical pattern: apathy, lack of motivation, daytime sleepiness
- **Not expected:** expressive/receptive dysphasia, dysnomia, difficulty recognizing familiar people, hallucinations

### 3. Urinary Incontinence
- Urinary urgency with impaired inhibition of bladder emptying
- Incontinence **without awareness** is not characteristic (suggests other dementing process)

## NPH vs Alzheimer's vs Parkinson's

| Feature | NPH | Alzheimer's | Parkinson's |
|---------|-----|-------------|-------------|
| Gait disturbance | + (early) | ± (late) | ± |
| Memory/cognitive | ± | + | ± |
| Urinary disturbance | ± | ± | ± |
| Limb rigidity/tremor | -- | -- | + |
| Bradykinesia | ± | -- | + |
| Behavioral changes | ± | + | ± |

11% of iNPH patients have symptoms identical to idiopathic Parkinsonism.

## Diagnostic Workup

### "Dementia Blood Testing"
CBC, electrolytes, B12, folate, TSH. Consider: RPR (syphilis), Lyme ELISA, vitamin D.

### Imaging
- **CT/MRI:** ventriculomegaly out of proportion to sulcal atrophy (Evans index >0.3)
- High-volume tap test or extended lumbar drainage as shunt-responsiveness predictor

### Features Suggesting NPH (not other dementia)
- Ventriculomegaly disproportionate to cortical atrophy
- Callosal angle <90° on coronal MRI
- Periventricular T2 hyperintensity (transependymal CSF migration)

### Features NOT Expected in NPH
Papilledema, seizures (pre-shunt), headaches, spasticity, hyperreflexia, lateralizing findings.

## Treatment

**CSF shunting** — [[ventriculoperitoneal-shunt\|VP shunt]] is treatment of choice.
- Gait disturbance: most responsive to treatment
- Dementia: partially responsive (better if early, worse if advanced)
- Incontinence: variable response

**Predictors of shunt response:**
- Gait predominant symptom
- Known etiology (secondary NPH)
- Short symptom duration
- Improvement after high-volume tap test (30-50 mL CSF removal)
- B-waves on continuous ICP monitoring

**Glymphatic pathway:** emerging concept — ventricular enlargement may not be primary pathology. CNS lymphatics ("glymphatics") gaining acceptance as key to understanding iNPH. ^[inferred]

## Differential Diagnosis

Neurodegenerative: Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, Lewy body, frontotemporal dementia, PSP, MSA

Vascular: multi-infarct dementia, Binswanger's, CADASIL, vertebrobasilar insufficiency

Other: aqueductal stenosis, arrested [[hydrocephalus]], LOVA syndrome, spinal stenosis, [[chiari-malformation]], B12 deficiency, Wernicke's encephalopathy
