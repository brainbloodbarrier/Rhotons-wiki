# Trigeminal Neuralgia

Disorder of ephaptic transmission — neurovascular compression of CN V causes unilateral paroxysmal lancinating facial pain. Also known as tic douloureux.

## Epidemiology

- **Incidence:** >15,000 new cases/year (USA)
- **Age:** Peak in 6th–8th decade; can occur at any age
- **Sex:** F > M (3:2)
- **Side:** Right > Left (3:2)
- **Distribution:** 95% in V2/V3; V1 more common in elderly men with dolichoectatic vessels
- **Bilateral:** ~5% of cases (1.5% in Fukushima series)
- **Familial:** Up to 5%
- **Multiple sclerosis:** 1,000× increased risk

## Pathophysiology

Neurovascular compression → ephaptic transmission (cross-excitation between nerve fibers). Pulsatile compression-distortion of CN V by arterial or venous structures at the root entry zone.

### Offending Vessels (Fukushima, n=2,488)

| Vessel | Cases (%) |
|--------|-----------|
| SCA alone | 55.8% |
| SCA + AICA | 16.7% |
| AICA alone | 6.6% |
| VA-BA (dolichoectatic) | 4.3% |
| Vein alone | 2.7% |
| No vessel found | 5.7% |
| **Brain tumor** | **7.6%** |
| AVM | 0.5% |

Tumors causing TN: epidermoid (4.0%), [[meningioma]] (1.9%), [[vestibular-schwannoma]] (1.2%), trigeminal neuroma (0.3%).

### Clinical-Pathological Correlations (Jannetta)

- **V2/V2-V3 pain:** Rostral compression of nerve
- **Isolated V2:** Medial/lateral compression; distal bridging vein (especially young women)
- **V1 pain:** Caudal compression (dolichoectatic VA/BA pushing into caudal nerve)
- **Constant burning (Type 2):** Compression of motor-proprioceptive fascicles
- **Autonomic symptoms:** Stretch of motor-proprioceptive fascicles → may progress to cluster headache

## Fukushima Classification

| Class | Description | MVD Cure Rate |
|-------|-------------|---------------|
| **I** | Typical classical tic douloureux | 96% |
| **II** | Atypical tic — mixed typical + atypical features | 70% |
| **III** | Atypical face pain — continuous dull ache/pressure | Surgery contraindicated |
| **IV** | Pain from tumors, AVMs, craniofacial lesions | Treat underlying pathology |

## Clinical Features

### History

- **Lancinating pain:** Brief, excruciating, memorable first attack
- **Trigger points:** Nose (high nerve ending density), face; patient holds hand ABOVE skin (diagnostic)
- **Precipitants:** Talking, chewing, face washing, shaving, fatigue, position changes
- **Refractory period:** Brief post-attack window — patients eat, brush teeth, wash face during this interval
- **Progression:** Pain spreads over face with time; episodes become more frequent (not more severe)
- **"Afterburn":** Later in disease, constant burning component develops (Type 2)
- **Weight loss:** Common — patients unable to eat solid food
- **Rarely awakened from sleep**

### Physical Examination

- Patient holds face still ("stiff upper lip") — movement precipitates attacks
- Mild sensory loss in 1/3 of TN Type 1 (often unnoticed)
- Test light touch with single cotton filament; go from abnormal to normal
- Previous ablative procedures → hypesthesia dolorosa

## Medical Treatment

### Carbamazepine (First-Line)

- Start 100 mg BID → increase 200 mg/day every 48 hours
- Max 1,200 mg/day before labs and exam
- Blood chemistries at 1, 2, 4 weeks; LFTs annually
- **Stop immediately** if skin rash → risk of Stevens-Johnson syndrome
- Re-challenge contraindicated after rash

### Gabapentin (Alternative)

- Better side effect profile, less effective
- Start 300 mg QD-BID → increase 300 mg every 2-3 days

## Surgical Treatment

### Indications

Surgery indicated when:
1. Pain not controlled by medication
2. Intolerable medication side effects
3. Medically refractory (>5% eventually develop drug-resistant pain)

### Microvascular Decompression (Treatment of Choice)

**Technique (Fukushima keyhole approach):**
- Lateral decubitus position, 3-pin Mayfield fixation
- 3-4 cm curvilinear incision, upper retromastoid
- Keyhole craniectomy at transverse-sigmoid junction
- Identify ALL offending vessels (brainstem to Meckel's cave)
- Neurovascular transposition with shredded Teflon
- "There is at least one offending blood vessel — I must find and treat all" (Jannetta)

**Outcomes (Fukushima, n=2,488):**
- Class I cure rate: 96%
- Class II: 70%
- Mean operative time: 1-3 hours
- Most patients discharge 30-40 hours post-admission

### Ablative Procedures

| Procedure | Mechanism | Pros | Cons |
|-----------|-----------|------|------|
| **RF rhizotomy** | Thermocoagulation of ganglion | Good initial pain relief | Temporary; 20% numbness problems; 2% anesthesia dolorosa |
| **Glycerol rhizotomy** | Chemical ablation in Meckel's cave | Easy; minimal nerve injury | High recurrence; slow onset; can't use in severe pain |
| **Balloon compression** | Mechanical compression of ganglion | Spares corneal sensation | Variable numbness (2 years); recurrence when numbness fades |
| **Gamma knife/SRS** | Focused radiation to nerve root | "Noninvasive" | Frequent recurrence; hypesthesia/dysesthesia; anesthesia dolorosa risk |

## Glossopharyngeal Neuralgia

Related ephaptic transmission disorder of CN IX/X. Same mechanism as TN but different distribution.

- **Pain:** Lancinating in tonsillar bed, throat, tongue base, ear
- **Precipitant:** Swallowing (weight loss common)
- **Age:** Mean onset 50 years
- **Key difference from TN:** May awaken from sleep; cardiovascular changes (vagal — syncope, bradycardia)
- **Treatment:** MVD of CN IX/X at brainstem; nerve section if MVD unavailable
- **Position changes:** Especially early in disease course, before vessel becomes deeply impacted
