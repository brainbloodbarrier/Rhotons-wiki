# Glioblastoma

Glioblastoma, IDH-wildtype (GBM) is the most common and most aggressive primary malignant brain tumor. WHO grade 4 by definition. Median survival ~14-16 months with standard treatment.

## Molecular Definition (WHO CNS5)

GBM is defined as an IDH-wildtype diffuse astrocytic glioma with **any** of:
- Microvascular proliferation or necrosis (histologic)
- TERT promoter mutation
- EGFR amplification
- Combined +7/-10 (whole chromosome 7 gain / chromosome 10 loss)

This means even a histologically grade 2-3 IDH-wildtype diffuse glioma with these molecular features is classified as GBM.

**Key distinction from astrocytoma IDH-mutant grade 4:**
- GBM IDH-WT: no IDH mutation, tends to have large central necrosis, more perilesional edema
- AIM grade 4: has IDH mutation, less necrosis, often larger at diagnosis, median survival ~10.9 years vs ~16 months

## Biomarker Testing (Mandatory)

| Marker | Strength | Clinical relevance |
|--------|----------|-------------------|
| IDH1/2 mutation | Strong | Defines IDH-WT vs IDH-mutant (prognosis, treatment) |
| MGMT promoter methylation | Strong | Predicts temozolomide benefit (methylated = better response) |
| TERT promoter mutation | Conditional | Supports GBM diagnosis in IDH-WT grade 2-3 |
| EGFR amplification | Strong | Molecular criterion for GBM in IDH-WT |
| +7/-10 | Strong | Molecular criterion for GBM in IDH-WT |

## Imaging

**CT:** central necrosis with ring enhancement. Nonenhancing center = necrosis or cyst.

**MRI:**
- T1: hypointense mass with ring enhancement post-gadolinium
- T2/FLAIR: extensive perilesional edema/infiltration
- Tumor cells extend ≥15 mm beyond the enhancing ring
- Location: temporal > parietal > frontal > occipital

**Butterfly glioma:** spread through corpus callosum (genu → bilateral frontal, splenium → bilateral parietal/occipital)

## Spread Mechanisms

1. White matter tracts (corpus callosum, internal capsule, uncinate fasciculus, cerebral peduncles)
2. CSF pathways (10-25% subarachnoid seeding in high-grade gliomas)
3. Systemic metastasis (rare)

Less than 10% of recurrent gliomas recur away from the original site.

## Treatment

### Stupp Protocol (Standard of Care)
1. **Maximal safe resection** — extent of resection correlates with survival
2. **Concurrent chemoradiation:** temozolomide (75 mg/m²/day) + fractionated radiotherapy (60 Gy in 30 fractions)
3. **Adjuvant temozolomide:** 150-200 mg/m², days 1-5 of 28-day cycle, 6-12 cycles

### MGMT Methylation Impact
- **Methylated:** median OS ~21-23 months (better temozolomide response)
- **Unmethylated:** median OS ~12-15 months
- In elderly (>65-70): methylated → temozolomide monotherapy may be equivalent to combined; unmethylated → RT alone may be preferred

### Tumor-treating Fields (TTFields)
Alternating electric fields (Optune). Added to maintenance temozolomide, improves median OS by ~5 months.

### Recurrence
- Bevacizumab (anti-VEGF): improves PFS but not OS
- Re-resection: selected cases
- Re-irradiation: selected cases
- Clinical trials

## Prognosis

| Factor | Better prognosis | Worse prognosis |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Age | Younger | Older (>65) |
| KPS | ≥70 | <70 |
| Extent of resection | Gross total | Biopsy only |
| MGMT methylation | Methylated | Unmethylated |
| Molecular | N/A | TERT+/EGFR+/+7-10 = definition |
