# Craniopharyngioma

Craniopharyngioma is a slow-growing, histologically benign tumor of the sellar-suprasellar junction that behaves clinically as a malignant disease because of its intimate relationship with the hypothalamus, optic apparatus, pituitary stalk, circle of Willis, and third-ventricle floor. It is the most common non-neuroepithelial tumor of the CNS in childhood and one of the most challenging surgical problems in the skull base.

## Origins and histology

Craniopharyngiomas arise from squamous epithelial rests along the **craniopharyngeal duct** — the involuting migration track of Rathke's pouch from the oropharyngeal roof to the sella. Two distinct WHO subtypes:

### Adamantinomatous
- Bimodal age peaks: 5–14 y and 50–75 y.
- Driven by **CTNNB1** mutations activating the WNT / β-catenin pathway.
- Gross: multi-cystic, calcified, with cystic fluid resembling "crankcase oil" (cholesterol crystals).
- Micro: peripheral palisading, stellate reticulum, wet keratin nodules.
- Classically adherent to hypothalamus and pituitary stalk — makes *total* excision hazardous.

### Papillary
- Adults only (40+).
- Driven by **BRAF V600E** mutations — a new target for MEK-BRAF therapy (vemurafenib, dabrafenib + trametinib).
- Gross: predominantly solid, well-circumscribed, typically more separable from hypothalamus.
- Micro: mature squamous epithelium, papillary architecture, no keratin.

## Clinical presentation

- **Visual** — bitemporal hemianopsia, optic atrophy, decreased acuity. Begin with supero-temporal quadrantanopia when chiasm compressed from below.
- **Endocrine** — growth failure in children, secondary amenorrhea, hypogonadism, central hypothyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, diabetes insipidus (~15% at presentation).
- **Hypothalamic** — obesity, hyperphagia, disordered thermoregulation, behavioral changes — particularly after aggressive surgery.
- **Mass effect / hydrocephalus** — headache, cognitive decline, 3rd-ventricle obstruction.
- **Rathke cleft cyst**-like chemical meningitis if cyst ruptures into subarachnoid space.

## Imaging

- **MRI**: mixed solid-cystic; adamantinomatous often T1-hyperintense (proteinaceous / cholesterol-rich fluid), calcifications visible; papillary predominantly solid; both enhance heterogeneously.
- **CT**: sensitive for calcifications in adamantinomatous form.
- **Visual fields / OCT** pre- and post-op.
- Full pituitary hormone panel including DDAVP challenge when DI suspected.

## Operative strategy

### Approach choice depends on morphology and extension

- **Endoscopic endonasal transsphenoidal** — midline suprasellar / infrachiasmal tumors; increasingly favored when no lateral extension into cavernous sinus or behind optic nerve.
- **Pterional / orbitozygomatic** — retrochiasmatic components, lateral extension, vascular proximity to ACA / circle of Willis.
- **Anterior interhemispheric / transcallosal** — pure third-ventricle / intraventricular lesions. See [[anterior-interhemispheric-approach]].
- **Subfrontal** — broad access to suprasellar cistern and chiasm.
- **Combined / staged** — for giant, complex lesions spanning multiple compartments.

### Principles (Yaşargil doctrine, modernized)

- Early identification and preservation of the **pituitary stalk** where possible (functional endocrine outcome).
- **Hypothalamic interface** — stop short of aggressive dissection off the floor of the third ventricle when adherent; better to leave residual than injure hypothalamus.
- Protect the **optic chiasm** and **optic-nerve pial blood supply**.
- Preserve the **perforators** to hypothalamus and brainstem arising from the ICA, PCoA, and basilar apex.
- Meticulous [[subarachnoid-dissection]] of the optico-carotid, opticochiasmatic, and carotid cisterns.
- For anterior interhemispheric corridors, respect the [[pericallosal-artery]] / [[callosomarginal-artery]] complex and the [[superior-sagittal-sinus]] draining veins.

### Adjuvant options

- **Stereotactic radiosurgery** — for residual / recurrent disease away from the optic apparatus. See [[stereotactic-radiosurgery]].
- **Intracystic therapy** — bleomycin or interferon-α into recurrent cysts via Ommaya reservoir.
- **BRAF-MEK inhibition** — dramatic responses in papillary subtype with V600E.
- **Proton-beam radiation** in pediatrics — reduces long-term cognitive effects.

## Postoperative management

- **Fluid-balance monitoring** — triphasic DI / SIADH response common.
- **Stress-dose glucocorticoids** perioperatively, tapered based on morning cortisol.
- **Thyroid, gonadal, growth-hormone replacement** per endocrine evaluation.
- **Appetite / weight** — hypothalamic obesity management is a lifelong issue.
- **Visual and cognitive rehabilitation**.

## Outcomes

- Gross-total resection: 10-year survival 80–90%; recurrence 15–30% even with GTR.
- Subtotal resection + adjuvant radiotherapy approaches equivalent tumor control with often better functional outcome.
- **Quality-of-life determinants**: hypothalamic dysfunction, endocrine replacement burden, visual outcome.
- Children with hypothalamic involvement are at risk for severe obesity and neurobehavioral sequelae — an increasingly recognized argument for subtotal resection + radiation in selected cases.
