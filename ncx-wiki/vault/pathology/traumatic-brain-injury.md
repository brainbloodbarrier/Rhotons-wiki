# Traumatic Brain Injury

Traumatic brain injury is the leading cause of death and long-term disability in patients under 45. Neurosurgical management is only one pillar of care — equally important is the systematic prevention of **secondary brain injury** driven by hypoxia, hypotension, hyperthermia, hyperglycemia, seizures, and intracranial hypertension. The core conceptual framework is the distinction between primary injury (at the moment of impact, irreversible) and secondary injury (subsequent, often preventable).

## Classification

### Severity (GCS)
- **Mild TBI / concussion**: GCS 13–15, brief or no LOC, no focal deficit.
- **Moderate TBI**: GCS 9–12.
- **Severe TBI**: GCS ≤8 — requires intubation and ICP-monitoring consideration.

### Mechanism
- **Blunt / closed** — falls (elderly), MVC (young), assault, sports.
- **Penetrating** — gunshot, stab wounds.
- **Blast** — primary (shockwave), secondary (fragment), tertiary (translation).

### Anatomic / radiographic
- **Focal** — contusion, EDH, SDH, intraparenchymal hematoma.
- **Diffuse** — concussion, diffuse axonal injury (DAI).
- **Marshall CT classification** (I–VI: diffuse injury → bilateral swelling → mass lesion) and **Rotterdam score** predict mortality.

## Primary injury patterns

- **Cortical contusion** — coup / contre-coup; frontal poles, temporal tips, orbital surfaces; "blossom" on follow-up imaging.
- **Diffuse axonal injury** — shear at gray-white junction, corpus callosum, dorsolateral brainstem; MRI SWI or DTI sensitive.
- **Epidural hematoma** — see [[epidural-hematoma]].
- **Acute subdural hematoma** — see [[subdural-hematoma]].
- **Traumatic subarachnoid hemorrhage** — convexity pattern, typically benign if isolated but raises vasospasm concern.
- **Intraventricular hemorrhage** — from torn subependymal veins; may obstruct CSF flow.

## Secondary injury cascade

The critical window. Drivers:

- **Hypoxia** (SpO₂ <90%) doubles mortality.
- **Hypotension** (SBP <90) doubles mortality; even a single episode.
- **Hyperthermia** worsens outcome; maintain 36–37°C.
- **Hyperglycemia** worsens ischemic penumbra.
- **Seizures** — occur in 10–20%; post-traumatic status is devastating.
- **Intracranial hypertension** drives [[herniation-syndromes]].
- **Cerebral edema** — cytotoxic + vasogenic, peaking 48–72 h.

## Initial assessment

- **ATLS** — airway (intubate GCS ≤8), breathing, circulation, disability (GCS, pupils), exposure.
- **GCS** with trend; pupils; focal deficits; spine precautions.
- **Non-contrast head CT** for all moderate/severe TBI and mild TBI with red flags (Canadian CT head rule, NOC).
- **C-spine imaging** mandatory in obtunded patients.
- Reverse anticoagulation aggressively.

## ICU management — BTF tiered algorithm

### Tier 0 — baseline
- Head of bed 30°, midline neck, avoid jugular venous obstruction.
- Normothermia, normonatremia (145–150 in at-risk patients), normoglycemia.
- Sedation / analgesia; PPI; DVT prophylaxis as bleeding allows.
- Seizure prophylaxis (levetiracetam) for 7 days.

### Tier 1
- Escalate sedation / analgesia.
- Hyperosmolar therapy (mannitol 0.25–1 g/kg *or* 3% NaCl bolus / infusion).
- Mild hyperventilation (PaCO₂ 32–35) as short-term rescue.
- CSF drainage via ventriculostomy.

### Tier 2
- Neuromuscular blockade.
- Higher-dose hyperosmolar therapy.
- MAP augmentation to support CPP 60–70 mm Hg.

### Tier 3
- Decompressive craniectomy (DECRA / RESCUE-ICP).
- Barbiturate coma (thiopental / pentobarbital) with continuous EEG.
- Induced hypothermia (not first-line; controversial).

## Surgical indications

- Evacuation of mass lesions per Bullock thresholds — [[epidural-hematoma]], [[subdural-hematoma]], large contusion with shift.
- Decompressive craniectomy for refractory intracranial hypertension.
- ICP monitor (parenchymal vs ventricular) for GCS ≤8 with abnormal CT, or ≥2 of age >40, motor posturing, SBP <90.
- Depressed skull fractures open or with dural penetration.

## Penetrating TBI

- Stabilize hemodynamics and coagulation.
- Liberal use of broad-spectrum antibiotics and tetanus prophylaxis.
- Operative debridement of accessible, devitalized tissue and watertight dural closure.
- Retained bullet fragments are generally left in place unless causing mass effect or CSF leak.
- Penetrating brain injury has high mortality; decisions about extent of surgery should consider prognosis.

## Outcomes

- Severe TBI mortality ~30%; good recovery in 25–35%.
- **Outcome modifiers**: age, GCS motor score, pupillary reactivity, CT pattern (Marshall/Rotterdam), secondary insult prevention.
- Validated prognostic tools: **IMPACT** and **CRASH** calculators.
- Chronic sequelae — post-concussive syndrome, post-traumatic epilepsy, behavioral and cognitive deficits, chronic traumatic encephalopathy.
