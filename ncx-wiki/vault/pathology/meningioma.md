# Meningioma

Meningiomas are the most common primary intracranial tumor, arising from arachnoid cap cells. Female predominance (2-3:1). Most are WHO grade 1 (benign) and potentially curable with surgery.

## WHO Classification

### Grade 1 (Benign)
Meningothelial, fibrous, transitional, psammomatous, angiomatous, microcystic, secretory, lymphoplasmacyte-rich, metaplastic

### Grade 2 (Atypical)
Chordoid, clear cell, atypical. Criteria: ≥4 mitoses per 10 HPF, or brain invasion, or ≥3 of: increased cellularity, small cells, prominent nucleoli, sheeting, necrosis.

### Grade 3 (Anaplastic/Malignant)
Papillary, rhabdoid, anaplastic. Criteria: ≥20 mitoses per 10 HPF, or frank anaplasia.

## Simpson Grading System (Extent of Resection)

| Grade | Degree of removal | Recurrence rate |
|-------|-------------------|-----------------|
| I | Complete removal + excision of dural attachment + abnormal bone | 9% |
| II | Complete removal + coagulation of dural attachment | 19% |
| III | Complete removal without resection/coagulation of dura | 29% |
| IV | Partial removal (tumor left in situ) | 44% |
| V | Simple decompression (± biopsy) | N/A |

## Surgical Principles

### General Approach
1. **Early interruption of arterial blood supply** (often on deep aspect — difficult to access early)
2. **Internal decompression** (debulking with ultrasonic aspirator)
3. **Collapse capsule into decompression zone**, dissect from brain
4. **Remove attached bone and dura** when possible (Simpson I goal)

### Pre-operative Embolization
Reduces vascularity of bloody tumors. Some advocate waiting 7-10 days post-embolization for tumor necrosis. Complications: hemorrhage, CN deficits, stroke, scalp necrosis.

### By Location

**Convexity:** craniotomy 1-2 cm beyond tumor attachment. Resect involved dura, reconstruct with fascia lata or pericranium.

**Parasagittal/Falx:**
- Anterior 1/3 SSS: may be divided safely
- Posterior to coronal suture: **must NOT divide SSS** — risk of severe venous infarction
- If SSS extensively involved: leave residual tumor + follow with MRI ± SRS ^[inferred]

**Sphenoid wing:**
- Lateral: approach similar to convexity
- Medial: [[pterional-approach\|pterional]] or FTOZ. ICA/MCA often encased. Leave cavernous sinus invasion → radiosurgery

**Olfactory groove:** bifrontal craniotomy. SSS ligated anteriorly. Posterior capsule may encase ACA branches and optic nerves/chiasm.

**Tuberculum sellae:** subfrontal or expanded [[pterional-approach\|pterional]]. Optic apparatus at risk.

## Sinus Involvement

Critical surgical decision — SSS involvement in middle/posterior third is treacherous:
- Attempting to occlude/bypass: 8% morbidity, 3% mortality even in expert hands
- Venous graft thrombosis rate ~50%, synthetic grafts ~100%
- **Preferred strategy for most:** leave residual, follow with serial imaging, SRS for documented growth

## Radiation Therapy

### Indications for Post-op XRT

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|---------------|
| Grade 1, any Simpson | NOT recommended (even subtotal) unless documented progression |
| Grade 2, subtotal (Simpson IV-V) | Recommended |
| Grade 2, gross total (Simpson I-III) | Follow; XRT for recurrence |
| Grade 2, recurrent | Recommended regardless of resection extent |
| Grade 3 | Recommended regardless of resection extent |

**Dose:** 55-60 Gy fractionated for grade 2-3.

**Stereotactic radiosurgery:** option for tumors <2.3-3 cm, residual after surgery, or poor surgical candidates.

## Recurrence

Recurrence rates by Simpson grade and WHO grade. UCSF series: total resection 4% recurrence, partial without XRT 60%, partial with XRT 32%. Mean time to recurrence longer with XRT (125 vs 66 months).

## Perioperative Considerations

- Morbidity significantly higher in patients >70 years (23% vs 3.5%)
- Meningiomas are often very bloody — autologous blood donation consideration
- Hyperostotic bone: tumor invasion present in some but not all. Bone biopsies 2 cm beyond hyperostosis negative in all grade 1-2 cases, positive in all grade 3 cases
- Dural tail: infrequently contains invasive tumor; excise what is reasonable without chasing
