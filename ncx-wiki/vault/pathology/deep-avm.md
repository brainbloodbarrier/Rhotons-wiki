# Deep Arteriovenous Malformation

Deep AVMs are situated in the [[insula]] and central core of the hemisphere. Their depth and reliance on perforating artery supply make them completely different from lobar AVMs ([[frontal-avm]], [[temporal-avm]], [[parieto-occipital-avm]]) and from [[ventricular-avm]], which reside in CSF-filled chambers rather than parenchyma. Deep AVMs are intimidating, but small AVMs with hemorrhagic presentation, secondary deficits, or hematoma cavities should not be considered inoperable.

## Microsurgical Anatomy

### Central Core

The central core sits between the [[insula]] and the midline, composed of the [[basal-ganglia]] ([[putamen]], [[globus-pallidus]], [[caudate-nucleus]]), [[thalamus]], [[fornix]], internal/external/extreme capsules, and claustrum. All information between cortex and brainstem traverses this region.

### Perforating Arteries

Small end-arteries that terminate in critical deep structures are the defining feature of deep AVM surgery:

| Perforator | Parent | Structures Supplied |
|---|---|---|
| Lateral lenticulostriate (lLSA) | M1 [[middle-cerebral-artery]] (~10 perforators) | Upper [[internal-capsule]], [[globus-pallidus]], [[caudate-nucleus]] |
| Medial lenticulostriate (mLSA) | A1 [[anterior-cerebral-artery]] (~8 perforators) | Hypothalamus, optic structures |
| Recurrent artery of Heubner (RAH) | A1-A2 ACA junction | Caudate head, anterior [[internal-capsule]] |
| Insular perforators (InsP) | M2 MCA (distal to [[limen-insulae]]) | Insula, lateral [[basal-ganglia]] |
| Anterior thalamoperforators (AntThaP) | PCoA (~8 perforators) | Posterior hypothalamus, ventral [[thalamus]] |
| Posterior thalamoperforators (PosThaP) | P1 [[posterior-cerebral-artery]] | [[Thalamus]], subthalamus, medial midbrain |
| Thalamogeniculate (ThGenP) | P2 PCA | Posterior lateral [[thalamus]], [[internal-capsule]] |

Loss of even one perforator can be devastating. Unlike enlarged cortical feeders that are superficial and controllable, perforators are deep, small, difficult to cauterize, and persistently supply the nidus after cortical feeders are interrupted.

### Venous Drainage

Deep veins include caudate veins, thalamostriate vein (in the striothalamic sulcus), internal cerebral vein (in velum interpositum), [[vein-of-galen]], [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal]], and lateral atrial veins. The [[insula]] drains through insular veins to the deep sylvian vein and [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal]].

## Four Deep AVM Subtypes

### 1. Pure Sylvian AVM

Located entirely in the subarachnoid space of the [[sylvian-fissure]] without a parenchymal base (14% of deep AVMs). Entirely extraparenchymal, with arachnoidal rather than pial/parenchymal planes. Fed by M2 and M3 MCA stem arteries from both superior and inferior trunks. High-flow terminal feeders frequently produce variceal draining veins. Exposed through [[pterional-approach]] and transsylvian dissection, as for [[mca-aneurysm]]. Non-eloquent but adjacent to [[broca-area]] and [[wernicke-area]] in the dominant hemisphere.

### 2. Insular AVM

Most common deep subtype (45%). Sits in the [[limen-insulae]], short gyri, and long gyri on the insular surface, lateral to [[basal-ganglia]] structures and respectful of the claustrum. Fed by M2 stem arteries and insular perforators. Deep sylvian vein more dominant than superficial. Exposed via [[pterional-approach]] and transsylvian approach, requiring a more extensive [[sylvian-fissure]] split and deeper dissection than pure sylvian AVMs. The medial margin adjacent to [[basal-ganglia]] may have lLSA supply, connecting insular AVM anatomy to the basal ganglial subtype.

### 3. Basal Ganglial AVM

Deep to the [[insula]] in the [[putamen]], [[globus-pallidus]], anterior [[internal-capsule]], or [[caudate-nucleus]]. No cortical representation; reached only through non-anatomic transcortical routes or hematoma cavities. Two variants:

- **Lateral (putamenal)** -- extension of insular AVM surgery via [[pterional-approach]], transsylvian-transinsular approach. Fed by InsP and lLSA. The deep medial side abuts the [[internal-capsule]]; perforator supply is meticulously controlled with microclips.
- **Medial (caudate)** -- extension of [[ventricular-avm]] body surgery via bifrontal craniotomy and contralateral [[transcallosal-approach]]. The crossing trajectory maximizes lateral ventricular wall exposure. Fed by mLSA and RAH. Ependymal dissection on the ventricular wall accesses the inferomedial perforator front.

### 4. Thalamic AVM

The [[thalamus]] is one of the inviolable brain structures. Compelling indications required: hemorrhage, preexisting deficits, radiosurgery failure, young age. Two operable variants access surgically favorable surfaces:

- **Superior** -- on the floor of the ventricular body behind the [[foramen-of-monro]]. Accessed via anterior [[transcallosal-approach]] (ipsilateral). Fed superiorly by mPChA and lPChA, inferiorly by PosThaP. Drains to thalamostriate vein and internal cerebral vein.
- **Medial** -- on the lateral wall of the third ventricle deep to the [[foramen-of-monro]]. Accessed via transcallosal-transchoroidal approach through the velum interpositum. Fed only by thalamoperforators (AntThaP and PosThaP). Drains to [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal]]. The genu of the [[internal-capsule]] nears the thalamic surface just lateral to the foramen.

Seven surgical routes to thalamic AVMs exist: anterior-ipsilateral transcallosal, anterior-contralateral transcallosal, posterior transcallosal, transcallosal-transchoroidal, transfrontal, transtemporal, and transparietal. Hematoma cavities create nonanatomic corridors that should be exploited when present.

## Resection Principles

- **Perforator preservation** is the cardinal rule; microclips preferred over cautery for deep perforators
- **Skeletonization** of transit arteries (M2 stems, PCA trunk, [[anterior-choroidal-artery]]) in a distal-to-proximal direction preserves en passage flow
- **Selection threshold** must be high: small AVMs with hemorrhage, preexisting deficits, or hematoma cavities are the best surgical candidates
- **Hematoma corridors** provide direct access that would otherwise require transgression of eloquent tissue
- **Occlusion in situ** (from [[brainstem-avm]] strategy) may occasionally apply to thalamic AVMs that prove too risky to resect completely

Deep AVMs account for fewer cases than lobar AVMs but require the most complex exposures and techniques in AVM surgery.
