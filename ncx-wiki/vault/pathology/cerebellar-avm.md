# Cerebellar Arteriovenous Malformation

Cerebellar AVMs comprise nearly 15% of all brain AVMs. Patients present with hemorrhage significantly more often than with cerebral AVMs (75% vs. 50%), and deep venous drainage -- associated with increased rupture risk -- is significantly more common. This increased hemorrhage rate and its costly sequelae support an aggressive management posture. Although infratentorial, cerebellar AVMs are fundamentally different from [[brainstem-avm]]: exposures are more satisfactory, risks are lower, and results more favorable.

## Microsurgical Anatomy

### Cerebellar Surfaces and Fissures

The [cerebellum](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/cerebellum.md) has three surfaces, each divided by a major fissure:

| Surface | Major Fissure | Lobules |
|---|---|---|
| Tentorial | Tentorial (primary) fissure | Quadrangular, simple, superior semilunar |
| Suboccipital | Suboccipital fissure | Superior/inferior semilunar, biventral, tonsil |
| Petrosal | Petrosal (horizontal) fissure | Anterior surfaces of all hemispheric lobules, flocculus |

The superior vermis sits under the [[straight-sinus]] where tentorial leaflets meet the [falx-cerebri](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/falx-cerebri.md). The inferior vermis lies in the posterior cerebellar incisura containing the falx cerebelli. The cerebellar tonsils attach to the hemisphere superolaterally through tonsillar peduncles; all other tonsillar surfaces are free.

### Arterial Supply

The cortical segments of the three cerebellar arteries supply cerebellar AVMs:

**[Superior-cerebellar-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/superior-cerebellar-artery.md)** (SCA) -- s3 segment climbs through the cerebellomesencephalic fissure; bifurcates into rostral (vermian) and caudal (hemispheric) trunks. Precerebellar arteries from the s3 segment supply the dentate nucleus and become deep "red devil" perforators that are difficult to coagulate. The s4 cortical segment supplies the tentorial surface.

**[[Anterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery]]** (AICA) -- courses through the [[cerebellopontine-angle]] near CN VII/VIII, bifurcating into rostral and caudal trunks. Important branches: labyrinthine artery, recurrent perforating arteries, subarcuate artery. The a4 cortical segment supplies the petrosal surface.

**[[Posterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery]]** (PICA) -- intimate with the cerebellomedullary fissure and suboccipital surface. After the telovelotonsillar (p4) segment, bifurcates into medial trunk (inferior vermis) and lateral trunk (suboccipital surface and tonsils). Cortical branches (p5) radiate outward from the tonsil. The same artery involved in [[pica-aneurysm]] pathology.

### Venous Drainage

Superficial veins drain each surface: superior hemispheric/vermian veins (tentorial surface), inferior hemispheric/vermian veins (suboccipital surface), and anterior hemispheric veins (petrosal surface). Deep veins course through the major cerebellar fissures. Bridging veins connect to the Galenic group (to [vein-of-galen](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/vein-of-galen.md)), tentorial group (to torcula/tentorial sinuses), and petrosal group (to SPS/IPS).

## Five Cerebellar AVM Subtypes

### 1. Suboccipital Cerebellar AVM

Based on the suboccipital surface in the semilunar and biventral lobules (20% of cerebellar AVMs). Unilateral. Receives supply from all three cerebellar arteries via cortical collaterals, with SCA/AICA/PICA dominance varying by location (superior/lateral/inferior, respectively). Superficial drainage to inferior hemispheric/vermian veins and torcula/[transverse-sinus](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/transverse-sinus.md). Exposed via lateral [[suboccipital-approach]], prone positioning. Convexity lesions with parallel attack angle and no associated cranial nerves. Combination approaches (far-lateral, extended retrosigmoid, torcular) for larger AVMs. Deep perforating arteries from PICA and SCA may require microclips along the deep plane.

### 2. Tentorial Cerebellar AVM

Based on the tentorial surface in the quadrangular, simple, and superior semilunar lobules (20%). Unilateral. Fed by hemispheric SCA branches. Drained by superior hemispheric veins bridging to [vein-of-galen](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/vein-of-galen.md), torcula, or tentorial sinus. Exposed via torcular craniotomy and [supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach](../rhoton-wiki/vault/references/supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach.md), lifting the [transverse-sinus](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/transverse-sinus.md) with dural tacking sutures. Prone positioning with neck flexion aligns the tentorium vertically. Perpendicular approach may require cortical incision to access posterior/inferior margins. May extend anteriorly near the [[midbrain]] and CN IV.

### 3. Vermian Cerebellar AVM

The most common cerebellar subtype (30%). Midline location in superior vermis (90%) or inferior vermis (10%). Bilateral arterial supply: SCA for superior vermian, PICA for inferior vermian AVMs. Superior vermian AVMs drain deep to superior vermian vein and [vein-of-galen](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/vein-of-galen.md); inferior vermian AVMs drain superficially to inferior vermian vein and torcula. Exposed through torcular craniotomy and midline [supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach](../rhoton-wiki/vault/references/supracerebellar-infratentorial-approach.md). Sitting position facilitates gravity retraction and retractor-less dissection for smaller apical vermian AVMs. Deep dissection may enter the cerebellomesencephalic fissure where CN IV, tectum, and [[midbrain]] perforators must be protected. Vermian division carries risk of cerebellar mutism. Shares midline posterior fossa corridor with [[brainstem-avm]] posterior midbrain subtype.

### 4. Tonsillar Cerebellar AVM

Least common cerebellar subtype (8%). Located in the cerebellar tonsils at the lower suboccipital surface. Small, unilateral. Fed by ipsilateral PICA (p4/p5 segments). Drained by tonsillar and retrotonsillar veins. Exposed through standard [[suboccipital-approach]]. Subarachnoid dissection opens the cisterna magna, widens the vallecula between tonsils, opens the cerebellomedullary fissure, and splits the tonsillobiventral fissure -- the same exposure used for [[far-lateral-approach]] to [[pica-aneurysm]]. Tonsillar surfaces are mostly free, limiting parenchymal dissection to the superolateral peduncle attachment. Non-eloquent but adjacent to medulla and CN IX/X/XI.

### 5. Petrosal Cerebellar AVM

Based on the petrosal surface facing the posterior petrous bone. Unilateral. Fed by cortical AICA branches (a3/a4 segments). Drained by anterior hemispheric veins and vein of the cerebellopontine fissure to superior petrosal vein (Dandy's vein) and SPS. Exposed through extended [retrosigmoid-approach](../rhoton-wiki/vault/references/retrosigmoid-approach.md) with sigmoid sinus skeletonization maximizing the [[cerebellopontine-angle]] opening. Distinct from lateral pontine [[brainstem-avm]] -- petrosal AVMs are cerebellar (lateral to CN VII/VIII), not pontine. The "back door" technique circumdissects through lateral cerebellum and rolls the AVM anteriorly away from [[pons]] and cranial nerves.

## Resection Principles

- **Prone positioning with neck flexion** is standard for all subtypes; the sitting position is reserved for select vermian AVMs
- **Torcular craniotomy** is the workhorse exposure, crossing the SSS, torcula, and [transverse-sinus](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/transverse-sinus.md) to access multiple cerebellar surfaces
- **Deep perforating arteries** from SCA and PICA (precerebellar branches) supply the dentate nucleus and are the "red devils" of cerebellar surgery -- difficult to coagulate, often requiring microclips
- **All cerebellar AVMs are resected** (not occluded in situ) -- unlike [[brainstem-avm]], the cerebellar parenchyma tolerates surgical transgression
- **Three-quarters hemorrhage rate** supports aggressive management, including surgery for high-grade ruptured AVMs

Cerebellar AVMs require the same complex skull base approaches as [[brainstem-avm]] but with more satisfactory exposures and more favorable outcomes. They share approaches with [[pica-aneurysm]] (far-lateral, suboccipital) and [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm]] (supracerebellar-infratentorial) surgery.
