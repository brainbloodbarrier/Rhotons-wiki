# Middle Cerebral Artery Aneurysm

[[Middle-cerebral-artery]] (MCA) aneurysms are the most common aneurysms treated by microsurgical clipping. Their superficial location in the [[sylvian-fissure]], broad necks that resist endovascular treatment, and complex branching anatomy make them quintessential clipping aneurysms. Mastery of the [[sylvian-fissure]] split is the prerequisite skill.

## Microsurgical Anatomy

### MCA Segments

MCA segmental anatomy is defined by curvature rather than branches:

| Segment | Name | Course |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Sphenoidal | ICA terminus to genu at [[limen-insulae]] |
| M2 | Insular | Genu through insular cleft to [[circular-sulcus]] |
| M3 | Opercular | Circular sulcus through opercular cleft to cortical surface |
| M4 | Cortical | Emergence from [[sylvian-fissure]] to final territory |

The MCA courses through the [[sylvian-fissure]], makes three right-angle bends, alternates between horizontal and vertical orientation, and branches distally like a candelabra.

### Bifurcation Anatomy

Most MCA aneurysms arise at the bifurcation into superior and inferior trunks. The bifurcation may be on M1 (proximal to genu), at the M1-M2 junction (at genu), or on M2 (distal to genu). Trifurcations occur in <20% of patients; quadrifurcations are rare.

Anterior branches include [[orbitofrontal-artery]], [[operculofrontal-artery]], and [[central-sulcus-artery]]; posterior branches include [[posterior-parietal-artery]], [[angular-artery]], and posterior temporal arteries.

### Critical Perforators

The **lateral [[lenticulostriate-arteries]]** originate from the superior M1 surface, enter the lateral anterior perforated substance, and supply the [[caudate-nucleus]], [[putamen]], [[globus-pallidus]], superior [[internal-capsule]], and [[corona-radiata]]. On average 10 lenticulostriates arise from the pre-bifurcation M1. Their preservation is mandatory.

### Variants

- **Anterior temporal artery (ATA)**: arises from inferior M1 surface; can create a "false bifurcation" or drape over the dome as a "false trunk"
- **Duplicated MCA**: second M1 from supraclinoid ICA or its terminus
- **Accessory MCA**: second M1 from A1 [[anterior-cerebral-artery]], resembling the [[recurrent-artery-of-heubner]] but with cortical branches

## Splitting the Sylvian Fissure

The [[sylvian-fissure]] split is the aneurysm surgeon's most important technique. Lawton describes a systematic approach:

### Veins and Superficial Dissection

1. **Cortical arachnoid incision** with up-facing No. 11 blade, lifting arachnoid off underlying veins
2. **Temporal mobilization of sylvian veins** -- veins drain inferiorly to the [[sphenoparietal-sinus]], so dissection along the frontal side preserves outflow
3. Three interconnected **sylvian venous systems** (anterior, posterior, superior) provide collateral that usually tolerates sacrifice of a vein

### Arteries and Deep Dissection

4. Follow cortical MCA branches into operculum (step 3)
5. Follow opercular branches to insular trunks (step 4)
6. **Inside-out spreading dissection** (Yasargil's "orange" analogy) -- radial force from center separates the lobes more effectively than superficial-to-deep
7. Transition from horizontal cortical arachnoid to vertical sphenoidal arachnoid

**Distal-to-proximal** dissection is preferred: approaches the neck without proximal control (safe for unruptured), avoids retraction, keeps CSF in the cistern. For ruptured aneurysms, begin distally to establish planes, then shift proximally for control.

## Aneurysm Dissection

### Dome Projection

MCA aneurysms obey Rhoton's third rule: they point where blood would have gone. Most project **laterally** along the M1 axis, with variable tilt:

- **Inferior projection**: upward M1 arc ends with downward flow at bifurcation
- **Superior projection**: downward M1 arc ends with upward flow
- **Anterior/posterior projection**: depends on M1 arc in axial plane

### Distal-to-Proximal Strategy

1. Follow the **superior trunk** (usually visible first) proximally along its outer surface to the neck
2. Prepare the **M1 segment** for proximal control retrograde
3. Dissect the superior trunk's **inner surface** down to the proximal neck and across
4. Find the **hidden trunk** (usually inferior) behind the neck in the insular recess
5. Open the **blind spot** at the distal neck -- dome adhesions, draped ATA, sylvian veins tether the aneurysm temporally

### Proximal-to-Distal Alternative

For ruptured aneurysms requiring early proximal control: dissect the supraclinoid ICA to its bifurcation, identify the A1 [[anterior-cerebral-artery]], then the [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] laterally along M1. Gains proximal control early but requires frontal retraction and splits the fissure inefficiently.

## Clipping Technique

MCA aneurysms demand the widest repertoire of clipping techniques:

- **Simple clipping**: blade perpendicular to M1, parallel to efferent trunks; for small, narrow-necked aneurysms
- **Multiple clipping**: deconstruct broad necks into portions; initial clip closes the deep distal neck, additional intersecting/stacked/overlapping clips close the remainder
- **Tandem clipping**: fenestrated blade on the shoulder of the hidden trunk repairs the distal neck; closing clips over the fenestration repair the proximal neck
- **Fenestration tubes**: overlapping fenestrated clips reconstruct a Y-shaped bifurcation
- **Thrombectomy with reconstruction**: giant thrombotic aneurysms are trapped, opened, debulked with ultrasonic aspirator, and reconstructed

### Post-Clipping Inspection

Assume a deep trunk has been missed until proven otherwise. After clipping, de-tether, mobilize, deflate, or transect the dome for full inspection of all trunks. Check superior, middle, and inferior trunks for patency.

## Special Situations

- **Proximal M1 aneurysms**: project superiorly, require angled fenestrated clipping from the inferior M1 approach trajectory
- **Distal MCA aneurysms**: uncommon; require distal [[sylvian-fissure]] splitting into the opercular cleft; may need trapping with [[STA-MCA-bypass]] or end-to-end reanastomosis
- **Contralateral clipping**: feasible for small, unruptured, inferiorly projecting MCA aneurysms by following the contralateral A1 to M1 through the opposite fissure
