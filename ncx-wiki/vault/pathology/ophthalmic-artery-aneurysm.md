# Ophthalmic Artery Aneurysm

Paraclinoid aneurysms arising from the ophthalmic segment of the [internal-carotid-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/internal-carotid-artery.md) (ICA) represent the most technically demanding anterior circulation aneurysms. They require anterior clinoidectomy, [[distal-dural-ring]] dissection, and navigation within the tight confines of the clinoidal triangle. The [optic-nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/optic-nerve.md) is at direct risk.

## Microsurgical Anatomy

### ICA Segmental Anatomy (van Loveren Classification)

| Segment | Name | Boundaries |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Cervical | Carotid bifurcation to skull base |
| C2 | Petrous | Carotid canal entrance to exit |
| C3 | Lacerum | Over foramen lacerum to petrolingual ligament |
| C4 | Cavernous | Lateral dural ring to proximal dural ring |
| C5 | Clinoidal | Between proximal and distal dural rings |
| C6 | Ophthalmic | Distal dural ring to [posterior-communicating-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/posterior-communicating-artery.md) |
| C7 | Communicating | PCoA to ICA terminus |

The **clinoidal** and **ophthalmic** segments together constitute the **paraclinoid ICA** due to their intimacy with the [anterior-clinoid-process](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/anterior-clinoid-process.md) (ACP).

### Ophthalmic Segment Branches

Only two named arteries arise from the 1.5-cm ophthalmic segment:

1. **[Ophthalmic-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/ophthalmic-artery.md)** (OphA): originates from the superior ICA surface just beyond the [[distal-dural-ring]], turns anteriorly under the [optic-nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/optic-nerve.md) to enter the [optic-canal](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/optic-canal.md). In 8% of patients, arises from the cavernous, clinoidal, or middle meningeal artery.
2. **Superior hypophyseal artery** (SHA): originates from the medial ICA surface, ~90 degrees medial to the OphA. Supplies the [[pituitary-stalk]], [pituitary-gland](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/pituitary-gland.md), [optic-nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/optic-nerve.md), [optic-chiasm](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/optic-chiasm.md), and floor of the [[third-ventricle]].

### Paraclinoid Aneurysm Classification

| Type | Origin | Projection | Key Relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OphA aneurysm** | Superior (dorsal) ICA wall | Superiorly | Impacts lateral [optic-nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/optic-nerve.md); "notches" on angiography |
| **SHA aneurysm** | Inferomedial ICA wall | Medially toward sella | Elevates [optic-chiasm](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/optic-chiasm.md); "opens" carotid siphon |
| **Dorsal carotid** | Superior wall distal to OphA | Superiorly | Blister-shaped; no branch relationship |
| **Carotid cave** | Medial wall at carotid sulcus | Medially | In subarachnoid outpouching |
| **Clinoidal segment** | Between dural rings | Variable | May erode ACP and [[optic-strut]] |
| **Ventral carotid** | Inferior ophthalmic segment | Inferiorly | Resembles [[pcoa-aneurysm]] but proximal to PCoA |

### Anterior Clinoid Process Anatomy

The ACP is a 1-cm triangular bone projecting posteriorly as the medial continuation of the [[sphenoid-ridge]]. It attaches medially to the optic canal roof and is buttressed below by the **optic strut** (posterior root of the lesser sphenoid wing). The strut occupies the critical intersection of [optic-canal](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/optic-canal.md), [[superior-orbital-fissure]], and orbital apex.

### Dural Ring Anatomy

- **Distal dural ring**: superior dural plane from superomedial ACP, slanting downward and inward; marks the beginning of the subarachnoid ICA. Thick laterally, thins medially into the carotid cave and [[diaphragma-sellae]].
- **Proximal dural ring**: inferior dural plane from inferolateral ACP; forms the [[carotid-oculomotor-membrane]] (roof of [cavernous-sinus](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/cavernous-sinus.md)). Thin, translucent; venous blood visible through it.
- The **clinoidal triangle** lies between the [optic-nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/optic-nerve.md) and [oculomotor-nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/oculomotor-nerve.md), with the optic strut at its apex, the dural rings in its midportion, and the cavernous sinus roof posteriorly.

## Anterior Clinoidectomy

### Approach Modifications

- Head positioned with less extension than other aneurysms (avoids steepening the view into the clinoidal triangle)
- Additional 10-20 degrees lateral rotation for improved view under the [optic-nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/optic-nerve.md)
- Cervical ICA routinely prepared; intraoperative angiography more useful than with any other aneurysm type

### Intradural Clinoidectomy Steps

1. **Incise superficial dura on ACP** in an arc from tip to sphenoid ridge
2. **Unroof the [optic-canal](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/optic-canal.md)** with 2-mm diamond drill; decompress nerve early
3. **Cavitate the ACP core** with drill, thinning cortical margins
4. **Remove the ACP** with minimal force directed away from aneurysm
5. **Open the optic sheath** lateral to the sheath mouth
6. **Incise distal dural ring superiorly** -- drill the optic strut progressively to transform the ring from bony lining to free-floating tissue
7. **Incise distal dural ring medially** under the OphA (transaxillary dissection follows the clinoidal ICA)
8. **Incise distal dural ring inferolaterally** -- cuts adjacent to cavernous/intercavernous sinuses

**Fibrin glue injection** (5-10 cc) into the [cavernous-sinus](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/cavernous-sinus.md) through the [[carotid-oculomotor-membrane]] dramatically reduces the venous bleeding that is the major deterrent to thorough ring dissection.

## Aneurysm Dissection

### OphA Aneurysms

Require incision around the **upper half** of the distal dural ring. Neck on the superior carotid wall; superior dome projection; relatively straightforward permanent clipping. Proximally, define the OphA origin and separate from the neck. Distally, dissect SHA and medial perforators away.

### SHA Aneurysms

Require **nearly circumferential** ring incision. Inferomedial dome projection with neck on the ICA's blind side. Fenestrated clippings encircling the ICA demand thorough ring dissection. Clip tips must advance past the ring at the proximal neck -- inadequate ring dissection blocks or splays the tips.

## Clipping Technique

### OphA Aneurysms

- **Simple side-angled or side-curved clip**: blades parallel the ophthalmic ICA segment; side angle deviates appliers laterally away from the [optic-nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/optic-nerve.md)
- **Broad-necked**: first clip with steep pitch for medial neck, understacked second clip at lateral neck
- **Overlapping fenestrated clip**: fenestration encircles initial clip blades to address medial remnant

### SHA Aneurysms

- **Angled fenestrated clips** encircling the ICA: visualize one blade toe-to-heel, inspect the other blade from a different viewing angle
- **Tandem angled fenestrated clipping** for large SHA or high-curvature ICA: applied proximal-to-distal, toe-to-heel overlap
- **Contralateral clipping** through the interoptic triangle: for small, medially projecting, unruptured aneurysms; obviates clinoidectomy

## Complications

### CSF Rhinorrhea

Occurs when a pneumatized optic strut (opticocarotid recess, 4-13% of patients) communicates with the [[sphenoid-sinus]]. Repaired intraoperatively with the **"yo-yo" technique**: temporalis muscle strip passed through the strut into the sphenoid sinus, then retracted back to form a plug sealed with fibrin glue.

### Monocular Blindness

The neurosurgeon's worst complication. Causes include drill heat, optic nerve manipulation, or perforator compromise. Prevention: generous cold irrigation, frequent pauses, light drill touch, early and wide canal unroofing, depress carotid artery rather than lift optic nerve, delicate perforator handling.
