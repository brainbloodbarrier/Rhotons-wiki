# Chiari Malformation

The Chiari malformations are a group of hindbrain abnormalities, probably unrelated to each other. Types 1 and 2 comprise the vast majority. "Chiari malformation" is preferred for type 1; "Arnold-Chiari malformation" is reserved for type 2.

## Classification

| Type | Key feature | Age of presentation | Myelomeningocele | Hydrocephalus |
|------|------------|--------------------|--------------------|---------------|
| **1** | Tonsillar herniation below FM | Young adult (mean 41 yr) | May be present | 7-9% |
| **2** | Vermis, medulla, 4th ventricle herniation | Infancy | Rarely absent | Rarely absent |
| **3** | Occipital/cervical encephalocele | Neonatal | Variable | Variable |
| **4** | Cerebellar hypoplasia | Neonatal | Variable | Variable |
| **0** | Syringomyelia without tonsillar herniation | Variable | -- | -- |

## Chiari Type 1 (Most Common)

### Key Concepts
- Heterogeneous entity — common feature: impaired CSF circulation through the foramen magnum
- May be congenital or acquired (post-LP shunt, multiple traumatic LPs)
- Prevalence ~0.5% radiographically
- Slight female predominance (1.3:1)
- Syringomyelia in **30-70%** of cases
- 15-30% are asymptomatic

### MRI Criteria
Cerebellar tonsillar herniation >5 mm below the foramen magnum is often cited, but is **neither essential nor diagnostic**. Tonsillar descent with impaction is no longer sine qua non — CSF flow obstruction is the key.

### Symptoms

| Symptom | Frequency |
|---------|-----------|
| Pain (suboccipital headache most common) | 69% |
| Weakness (one or more limbs) | 56% |
| Numbness | 52% |
| Loss of temperature sensation | 40% |
| Unsteadiness | 40% |
| Diplopia | 13% |

**Hallmark:** tussive headache — suboccipital pain exacerbated by coughing, straining, or Valsalva maneuver.

### Signs

Three main patterns:
1. **Foramen magnum compression** (22%): ataxia, corticospinal/sensory deficits, cerebellar signs, lower CN palsies
2. **Central cord syndrome** (65%): dissociated sensory loss (loss of pain/temperature, preserved touch/proprioception), segmental weakness — syringomyelic syndrome
3. **Cerebellar syndrome** (11%): truncal/limb ataxia, nystagmus, dysarthria

**Downbeat nystagmus** is considered characteristic. 10% have normal neuro exam with headache as only complaint.

### Associations
- Small posterior fossa (underdeveloped occipital bone)
- Craniocervical junction anomalies: basilar invagination, Klippel-Feil, occipitalization of atlas
- Ehlers-Danlos syndrome
- Pseudotumor cerebri (6-21% of pseudotumor patients have tonsillar descent ≥5 mm) — may explain some treatment failures
- Craniosynostosis

### Evaluation
1. **MRI brain + cervical spine** — assess FM compression, rule out intracranial mass, evaluate for syrinx
2. **Cine MRI** — CSF flow study at FM for uncertain cases
3. Rule out signs of elevated [[intracranial-pressure\|ICP]] (empty sella, optic nerve sheath dilation)

### Treatment
Surgery when symptomatic (aspects of surgical approach are controversial):
- **Suboccipital craniectomy** (foramen magnum decompression) — enlargement of FM is usually core of procedure
- ± Duraplasty (opening dura and expanding with patch)
- ± C1 laminectomy
- ± Tonsillar cauterization/shrinkage
- Syringomyelia almost always improves with treatment of the Chiari malformation itself

## Chiari Type 2 (Arnold-Chiari)

- Inferior vermis, medulla, and 4th ventricle herniate through FM into cervical canal
- Medullary "kink" present in 55%
- Almost always associated with myelomeningocele and [[hydrocephalus]]
- Presents in infancy with progressive hydrocephalus, respiratory distress
- Upper cervical nerves course cephalad (unlike type 1)
- Late mortality usually from shunt malfunction; 80% will have normal IQ
