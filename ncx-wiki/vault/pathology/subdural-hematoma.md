# Subdural Hematoma

A subdural hematoma collects between the inner dural layer and the arachnoid — a virtual space crossed by bridging cortical veins draining into dural venous sinuses. Shear between the mobile brain and the fixed dura tears these veins; the hematoma spreads broadly because it is not bounded by cranial sutures. The clinical spectrum is defined by the interval between trauma and imaging.

## Spectrum and timing

| Stage | Interval | Density on CT | Dominant patient |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acute (aSDH)** | <3 days | Hyperdense crescent | High-energy trauma, anticoagulated elderly |
| **Subacute** | 3–21 days | Iso- to mixed-density | Any |
| **Chronic (cSDH)** | >21 days | Hypodense ± membranes / septations | Elderly; often minor or forgotten trauma |
| **Acute-on-chronic** | Recurrent bleed into cSDH | Mixed-density with fluid level | Elderly on anticoagulation |

## Mechanism

- Bridging-vein rupture from rapid deceleration is the dominant mechanism; the thin bridging segment crossing the subdural space is fragile.
- Cortical artery laceration produces a focal, rapidly enlarging clot.
- In the elderly, cerebral atrophy stretches bridging veins and creates a larger potential subdural space — minor trauma or none at all can precipitate SDH.
- Chronic SDH develops neomembranes with fragile capillaries; repeated microhemorrhage perpetuates the collection.

## Presentation

- **Acute SDH**: depressed level of consciousness, hemiparesis, pupillary asymmetry, Cushing triad with herniation. Rapid deterioration over minutes to hours.
- **Lucid interval** — classic in epidural, but can also occur in SDH with slow arterial source.
- **Chronic SDH**: subacute gait disorder, cognitive slowing, headache, seizure, mild hemiparesis. Often misdiagnosed as dementia or TIA.

## Imaging

- **Non-contrast CT** is the workhorse. Acute SDH: hyperdense, crescent-shaped, crosses sutures, does not cross midline. Chronic SDH: hypodense, may have isodense bleeds mixed.
- **MRI** identifies subacute (T1/T2 hyperintense) lesions and neomembranes.
- **CTA** when arterial source suspected (young patient, no anticoagulation, atypical location).

## Management

### Acute SDH

**Surgical criteria (Bullock / BTF)**:

- Thickness >10 mm *or* midline shift >5 mm → evacuate regardless of GCS.
- Smaller SDH with GCS drop of ≥2 points, fixed dilated pupil, or ICP >20 mm Hg → operate.

Operative choice:

- **Trauma craniotomy** (large question-mark flap) with dural opening, clot evacuation, hemostasis over cortical sources, and dural closure ± duraplasty.
- **Decompressive craniectomy** in young patients with swollen brain and refractory ICP.
- Meticulous [[intracranial-pressure]] control perioperatively; minimize systolic swings; maintain MAP for CPP.

Anticoagulation reversal prior to surgery — PCC, FFP, platelets, idarucizumab, andexanet — is time-critical.

### Chronic SDH

- **Asymptomatic small cSDH** — observation with serial imaging; stop antiplatelets when safe; correct coagulopathy.
- **Symptomatic cSDH** — bedside or OR **burr-hole evacuation** (single vs double burr hole, closed-system drainage, irrigation). Subdural drain 24–48 h reduces recurrence.
- **Membranous / septated / recurrent cSDH** — mini-craniotomy with membrane excision.
- **Middle meningeal artery embolization** — adjunct or stand-alone therapy; cuts recurrence rate substantially. Uses [[middle-meningeal-artery]] branches as target.
- Perioperative corticosteroids (dexamethasone): evidence is mixed; recent RCTs favor limited use.
- Early mobilization is critical — trendelenburg / strict bedrest is no longer standard.

## Complications

- Recurrence (10–20% cSDH) — higher with bilateral collections, septations, coagulopathy.
- Tension pneumocephalus.
- Seizures — prophylactic AED debated.
- Cerebral contusion and delayed ischemia.
- Medical: VTE, pneumonia, UTI in elderly.

## Outcomes

- **aSDH** mortality 40–60%; <4-hour door-to-evacuation time roughly halves mortality (Seelig 1981).
- **cSDH** mortality <5% but 1-year mortality of 20–30% reflects the medical fragility of the elderly population.
- Functional outcome strongly correlates with preoperative GCS, age, comorbidity, and coagulation status.
