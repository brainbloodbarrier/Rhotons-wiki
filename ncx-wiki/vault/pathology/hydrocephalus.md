# Hydrocephalus

Hydrocephalus (HCP) is the pathologic accumulation of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) within the ventricular system, resulting from imbalance between CSF production and absorption.

## Classification

### By Mechanism

| Type | Mechanism | Examples |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Obstructive (non-communicating)** | Blockage within ventricular system | Aqueductal stenosis, colloid cyst at foramen of Monro, posterior fossa tumors compressing 4th ventricle |
| **Communicating** | Impaired absorption at arachnoid granulations | Post-SAH, post-meningitis, post-hemorrhagic (premature infants) |
| **Overproduction** (rare) | Excess CSF production | Choroid plexus papilloma |

### Special Forms

- [[normal-pressure-hydrocephalus]] — chronic communicating HCP in elderly
- Entrapped fourth ventricle — isolated 4th ventricle dilation
- Arrested hydrocephalus — stable ventriculomegaly without progression
- Constitutional ventriculomegaly — asymptomatic, no treatment needed
- X-linked hydrocephalus — aqueductal stenosis from L1CAM mutation

## Etiologies

**Congenital:**
- Aqueductal stenosis (most common cause of congenital HCP)
- [[chiari-malformation]] (Chiari II with myelomeningocele)
- [[dandy-walker-malformation]]
- Vein of Galen malformation

**Acquired:**
- Post-hemorrhagic: SAH, intraventricular hemorrhage (premature infants)
- Post-infectious: meningitis (bacterial, TB, fungal)
- Neoplastic: posterior fossa tumors (especially [[medulloblastoma]]), pineal region tumors
- Post-operative: 20% of pediatric patients develop permanent HCP after posterior fossa tumor removal (may be delayed up to 1 year)

## Signs and Symptoms

### Adults and Older Children (rigid vault)

Signs of elevated [[intracranial-pressure\|ICP]]:
- Headache (worse in morning, with Valsalva)
- Nausea/vomiting
- Papilledema
- CN VI palsy (false localizing sign — long intracranial course)
- Upgaze palsy (Parinaud's syndrome from pressure on tectal plate)
- Gait changes

### Infants (open fontanelle)

**Symptoms:** irritability, poor head control, failure to thrive, developmental delay

**Signs:**
- Increasing head circumference (OFC) — crossing percentile curves
- Bulging fontanelle
- Frontal bossing
- Dilated scalp veins (reversal of flow from intracranial sinuses)
- Macewen's sign (cracked-pot sound on percussion)
- Setting-sun sign (upgaze palsy)
- Hyperactive reflexes

**OFC monitoring:** progressive deviation >2 SD above normal, growth >1.25 cm/week, or OFC disproportionate to body length/weight warrants investigation.

## Imaging

- **CT:** ventricular dilation, periventricular lucency (transependymal CSF migration), effacement of sulci
- **MRI:** flow void at aqueduct (cine MRI for flow assessment), etiology identification
- **Ultrasound:** via open fontanelle in infants

### Differentiating HCP from Atrophy

| Feature | Hydrocephalus | Atrophy (ex vacuo) |
|---------|--------------|-------------------|
| Sulci | Effaced | Widened |
| Temporal horns | Dilated (early sign) | Not selectively dilated |
| Periventricular edema | Present | Absent |
| Evans index >0.3 | Yes | Variable |
| Third ventricle | Ballooned | Proportional |

## Treatment

### CSF Diversion

- [[ventriculoperitoneal-shunt\|VP shunt]] — most common
- Ventriculoatrial (VA) shunt — when peritoneal route contraindicated
- Lumboperitoneal (LP) shunt — communicating HCP only

### Endoscopic

- [ETV](../nsatlas-wiki/vault/procedures/endoscopic-third-ventriculostomy.md) — preferred for obstructive HCP, creates communication between 3rd ventricle floor and prepontine cistern
- ETV + choroid plexus cauterization (ETV/CPC) — increasingly used in pediatric HCP

### Medical (temporizing only)

- Acetazolamide (Diamox) — reduces CSF production
- Not effective long-term

## Complications of Blindness

Rare but serious: may result from PCA occlusion during [[herniation-syndromes\|transtentorial herniation]], chronic papilledema, or 3rd ventricle dilation compressing optic chiasm. Both pregeniculate and cortical blindness patterns reported.
