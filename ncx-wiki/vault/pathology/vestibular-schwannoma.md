# Vestibular Schwannoma

Vestibular schwannoma (VS), formerly "acoustic neuroma," is the most common cerebellopontine angle (CPA) tumor. WHO grade 1 schwannoma arising from Schwann cells of the vestibular division of CN VIII. Comprises ~8% of intracranial tumors.

## Management Algorithm by Size

### Small (<15 mm)
- **Observation ("wait and scan")** valid for asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic
- Serial MRI surveillance (annually initially)
- Consider treatment if documented growth or progressive symptoms
- Final decision often dictated by non-medical factors (patient perception, social situation)

### Medium (15-25 mm)
- Tumors >15-20 mm should generally be treated (especially in young patients)
- Close observation valid in older patients or those with comorbidities
- Complication rate increases and facial outcome worsens with increasing size
- **NF2 patients:** evaluate individually — lower success rates, consider bevacizumab (significant hearing improvement and tumor shrinkage in >50% of NF2 patients)

### Large (>25 mm)
- **Treatment recommended**
- Microsurgical resection favored (reduce mass effect, decompress brainstem)
- SRS also useful for older patients or significant comorbidities
- If >3 cm with symptomatic [[hydrocephalus]]: consider pre-op EVD, VP shunt if HCP persists post-op

## Microsurgery vs Radiosurgery

### Hearing Preservation

| | Microsurgery | SRS |
|---|-------------|-----|
| 2 years | Variable by team experience | 50-75% maintain hearing class |
| 5 years | Similar to SRS long-term | 50-75% |
| 10 years | ~Same as SRS | 25-50% |

SRS advantage is more pronounced for larger tumors and greater preoperative hearing loss. Hearing preservation with SRS related to cochlear dose (not tumor dose). Direct cochlear nerve monitoring (CNAP) dramatically improves microsurgical hearing preservation (91% in small tumors with class A hearing).

### Facial Nerve Preservation
- **Microsurgery:** 98.5% overall, 100% for tumors not touching brainstem
- **SRS:** 98% (incidence decreased since dose reduced to 12-13 Gy)

### Tumor Control
- **Microsurgery:** recurrence 0.5% at 6 years to 9.2% (varies by series)
- **SRS:** 97% local control for tumors growing <2.5 mm/year, 69% for ≥2.5 mm/year. 10-18% show "pseudogrowth" (transient enlargement within 6-12 months)

### Trigeminal Neuropathy
- SRS: 7% (mainly at higher doses, none at <13 Gy)
- Microsurgery: not commonly reported

## Surgical Approaches

| Approach | Hearing preserving | Best for |
|----------|-------------------|----------|
| **Middle fossa (MF)** | Yes | Intracanalicular tumors, poor posterior fossa access |
| **Retrosigmoid (RS)** | Yes | CPA extension >few mm, most versatile |
| **Translabyrinthine (TL)** | No | Non-salvageable hearing, any size |

### Decision Algorithm
1. **Salvageable hearing:**
   - Intracanalicular → middle fossa (some centers use retrosigmoid exclusively)
   - CPA extension → retrosigmoid
2. **Non-salvageable hearing:** translabyrinthine or retrosigmoid (team preference)

### Intraoperative Monitoring
- **CN VII monitoring:** recommended (Level III) — favorable response predicts good outcome, but poor response with anatomically intact nerve does NOT predict poor long-term function
- **CN VIII monitoring:** use ABR when hearing preservation is a goal; consider direct cochlear nerve action potential when feasible
- For tumors >3 cm: consider CN V, lower CN (IX, X), and SSEPs

## Key Points for Microsurgery
- MS after prior SRS: increased likelihood of subtotal resection and decreased facial nerve function
- Staged resection advocated by some for giant VS (>4-4.5 cm) to improve facial nerve preservation
- Vertigo after MS depends on preoperative vestibular function: if absent pre-op, minimal post-op dizziness

## NF2 Considerations
- Bilateral VS is pathognomonic of NF2
- Lower success rates overall (higher CN deficit, higher recurrence)
- Early management generally favored for better outcomes
- Bevacizumab shows promise for hearing improvement and tumor shrinkage
