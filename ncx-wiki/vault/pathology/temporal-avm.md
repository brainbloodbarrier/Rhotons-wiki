# Temporal Arteriovenous Malformation

Temporal AVMs are the second most common of the seven AVM locations. The lateral temporal subtype is the single most common AVM subtype overall (13% of 600 surgical patients). The [[temporal-lobe]] is hospitable territory for AVM surgery: craniotomies provide good exposure, approaches mirror familiar aneurysm exposures, and much of the lobe is non-eloquent. Four subtypes are defined by the lobe's four surfaces.

## Microsurgical Anatomy

### Brain Surfaces

| Surface | Key Structures | Eloquence |
|---|---|---|
| Lateral | STG, MTG, ITG | [[wernicke-area]] (dominant STG beyond 5 cm from pole) |
| Basal | ITG, fusiform gyrus, parahippocampal gyrus | Non-eloquent |
| Sylvian | Planum polare, planum temporale, Heschl's gyrus | Auditory cortex, [[wernicke-area]] posteriorly |
| Medial | Uncus, [[parahippocampal-gyrus]], [[hippocampus]] | Memory (dominant hemisphere) |

### Arterial Supply

The temporal lobe has intimate relationships with three arteries:

- **[[Middle-cerebral-artery]]** -- the inferior trunk divides into ~4 stem arteries, emerging as M4 cortical arteries: middle temporal (MidTempA), posterior temporal (PosTempA), temporo-occipital (TempOccA), and [[angular-artery]]. "Early branches" -- temporopolar and anterior temporal arteries -- arise from M1 proximal to the bifurcation.
- **[[Posterior-cerebral-artery]]** -- P2 segment branches supply the basal and medial surfaces: hippocampal artery (first cortical PCA branch) and posterior temporal arteries coursing over the [[tentorial-incisura]].
- **[[Anterior-choroidal-artery]]** -- supplies the [[choroid-plexus]] of the temporal horn and contributes to larger AVMs with ependymal planes.

### Venous Drainage

The temporal lobe's venous network is complex with four drainage directions: anteriorly to sphenoparietal sinus, posteriorly to the **vein of Labbe** and [[transverse-sinus]], medially to [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal]] and [[vein-of-galen]], and superiorly to the superficial sylvian veins. Preservation of the vein of Labbe is a cardinal principle in temporal AVM surgery.

## Four Temporal AVM Subtypes

### 1. Lateral Temporal AVM

The most common of all AVM subtypes (n = 76, 13%). Convexity lesion fed by inferior MCA trunk branches superiorly and PCA branches inferiorly. Drainage descends posteriorly through the vein of Labbe to [[transverse-sinus]]. Exposed via temporal craniotomy (behind external auditory canal) or [[pterional-approach]] (anterior lesions). Three arterial fronts: superior (MCA), anterior (M1 early branches), and inferior (PCA). Awake anesthesia with cortical stimulation may localize [[wernicke-area]] in dominant hemisphere cases. Deep dissection may reach the temporal horn with [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] supply, connecting to [[ventricular-avm]] anatomy.

### 2. Basal Temporal AVM

Located on the basal surface in ITG, fusiform, and/or [[parahippocampal-gyrus]]. Not visible on the lateral surface -- apparent only after subtemporal dissection. PCA-dominant supply with PosTempA coursing over the [[tentorial-incisura]]. Meningeal feeders in nearly one quarter of patients. Perpendicular exposure via temporal craniotomy with 90-degree head rotation and subtemporal retraction. The vein of Labbe must not be avulsed during retraction. Non-eloquent territory.

### 3. Sylvian Temporal AVM

Based on the superior temporal surface, oriented vertically down the [[sylvian-fissure]]. Deep to and supplied by M3 opercular MCA segments. Resected like the sylvian [[frontal-avm]] subtype, via [[pterional-approach]] and transsylvian dissection similar to [[mca-aneurysm]] exposure. Arteries classified as terminal feeders, transit arteries (skeletonized distal-to-proximal), or bystanders. Eloquent when involving Heschl's gyrus or [[wernicke-area]].

### 4. Medial Temporal AVM

Resides in the uncus, [[parahippocampal-gyrus]], and [[hippocampus]] medial to the temporal horn. The most challenging temporal subtype due to deep location. Exposed with [[orbitozygomatic-approach]] and transsylvian dissection, identical to the corridor for [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm]]. Supply from [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] (always a transit artery to be skeletonized), PCoA thalamoperforators, and P2 PCA branches including hippocampal artery and PosTempA. Drains deep to [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal]].

Medial temporal AVMs anterior to the [[cerebral-peduncle]] use the orbitozygomatic-transsylvian approach; those posterior to the peduncle require a transtemporal-transventricular approach through fusiform or [[parahippocampal-gyrus]], paralleling basal temporal AVM exposure.

## Resection Principles

- **Parallel vs. perpendicular** -- lateral and sylvian subtypes have parallel attack angles; basal and medial subtypes require perpendicular exposure with nidal mobilization
- **Vein of Labbe preservation** is mandatory; subtemporal retraction must not avulse it
- **Meyer's loop** -- the optic radiations pass anteriorly in the temporal lobe; transcortical approaches to the temporal horn must stay beneath this structure
- **Transit arteries** -- the [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] and P2 [[posterior-cerebral-artery]] are always transit arteries requiring skeletonization, never sacrifice
- **Deep planes** -- larger AVMs extend to the temporal horn with [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] supply through the choroidal fissure, connecting to [[ventricular-avm]] and [[deep-avm]] anatomy

## Outcome

Temporal AVMs are associated with excellent outcomes in terms of curative resection and seizure control. They are the second most common AVM location after [[frontal-avm]] and contain the single most common subtype (lateral temporal). The [[parieto-occipital-avm]] is the third most common location.
