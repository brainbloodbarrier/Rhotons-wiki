# Anterior Communicating Artery Aneurysm

The [anterior-communicating-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/anterior-communicating-artery.md) (ACoA) aneurysm is the most common intracranial aneurysm location. It sits at the epicenter of the most complex perforator anatomy in the anterior circulation, with 12 arteries entering the surgical field. ACoA aneurysms are variable, frequently ruptured, and often ill-suited for endovascular treatment -- making microsurgical expertise essential.

## Microsurgical Anatomy

### ACA Segments

| Segment | Name | Course |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Precommunicating/Horizontal | ICA terminus to ACoA |
| A2 | Postcommunicating/Infracallosal | ACoA along rostrum of [corpus-callosum](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/corpus-callosum.md) |
| A3 | Precallosal | Around genu of [corpus-callosum](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/corpus-callosum.md) |
| A4 | Supracallosal | Anterior half of callosal body |
| A5 | Postcallosal | Posterior half of callosal body |

### The 12 Arteries

ACoA aneurysm dissection must identify 12 arteries:

1. Ipsilateral A1
2. Contralateral A1
3. Ipsilateral A2
4. Contralateral A2
5. The ACoA itself
6. Ipsilateral [recurrent-artery-of-heubner](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/recurrent-artery-of-heubner.md)
7. Contralateral [recurrent-artery-of-heubner](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/recurrent-artery-of-heubner.md)
8. Ipsilateral [[orbitofrontal-artery]]
9. Contralateral [[orbitofrontal-artery]]
10. Ipsilateral [[frontopolar-artery]]
11. Contralateral [[frontopolar-artery]]
12. Collection of ACoA perforators

### A1 Dominance

A1 segments are symmetric in ~90% of the general population, but **A1 dominance is present in up to 80% of ACoA aneurysm patients**. Dominance influences dome projection, side of hematoma, and surgical approach selection.

### Recurrent Artery of Heubner

The [recurrent-artery-of-heubner](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/recurrent-artery-of-heubner.md) is the "medial-most" lenticulostriate, defining the inner border of the A1 perforator collection. It originates on the lateral A2 wall within 4 mm of the ACoA in 95% of patients, lies superior (60%) or anterior (40%) to the A1, and is often seen before the A1 during frontal retraction. It supplies the [[caudate-nucleus]] head, [[putamen]], outer [[globus-pallidus]], and anterior [internal-capsule](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/internal-capsule.md). Injury produces contralateral face/arm weakness and dominant-hemisphere expressive aphasia.

### ACoA Perforators

Perforating arteries from the ACoA's superior and posterior surfaces supply the [hypothalamus](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/hypothalamus.md), fornix columns, [septum-pellucidum](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/septum-pellucidum.md), [corpus-callosum](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/corpus-callosum.md) genu, and [anterior-perforated-substance](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/anterior-perforated-substance.md). Additional perforators from the anterior/inferior ACoA supply the dorsal [optic-chiasm](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/optic-chiasm.md). These perforators prevent trapping or dividing the ACoA.

### Important Variants

- **Azygos ACA** (<2%): single midline artery from A1 confluence
- **Bihemispheric ACA** (~12%): one A2 supplies both hemispheres
- **Accessory A2**: third artery from ACoA (remnant of median artery of corpus callosum)
- **ACoA duplication** (33%), triplication (10%), fenestration

## Side of Approach

The right side is chosen with symmetric A1 segments (avoids dominant hemisphere). The **dominant A1 side** is chosen with asymmetric A1s, providing:

- Early proximal control of the dominant inflow
- Dome avoidance (dome deflects away from dominant A1)
- Favorable neck visualization

Standard [[pterional-approach]] suffices for most ACoA aneurysms. The [[orbitozygomatic-approach]] increases exposure for large, giant, and complex cases (~16%).

## Dissection Strategy

### The 7-Step Dissection

1. **Follow ipsilateral A1 and [recurrent-artery-of-heubner](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/recurrent-artery-of-heubner.md)** medially over the [optic-tract](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/optic-tract.md) and [optic-chiasm](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/optic-chiasm.md)
2. **Identify ipsilateral A2** by opening the inferior interhemispheric fissure where the gyri recti meet
3. **Cross the midline via the ACoA** to the contralateral anatomy
4. **Control contralateral A1** -- completes proximal control
5. **Enter distal interhemispheric fissure** above the aneurysm
6. **Trace contralateral A2 proximally** to the neck
7. **Dissect ACoA perforators from posterior neck** -- prepare for clip blade

### Dome Projection and Blind Spots

Each dome direction creates a unique blind spot:

| Projection | Frequency (Yasargil) | Blind Spot | Key Challenge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Superior | 34% | Contralateral A2 | Hidden behind dome; requires dissection behind ipsilateral A2 |
| Anterior | 23% | Contralateral A1-A2 junction | Clip tips must not pass beyond neck |
| Posterior | 14% | ACoA perforators | Dome displaces perforators deep in fissure |
| Inferior | 13% | Contralateral A1 | May clip without complete contralateral control |
| Mixed | 16% | Variable | Requires careful analysis |

### ACoA Complex Rotation

The ACoA complex can be **twisted** (rotated about a vertical axis, shifting A2 segments anteroposteriorly) or **tilted** (rotated about a horizontal axis, shifting A2 segments superoinferiorly). Dominant A1 segments tend to twist the complex toward the contralateral side. Misinterpreted rotation is a common source of clipping error.

## Clipping Technique

### By Dome Projection

- **Inferiorly/anteriorly projecting**: usually simple straight clips; operative view is along the neck with afferent/efferent branches away from blade path
- **Superiorly projecting**: tandem clipping or anteroposterior clip trajectory; ipsilateral A2 can interfere; fenestrated clips may encircle the ipsilateral A2
- **Posteriorly projecting**: angled fenestrated clips required; blades navigate around and under the interposed ACoA complex; view of perforators limited

### Advanced Techniques

- **Fenestration tubes**: antegrade tubes around the ipsilateral A1-A2 junction for wide-necked or dolichoectatic aneurysms
- **Stacked fenestrated clips**: reconstruct the proximal neck without overdissecting adherent planes
- **Gyrus rectus resection**: sometimes needed for high-riding ACoA complex, swollen brain, or tight interhemispheric fissure, but subarachnoid dissection is preferred

## Key Pearls

- The [recurrent-artery-of-heubner](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/recurrent-artery-of-heubner.md) is the most reliable guide to the ACoA -- exploit its proximity (<4 mm)
- Orbitofrontal and frontopolar arteries can drape the dome and appear as "false A2 segments"
- With asymmetric A1s, dividing the A1 segment is safer than dividing the ACoA (preserves perforators) if anterior circle interruption is needed
- Endovascular techniques (stent-assisted coiling, flow diversion) requiring antiplatelet agents are often contraindicated acutely with ruptured ACoA aneurysms
- ACoA aneurysms have the highest rate of complex anatomy among the seven aneurysm types
