# Pituitary Neuroendocrine Tumors (PitNET)

The WHO CNS5 reclassified pituitary adenomas as pituitary neuroendocrine tumors (PitNET), recognizing their neuroendocrine biology. Most common sellar region tumor, accounting for ~15% of intracranial neoplasms. Almost all are benign.

## Classification by Lineage

| Lineage | Transcription Factor | Subtypes |
|---------|---------------------|----------|
| **PIT1** | PIT1 | Somatotroph (GH), lactotroph (PRL), mammosomatotroph, thyrotroph (TSH) |
| **TPIT** | TPIT | Corticotroph (ACTH) |
| **SF1** | SF1 | Gonadotroph (FSH/LH) |
| **No lineage** | -- | Null cell, plurihormonal |

## Classification by Size

| Category | Size | Notes |
|----------|------|-------|
| Microadenoma | <10 mm | Confined to sella |
| Macroadenoma | ≥10 mm | Suprasellar extension, cavernous sinus invasion |
| Giant | ≥40 mm | Significant mass effect |

## Clinical Presentation

### Functioning Tumors (hormone-secreting)

**Prolactinoma (lactotroph)** — most common functioning PitNET (~40%):
- Women: amenorrhea, galactorrhea, infertility
- Men: decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, gynecomastia
- Diagnosis: serum prolactin (generally PRL >200 ng/mL = prolactinoma; 100-200 = possible stalk effect)
- **Treatment: medical (dopamine agonists)** — cabergoline is first-line

**GH-secreting (somatotroph):**
- Acromegaly (adults), gigantism (children)
- IGF-1 and GH after OGTT for diagnosis
- First-line: transsphenoidal surgery

**ACTH-secreting (corticotroph):**
- Cushing's disease
- 24-hour urinary free cortisol, late-night salivary cortisol, dexamethasone suppression test
- First-line: transsphenoidal surgery
- Bilateral inferior petrosal sinus sampling (BIPSS) to confirm pituitary source

**TSH-secreting (thyrotroph)** — rare:
- Central hyperthyroidism
- Elevated TSH + elevated free T4

### Non-functioning PitNET (NFPA)
Most common macroadenoma type. Present with mass effect:
- **Visual field defect** — bitemporal hemianopia (chiasmal compression)
- Headache
- Hypopituitarism (from compression of normal pituitary)
- Pituitary apoplexy (acute hemorrhage/infarction) — sudden headache, visual loss, ophthalmoplegia

## Imaging

**MRI (gold standard):**
- Dedicated pituitary protocol with thin coronal/sagittal cuts
- Microadenoma: hypointense on post-contrast T1 (delayed enhancement)
- Macroadenoma: assessment of suprasellar extension, cavernous sinus invasion (Knosp classification), ICA encasement
- PitNET enlarges the sella (vs craniopharyngioma which erodes posterior clinoids)

## Treatment

### Prolactinoma
- **Dopamine agonists first** — cabergoline (0.25-2 mg/week) normalizes PRL in >90%, shrinks tumor in >80%
- Surgery only if: medical failure, intolerance, CSF leak, or patient preference
- Do NOT operate on prolactinoma without trial of dopamine agonists

### All Others (GH, ACTH, TSH, NFPA)
- **Transsphenoidal surgery** — endoscopic or microscopic
- Radiation therapy (SRS or fractionated) for residual/recurrent
- Medical adjuncts: somatostatin analogues (GH), pasireotide (ACTH), temozolomide (aggressive PitNET)

### Pituitary Apoplexy
- Medical emergency — high-dose steroids immediately
- Urgent surgical decompression if: progressive visual loss, declining consciousness
- Some cases managed conservatively if stable

## Post-operative Concerns

- **Diabetes insipidus:** transient in ~20%, permanent in ~2%. Triphasic pattern (DI → SIADH → DI)
- **Hypopituitarism:** assess anterior pituitary function post-op
- **CSF leak:** risk increases with larger tumors and revision surgery
- **Recurrence:** follow with serial MRI + hormonal levels
