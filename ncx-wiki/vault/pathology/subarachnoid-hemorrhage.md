# Subarachnoid Hemorrhage

Subarachnoid hemorrhage is the most time-sensitive cerebrovascular emergency in neurosurgery. In non-traumatic SAH the dominant etiology is rupture of a saccular cerebral aneurysm, producing a thunderclap headache, diffuse basal cisternal blood, and an early mortality of 25–40%. Outcome depends on how quickly the ruptured aneurysm is secured, how aggressively delayed ischemic injury is prevented, and how neurosurgical, interventional, and critical-care teams coordinate management.

## Etiology

- **Aneurysmal (~85%)** — rupture of a saccular [[aneurysm]] at the circle of Willis (AComm > PComm > MCA bifurcation > basilar apex > PICA origin).
- **Perimesencephalic (~10%)** — pretruncal blood pattern; typically venous; benign natural history; negative angiogram.
- **AVM / DAVF-related** — usually parenchymal with SAH extension.
- **Vasculopathy** — RCVS, dissection, mycotic aneurysm, vasculitis.
- **Coagulopathy / drugs** — anticoagulants, sympathomimetics.
- **Traumatic** — convexity pattern, usually benign if isolated.

## Clinical presentation

- **Thunderclap headache** ("worst of life") — the sentinel feature; peak intensity within seconds.
- **Meningismus** — nuchal rigidity, photophobia, Kernig / Brudzinski signs.
- **Transient loss of consciousness** — from acute ICP spike.
- **Focal deficit** — CN III palsy in PComm aneurysm; hemiparesis with MCA hematoma; mutism with ACoA bleed.
- **Cardiopulmonary** — neurogenic stunned myocardium, pulmonary edema, arrhythmia.

## Grading

### Hunt-Hess
1. Asymptomatic or mild headache.
2. Severe headache, nuchal rigidity, CN palsy only.
3. Mild focal deficit, drowsy, confused.
4. Stupor, moderate-severe hemiparesis, early decerebrate.
5. Deep coma, decerebrate rigidity, moribund.

### WFNS (GCS-based)
- **I** GCS 15, no deficit.
- **II** GCS 13–14, no deficit.
- **III** GCS 13–14 + deficit.
- **IV** GCS 7–12.
- **V** GCS 3–6.

### Fisher / modified Fisher
Fisher grades 1–4 by CT pattern (no blood → diffuse / vertical clot → parenchymal/IVH); modified Fisher adds IVH as an independent predictor of vasospasm.

## Diagnosis

- **Non-contrast CT** within 6 h of ictus has sensitivity >95% in expert hands.
- **LP** for SAH when CT is negative beyond 6 h — xanthochromia in 2nd and 3rd tubes.
- **CTA** for vascular source; [[avm-feeding-arteries]] and [[avm-draining-veins]] when AVM suspected.
- **DSA** — gold standard; repeat at 1–2 weeks if initial negative.

## Complications

- **Rebleeding** — 4% first 24 h, 1–2%/day for 2 weeks untreated. Secure the aneurysm early.
- **Hydrocephalus** — acute obstructive (IVH) or communicating; temporary external drainage followed by permanent shunt in 10–20%.
- **Vasospasm / DCI** — days 3–14, peak 7–10; nimodipine, euvolemia, induced HTN, endovascular intervention.
- **Seizures** — prophylactic AEDs during early phase.
- **Hyponatremia** — cerebral salt wasting vs SIADH; hypertonic saline.
- **Medical** — pulmonary edema, sepsis, DVT/PE, stress ulcer, cardiac dysfunction.

## Management

1. **Airway / hemodynamics** — intubate poor-grade; keep SBP <140 pre-securing.
2. **Coagulopathy reversal**.
3. **Pain / agitation control**.
4. **Aneurysm securement within 24–72 h** — microsurgical [[aneurysm-clipping-techniques|clipping]] with [[temporary-clipping]] and [[intraoperative-rupture]] readiness, vs endovascular coiling. ISAT favors coiling when anatomically suitable. See [[herniation-syndromes]] for ICP-crisis management, [[subarachnoid-dissection]] for cistern entry.
5. **Cerebral protection** — nimodipine 60 mg q4h for 21 days.
6. **ICU monitoring** — serial TCD, CTP, continuous EEG for delayed ischemia.
7. **Rescue therapy** — induced HTN, intra-arterial vasodilators, balloon angioplasty.
8. **Rehab and follow-up** — cognitive deficits are common even with good-grade SAH.

## Outcomes

- 30-day mortality 25–40% (higher with poor Hunt-Hess).
- ~30% of survivors dependent; cognitive and affective sequelae remain common.
- Good-grade (I–II) patients with secured aneurysms and aggressive vasospasm management return to independence in 70–80% of cases.
