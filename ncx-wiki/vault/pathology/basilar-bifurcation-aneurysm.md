# Basilar Bifurcation Aneurysm

Basilar bifurcation aneurysms are the most challenging posterior circulation aneurysms for microsurgical clipping. They reside at the depth of the interpeduncular cistern, surrounded by the five most critical arteries in the posterior circulation and their thalamoperforating branches. A single occluded thalamoperforator can devastate a patient -- a fact not associated with any other aneurysm.

## Microsurgical Anatomy

### Basilar Quadrifurcation

The [[basilar-artery]] terminates in a quadrifurcation producing bilateral [[posterior-cerebral-artery]] (PCA) and bilateral [[superior-cerebellar-artery]] (SCA).

### PCA Segments

| Segment | Name | Course |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Precommunicating | Basilar bifurcation to PCoA junction |
| P2A | Crural | Around [[cerebral-peduncle]] through crural cistern |
| P2P | Ambient | Around lateral [[midbrain]] through ambient cistern |
| P3 | Quadrigeminal | Over free tentorial edge through quadrigeminal cistern |
| P4 | Calcarine | Anterior calcarine fissure to occipital cortex |

### SCA Segments

Four segments: **anterior pontomesencephalic** (origin to anterolateral brainstem), **lateral pontomesencephalic** (dips to trigeminal root), **cerebellomesencephalic** (within the fissure), and **cortical** (tentorial cerebellar surface).

### Thalamoperforating Arteries

The **posterior thalamoperforators** arise from the P1 segments (majority from the middle third) and supply the [[thalamus]], [[hypothalamus]], subthalamus, medial [[midbrain]], [[substantia-nigra]], [[red-nucleus]], [[oculomotor-nerve]] nucleus, trochlear nucleus, reticular formation, and posterior [[internal-capsule]]. Three patterns exist:

- **Bilateral symmetric**: individual branches from both P1s
- **Bilateral asymmetric**: dominant supply from one side
- **Artery of Percheron**: single trunk from one P1 supplying bilateral territory

Thalamoperforator compromise causes somatesthetic disturbances, weakness, memory deficits, autonomic imbalance, diplopia, altered consciousness, and endocrine dysfunction.

### Reciprocal PCoA-P1 Relationship

Diminutive PCoAs associate with large P1 segments; fetal PCoAs associate with hypoplastic P1s. This reciprocity affects collateral supply and tolerance to temporary clipping.

## Operative Approach

Approached from the **right side** (avoids dominant hemisphere) unless anatomy favors the left, left CN III palsy exists, right hemiparesis exists, or additional left-sided aneurysms need clipping. The [[orbitozygomatic-approach]] is used routinely.

### Three Anatomic Triangles

| Triangle | Borders | View | Utility |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Optic-carotid** | [[Optic-nerve]], ICA, A1 | Medial-superior | Pituitary stalk, mammillary bodies; small and confining |
| **Carotid-oculomotor** | ICA, [[oculomotor-nerve]], [[uncus]] | Largest, best panorama | Main working corridor for basilar apex |
| **Supracarotid** | A1, M1, basomedial frontal lobe | Above ICA bifurcation | Short/atherosclerotic ICA; obstructed by perforators |

## Dissection Strategy

### Superficial Dissection (Temporal Lobe Mobilization)

1. Split the [[sylvian-fissure]] (separate frontal and temporal lobes)
2. Free inferior temporal lobe from middle fossa floor (cut arachnoid adhesions)
3. Untether anterior temporal pole (sacrifice vein to [[sphenoparietal-sinus]])
4. Mobilize temporal lobe posterolaterally through the pretemporal corridor
5. Dissect [[anterior-choroidal-artery]] cisternal segment (opens [[tentorial-incisura]] and crural cistern, liberates [[oculomotor-nerve]])
6. Dissect anterior temporal artery to relieve retraction tension
7. Fenestrate the [[lamina-terminalis]] early to relax the brain

### Deep Dissection

1. Identify PCoA at its ICA origin and follow to P1-P2 junction
2. Dissect P2 laterally over CN III to tentorial edge
3. Follow P1 undersurface medially through **Liliequist's membrane** (incised behind posterior clinoid, medial to CN III)
4. Identify ipsilateral SCA (below CN III) and contralateral [[oculomotor-nerve]]
5. Secure proximal control on the **basilar trunk** in a perforator-free zone below the SCA origins
6. Trace ipsilateral P1 superior surface to the aneurysm neck
7. Cross the basilar apex to identify contralateral SCA and PCA
8. Dissect anterior neck pathway (easily visualized)
9. Dissect posterior neck pathway (saved for last; often requires temporary clipping and aneurysm manipulation)

### Dome Projection and Blind Spots

| Projection | Frequency | Hidden Anatomy | Challenge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Superior (most common) | Common | Thalamoperforators behind distal neck | Perforators intersect clip blade path |
| Anterior (least common) | Rare | Contralateral PCA and SCA | Work above/behind dome from ipsi- to contralateral PCA |
| Posterior | Variable | Thalamoperforators displaced posteriorly | Most difficult; perforators plastered to back wall |
| Mixed | Variable | Large domes with posterior "rump" | May displace perforators despite superior projection |

### Basilar Height Variations

- **High-riding** (~10%): migrates out of carotid-oculomotor window; requires steeper viewing angle, ICA mobilization, or optic-carotid window; thalamoperforators course inferiorly (easier to dissect)
- **Low-riding**: obstructed by [[posterior-clinoid-process]]; may need **posterior clinoidectomy** (simpler than anterior -- no dural ring, no major artery involvement); fibrin glue injection controls venous bleeding
- Tentorial incision behind the [[trochlear-nerve]] dural sheath may expose laterally deviated low-lying aneurysms

## Clipping Technique

### By Dome Projection

**Superior projection**: anterolateral clip trajectory; blades perpendicular to ascending perforators. Narrow necks -- simple straight clips; wide necks -- stacked or tandem clipping with fenestration encircling proximal perforators and adherent ipsilateral P1.

**Anterior projection**: lateral clip trajectory; superior blade guides application; perforators separate easily (course differently from dome).

**Posterior projection**: fenestrated clips navigated around and under ipsilateral PCA, SCA, and perforators; tandem clipping partitions reconstruction; overlapping fenestrated clips for mixed projection.

### Seven-Point Post-Clipping Inspection

1. Aneurysm is occluded
2. Parent artery is patent
3. Efferent branches are patent
4. Perforating arteries are patent
5. Adjacent arteries are patent
6. No neck remnant
7. Blind spots are clear

### Troubleshooting

- **Persistent filling**: usually from incompletely occluded distal neck (deepest, hardest to see); advance tandem clip tips or add stacked fenestrated booster
- **Filling from proximal neck**: fenestrated clips allowing flow at heels; additional stacked clips close the fenestration
- **Thalamoperforator occlusion**: trace clip blades heel to toe; perforator usually one not seen before clipping; attempt to maneuver from tips before clip removal
- **P1 occlusion**: clips can migrate onto P1; use tentative clips then stack higher permanent clips; remove tentative clips to restore P1 flow

### When Clipping Fails

Patients with large PCoAs may tolerate **proximal basilar occlusion**. A clip on the basilar trunk alters hemodynamics and promotes thrombosis -- a useful last resort.

## SCA Aneurysms

[[Superior-cerebellar-artery]] aneurysms are more favorable than basilar bifurcation aneurysms: not intimate with thalamoperforators, lie off the midline within the carotid-oculomotor triangle, no high-riding variants. The PCA and SCA are identified distally on either side of the [[oculomotor-nerve]] and dissected proximally.
