# Approach to the Comatose Patient

Systematic evaluation of the comatose patient, designed to protect the brain while rapidly identifying surgical emergencies. Coma is operationally defined as [[glasgow-coma-scale\|GCS]] ≤ 8.

## Step-by-Step Protocol

### 1. Cardiovascular Stabilization
- Establish airway (intubate if GCS ≤ 8)
- Check circulation: heartbeat, BP, carotid pulse
- CPR if necessary

### 2. Labs (STAT)
- Electrolytes (Na, glucose, BUN)
- CBC with differential
- ABG
- As appropriate: toxicology screen (serum + urine), calcium, ammonia, antiseizure medication levels

### 3. Emergency Supportive Medications
| Drug | Indication | Dose |
|------|-----------|------|
| Glucose (D50) | Hypoglycemia | ≥25 mL IVP (check fingerstick first if possible) |
| Naloxone (Narcan) | Narcotic overdose | 0.4 mg IVP |
| Flumazenil (Romazicon) | Benzodiazepine overdose | 0.2 mg IV over 30s, then 0.3 mg q1min up to 3 mg |
| Thiamine | Wernicke's encephalopathy | 50-100 mg IVP (3% of Wernicke's presents with coma) |

### 4. Core Neuro Exam

**Pupils** — most useful sign to distinguish metabolic from structural coma:
- **Equal and reactive** → metabolic cause (few exceptions: glutethimide, anoxic encephalopathy, anticholinergics)
- **Unilateral fixed/dilated** → oculomotor palsy → possible [[herniation-syndromes\|herniation]]
- **Pinpoint with minute reaction** → pontine lesion
- **Bilateral fixed/dilated (7-10 mm)** → subtotal medullary damage, immediate post-anoxia, or hypothermia (<32°C)
- Narcotics → miosis (may need magnifying glass to see reaction)

**Oculomotor:**
- Doll's eyes (oculocephalic reflex) — tests brainstem integrity
- Cold water calorics (oculovestibular reflex) — more potent stimulus

**Motor:** assess best response — asymmetry suggests structural cause

### 5. If Herniation Signs Present
Signs: unilateral CN III palsy, progressive obtundation, decorticate/decerebrate posturing, unilateral motor deficit

→ Initiate measures to lower [[intracranial-pressure\|ICP]] → CT → emergency surgery if no improvement

**Do NOT perform lumbar puncture**

### 6. If Meningitis Suspected
(Altered mental status + fever + meningeal signs)

- No signs of mass effect → LP + immediate empiric antibiotics
- Signs of possible mass → CT first → if delay anticipated, empiric antibiotics before imaging

### 7. Seizures
Treat if present. If status epilepticus suspected → treat per protocol, obtain emergency EEG.

## Metabolic vs Structural Coma

| Feature | Metabolic | Structural |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Pupils | Equal, reactive | Asymmetric or fixed |
| Motor | Symmetric | Asymmetric (hemiparesis) |
| Onset | Gradual (usually) | May be sudden |
| Focal signs | Absent | Present |
| Breathing | Regular or Cheyne-Stokes | Irregular patterns |
| Tremor/myoclonus | Common | Uncommon |

## Midline Shift and Consciousness

| Midline shift | Level of consciousness |
|--------------|----------------------|
| 0-3 mm | Alert |
| 3-4 mm | Drowsy |
| 6-8.5 mm | Stuporous |
| 8-13 mm | Comatose |

## Pseudocoma (differential)

- **Locked-in syndrome:** ventral pontine infarction — patient aware but quadriplegic, can only blink/move eyes vertically
- **Psychiatric:** catatonia, conversion reaction
- **Neuromuscular:** myasthenia gravis, Guillain-Barré

## Hypoxic Coma Prognosis

After cardiac arrest with anoxic encephalopathy:
- **Best chance of independence:** pupillary light reflex present + GCS motor >1 + orienting eye movements at <6 hours
- **Virtually no chance:** absent pupillary reflex at <6 hours, or GCS motor <4 at day 1, or GCS motor <4 at day 3
- Bilateral fixed pupils at 3 days: 100% poor outcome
- Steroids have no beneficial effect on neurological recovery after cardiac arrest
