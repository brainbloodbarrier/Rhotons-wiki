# Brain Death Determination

Brain death is the irreversible cessation of all functions of the entire brain, including the brainstem. Most U.S. states follow the Uniform Determination of Death Act (UDDA).

## Prerequisites

Before clinical exam can be considered valid:

| Condition | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Core temperature | >36°C (96.8°F) |
| Systolic BP | ≥100 mmHg (with or without pressors) |
| Drug intoxication | Excluded (BAC <0.08%, no NMBAs, barbiturates, benzodiazepines) |
| Metabolic derangement | Corrected |
| Post-resuscitation | Adequate time elapsed |
| Pentobarbital coma | Level ≤10 mcg/mL |

## Clinical Examination

### 1. Absence of Brainstem Reflexes

| Reflex | Test | Positive for brain death |
|--------|------|-------------------------|
| Pupillary | Light in each eye | Fixed pupils, no reaction |
| Corneal | Gauze touch to cornea | No eye closure |
| Oculovestibular | 60-100 mL ice water in ear, HOB 30° | No eye movement of any kind |
| Oculocephalic | Head turning (clear C-spine first) | No contralateral eye deviation |
| Gag | ET tube stimulation | No gag response |
| Cough | Bronchial suctioning | No cough |

### 2. Absence of Motor Function

- No cerebrally mediated motor response to noxious stimulation (peripheral or central)
- No eye opening, eye movement, or facial movement
- True decerebrate or decorticate posturing = **incompatible** with brain death
- Spinal cord reflexes (flexor withdrawal, muscle stretch, even Lazarus sign) ARE compatible with brain death

### 3. Apnea Test (Apnea Challenge)

Assesses medullary function. Reserve for last — rising PaCO2 increases [[intracranial-pressure\|ICP]].

**Protocol:**
1. Pre-oxygenate ≥10 min with 100% FiO2 to PaO2 >200 mmHg
2. Reduce ventilator rate to achieve normocarbia (PaCO2 35-40 mmHg)
3. Disconnect ventilator, provide passive O2 at 6 L/min via tracheal catheter
4. Monitor O2 saturation continuously
5. Wait ≥2 minutes, send serial ABGs

**Positive result (compatible with brain death):**
- No respirations AND PaCO2 ≥60 mmHg (or rise ≥20 mmHg above baseline)
- Average time to reach target: ~6 minutes (rate of rise: 3.7 ± 2.3 mmHg/min)

**Abort if:**
- Patient breathes (chest/abdominal excursion) → brain death excluded
- SBP <90 mmHg
- O2 saturation <80% for >30 seconds
- Significant cardiac arrhythmia

## Confounders (Simulate Brain Death)

- **Hypothermia** (<36°C): pupils may be fixed/dilated, recovery possible. Wait 48-72 hrs after rewarming
- **Drug intoxication:** barbiturates, benzodiazepines, NMBAs, methaqualone
- **Pseudocholinesterase deficiency** (1:3000): succinylcholine effect up to 8 hrs instead of 5 min — use twitch monitor
- **Shock/anoxia:** immediately post-resuscitation, fixed pupils possible
- **Severe COPD:** respiratory drive from low PaO2, not CO2 — apnea test may be invalid

## Ancillary Confirmatory Tests

Not required but may be used at physician discretion. Preferred tests:

| Test | Method | Limitations |
|------|--------|------------|
| Cerebral angiography | Absence of intracranial flow at carotid bifurcation/circle of Willis | Costly, invasive, not optimal for brainstem flow |
| EEG | Electrocerebral silence (no activity >2 mcV) | Does not detect brainstem activity; drug intoxication invalidates |
| CRAG (radionuclide) | Absence of cerebral perfusion on 99mTc-HMPAO SPECT | May miss minimal brainstem flow |

### Indications for Ancillary Testing

- Complicating conditions (hypothermia, drug intoxication, metabolic)
- Severe facial trauma limiting ocular exam
- Severe COPD/CHF invalidating apnea test
- To shorten observation period

## Observation Period

No universally mandated minimum period. Many states require two exams by statute. In clear-cut cases (massive ICH, GSW traversing brain) with several hours elapsed, single exam may suffice.
