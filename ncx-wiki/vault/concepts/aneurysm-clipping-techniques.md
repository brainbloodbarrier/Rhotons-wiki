# Aneurysm Clipping Techniques

Permanent clip application is the definitive step of [[cerebrovascular-aneurysms-overview|aneurysm surgery]], following [[subarachnoid-dissection|cisternal dissection]] and [[temporary-clipping|temporary occlusion]]. Lawton describes clip application as "landing an airplane," requiring precise **yaw** (side-to-side rotation about a vertical axis), **pitch** (slope of the appliers), and **roll** (rotation about the instrument axis).

## Rhoton's Four Rules

Rhoton's rules govern clip orientation and guide aneurysm analysis:

1. Aneurysms arise at **branching sites** on the parent artery (side branch or bifurcation)
2. Aneurysms arise at **turns or curves** in the outer wall where hemodynamic stress is greatest
3. Aneurysms **point in the direction** blood would have gone if the curve was absent
4. Each aneurysm is associated with a set of [perforating arteries](../rhoton-wiki/vault/synthesis/perforating-arteries.md) that must be preserved

From Rule 1: clip perpendicular to the afferent artery and parallel to efferent branches at bifurcation aneurysms (e.g., [[mca-aneurysm|MCA]], [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm|basilar bifurcation]]). From Rules 2-3: clip parallel to the parent artery at curve aneurysms (e.g., [[ophthalmic-artery-aneurysm|OphA]], [SHA](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/superior-hypophyseal-artery.md)). From Rule 4: clip parallel to the line of perforators across the aneurysm base (e.g., [[acoa-aneurysm|ACoA]], [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm|basilar bifurcation]]).

## Visualization

Complete visualization from start to finish is mandatory. Both sides of the aneurysm neck, both clip blades, and adjacent anatomy must be in one panoramic view. The clip appliers' working line must be **15 to 30 degrees off** the microscope sight line to preserve the view. The appliers' line is often fixed by aneurysm anatomy, requiring microscope repositioning.

## Simple Clipping

The basic rule: **simple is best**. A single clip is used for small aneurysms with narrow necks and uncomplicated anatomy. Requires selecting the perfect clip size, contour, and configuration. Small, deep aneurysms in tight corridors (e.g., [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm|basilar apex]]) may still need long blades to move the coiled spring back and keep the applier head out of the sight line.

## Multiple Clipping

For large aneurysms with broad necks and complex anatomy. Multiple clips close the neck in sequential steps, progressing from deep to near neck and tackling the most difficult, inaccessible part first. Strategies include:

- **Intersecting clips** -- a second clip angled into the initial clip, tips intersecting at acute, right, or obtuse angles. Can be simple (two straight clips in T-configuration) or complex (multiple clips converging like a camera aperture)
- **Stacked clips** -- applied parallel to each other. **Understacking** places subsequent clips beneath the initial to contour efferent artery origins; mini-clips eliminate dog-ear remnants. **Overstacking** places clips above when the initial clip does not adequately reconstruct the neck. All applied in the same direction, facilitating clipping in tight corridors
- **Overlapping fenestrated clip** -- applied over an initial straight clip at various angles, encircling the initial blade to close a distal neck remnant

## Tandem Clipping

A technique developed by **Drake** using a straight fenestrated clip to first close the distal aneurysm neck, then shorter simple clips to close the proximal neck encircled by the fenestration. The fenestrated clip's closing force is maintained distally near the tips, gathering aneurysm tissue and collapsing the neck to visualize deep anatomy. Superior to simple clipping with longer clips because long clips can splay at the tips and permit refilling.

Variants:
- **Understacked tandem** -- closing clip below the fenestration
- **Overstacked tandem** -- closing clip above the fenestration
- **Booster clip** -- stacked fenestrated clip reinforcing closure at the distal neck

## Tandem Angled Fenestrated Clipping

Developed by **Sugita** for broad-necked aneurysms projecting away from the surgeon, as in [[ophthalmic-artery-aneurysm|paraclinoid ICA aneurysms]]. Uses multiple 90-degree angled fenestrated clips applied sequentially across the neck, toe to heel, from deep to near. Clips must overlap at their interface -- clips that simply meet may permit refilling.

**Counterclipping** variants:
- **Facing** -- blade tips in opposite directions, toe to toe
- **Cross-wise** -- heel to heel

## Fenestration Tubes

Straight fenestrated clips stacked to close the neck while creating a tube that reconstructs an efferent artery. Three configurations:

| Type | Flow Direction | Function |
|------|---------------|----------|
| **Antegrade tube** | Perpendicular to blades, antegrade | Transmits blood flow to efferent artery |
| **Retrograde tube** | Closed tube, retrograde | Redirects flow to efferent artery at base |
| **Dome tube** | Fenestrates the dome | Tips reconstruct the neck (clips perpendicular to neck) |

Used for giant aneurysms, efferent arteries adherent to the dome, or unusual branch anatomy.

## Clip Reconstruction

Sometimes requires deliberately entering the aneurysm. Indications include thrombectomy for thrombotic aneurysms, coil mobilization in recurrent coiled aneurysms, and decapitation of giant aneurysms. These maneuvers leave a gaping hole requiring reconstructive repair under ongoing [[temporary-clipping|ischemia from temporary clipping]]. Complications include incomplete proximal/distal control with back-bleeding, adherent [perforators](../rhoton-wiki/vault/synthesis/perforating-arteries.md), difficulty reconstructing the neck post-transection, and atherosclerotic tissue at the neck. The brain can be reperfused only after the opened aneurysm is completely closed.
