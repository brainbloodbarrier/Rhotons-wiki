# Basal Cisterns

The basal cisterns are expansions of the subarachnoid space at the base of the brain. They contain arteries, veins, cranial nerves, and perforating vessels — making them critical surgical corridors and danger zones.

## Anterior Basal Cisterns

| Cistern | Location | Key contents |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| **Carotid** | Around supraclinoid ICA | ICA, AChA origin, [[oculomotor-nerve\|CN III]], PCoA origin |
| **Chiasmatic** | Above sella, below optic chiasm | Optic nerves/chiasm, pituitary stalk, superior hypophyseal arteries |
| **Sylvian** | Within [[sylvian-fissure]] | MCA and branches, superficial Sylvian vein |
| **Olfactory** | Along olfactory tracts | CN I, anterior ethmoidal artery |
| **Lamina terminalis** | Anterior 3rd ventricle wall | ACoA complex, Heubner's artery, A1-A2 junction |
| **Pericallosal** | Above corpus callosum | ACA (A3+), callosomarginal artery |
| **Hemispheric** | Along medial hemisphere | ACA branches, cingulate gyrus |

### Lamina Terminalis Cistern — Surgical Significance
The lamina terminalis is a thin membrane forming the anterior wall of the 3rd ventricle. Opening it during [[acoa-aneurysm\|ACoA aneurysm]] surgery provides:
- CSF drainage for brain relaxation
- Access to the anterior 3rd ventricle
- A corridor for fenestration (alternative to ETV for some hydrocephalus cases) ^[inferred]

## Posterior Basal Cisterns

| Cistern | Location | Key contents |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| **Interpeduncular** | Between cerebral peduncles | Basilar bifurcation, P1 segments, perforators, CN III |
| **Crural** | Between peduncle and uncus | P2A (anterior), AChA, basal vein of Rosenthal, CN III |
| **Ambient** | Around midbrain lateral surface | P2P (posterior), SCA, basal vein, CN IV |
| **Quadrigeminal** | Behind tectal plate | P3, vein of Galen, internal cerebral veins, pineal gland |
| **Velum interpositum** | Between fornix and thalamus roof | Internal cerebral veins, medial posterior choroidal arteries |

### Tentorial Incisura Relations
The tentorial incisura (notch) is the gap in the tentorium cerebelli through which the midbrain passes. The posterior basal cisterns surround the midbrain within this space. During [[herniation-syndromes\|transtentorial herniation]], these cisterns are progressively obliterated:
- First: crural cistern → ambient cistern compressed
- Then: interpeduncular cistern effaced
- CT finding: loss of perimesencephalic cisterns = impending herniation

### Arterial Relations
- **PCA (P1-P3):** courses through interpeduncular → crural → ambient → quadrigeminal cisterns
- **SCA:** courses through pontomesencephalic groove, then cerebellomesencephalic fissure
- **Basilar perforators:** arise within interpeduncular cistern — **must be preserved** during [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm\|basilar tip aneurysm]] surgery

### Venous Relations
- **Basal vein of Rosenthal:** formed in Sylvian cistern → courses through crural → ambient → quadrigeminal cisterns → drains to vein of Galen
- **Vein of Galen:** formed by junction of internal cerebral veins and basal veins in the quadrigeminal cistern → drains to straight sinus

## Surgical Approaches to Cisterns

| Cistern | Primary approach |
|---------|-----------------|
| Carotid, chiasmatic | [[pterional-approach\|Pterional]] |
| Sylvian | Transsylvian |
| Lamina terminalis | Pterional, interhemispheric |
| Interpeduncular | Pterional, subtemporal, [[orbitozygomatic-approach\|OZ]] |
| Crural, ambient | Subtemporal, supracerebellar |
| Quadrigeminal | Supracerebellar-infratentorial, occipital-transtentorial |
