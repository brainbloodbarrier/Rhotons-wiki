# Anterior Cerebral Artery (ACA)

The anterior cerebral artery (ACA) is one of the two terminal branches of the internal carotid artery, arising at the ICA bifurcation and coursing medially and superiorly to enter the [[interhemispheric-fissure]]. Distally it becomes the pericallosal artery.

## Segmentation (Fischer)

| Segment | Course | Branches |
|---------|--------|----------|
| **A1 (precommunicating)** | ICA bifurcation → ACoA | Medial lenticulostriate perforators, recurrent artery of Heubner |
| **A2 (infracallosal)** | ACoA → genu of corpus callosum | Orbitofrontal, frontopolar |
| **A3 (precallosal)** | Around genu | Callosomarginal origin |
| **A4 (supracallosal)** | Above body of callosum | Internal frontal (anterior/middle/posterior) |
| **A5 (postcallosal)** | Around splenium | Superior parietal, precuneal |

A3–A5 are collectively the pericallosal artery.

## Key Structures

### ACoA Complex
The A1–A2 junction on each side is bridged by the **anterior communicating artery (ACoA)**, forming the anterior portion of the circle of Willis. Perforators from ACoA and adjacent A1/A2 supply the hypothalamus, fornix column, and optic chiasm — injury produces memory and endocrine deficits. See [[acoa-aneurysm]] for detailed surgical anatomy.

### Recurrent Artery of Heubner
Arising from A2 or proximal A2 (variable), Heubner's artery loops retrograde lateral to A1 to supply the head of the caudate nucleus, anterior limb of internal capsule, and anterior putamen. Sacrifice produces contralateral face-arm weakness and frontal-subcortical cognitive syndromes.

### Callosomarginal
Largest A4 branch, coursing in the cingulate sulcus. Pattern variations determine whether A4 or callosomarginal dominates distal flow.

## Surgical Relevance

- **A1 segment**: proximal control for ACoA aneurysm surgery, accessed via pterional or orbitozygomatic craniotomy. Aneurysms here are rare but associated with A1 fenestration.
- **ACoA aneurysms**: most common anterior-circulation aneurysm (≈30 %). See [[acoa-aneurysm]].
- **Distal ACA (A3–A5) aneurysms**: pericallosal aneurysms, approached via [[anterior-interhemispheric-approach]].
- **Bypass**: A3–A3 in-situ bypass (side-to-side) for proximal ACA sacrifice scenarios; see [[in-situ-bypass]].

## Cortical Territory

Medial frontal and parietal lobes — orbital frontal, cingulate, supplementary motor area, medial precentral/postcentral (leg), precuneus. Occlusion syndrome: contralateral leg weakness, abulia/akinetic mutism (bilateral cingulate), urinary incontinence.
