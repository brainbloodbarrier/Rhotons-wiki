# Dura Mater

The dura mater is the outermost and toughest of the three meningeal layers (pachymeninx; vs. leptomeninges = arachnoid + pia). It consists of two fibrous laminae that fuse throughout most of the cranial vault except where they separate to enclose the dural venous sinuses and form the major septa.

## Layers

| Layer | Attachments |
|-------|-------------|
| **Periosteal (outer)** | Adherent to inner table of calvaria; continuous with periosteum of sutures; at the foramen magnum continues as spinal-canal epidural connective tissue (does not continue as spinal dura) |
| **Meningeal (inner)** | Fused with periosteal over vault; forms the dural reflections (falx, tentorium); continuous with spinal dura at foramen magnum |

The laminae separate at dural venous sinuses (sinus lumen lined by endothelium only).

## Dural Reflections

| Reflection | Course | Separates |
|------------|--------|-----------|
| **Falx cerebri** | Crista galli → internal occipital protuberance, in the [[interhemispheric-fissure]] | Cerebral hemispheres |
| **Tentorium cerebelli** | From transverse / petrosal ridges → meeting falx at internal occipital protuberance | Cerebrum from cerebellum |
| **Falx cerebelli** | Posterior, below tentorium | Cerebellar hemispheres |
| **Diaphragma sellae** | Covers sella, pierced by pituitary stalk | Sella contents from suprasellar cistern |

## Dural Venous Sinuses

Formed between the two dural laminae. Major sinuses:

- [[superior-sagittal-sinus|Superior sagittal sinus]] — in upper edge of falx
- Inferior sagittal sinus — in free edge of falx
- Straight sinus — in junction of falx and tentorium, receiving great cerebral vein of Galen
- Transverse sinuses — in posterior tentorial attachment
- Sigmoid sinuses — continue transverse to jugular bulbs
- Cavernous sinus — parasellar, complex venous lake with ICA and CN III-VI traversing
- Superior/inferior petrosal sinuses — along petrous ridges

## Vascular Supply of Dura

Region-specific:

| Region | Supply |
|--------|--------|
| Anterior cranial fossa | Anterior/posterior ethmoidal arteries (ophthalmic); anterior meningeal |
| Middle cranial fossa | **Middle meningeal artery** (MMA, branch of maxillary via foramen spinosum) — dominant dural supply |
| Posterior cranial fossa | Meningeal branches of vertebral and occipital arteries |
| Tentorium | Tentorial artery of Bernasconi–Cassinari (from ICA meningohypophyseal trunk) |

## Sensory Innervation

Trigeminal-dominant:

- V1 — anterior and middle fossa, tentorium, falx
- V2 — middle fossa
- V3 — middle fossa laterally
- Upper cervical (C1–C3) — posterior fossa dura
- Meningeal branches of vagus / glossopharyngeal — posterior fossa

Dural manipulation / traction is the substrate of many headache syndromes.

## Surgical Relevance

### Dural Opening
- Base toward major dural venous sinus to allow reflection away from it (protect sinus and bridging veins)
- Preserve cortical anatomy via minimal retraction, gravity-assisted exposure
- Multiple tacking sutures to calvaria during opening to prevent epidural ooze and hematoma

### Dural Closure and Reconstruction
- Watertight closure is first-line prevention of CSF leak
- Grafts: pericranium (vascularized pedicle), fascia lata, temporalis fascia, commercial collagen matrices
- Sealants (fibrin glue, dural sealants) are adjunctive, not primary

### Dural Pathology
- **Meningioma**: arises from arachnoid cap cells attached to inner dural surface
- **Dural arteriovenous fistula (dAVF)**: abnormal shunting within dural leaflets or at sinus wall
- **Chronic subdural hematoma**: collection between dura and arachnoid; expanding dural neomembrane with fragile neovasculature
- **Epidural hematoma**: classic MMA injury with fracture across its groove on squamous temporal bone
