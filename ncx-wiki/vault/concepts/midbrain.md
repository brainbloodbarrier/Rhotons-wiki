# Midbrain (Mesencephalon)

The midbrain is the rostral-most brainstem segment, traversing the tentorial incisura to connect the pons and the diencephalon. It contains the pathways for consciousness, ocular movement, and extrapyramidal motor modulation.

## Gross Anatomy

Three anteroposterior zones:

| Zone | Contents |
|------|----------|
| **Tectum** (posterior) | Superior colliculi (visual reflexes, saccades); inferior colliculi (auditory) |
| **Tegmentum** (middle) | CN III and IV nuclei, red nucleus, substantia nigra, PAG, MLF, reticular formation |
| **Crus cerebri (basis)** (anterior) | Corticospinal + corticobulbar + corticopontine fibers on either side, separated medially by substantia nigra from tegmentum |

Key surfaces:
- **Anterior**: cerebral peduncles (crus cerebri) with CN III emerging medially
- **Lateral**: lateral mesencephalic sulcus, below which lies the substantia nigra
- **Posterior**: tectal plate (quadrigeminal), vermian tonsil immediately below
- **Superior**: mammillary bodies, interpeduncular fossa

## Key Nuclei and Tracts

- **CN III nucleus**: periaqueductal, at level of superior colliculus → nerve exits medially between the peduncles into the interpeduncular cistern
- **CN IV nucleus**: periaqueductal, at level of inferior colliculus → nerve exits dorsally, only cranial nerve to do so; crosses before decussating
- **Red nucleus**: rubrospinal tract origin; lesions produce contralateral ataxic tremor
- **Substantia nigra**: dopaminergic (pars compacta) and GABAergic (pars reticulata); Parkinson disease target
- **Medial longitudinal fasciculus (MLF)**: coordinates horizontal gaze; lesions → internuclear ophthalmoplegia (INO)
- **Cerebral aqueduct (Sylvius)**: connects 3rd → 4th ventricle; aqueductal stenosis → obstructive hydrocephalus

## Vascular Supply

| Zone | Supply |
|------|--------|
| Anterior (peduncles) | Perforators from P1 of PCA (peduncular, thalamoperforating) |
| Lateral | Perforators from P2 PCA, SCA, anterior choroidal (variable) |
| Posterior (tectum) | SCA and quadrigeminal branches of PCA |

## Surgical Relevance

### Peri-Mesencephalic Approaches

| Approach | Corridor | Target |
|----------|----------|--------|
| **Subtemporal** | Below temporal lobe over tentorium | Lateral midbrain, PCA-SCA, CN III/IV, P2 aneurysms |
| **Supracerebellar-infratentorial (Krause)** | Between tentorium and cerebellum | Posterior midbrain (tectal plate), pineal region, P3 aneurysms |
| **Occipital-transtentorial** | Through tentorium from occipital lobe | Posterior midbrain, pineal, splenium |
| **Endoscopic third ventriculostomy** | Via [[foramen-of-monro]] → tuber cinereum | Not midbrain per se, but decompresses aqueductal obstruction |

### Tentorial Incisura
The midbrain passes through the tentorial notch (incisura). Its anterior, middle, and posterior incisural spaces (Yasargil–Rhoton classification) are key corridors. Pathology: transtentorial herniation progressively compresses the midbrain, producing Duret hemorrhages and irreversible coma.

### Oculomotor (CN III) Palsy
Midbrain-level CN III injury: ipsilateral ptosis, "down and out" eye, dilated pupil. Distinguishes from PCoA aneurysm (peripheral compression, often pupil-involving) by presence of other midbrain signs (Weber syndrome: contralateral hemiparesis from crus involvement).
