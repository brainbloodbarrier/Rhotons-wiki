# Wernicke Area

Wernicke area is the classical language-comprehension cortex, located in the posterior third of the superior temporal gyrus (pSTG) of the dominant hemisphere (left in ~95 % of right-handers, ~70 % of left-handers). It corresponds roughly to the posterior portion of Brodmann area 22, extending variably into the supramarginal (BA 40) and angular (BA 39) gyri.

## Anatomical Boundaries

| Border | Structure |
|--------|-----------|
| Anterior | Primary auditory cortex (Heschl's gyrus, BA 41/42) |
| Posterior | Angular gyrus (BA 39) |
| Superior | Sylvian fissure (planum temporale) |
| Inferior | Superior temporal sulcus |

Contemporary definitions emphasize that "Wernicke's area" is not a discrete cytoarchitectonic unit but a functional network centered on pSTG, with co-active regions in supramarginal / angular gyri and posterior middle temporal gyrus (pMTG).

## Function

- Phonological decoding of heard and read speech
- Lexical-semantic access (mapping speech sounds to meaning)
- Sentence-level comprehension
- Short-term phonological memory (in concert with inferior parietal)

### Connectivity
- **Arcuate fasciculus** (dorsal language stream): Wernicke ↔ Broca, supports phonology / repetition. Lesion → conduction aphasia (fluent, poor repetition).
- **Inferior longitudinal fasciculus** (ventral stream, in combination with IFOF): semantic processing.

## Aphasia Syndromes

### Wernicke (Receptive / Fluent) Aphasia
Lesion in Wernicke area:
- Fluent speech with paraphasias (phonemic/semantic substitutions)
- Poor auditory comprehension
- Impaired repetition
- Patient often unaware of deficit (anosognosia for aphasia)

### Transcortical Sensory Aphasia
Lesion posterior or inferior to Wernicke proper (BA 37/39 watershed):
- Fluent, empty speech
- Poor comprehension
- **Preserved repetition** (key differentiating feature)

### Global Aphasia
Large MCA territory infarct affecting Broca + Wernicke + arcuate: non-fluent, no comprehension, no repetition.

## Surgical Relevance

### Awake Craniotomy Mapping
Dominant-hemisphere temporo-parietal glioma surgery mandates awake mapping:
- **Cortical stimulation** during object naming and sentence comprehension identifies eloquent patches (variable from patient to patient by ~1–2 cm).
- **Subcortical stimulation** identifies arcuate and inferior longitudinal fasciculi — functional limits of resection.
- Duffau's principle: resect to functional (not anatomical) boundaries — maximizes extent of resection while preserving language.

### Temporal Lobectomy for Epilepsy
Dominant-side anterior temporal lobectomy standard limit is **4.5 cm** from the temporal tip along the superior temporal gyrus (vs. 5.5 cm non-dominant) — chosen to respect Wernicke area. Recent practice favors selective amygdalohippocampectomy to avoid lateral cortex altogether.

### Meyer's Loop
The optic radiation (Meyer's loop) sweeps anteriorly into the temporal stem adjacent to Wernicke — visual-field integrity (pie-in-the-sky quadrantanopia) is a limiting factor for temporal surgery.
