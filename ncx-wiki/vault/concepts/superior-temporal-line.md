# Superior Temporal Line

The superior temporal line is an arched bony crest on the lateral surface of the calvaria, demarcating the superior origin of the [[temporalis-muscle|temporalis muscle]]. It is a principal landmark for pterional, frontotemporal, and temporal craniotomies.

## Anatomy

Starts at the zygomatic process of the frontal bone → arches backward across the frontal and parietal bones → ends where it becomes continuous with the supramastoid crest.

Immediately below runs the **inferior temporal line**, ~1 cm inferior, which marks the upper border of the temporalis insertion onto the bone; the space between the two is the **temporal fossa** where the muscle lies.

## Surface Landmarks

- Anteriorly crosses the frontozygomatic region, intersecting the coronal suture at/near its superior limit
- Posteriorly merges with the supramastoid crest
- Overlies approximately the lateral convexity of the frontal and parietal lobes

## Surgical Relevance

### Pterional Craniotomy
The superior temporal line defines:
- **Superior extent of temporal muscle reflection**: the myocutaneous flap is reflected inferiorly and anteriorly; incision of the muscle and periosteum along the STL allows a cuff for later reattachment.
- **Superior limit of the craniotomy** for a classical pterional flap (though extensions above are common for larger exposures).
- **Cosmetic reattachment reference**: re-approximation of the temporalis to its original STL origin (with the cuff left at the STL) minimizes temporal hollowing.

### Frontotemporal Approaches
Frontal burr hole is typically placed just above the STL, immediately posterior to the coronal suture ("MacCarty's keyhole" in orbitozygomatic variants). The lateral sphenoid ridge is reached by drilling the bone inferior to the STL.

### Coronal / Bicoronal Incisions
The STL is palpable through the scalp and is used as a soft-tissue landmark for planning the temporal extension of bicoronal incisions in craniofacial and bilateral anterior-fossa surgery.
