# Microsurgical Anastomosis

Arterial bypasses, regardless of complexity, are built from three fundamental anastomotic techniques: **end-to-side**, **side-to-side**, and **end-to-end**. The application of these three techniques in different locations, with different donor-recipient pairings, in unique combinations, and with subtle variations yields the full range of [[cerebral-revascularization-overview|microsurgical bypasses]].

## End-to-Side Anastomosis

The end-to-side anastomosis converges donor and recipient arteries, structurally opposite to a bifurcation. This Y-shaped construct is the most frequently used anastomosis in [[cerebrovascular-aneurysms-overview|cerebrovascular surgery]], forming the basis of EC-IC bypasses such as the [[superficial-temporal-artery|STA]]-MCA bypass.

A linear [[bypass-arteriotomy|arteriotomy]] in the recipient is made two to three times the arterial diameter, lowering resistance per Poiseuille's law. The donor artery is widened with a [[bypass-arteriotomy#Fish-Mouth Arteriotomy|fish-mouth arteriotomy]] -- an oblique transection at 60 degrees plus a longitudinal sidewall incision -- transforming the circular end into a quadrangular opening.

The anastomosis proceeds through 14 steps: recipient exposure, [[temporary-clipping|temporary clip]] application, arteriotomy with a 25-gauge needle and right-angled microscissors, heel and toe stay sutures (surgeon's knots), continuous suturing in a loose spiral, loop-by-loop tightening, intraluminal inspection, second suture line, and sequential clip removal (distal first to back-bleed, then proximal). Patency is confirmed with ICG videoangiography and Doppler insonation.

### Variations

Three principal variations exist:

| Variation | Configuration | Indication |
|-----------|--------------|------------|
| Fish-mouthed (Y-shaped) | Equal-caliber donor and recipient | Classic STA-MCA bypass for [[moyamoya]] |
| Right-angled (T-shaped) | Oversized graft to smaller recipient | [[radial-artery-graft]] or [[saphenous-vein-graft]] interpositional bypasses |
| Patch | Donor bifurcation transected obliquely | [[superficial-temporal-artery|STA]] bifurcation into frontal and parietal branches |

## Side-to-Side Anastomosis

The side-to-side anastomosis creates a communicating bypass mimicking the brain's [[anterior-communicating-artery|anterior]] and [[posterior-communicating-artery|posterior communicating arteries]]. No structural donor limb exists -- flow is governed by demand from pressure gradients or remote occlusion.

This "kissing" bypass requires parallel arteries in close proximity, each receiving linear arteriotomies two to three times their diameter. It is a **one-way-up anastomosis**: the intraluminal suture line is sewn first (with entrance and exit stitches), followed by the extraluminal line. No arterial rotation is needed, making it ideal for immobile in-situ donors such as PICA-to-PICA or [[posterior-cerebral-artery|PCA]]-to-PCA bypasses. Intraluminal suturing proceeds at five stitches per millimeter; the needle driver and microforceps displace outer walls downward while lifting inner walls upward, preventing through-catches.

## End-to-End Anastomosis

The end-to-end anastomosis reconnects transected arterial ends after pathology excision, restoring original blood flow without introducing a new donor. The circular suture arc equals the arterial circumference (approximately 3d), requiring half the bites of the other anastomoses.

The principal challenge is the third dimension: semicircular suture arcs with vertical orientation require ongoing adjustments. Arteries are rotated via [[temporary-clipping|temporary clip]] manipulation (shanks applied vertically at 12 o'clock, then rotated to 3 or 9 o'clock) to expose both arcs.

### In-Situ Variation

For deep fields where arterial rotation is impractical, the in-situ variation sews the first suture line intraluminally (using entrance and exit stitches) without any rotation, then completes the second suture line extraluminally. This eliminates clip movement and is invaluable in tight corridors such as the [[far-lateral-approach|far lateral]] or [[pterional-approach|pterional]] exposure.

### Double Fish-Mouth Variation

The double fish-mouth joins two diminutive arteries with fish-mouthed [[bypass-arteriotomy|arteriotomies]], connecting toe-to-heel and heel-to-toe. This transforms semicircular suture arcs into quadrangular suture lines, increases the anastomotic area, and decreases resistance. It requires extra arterial length beyond the resected segment.

## Suture Technique Principles

All three anastomoses share core principles:

- **Continuous suturing in a loose spiral** followed by loop-by-loop tightening aligns loops, everts edges, and removes slack
- **Surgeon's knots** on all stay sutures prevent arterial separation between throws
- **Bite spacing**: approximately 4-5 stitches per millimeter; depth of two wall thicknesses extraluminally, one to two wall thicknesses intraluminally
- **Intraluminal inspection** between suture lines detects through-stitches, gaps, and pullouts
- **Back-bleeding** by removing the distal clip first clears debris before restoring antegrade flow
