# Pterion

The pterion is the H-shaped junction of four cranial bones on the lateral surface of the skull. It is the single most important surface landmark in neurosurgery because it marks the thinnest region of the calvaria, overlies the anterior branch of the middle meningeal artery, and projects directly onto the stem of the [[sylvian-fissure]]. It is the defining reference for the [[pterional-approach]].

## Bony Anatomy

The pterion is formed by the convergence of four bones:

| Bone | Contribution |
|------|--------------|
| **Frontal bone** | Anterior-superior limb |
| **Parietal bone** | Posterior-superior limb |
| **Greater wing of sphenoid** | Anterior-inferior limb |
| **Temporal squama** | Posterior-inferior limb |

The H-configuration (sphenoparietal type, ~70% of skulls) is most common; stellate, epipteric, and frontotemporal variants occur in the remainder. The sphenosquamous suture forms the central bar of the H.

### Surface Localization

- **Transverse plane:** ~4 cm superior to the [[zygomatic-arch]]
- **Sagittal plane:** ~3 cm posterior to the frontozygomatic suture
- Lies immediately behind the frontal process of the zygoma, beneath the anterior [[temporalis-muscle]] and the temporal fascia
- Sits just below the anterior end of the [[superior-temporal-line]]

## Underlying Neurovascular Structures

The intracranial relationships are what make the pterion neurosurgically pivotal:

- **Epidural:** anterior (frontal) branch of the middle meningeal artery runs in a bony groove deep to the pterion — a blunt trauma over this point is the classic source of an epidural hematoma
- **Subdural/cisternal:** pterional craniotomy exposes the stem of the [[sylvian-fissure]], giving access to the carotid cistern and the [[basal-cisterns]]
- **Parenchymal projection:** the inferior [[frontal-lobe]] (pars triangularis / pars opercularis) and the superior [[temporal-lobe]] lie directly beneath
- **Deep target:** the internal carotid bifurcation, [[anterior-cerebral-artery]] origin, and ACoA complex sit ~5–6 cm deep to the pterion along the long axis of the sphenoid ridge

## Surgical Relevance

### Pterional Craniotomy

The burr hole placed at or just posterior to the pterion ("MacCarty keyhole" when placed at the frontozygomatic suture) exposes the frontal dura, the periorbita, and the temporal dura in one opening — the cornerstone of the [[pterional-approach]]. Drilling the sphenoid ridge flush to the superior orbital fissure removes the deepest bone obstruction and straightens the working corridor to the [[carotid-cistern]], [[interhemispheric-fissure]] (for ACoA), and the [[anterior-cranial-fossa]] floor.

### Trauma

The thinness of bone at the pterion (often <2 mm) and the adherence of the middle meningeal artery to the inner table make this region the stereotypical site of:

- **Epidural hematoma** following temporal blunt trauma (classic "lucid interval" presentation)
- **Sphenoid-wing fractures** extending into the orbital apex — threaten the optic nerve and the superior orbital fissure contents

### Anatomic Variants

- **Epipteric (Wormian) bones** at the pterion are present in ~8–10% of skulls and can complicate burr-hole placement
- **Frontotemporal articulation** (no sphenoparietal suture) occurs in ~1% and realigns the "H" into an "X"

## Clinical Pearls

- Always palpate the pterion preoperatively: it is located at the posterior edge of the hairless triangle above the zygomatic arch
- In a pterional craniotomy, the first burr hole is traditionally placed at the MacCarty keyhole, not on the pterion itself, to avoid laceration of the dura adherent to the sphenoid ridge
- Scalp incision ("question mark" or curvilinear) must preserve the frontotemporal branch of the facial nerve, which crosses the zygomatic arch anterior to the pterion — interfascial or subfascial dissection of the [[temporalis-muscle]] protects this branch
