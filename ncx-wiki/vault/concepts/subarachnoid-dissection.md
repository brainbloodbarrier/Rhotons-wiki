# Subarachnoid Dissection

Subarachnoid dissection is the foundational technique of [[cerebrovascular-aneurysms-overview|cerebrovascular aneurysm surgery]]. The [[subarachnoid-space]] houses the brain's arteries and provides a navigable labyrinth to deep targets that can be dissected without violating or harming the brain parenchyma.

## The Subarachnoid Cisterns

The subarachnoid space is compartmentalized into cisterns built with sheets of [[arachnoid-mater|arachnoid tissue]], bridged by internal arachnoid trabeculations, and filled with [[cerebrospinal-fluid|CSF]]. Subarachnoid dissection opens and interconnects these cisterns en route to an aneurysm. Intercommunication between cisterns drains CSF and untethers lobes and lobules, relaxing the brain, facilitating retraction, and widening surgical corridors.

Every aneurysm is associated with a cistern:

| Aneurysm | Cistern |
|----------|---------|
| [[middle-cerebral-artery|MCA]] | [[sylvian-cistern]] |
| [[posterior-communicating-artery|PCoA]] / [[ophthalmic-artery|OphA]] | [[carotid-cistern]] |
| [[anterior-communicating-artery|ACoA]] | [[lamina-terminalis-cistern]] |
| [[pericallosal-artery|PcaA]] | [[callosal-cistern]] |
| [[basilar-artery|Basilar bifurcation]] | [[interpeduncular-cistern]] |
| [[posterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery|PICA]] | [[lateral-cerebellomedullary-cistern]] / [[cisterna-magna]] |

The pathway to some aneurysms traverses several cisterns. The route to [[acoa-aneurysm|ACoA aneurysms]] progresses from [[carotid-cistern]] to [[chiasmatic-cistern]] to [[lamina-terminalis-cistern]]. The pathway to [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm|basilar bifurcation aneurysms]] progresses from [[sylvian-cistern]] to [[carotid-cistern]] to [[lamina-terminalis-cistern]] to [[crural-cistern]] to [[interpeduncular-cistern]], traversing the [[membrane-of-liliequist]] to reach the basilar apex. Most of an aneurysm's initial dissection has nothing to do with the aneurysm itself -- it deconstructs cisternal architecture to open fissures, separate brain surfaces, and expose normal arterial anatomy.

## Arterial Landmarks

Arteries define a trail through the center of the subarachnoid space that can be dissected continuously from the cortical surface to the [[circle-of-willis]]. A single cortical artery invariably guides the dissection inward to larger branches and deeper trunks. In patients with [[subarachnoid-hemorrhage|SAH]], arteries are buried in dense clot and require excavation by suctioning outward from an arterial landmark rather than suctioning inward toward an unidentified artery.

Every artery has a **safe surface** to follow during subarachnoid dissection. Safe surfaces have smooth contours and few branches; dangerous surfaces have [[perforating-arteries|perforating arteries]], the aneurysm neck, or are poorly visualized:

- The **inferior surface** of the [[middle-cerebral-artery|M1 segment]] is safe; the superior surface gives off [[lenticulostriate-arteries]] and dissection there risks injury
- The **superior surface** of the supraclinoid [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] is safe
- The **inferior surface** of the [[anterior-cerebral-artery|A1 segment]] is safe
- The **inferior surface** of the [[posterior-cerebral-artery|P1 segment]] is safe; the superior surface has thalamoperforators leading directly to the neck of a [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm]]

Careful subarachnoid dissection should never require division or sacrifice of a small arterial branch. Branches travel to the brain they supply and can be mobilized in that direction. As a general rule, arteries supply only one lobe and can be swept to that lobe safely.

## Dissection Technique

Subarachnoid dissection consists of three basic maneuvers with three simple instruments:

1. **Cutting with microscissors** -- incise and open cisternal walls. The deep blade lifts arachnoid away from underlying arteries and veins before cutting, making microscissors both a cutting and dissecting instrument.
2. **Spreading with bipolar forceps** -- bluntly separate pial planes and open fissures. Forceps are aligned parallel to arteries and parallel to pial surfaces, distributing opening force perpendicular to brain surfaces. Useful for splitting tight fissures in swollen brains (e.g., the [[sylvian-fissure]] after SAH).
3. **Probing with Rhoton No. 6 dissector** -- develop tissue planes and mobilize arteries and nerves. Back-and-forth movements parallel arachnoidal planes. For example, the plane between the [[optic-nerve]] and the inferior frontal lobe is opened with side-to-side movements rather than up-and-down.

The dissection cycles from blunt to sharp to spreading and back. Subarachnoid dissection remains "outside" of the brain by respecting and preserving pial boundaries -- subpial transgression causes brain injury, swelling, and contusions. Developing the right touch is the biggest challenge.
