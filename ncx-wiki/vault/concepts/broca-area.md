# Broca Area

Broca area is the classical expressive-language cortex of the dominant hemisphere, occupying the pars opercularis (BA 44) and pars triangularis (BA 45) of the inferior frontal gyrus. Together with [[wernicke-area]] in the posterior superior temporal gyrus, it anchors the perisylvian language network and is a primary target of intraoperative mapping during any dominant-hemisphere surgery around the [[sylvian-fissure]].

## Surface Anatomy

### Localization

- Occupies the posterior third of the inferior frontal gyrus on the dominant (typically left) [[frontal-lobe]]
- **Pars opercularis (BA 44):** between the ascending ramus of the sylvian fissure and the inferior precentral sulcus
- **Pars triangularis (BA 45):** between the ascending and horizontal rami of the sylvian fissure — forms a triangle whose apex points inferiorly
- Anterior to BA 45 is the pars orbitalis (BA 47), which is **not** classically Broca
- Deep to Broca, the [[sylvian-fissure]] opens into the frontal operculum overlying the insula

### Hemispheric Dominance

- Left-hemisphere dominance in ~95% of right-handed individuals
- Left-dominant in ~70% of left-handers; bilateral or right-dominant in the remainder
- Preoperative fMRI language mapping and/or Wada testing is recommended when a lesion is near the inferior frontal gyrus, regardless of handedness

## Cortico-Subcortical Connectivity

Broca is the cortical terminus of the **ventral and dorsal language streams**:

- **Arcuate fasciculus** — dorsal stream; connects Broca ↔ [[wernicke-area]] directly
- **Superior longitudinal fasciculus III** — dorsal stream; articulatory loop
- **Frontal aslant tract** — connects Broca to pre-SMA; speech initiation
- **Uncinate fasciculus** — ventral stream; semantic retrieval
- **Inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus (IFOF)** — ventral stream; semantic processing

Subcortical injury to the arcuate or frontal aslant produces lasting language deficits even if cortical Broca is spared — a central principle of modern awake mapping.

## Function

| Component | Deficit if Injured |
|-----------|--------------------|
| BA 44 (pars opercularis) | Phonologic sequencing, articulatory planning |
| BA 45 (pars triangularis) | Syntactic processing, verb retrieval |
| Pre-motor ventral cortex | Speech apraxia, anarthria |

Classical **Broca aphasia** (non-fluent, effortful, agrammatic, with relatively preserved comprehension) usually requires injury that extends beyond BA 44/45 alone — into the insula, underlying white matter, or basal ganglia.

## Surgical Relevance

### Approaches Near Broca

- **[[pterional-approach]]**: the craniotomy passes directly over the inferior frontal gyrus; retraction of the pars opercularis/triangularis during [[sylvian-fissure]] splitting risks transient expressive aphasia
- **Sylvian approaches to MCA bifurcation**: dissection along the anterior sylvian fissure abuts Broca — [[mca-aneurysm]] surgery in the dominant hemisphere requires minimal frontal retraction
- **Insular or opercular gliomas**: awake craniotomy with intraoperative cortical stimulation is standard of care
- **Anterior temporal lobectomy** for epilepsy: the dominant-side resection must respect a 4.5–5 cm anterior-limit rule to avoid Wernicke; Broca is distant but its arcuate fibers pass through the temporal stem

### Intraoperative Mapping

- **Bipolar cortical stimulation** at 2–6 mA (60 Hz, 4 s trains) during an object-naming task identifies speech-arrest sites
- **Subcortical stimulation** during white-matter resection protects the arcuate fasciculus and IFOF
- Positive sites are preserved with a 1 cm margin; resection is halted when persistent anomia or speech arrest appears

### Postoperative Considerations

- Transient expressive aphasia after dominant [[pterional-approach]] craniotomy (even without resection) — attributable to retraction injury — typically resolves within days to weeks
- Permanent Broca-type deficits require either resection of subcortical arcuate fibers or large cortical-insular injury
