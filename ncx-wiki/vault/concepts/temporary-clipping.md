# Temporary Clipping

Temporary clips are applied to afferent and efferent arteries during [[cerebrovascular-aneurysms-overview|aneurysm surgery]] to soften the aneurysm sac, enable aggressive manipulation, and facilitate the final dissection before [[aneurysm-clipping-techniques|permanent clipping]]. While occasionally used to control [[intraoperative-rupture]], their primary role is completing the orderly dissection sequence: controlling afferent arteries, finding efferent arteries, and dissecting the neck.

## Final Dissection

Aneurysm dissection proceeds through visible anatomy first; the final dissection addresses **surgical blind spots** -- areas concealed by the aneurysm dome. Seeing into a blind spot typically requires mobilizing the aneurysm. A turgid, pulsatile aneurysm moves only with great force, whereas a softened aneurysm moves easily. Temporary clipping enables the neurosurgeon to:

- Manipulate the aneurysm aggressively and visualize hidden anatomy behind the dome
- Peel efferent arteries stuck to the aneurysm side wall where tearing into a thin wall is possible
- Develop cleavage planes around delicate [[perforating-arteries|perforators]] adherent to the back of the aneurysm
- Pull the aneurysm away from adherent perforators under gentle traction, relieving deflected perforators rather than distorting them further

These risky maneuvers are deliberately saved for the final dissection phase. Aneurysm traction widens the plane and adhesions are cut under tension.

## Extent of Temporary Clipping

The extent of temporary clipping is individualized according to afferent artery anatomy and the degree of softening needed:

**Single proximal clip sufficient:**
- [[mca-aneurysm|MCA aneurysms]] -- only one afferent artery, soften dramatically with a single clip on the [[middle-cerebral-artery|M1 segment]]
- [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm|Basilar bifurcation aneurysms]] -- single clip on the [[basilar-artery|basilar trunk]]
- Aneurysms with contrast jetting on preoperative angiography

**Multiple clips required (backfilling):**
- [[ophthalmic-artery-aneurysm|Ophthalmic artery aneurysms]] -- can backfill from the [[posterior-communicating-artery|PCoA]] or [[ophthalmic-artery|OphA]] despite cervical [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]] occlusion
- [[acoa-aneurysm|ACoA aneurysms]] -- cross-fill from contralateral [[anterior-cerebral-artery|A1 segment]] despite ipsilateral A1 occlusion
- [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm|Basilar bifurcation aneurysms]] -- can fill from contralateral PCoA despite basilar trunk occlusion

**Trapping (proximal + distal clips):**
Trapping arrests all flow and is necessary when final dissection requires deliberately opening an aneurysm:
- Thrombotic aneurysms requiring thrombectomy to debulk mass and clip the neck
- Coiled aneurysms requiring coil removal or mobilization
- Giant aneurysms requiring suction decompression

Suction decompression collapses the aneurysm through an afferent artery outside the cranial field (e.g., cervical ICA for an [[ophthalmic-artery-aneurysm]]), through dome puncture, or endovascularly via balloon catheter. It requires complete trapping to prevent re-expansion and a soft, collapsible aneurysm wall -- elderly patients with atherosclerotic aneurysms may not be amenable.

## Neurosurgeon Efficiency

Temporary clipping carries disadvantages: the clip consumes precious space, interrupts blood flow causing potential brain ischemia, and triggers changes in [[somatosensory-evoked-potentials|somatosensory]] or [[motor-evoked-potentials|motor evoked potentials]]. There is a direct relationship between dramatic aneurysm softening and the onset of ischemic signs.

Temporary clipping adds time pressure and stress. Cerebral protection with barbiturates and raising blood pressure extends patient tolerance, but **neurosurgeon speed is most important**:

- Technical steps during final dissection must be clear beforehand
- Contingency plans reviewed in advance
- Instruments and permanent clips preselected
- Preparation translates into surgical efficiency

The precious few minutes after temporary clips are applied are the **crux of the operation** -- exposure is optimized, the aneurysm is slack, risky moves must be made, and the outcome is determined. [[Intraoperative-rupture|Intraoperative rupture]] may be the biggest fear, but [[perforating-arteries|perforator]] infarcts have no solution. The key to becoming aggressive with aneurysms is the temporary clip: it pressures the surgeon to complete the task, but signals the right time to battle the aneurysm.
