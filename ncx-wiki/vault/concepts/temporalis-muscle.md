# Temporalis Muscle

The temporalis is a fan-shaped muscle of mastication occupying the temporal fossa. It originates from the temporal fossa (superior temporal line, squamous temporal bone) and inserts onto the coronoid process of the mandible. Its fascial coverings and neurovascular supply are central to pterional-type craniotomies.

## Anatomy

| Layer | Structure |
|-------|-----------|
| Superficial | Skin, subcutaneous fat, superficial temporal fascia (SMAS continuation) |
| Intermediate | Deep temporal fascia — splits into superficial and deep laminae around the superficial temporal fat pad at the level of the [[zygomatic-arch|zygomatic arch]] |
| Deep | Temporalis muscle proper |
| Deepest | Pericranium over squamous temporal bone |

### Neurovascular Supply

- **Motor**: deep temporal nerves (anterior and posterior) from V3 (mandibular nerve). Enter the muscle on its deep surface near the infratemporal crest.
- **Vascular**: deep temporal arteries (branches of maxillary artery) + middle temporal artery (superficial temporal artery). Arterial supply enters primarily from the deep surface.
- **Sensory of overlying skin**: auriculotemporal nerve (V3), zygomaticotemporal nerve (V2).

## Surgical Relevance

### Pterional Craniotomy
The temporalis must be reflected to expose the squamous temporal bone and frontal process underneath. Two techniques for fascial dissection:

| Technique | Plane | Protects |
|-----------|-------|----------|
| **Subfascial** | Deep to superficial layer of deep temporal fascia | Temporal branch of facial nerve |
| **Interfascial** | Between superficial and deep laminae of deep temporal fascia | Same; preferred when broader exposure needed |
| **Subperiosteal** (myocutaneous flap) | Deep to pericranium and muscle | Simpler but risks frontotemporal branch injury |

The muscle is then retracted **inferiorly and anteriorly** (not just inferiorly) to expose the pterion and sphenoid ridge. Inadequate anterior retraction leaves the sphenoid wing obscured.

### Reconstruction and Cosmesis

Post-operative temporal hollowing is the most common cosmetic complaint after pterional / frontotemporal craniotomies. Causes:

- **Denervation atrophy** — deep temporal nerve injury during dissection (avoid electrocautery on deep surface of muscle)
- **Muscle shortening** — tight reattachment to the superior temporal line shortens the fiber length
- **Pericranial scar retraction**

Mitigation: subperiosteal dissection to preserve pedicle, meticulous reattachment at original superior temporal line insertion, and interposition grafts (pericranial / fat) in high-risk cases.

### Temporalis Flap for Reconstruction
Pedicled on the deep temporal vessels, the temporalis can be rotated for skull-base reconstruction (e.g., after endoscopic endonasal resection with large dural defect, or for orbital/maxillofacial reconstruction). The arc of rotation reaches the anterior cranial fossa, clivus, and orbit.
