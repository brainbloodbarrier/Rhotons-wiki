# Interhemispheric Fissure

The interhemispheric fissure is the median sagittal cleft separating the two cerebral hemispheres. Its upper portion is occupied by the falx cerebri and the superior sagittal sinus; the lower portion forms the pericallosal cistern, containing the pericallosal and callosomarginal arteries.

## Walls and Contents

| Structure | Relation |
|-----------|----------|
| **Falx cerebri** | Median dural septum; free edge hangs above corpus callosum |
| **Superior sagittal sinus** | Dorsal edge of falx |
| **Inferior sagittal sinus** | Free edge of falx |
| **Corpus callosum** | Floor (genu, body, splenium) |
| **Cingulate gyri** | Medial hemispheric walls |
| **Pericallosal cistern** | Subarachnoid space above corpus callosum |
| **ACA distal branches** | A3–A5, callosomarginal |
| **Bridging veins → SSS** | Frontal ascending, rolandic, parietal |

## Surgical Corridor

The fissure is the primary corridor for midline supratentorial targets:

- **Anterior** (frontal): ACoA region, pericallosal aneurysms, 3rd-ventricle via transcallosal route, callosal tumors.
- **Middle** (rolandic–parietal): parasagittal / falx meningiomas, medial rolandic cortex lesions.
- **Posterior**: pineal region via interhemispheric-transcallosal / occipital-transtentorial variants.

See [[anterior-interhemispheric-approach]] for the operative technique.

## Key Surgical Principles

1. **Unilateral craniotomy** with medial edge to midline — never cross the superior sagittal sinus.
2. **Preserve bridging veins**, especially the dominant posterior-third drainage to SSS.
3. **Gravity retraction**: lateral decubitus positions the dependent hemisphere to fall away from the falx, opening the fissure without brain retractors.
4. **Corpus callosum orientation**: the pericallosal arteries are the key landmark — identify them before entering the callosum for transcallosal procedures.
