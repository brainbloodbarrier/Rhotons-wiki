# Intracranial Pressure

Intracranial pressure (ICP) is the pressure within the rigid cranial vault, normally 5-15 mmHg in adults. Elevated ICP is a neurosurgical emergency that can lead to [[herniation-syndromes]] and death if untreated.

## Monro-Kellie Doctrine

The skull is a rigid container with fixed volume composed of three compartments:
- **Brain parenchyma** (~80%)
- **Blood** (arterial + venous, ~10%)
- **CSF** (~10%)

An increase in volume of any one compartment must be compensated by a decrease in another, or ICP rises. Initial compensation occurs through CSF displacement into the spinal canal and venous blood drainage. Once compensatory mechanisms are exhausted, small volume increases cause exponential ICP elevation (pressure-volume curve).

## Normal Values

| Age | Normal ICP |
|-----|-----------|
| Adults (supine) | 5-15 mmHg |
| Children | 3-7 mmHg |
| Infants | 1.5-6 mmHg |

**Cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP):** CPP = MAP - ICP. Target CPP >60 mmHg in adults (some guidelines: 60-70 mmHg).

## Causes of Elevated ICP

| Mechanism | Examples |
|-----------|---------|
| Mass lesion | Tumor, hematoma (EDH, SDH, ICH), abscess |
| Cerebral edema | Vasogenic (tumors), cytotoxic (ischemia), interstitial (hydrocephalus) |
| Increased CSF | [[hydrocephalus]] (obstructive or communicating) |
| Increased blood volume | Venous outflow obstruction, hyperemia, venous sinus thrombosis |
| Diffuse brain swelling | TBI, malignant MCA infarct, fulminant hepatic failure |

## Clinical Signs

- Headache (worse in morning, with cough/strain)
- Nausea/vomiting (often projectile)
- Papilledema (takes hours to develop)
- CN VI palsy (false localizing sign)
- Cushing's triad (hypertension, bradycardia, irregular respirations) — late, ominous sign
- Declining [[glasgow-coma-scale\|GCS]]
- Pupil changes (see [[herniation-syndromes]])

## ICP Monitoring

### Indications (TBI guidelines)

- GCS ≤ 8 after resuscitation with abnormal CT
- GCS ≤ 8 with normal CT if ≥2 of: age >40, unilateral/bilateral posturing, SBP <90

### Methods

| Device | Location | Accuracy | Can drain CSF |
|--------|----------|----------|---------------|
| EVD (external ventricular drain) | Lateral ventricle | Gold standard | Yes |
| Intraparenchymal (e.g., Codman) | Brain parenchyma | Good (no re-zero) | No |
| Subdural bolt | Subdural space | Less accurate | No |
| Epidural | Epidural space | Least accurate | No |

### ICP Waveforms (Lundberg Waves)

| Wave | Amplitude | Duration | Significance |
|------|-----------|----------|-------------|
| A (plateau waves) | 50-100 mmHg | 5-20 min | Pathological — exhausted compliance, impending herniation |
| B waves | Up to 50 mmHg | 0.5-2 min | May indicate decreased compliance |
| C waves | Up to 20 mmHg | 4-8/min | Normal, synchronous with arterial waveform |

## Treatment Algorithm (Tiered Approach)

### Tier 0 — General Measures
- Head of bed elevation 30°
- Head midline (avoid jugular venous compression)
- Adequate sedation and analgesia
- Normothermia (treat fever aggressively)
- Normocarbia (PaCO2 35-40 mmHg)
- Avoid hyponatremia and hypotension

### Tier 1 — First-line
- CSF drainage via EVD (if in place)
- Osmotherapy: mannitol 0.25-1 g/kg IV bolus (maintain serum osmolality <320 mOsm/L) or hypertonic saline (23.4% NaCl 30 mL via central line, or 3% NaCl infusion)
- Brief hyperventilation (PaCO2 30-35 mmHg) — temporizing only ^[inferred]

### Tier 2 — Second-line
- Moderate hypothermia (32-34°C)
- Barbiturate coma (pentobarbital: loading 10 mg/kg over 30 min, then 5 mg/kg/hr × 3 hrs, maintenance 1-3 mg/kg/hr — titrate to burst suppression on EEG)
- [[decompressive-craniectomy]] — for refractory elevation

### Tier 3 — Salvage
- Decompressive craniectomy if not yet performed
- Consider futility

## CPP Management

| Target | Note |
|--------|------|
| CPP >60 mmHg | Minimum to prevent ischemia |
| CPP 60-70 mmHg | Optimal range (BTF guidelines) |
| CPP >70 mmHg | Risk of ARDS with aggressive vasopressor use |
