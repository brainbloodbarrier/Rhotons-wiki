# Zygomatic Arch

The zygomatic arch is the bony bridge forming the lateral contour of the orbit and midface. It is composed of:

- **Anterior**: temporal process of the zygomatic bone
- **Posterior**: zygomatic process of the temporal bone

The two meet at the **zygomaticotemporal suture**, angled obliquely inferolaterally.

## Relations

| Relation | Structure |
|----------|-----------|
| Superior (cranial) | Temporalis muscle belly; deep temporal fascia |
| Inferior (caudal) | Infratemporal fossa, masseter attachment |
| Medial (deep) | Coronoid process of mandible (free space), temporalis tendon |
| Lateral (subcutaneous) | Skin, SMAS, auriculotemporal nerve branches |

The **temporal branch of the facial nerve** crosses the zygomatic arch obliquely — typically within the middle third, between a line from tragus to lateral canthus and tragus to lateral brow. Injury produces brow ptosis.

## Surgical Relevance

### Pterional Craniotomy
Standard pterional exposure **does not** require arch resection. The arch is preserved; the superior temporal line serves as the craniotomy's superior limit. However, the skin flap is reflected in a **subfascial** or **interfascial** plane below the superficial layer of the deep temporal fascia to protect the frontotemporal branch of the facial nerve.

### Orbitozygomatic Craniotomy (OZ)
The OZ extends the pterional by removing the zygomatic arch + lateral/superior orbital rim as an orbitozygomatic bone flap. Benefits:
- Downward mobilization of the temporalis → wider sub-temporal exposure
- Increased working angle toward the [[basal-cisterns]] (interpeduncular, crural, ambient)
- Particularly useful for basilar apex aneurysms, cavernous sinus tumors, and upper-third clival lesions

### Subtemporal / Zygomatic Osteotomy
Isolated zygomatic osteotomy (without full OZ) flattens the trajectory for middle-fossa approaches. The arch is divided at zygomaticomaxillary suture (anterior) and just anterior to the zygomaticotemporal suture (posterior), reflected downward with attached masseter.

## Reconstruction

The arch is replated with titanium miniplates at the original osteotomy sites. Malunion produces cosmetic deformity (cheek flattening) and trismus (coronoid-arch mechanical impingement).
