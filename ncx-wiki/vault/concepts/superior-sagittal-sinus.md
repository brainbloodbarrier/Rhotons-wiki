# Superior Sagittal Sinus (SSS)

The superior sagittal sinus is the largest unpaired dural venous sinus. It runs in the convex superior attached edge of the falx cerebri, from the foramen cecum / crista galli anteriorly to the torcular Herophili (confluence of sinuses) posteriorly.

## Course and Morphology

| Segment | Course | Clinical note |
|---------|--------|---------------|
| Anterior third | Crista galli → coronal suture | Usually small; may be sacrificed with acceptable risk |
| Middle third | Coronal → lambdoid | Dominant segment; sacrifice risks bilateral venous infarction |
| Posterior third | Lambdoid → torcular | Highest bridging-vein density; sacrifice contraindicated |

Triangular in cross-section, with arachnoid granulations (Pacchionian bodies) protruding into lacunae laterally. The sinus groove on the inner table of the calvaria is a key craniotomy landmark.

## Tributaries

Cortical bridging veins drain into the SSS at roughly orthogonal angles. The **vein of Trolard** (superior anastomotic vein) is the dominant cortical tributary, connecting the SSS to the Sylvian venous system. Bridging veins are tethered over a short subdural span — the classical substrate for traumatic acute subdural hematoma.

### Bridging Vein Danger Zone
The region 3–4 cm lateral to midline, from coronal suture to parietal area, contains the highest density of large bridging veins. Craniotomies approaching the [[interhemispheric-fissure]] must skeletonize these veins carefully.

## Surgical Relevance

### Interhemispheric Approaches
The [[anterior-interhemispheric-approach]] requires crossing a para-sagittal corridor between SSS bridging veins. Key steps:
- Craniotomy flap placed **unilaterally** (typically right, non-dominant) with medial edge up to midline — avoid crossing SSS.
- Dural opening with base toward SSS; reflect laterally.
- Preserve every identifiable bridging vein in the middle third of the SSS.

### Parasagittal Meningioma
Meningiomas arising from the wall of the SSS are classified by Sindou grade (I–VI) based on sinus invasion. Surgical strategy depends on:
- Sinus patency (MR venography / DSA)
- Segment affected (anterior third > middle > posterior in sacrifice tolerance)
- Collateral drainage pathways

### Torcular and Posterior Third
Never sacrifice the SSS posterior to the rolandic vein entry zone. Loss of posterior SSS flow → bilateral parieto-occipital venous infarction with cortical blindness, hemorrhagic transformation, and malignant edema.

## Venous Sinus Thrombosis

SSS thrombosis is the most common intracranial venous thrombosis pattern. Presents with headache, papilledema, seizures, motor deficits (often bilateral or alternating). Imaging: **empty delta sign** on post-contrast CT; T2*/SWI hypointensity. Anticoagulation is first-line despite hemorrhagic presentation.
