# Aneurysm (Intracranial)

An intracranial aneurysm is a focal dilatation of a cerebral artery at a defect in the arterial wall (loss of internal elastic lamina and attenuation of media). Rupture produces subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH), which carries high early mortality and morbidity.

## Classification

### Morphology

| Type | Features |
|------|----------|
| **Saccular ("berry")** | Most common. Distinct neck and dome; arises at arterial bifurcations. |
| **Fusiform** | Circumferential dilatation without distinct neck; dolichoectatic variants. |
| **Dissecting** | Intimal tear with intramural hematoma; common in VA/PICA, basilar. |
| **Blister** | Small, fragile, wide-neck; ICA dorsal wall predominant. Very high rupture-during-surgery risk. |
| **Mycotic (infectious)** | Distal, often MCA M3–M4, from septic emboli (endocarditis). |
| **Traumatic** | Pseudoaneurysms after penetrating or blunt trauma with vessel injury. |
| **Oncotic** | Rare, from tumor invasion (choriocarcinoma, myxoma). |

### Size
- Small: < 7 mm
- Medium: 7–12 mm
- Large: 13–24 mm
- Giant: ≥ 25 mm

### Location (most common)

| Location | Frequency |
|----------|-----------|
| [[acoa-aneurysm\|Anterior communicating (ACoA)]] | ~30 % |
| Posterior communicating (PCoA) | ~25 % |
| MCA bifurcation | ~20 % |
| ICA (ophthalmic, carotid cave, ICA bifurcation) | ~10 % |
| Basilar apex | ~5–7 % |
| [[posterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery\|PICA]] / VA | ~3 % |
| [[pericallosal-artery\|Pericallosal]] / distal ACA | ~4 % |

## Presentation

- **Ruptured** (SAH): thunderclap headache, Hunt-Hess grade I–V, Fisher / modified Fisher radiographic grade.
- **Unruptured**: incidental imaging finding, mass effect (CN III palsy from PCoA aneurysm), TIA from thromboembolism.
- **Sentinel headache**: warning leak in 10–40 % of ruptured cases, preceding major rupture by days to weeks.

## Rupture Risk (ISUIA, UCAS)

Predictors:
- Size (> 7 mm, especially > 10 mm)
- Location (posterior circulation, PCoA > anterior)
- Morphology (daughter sac, irregular shape, aspect ratio > 1.6)
- Prior SAH from another aneurysm
- Family history, smoking, hypertension

## Treatment

### Surgical Clipping
Gold-standard durable occlusion. Approaches depend on location:
- **Pterional**: anterior circulation (ACoA, MCA, PCoA, ICA bifurcation) — open [[carotid-cistern]] and lateral Sylvian
- **[[anterior-interhemispheric-approach]]**: pericallosal (via [[interhemispheric-fissure]])
- **Orbitozygomatic**: basilar apex, high-riding ICA
- **Far-lateral / suboccipital**: VA, PICA, vertebrobasilar junction

See [[aneurysm-clipping-techniques]] for technical detail.

### Endovascular
- Coil embolization (detachable platinum coils)
- Stent-assisted coiling (wide-neck)
- Flow diversion (Pipeline, PED) — for large/giant ICA, fusiform
- Intrasaccular flow disruption (WEB) — bifurcation aneurysms

### Bypass + Trapping
For fusiform/giant/blister aneurysms not amenable to clip or coil: parent vessel sacrifice after revascularization. See [[reimplantation-bypass]], [[in-situ-bypass]], and [[microsurgical-anastomosis]].
