# Medulla Oblongata

The medulla oblongata is the caudal-most brainstem segment, extending from the ponto-medullary sulcus to the pyramidal decussation at the foramen magnum, where it becomes the spinal cord.

## Gross Anatomy

### Anterior Surface
- **Pyramids**: longitudinal ridges containing corticospinal tract fibers
- **Pyramidal decussation**: ~85 % of fibers cross at the cervicomedullary junction
- **Olive (inferior olivary nucleus)**: lateral prominence; anterolateral sulcus separates from pyramid

### Posterior Surface
- Upper half: floor of the fourth ventricle (rhomboid fossa)
- Lower half: posterior columns (gracile + cuneate fasciculi) ending in the gracile and cuneate tubercles → decussation of the medial lemniscus
- Obex: caudal-most point of the 4th ventricle

### Cranial Nerve Emergence

| Nerve | Exit | Location |
|-------|------|----------|
| **CN IX** (glossopharyngeal) | Postolivary sulcus (upper) | Exits via jugular foramen |
| **CN X** (vagus) | Postolivary sulcus (middle) | Exits via jugular foramen |
| **CN XI** (accessory) cranial roots | Postolivary sulcus (lower) | Joins spinal roots, exits via jugular foramen |
| **CN XII** (hypoglossal) | Preolivary sulcus (between pyramid and olive) | Exits via hypoglossal canal |

## Key Nuclei and Tracts

- **Nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS)**: taste, visceral afferent
- **Nucleus ambiguus**: motor IX, X, XI (cranial) — palate, pharynx, larynx
- **Dorsal motor nucleus of vagus**: parasympathetic to thoracic/abdominal viscera
- **Hypoglossal nucleus**: paramedian, tongue innervation
- **Inferior olivary complex**: climbing fibers to cerebellum
- **Pyramidal tract (corticospinal)**: decussates at cervicomedullary junction
- **Medial lemniscus**: posterior-column DCML pathway; ascends dorsomedially after decussation
- **Spinothalamic tract**: ascends laterally (already decussated)
- **Spinal trigeminal tract and nucleus**: pain/temperature from face, descends to upper cervical levels
- **Reticular formation**: respiratory (dorsal + ventral groups), cardiovascular, reticular activating system (caudal portion)

## Vascular Supply

| Zone | Supply |
|------|--------|
| Medial (pyramids, hypoglossal, medial lemniscus) | Anterior spinal artery (from VA) |
| Lateral (spinal trigeminal, spinothalamic, nucleus ambiguus, inf cerebellar peduncle) | [[posterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery\|PICA]] branches, vertebral artery perforators |
| Dorsal (nuclei of 4th ventricle floor) | PICA, posterior spinal artery |

## Clinical Syndromes

### Lateral Medullary Syndrome (Wallenberg)
[[posterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery|PICA]] occlusion (often VA dissection):
- Ipsilateral: Horner, facial hemianesthesia (V spinal), ataxia, bulbar (IX/X) weakness, nystagmus
- Contralateral: body hemianesthesia (spinothalamic)

### Medial Medullary Syndrome (Dejerine)
Anterior spinal artery / VA perforator occlusion:
- Contralateral hemiparesis (pyramid), contralateral deep-sensation loss (medial lemniscus), ipsilateral tongue deviation (CN XII)

## Surgical Relevance

### Far-Lateral / Transcondylar Approach
Standard approach to the foramen-magnum region, ventral to the medulla: targets VA-PICA aneurysms, foramen-magnum meningioma, craniocervical chordoma. Requires partial occipital condyle drilling for medial exposure.

### Retrosigmoid Approach
Lateral medulla, CN IX-XI root entry zone access for microvascular decompression (MVD).

### Medullary Safe Entry Zones
Brainstem cavernoma surgery uses defined safe entry zones: postolivary sulcus (lateral medulla), median sulcus (dorsal), supra-olivary fossette.
