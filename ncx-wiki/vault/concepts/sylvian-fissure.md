# Sylvian Fissure

The Sylvian fissure (lateral sulcus) is the deepest and most prominent sulcus of the cerebral hemisphere. It separates the frontal and parietal lobes superiorly from the temporal lobe inferiorly, and conceals the insular cortex and the middle cerebral artery (MCA) within its depth.

## Divisions

| Segment | Course | Contents |
|---------|--------|----------|
| **Stem** | Basal surface → lateral surface | Anterior temporal artery, limen insulae |
| **Anterior ramus** | Turns anteriorly into frontal lobe | Pars triangularis landmark |
| **Ascending ramus** | Turns superiorly into frontal lobe | Pars opercularis landmark |
| **Posterior ramus** | Continues posteriorly and superiorly | MCA branches, insular surface |

The anterior and ascending rami bracket the **pars triangularis** of the inferior frontal gyrus (Broca's area on the dominant hemisphere).

## Surgical Anatomy

### Sylvian Cistern
The arachnoid space within the Sylvian fissure contains:
- **MCA and branches** — M1 (sphenoid), M2 (insular), M3 (opercular), M4 (cortical)
- **Sylvian veins** — superficial middle cerebral vein draining to cavernous sinus or sphenoparietal sinus
- **Bridging veins** connecting to superior sagittal sinus

### Insular Cortex (within the fissure)
The insula is exposed by splitting the Sylvian fissure. It is bounded by the circular sulcus and divided into anterior short gyri and posterior long gyri by the central sulcus of the insula.

### Opercula (covering the insula)
| Operculum | Gyri |
|-----------|------|
| Frontal | Pars orbitalis, triangularis, opercularis, precentral gyrus |
| Parietal | Postcentral and supramarginal gyri |
| Temporal | Superior temporal gyrus (Heschl's gyrus — primary auditory cortex) |

## Transsylvian Approach

Splitting the Sylvian fissure is the fundamental technique for accessing:
- [[mca-aneurysm\|MCA aneurysms]] — bifurcation aneurysms
- [[pcoa-aneurysm\|PCoA]] and [[acoa-aneurysm\|ACoA]] aneurysms (via [[pterional-approach]])
- Insular tumors and deep temporal pathology
- Mesial temporal structures (hippocampus, amygdala)

### Technique (Chaddad-Neto)
1. Begin dissection at the Sylvian point (where fissure meets the sphenoid bone convexity)
2. Identify the superficial Sylvian vein — preserve if possible
3. Open arachnoid sharply between frontal and temporal opercula
4. Dissect toward the limen insulae following the MCA branches
5. Avoid monopolar cautery near the insular cortex ^[inferred]

### Key Landmarks
- **Sylvian point** (angiographic) — most lateral extent of MCA on AP view, marks the lateral surface of the insula
- **Limen insulae** — threshold of the insula at the junction of stem and anterior ramus
- **Inferior frontal gyrus** — pars triangularis between anterior and ascending rami (Broca's area on left)

## Clinical Significance

- **MCA territory infarct:** edema and swelling along the Sylvian fissure; effacement is a sign of mass effect
- **Arachnoid cysts:** Sylvian fissure is the most common location for intracranial arachnoid cysts
- **Insular gliomas:** require Sylvian fissure splitting for surgical access
