# Bypass Arteriotomy

Arteriotomies initiate new connections in [[microsurgical-anastomosis|bypass construction]] by transforming closed cylindrical tubes into open geometries. Three basic cuts -- **incision**, **excision**, and **transection** -- produce openings in five shapes (lines, circles, ovals, quadrilaterals, crescents) across seven arteriotomy types used in [[cerebral-revascularization-overview|bypass surgery]].

## Arteriotomy Types

| Arteriotomy | Cut Type | Opening Shape | Location | Anastomoses | Circumference |
|-------------|----------|---------------|----------|-------------|---------------|
| Linear incision | Incision | Line | Sidewall | E-S, S-S | 6d |
| Perpendicular transection | Transection | Circle | End | E-S, E-E | pi*d |
| Oblique transection | Transection | Oval | End | E-S, E-E | pi*d/sqrt(2) |
| Fish-mouth | Transection + incision | Quadrilateral | End + sidewall | E-S, E-E | 2*pi*d |
| Excision | Excision | Oval | Sidewall | E-S, S-S | 6d |
| Transection bifurcotomy | Transection | Oval | Bifurcation | E-S, E-E | 6d |
| Incision bifurcotomy | Incision | Crescent | Bifurcation | E-S | 6d |

## Linear Incision Arteriotomy

The simplest and most common arteriotomy. A longitudinal sidewall incision paralleling the artery's axis, used on intracranial recipients in end-to-side and side-to-side [[microsurgical-anastomosis|anastomoses]]. Length: **3d** (three times diameter). Initiated with a beveled 27-gauge needle at 45 degrees, extended with right-angled microscissors.

Conserves arterial wall and produces straight suture lines. Ideal for thin-walled cerebral arteries easily splayed by flow, but may not open widely in thick arteries like the [[external-carotid-artery|ECA]] or [[common-carotid-artery|CCA]].

## Fish-Mouth Arteriotomy

Optimizes anastomotic area. Combines a **60-degree oblique transection** with a **longitudinal linear incision** equal to the transection length. Fish-mouthing **doubles the circumference** and **quadruples the area** compared to perpendicular transection, forming a kite-shaped opening.

Widely used in EC-IC end-to-side anastomoses ([[superficial-temporal-artery|STA]]-MCA bypass), IC-IC reimplantations, and [[microsurgical-anastomosis#End-to-End Anastomosis|reanastomoses]]. The matching recipient arteriotomy must be 3d.

## Perpendicular Transection Arteriotomy

Used in interpositional bypasses where an oversized [[radial-artery-graft|RAG]] or [[saphenous-vein-graft|SVG]] meets a smaller recipient. The circular cross-section **minimizes** anastomotic area, counteracting caliber mismatch. Creates a T-shaped junction; graft diameter is kept to no more than twice the recipient's to avoid thrombosis. Also used on cervical arteries ([[external-carotid-artery|ECA]], [[internal-carotid-artery|ICA]]) for end-to-end proximal anastomoses.

## Oblique Transection Arteriotomy

Enlarges the area above perpendicular transection but less than fish-mouthing. The **smaller artery is sectioned more obliquely** to equalize arteriotomy lengths. The transection creates a heel and toe that must be oriented heel-to-toe for a funnel shape with gradually transitioning caliber.

## Excision Arteriotomy

Removes an elliptical piece of sidewall. On cerebral arteries, tented with microforceps and cut along its base. On cervical arteries ([[external-carotid-artery|ECA]], [[common-carotid-artery|CCA]], V3 [[vertebral-artery|VA]]), an **aortic punch** (4-5 mm) cuts a precise circular hole -- ideal for thick, atherosclerotic walls. Incisional arteriotomies are preferable for small recipients where excision would narrow the lumen.

## Transection Bifurcotomy

Transects a bifurcation across its branches, removing the dividing crotch to create a **funnel-shaped enlargement** wider than the parent artery. Used with the [[superficial-temporal-artery|STA]] bifurcation and branched [[saphenous-vein-graft|SVGs]]. Creates a "patch" anastomosis with a linear incision arteriotomy on the recipient.

## Incision Bifurcotomy

The opposite of transection bifurcotomy: an incision in the **recipient artery's** dividing inner wall extends along both branches and across the branch point, incorporating both into the anastomotic circumference. Used with oversized SVGs when the recipient is small -- delivers flow in line with the parent artery into three arteries at once. The concave suture line closes at its midpoint and is more susceptible to through-stitches.
