# AVM Feeding Arteries

Afferent arteries to cerebral [[vascular-malformation|arteriovenous malformations]] are classified into five functional categories: terminal feeding arteries, transit arteries, perforating arteries, choroidal arteries, and bystander arteries. Correct identification determines whether a vessel can be safely interrupted or must be preserved -- misclassification leads to either incomplete disconnection or downstream infarction.

## Arterial Occlusion Techniques

Arteries are occluded at their point of contact with the AVM nidus. Proximal occlusion sacrifices normal branches and infarcts adjacent brain; distal occlusion risks nidal bleeding or preserves feeding branches. The coagulate-cut technique walks [[microsurgery|bipolar forceps]] proximal-to-distal along a flow-arrested artery, shrinking wall tissues to obliterate the lumen. Arteries and arterioles have thick elastic and smooth muscle layers that coagulate easily. In contrast, small [perforators](../rhoton-wiki/vault/synthesis/perforating-arteries.md) have thin walls that resist cautery and require microclips. At the opposite extreme, large arteries with high flow may require permanent [[aneurysm-clipping-techniques|aneurysm clips]] (typically 5-7 mm straight) for closure.

Irradiated arteries from prior [[radiosurgery]] develop intimal disintegration and medial hyperplasia that paradoxically enhances coagulability -- they are among the most responsive to bipolar cautery. Embolized arteries contain intraluminal casts: NBCA (hard, translucent), Onyx (soft, rubbery), PVA (particulate), or coils (cut with heavy microscissors).

## Terminal Feeding Arteries

The terminal feeding artery is dedicated to the AVM, terminates in the nidus, and has no normal territory to supply distally. It travels in the [[cisterns|subarachnoid space]], often large in caliber, with a single high-flow shunt or multiple peri/intranidal branches. Terminal feeders are interrupted at the AVM margin with cautery or clips without concern for distal flow. They are quick victories -- the welcome targets of early dissection.

## Transit Arteries (En Passage)

The transit artery is treacherous. It is not dedicated to the AVM: proximal branches supply normal territories, AVM branches supply the nidus, and distal branches supply normal territories beyond. Occlusion of the distal trunk causes downstream infarction. The critical technique is **skeletonization**: occluding feeding twigs one-by-one to prune the parent trunk while preserving distal flow. Retrograde dissection (distal-to-proximal) solves the differentiation problem -- the distal trunk is identified past the AVM and traced backward toward the nidus. Branches traceable to the AVM margin are interrupted; those traceable to adjacent cortex are spared. The [[pericallosal-artery-aneurysm|pericallosal arteries]] and insular segments of the [MCA](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/middle-cerebral-artery.md) are classic transit arteries with callosal and sylvian AVMs, respectively.

## Perforating Arteries -- The Red Devils

Perforators are approximately 1 mm in diameter and include medial lenticulostriates from A1 [ACA](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/anterior-cerebral-artery.md), lateral lenticulostriates from M1 [MCA](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/middle-cerebral-artery.md), insular perforators from M2 MCA, thalamoperforators from [PCoA](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/posterior-communicating-artery.md) and P1 [PCA](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/posterior-cerebral-artery.md), and [brainstem perforators](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/brainstem.md). Charlie Wilson called these "red devils": they bleed briskly, resist cautery, course through eloquent [[white-matter|white matter tracts]], and are encountered on the deep plane where visibility is worst. Their thin walls provide little substrate for electrocautery -- microclips are mandatory, preferably before they start bleeding. An uncontrolled perforator can retract into white matter and force pursuit deeper into vital tracts and nuclei.

## Choroidal Feeding Arteries

Choroidal feeders supply the ependymal surface and originate from [AChA](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/anterior-choroidal-artery.md) in the temporal horn, lateral PChA in the atrium (supplying atrial, thalamic, and parieto-occipital AVMs), and medial PChA in the [[ventricles|velum interpositum]]. Anastomotic interconnections between choroidal arteries enable multiple inputs. Their plexal segments swim in [[cerebrospinal-fluid|CSF]], are based in non-eloquent [choroidal fissures](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/choroidal-fissure.md), and only supply [choroid-plexus](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/choroid-plexus.md), making them less threatening than perforators. However, their deep location makes bleeding dangerous -- ventricular filling and outward brain herniation can occur rapidly.

## Bystander Arteries

Uninvolved bystander arteries are normal arteries without AVM supply that course adjacent to the nidus. Their caliber is smaller than feeders, with normal morphology and bifurcations. Distal-to-proximal dissection separates bystanders safely, after which they are swept aside and covered with cottonoids. Insular [[mca-aneurysm|MCA segments]] are bystander arteries with many sylvian AVMs; contralateral [[acoa-aneurysm|ACA vessels]] serve this role with medial frontal AVMs.

## AVM Subtype Supply

Each AVM subtype has a unique arterial fingerprint. Progressive throttling of arterial input during [[avm-resection-principles|circumdissection]] de-pressurizes, quiets, and softens the AVM, reducing it to something resembling a [[cavernoma|cavernous malformation]]. The feeding artery combinations for all seven AVM types are detailed in the [[cerebrovascular-avms-overview|AVM overview]].

| Parent Artery | Key Branches |
|---|---|

| [ACA](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/anterior-cerebral-artery.md) | mLSA, RAH, OrbFrA, FrPolA, CmaA, PcaA |
| [MCA](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/middle-cerebral-artery.md) | lLSA, InsP, TempPolA, ATA, cortical M4 branches |

| Vertebrobasilar | SCA, AICA, [[pica-aneurysm\|PICA]] |
