# Anticoagulation in Neurosurgery

Management of anticoagulation is critical in neurosurgery — both for emergency reversal (intracranial hemorrhage) and perioperative planning. The consequences of bleeding in the CNS are disproportionately severe.

## Emergency Reversal Protocols

### Warfarin (Vitamin K Antagonist)

| INR | Action |
|-----|--------|
| Supratherapeutic, no bleeding | Hold warfarin, oral vitamin K 2.5-5 mg |
| Active bleeding or urgent surgery | IV vitamin K 10 mg + **4-factor PCC** (Kcentra) 25-50 units/kg |
| PCC unavailable | FFP 10-15 mL/kg (slower, volume overload risk) |

**Target:** INR ≤1.4 before neurosurgical procedure. Recheck INR 15-30 minutes after PCC.

### Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs)

| Agent | Target | Reversal |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Dabigatran (Pradaxa) | Thrombin | **Idarucizumab** (Praxbind) 5g IV |
| Rivaroxaban (Xarelto) | Factor Xa | **Andexanet alfa** (Andexxa) or 4-factor PCC 50 units/kg |
| Apixaban (Eliquis) | Factor Xa | **Andexanet alfa** or 4-factor PCC 50 units/kg |
| Edoxaban (Savaysa) | Factor Xa | 4-factor PCC 50 units/kg |

**Note:** If specific reversal agent unavailable, activated PCC (FEIBA) or 4-factor PCC as alternative.

### Heparin

| Agent | Reversal | Dose |
|-------|---------|------|
| Unfractionated heparin (UFH) | Protamine sulfate | 1 mg per 100 units heparin (max 50 mg) |
| LMWH (enoxaparin) | Protamine (partial reversal) | 1 mg per 1 mg enoxaparin (within 8 hrs) |

### Antiplatelet Agents

| Agent | Mechanism | Reversal |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Aspirin | COX-1 inhibitor (irreversible) | Platelet transfusion (controversial) + DDAVP 0.3 mcg/kg |
| Clopidogrel (Plavix) | P2Y12 inhibitor (irreversible) | Platelet transfusion + DDAVP |
| Ticagrelor (Brilinta) | P2Y12 inhibitor (reversible) | Wait for clearance (~48h), platelets less effective |
| Prasugrel (Effient) | P2Y12 inhibitor (irreversible) | Platelet transfusion |

**Platelet transfusion note:** efficacy in aspirin/clopidogrel reversal for neurosurgical emergencies is debated. Some studies suggest no clear benefit for ICH outcomes. DDAVP may improve platelet function.

## Perioperative Management

### Pre-operative

| Agent | Hold before surgery |
|-------|-------------------|
| Warfarin | 5 days (target INR ≤1.4) |
| Rivaroxaban/Apixaban | 48-72 hours (24-48h if CrCl >50) |
| Dabigatran | 48-72 hours (72-96h if CrCl <50) |
| Clopidogrel | 5-7 days |
| Aspirin | 7 days (may continue for some spine/cranial procedures) |
| LMWH (therapeutic) | 24 hours |

### Bridging Anticoagulation
- Consider for high-risk patients (mechanical heart valve, recent DVT/PE)
- Use UFH or LMWH during warfarin interruption
- Stop LMWH 24h before surgery, UFH 4-6h before
- **NOT recommended for DOACs** — short half-life makes bridging unnecessary

### Post-operative DVT Prophylaxis

**Timing of pharmacologic prophylaxis:**
- Craniotomy: typically 24-48 hours post-op (assess hemostasis on post-op CT)
- Spine: 24-48 hours post-op
- **Mechanical prophylaxis (SCDs/IPC) from admission** — no contraindication

| Agent | Dose | Start |
|-------|------|-------|
| Enoxaparin | 40 mg SQ daily | 24-48h post-op |
| UFH | 5000 units SQ q8-12h | 24-48h post-op |

**VTE risk in neurosurgery:** craniotomy patients have ~20-30% DVT rate without prophylaxis. Brain tumors confer additional hypercoagulability (especially GBM).

## Special Scenarios

### Intracranial Hemorrhage on Anticoagulation
- Reverse immediately — do not wait for coagulation studies
- Target: INR ≤1.4, or DOAC reversal within 30-60 minutes
- Surgical evacuation if indicated (same criteria as non-anticoagulated ICH)
- Hold anticoagulation ≥7-14 days post-hemorrhage before restarting (if indication persists)

### Mechanical Heart Valve + Neurosurgical Emergency
- Reverse anticoagulation for surgery
- Restart at lowest effective dose as soon as hemostasis confirmed
- Multidisciplinary decision (neurosurgery + cardiology)
