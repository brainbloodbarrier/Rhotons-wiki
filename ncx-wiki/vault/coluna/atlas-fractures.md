# Atlas Fractures (C1)

Second most common upper cervical bony injury (after odontoid fractures). Predominantly caused by axial compression. Account for 2–13% of cervical spine injuries, 1–3% of all spine fractures.

## Key Points

- **50% have concomitant cervical fractures** — always search for odontoid fracture
- Isolated atlas fractures can be treated conservatively
- Transverse atlantal ligament (TAL) integrity determines treatment
- Neurologic deficit from isolated C1 fracture is rare
- Increase in frequency with age

## Clinical Presentation

- Upper cervical pain
- Headache
- Difficulty with cervical rotation
- Neurologic deficit rare (wide spinal canal at C1)
- Lower cranial nerve palsies (IX-XII) = Collet-Sicard syndrome → suspect associated injuries

## Gehweiler Classification

| Type | Description | Stability |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| **I** | Isolated anterior arch fracture | Stable |
| **II** | Isolated posterior arch fracture | Stable |
| **IIIa** | Bilateral lateral mass fracture — TAL intact | Stable |
| **IIIb** | Bilateral lateral mass fracture — TAL disrupted (Jefferson fracture) | Unstable |
| **IV** | Lateral mass fracture | Usually stable |
| **V** | Isolated transverse ligament injury | Unstable |

## Dickman TAL Classification

Critical for treatment decision — determines need for atlantoaxial fusion.

| Type | Description | Treatment |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| **Type 1** | Intraligamentous (midsubstance) rupture | **Surgical fusion** — does NOT heal |
| **Type 2** | Bony avulsion from lateral mass | May heal with external immobilization |

**Diagnosis:** MRI is essential to assess TAL integrity. CT alone cannot distinguish Type 1 from Type 2.

## Rule of Spence

Lateral mass overhang >6.9 mm (combined bilateral) on open-mouth X-ray → suggests TAL disruption. However, CT and MRI are now standard for definitive assessment.

## Treatment Algorithm

### Conservative (Stable Fractures)

- **Indications:** Gehweiler types I, II, IIIa, IV; Dickman type 2 with adequate reduction
- **Method:** Rigid cervical collar (Philadelphia) for ≥6 weeks
- **Follow-up:** Serial imaging to monitor for delayed instability

### Atlas Osteosynthesis (Motion-Preserving)

- **Indications:** Unstable burst fractures with bony TAL avulsion (Dickman type 2); Gehweiler IIIb
- **Goal:** Preserve C1-C2 rotation (~50% of total cervical rotation)
- **Approaches:**
  - **Posterior** (preferred) — C1 lateral mass screws + rod; lower morbidity
  - **Transoral anterior** — screws + plate; good fusion rates but higher morbidity risk
  - **Combined posterior + transoral** — best reduction but most invasive

**Technique (posterior):**
- Entry point: midpoint of inferior lateral mass at junction with posterior arch
- Bicortical screws recommended (higher pullout strength)
- C2 venous plexus and dorsal root ganglion carefully mobilized caudally
- Intraoperative CT/3D fluoroscopy recommended for reduction assessment

### Atlantoaxial Fusion (C1-C2)

- **Indications:** Intraligamentous TAL rupture (Dickman type 1); failed osteosynthesis; delayed atlantoaxial instability
- **Sacrifice:** Loses ~50% of cervical rotation

#### Harms vs Magerl Techniques

| Feature | Magerl (Transarticular) | Harms (Screw-Rod) |
|---------|------------------------|---------------------|
| **Fixation** | 3-point with Gallie wire | Polyaxial screw-rod |
| **Reduction** | Required BEFORE drilling | Possible AFTER screw placement |
| **Cost** | Implants cheaper | Implants expensive |
| **Flexibility** | Limited by anatomy | Modifications possible (short isthmus, laminar screws) |
| **VA injury** | ~5% (wrong trajectory or high-riding VA) | Lower (direct visualization) |
| **Temp fixation** | Damages C1-C2 joint | Ideal for temporary fixation |
| **Contraindication** | Bilateral posterior arch fracture; high-riding VA | High-riding VA (pedicle screws impossible) |

## Complications

- Vertebral artery injury (rare but catastrophic)
- C2 venous plexus bleeding (common, usually manageable with Gelfoam)
- C2 root ganglion injury (usually benign, no clinical impairment)
- Incomplete reduction → nonunion → delayed atlantoaxial instability
- Wound infection (higher risk with transoral approach)

## Pearls & Pitfalls

**Pearls:**
- 50% of atlas fractures have associated C2 fractures — always image the entire cervical spine
- Both injuries are easily missed on conventional X-rays — CT is gold standard
- Dickman TAL classification determines surgical strategy

**Pitfalls:**
- Do NOT rely on conventional X-rays for high-speed MVA patients
- Incomplete reduction leads to nonunion and delayed instability
- Intraoperative CT/3D fluoroscopy is highly recommended for adequate reduction
