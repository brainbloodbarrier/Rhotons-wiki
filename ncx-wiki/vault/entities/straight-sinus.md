# Straight Sinus

The straight sinus (sinus rectus) is a major dural venous channel that traverses the midline junction of the **falx cerebri and tentorium cerebelli**. It collects the **vein of Galen** (draining the deep cerebral venous system) and the **inferior sagittal sinus** (draining the medial hemisphere) at the falcotentorial junction, and courses posteroinferiorly to empty at the torcular Herophili, where it merges with the [[superior-sagittal-sinus]] and the paired transverse sinuses.

## Anatomy

- **Course**: from the falcotentorial junction anteriorly to the internal occipital protuberance posteriorly.
- **Length**: ~5–6 cm; **caliber**: 4–6 mm.
- **Tributaries**:
  - Vein of Galen (great cerebral vein) — the principal deep tributary, formed by fusion of the two internal cerebral veins and the two basal veins of Rosenthal.
  - Inferior sagittal sinus — arriving along the free edge of the falx.
  - Minor tentorial veins from the superior cerebellar surface and adjacent occipital lobes.
- **Angulation**: the tentorium is steeper in brachycephalic skulls; the straight sinus descends more obliquely in hyperpneumatized / platybasic configurations — a relevant planning variable for the [[posterior-interhemispheric-approach]].

## Functional role — deep venous drainage

Unlike the [[superior-sagittal-sinus]] (surface cortical drainage) the straight sinus is the terminal conduit of the **deep cerebral venous system**:

1. Medullary and ependymal veins → septal vein + thalamostriate vein → **internal cerebral vein**.
2. Deep sylvian, olfactory, and anterior perforated-substance veins → **basal vein of Rosenthal**.
3. Internal cerebral veins (paired) + basal veins of Rosenthal (paired) → **vein of Galen** (single).
4. Vein of Galen + inferior sagittal sinus → **straight sinus** → torcular.

Because all deep parenchymal drainage converges here, the straight sinus is an absolute no-sacrifice structure: acute occlusion produces fatal bilateral thalamic and basal ganglia venous infarction (classic "deep cerebral vein thrombosis" pattern). This stands in contrast to the posterior third of the [[superior-sagittal-sinus]], whose occlusion is merely disabling.

## Surgical relevance

### Pineal-region access
The straight sinus is the **roof landmark** of the supracerebellar-infratentorial corridor and the posterior wall of the [[posterior-interhemispheric-approach]] (Poppen / occipital-transtentorial). Tentorial incision must be made **lateral** to the straight sinus — the standard "L-shaped" or "triangular" tentorial cut preserves the sinus along its entire course. Incision medial to the straight sinus will cause catastrophic deep venous infarction.

### AVMs / dural AVFs
A dural AVF of the straight sinus / torcular region is a high-risk Borden III lesion with retrograde cortical venous drainage; see [[vascular-malformations-overview]]. A parafalcine or parieto-occipital [[parieto-occipital-avm]] frequently drains into the straight sinus — this deep venous drainage contributes a full point to the [[spetzler-martin-grading]] score.

### Venous sinus thrombosis
Isolated straight sinus thrombosis is a neurologic emergency; bilateral thalamic T2 hyperintensity with restricted diffusion is the hallmark MRI pattern. Treatment escalates from systemic anticoagulation to endovascular thrombectomy; neurosurgical decompression is rarely possible given the depth of the infarct territory.

### Tentorial / falcotentorial meningiomas
Tumors at the falcotentorial junction (falcotentorial [[meningioma]]) frequently encase or invade the straight sinus. Resection strategy mirrors posterior [[superior-sagittal-sinus]] sinus meningiomas: patent sinus must be preserved; encasement with complete occlusion on MRV permits resection-with-sinus-sacrifice, but only after bilateral collateral verification.

## Imaging

- **MRV** (phase-contrast or time-of-flight): best non-invasive imaging; demonstrates patency, collateralization, and tributary anatomy.
- **DSA** (venous phase): gold-standard for AVM feeder / drainage mapping and dAVF characterization.
- **CT venogram**: useful in the acute setting for thrombosis.
