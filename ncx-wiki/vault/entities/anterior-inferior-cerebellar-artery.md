# Anterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery (AICA)

The anterior inferior cerebellar artery is a branch of the basilar artery that travels through the [[cerebellopontine-angle]] (CPA) intimately associated with cranial nerves VII and VIII. It supplies the lateral pons, the middle cerebellar peduncle, the flocculus, and the inferolateral cerebellum. AICA is arguably the single most surgically relevant posterior-fossa artery because it defines the neurovascular crossings seen in hemifacial spasm, trigeminal neuralgia (occasionally), and vertigo of vascular origin.

## Origin and Course

- Arises from the lower or mid **basilar artery** (usually 1–3 cm above the vertebrobasilar junction)
- Courses laterally and inferiorly across the ventral pons
- Enters the **cerebellopontine angle** typically below the abducens nerve (CN VI) and loops around the facial–vestibulocochlear bundle (CN VII–VIII)
- Gives the **labyrinthine artery** (internal auditory artery) to the inner ear — often from AICA itself, sometimes from a separate basilar branch
- Terminates on the petrosal surface of the cerebellum, anastomosing with [[posterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery]] (PICA) and the superior cerebellar artery

## Segmental Anatomy (Rhoton)

| Segment | Location |
|---------|----------|
| **a1 — anterior pontine** | Basilar origin to olive; ventral pontine perforators |
| **a2 — lateral pontine** | Alongside CN V–VIII; gives labyrinthine and recurrent perforators |
| **a3 — flocculopeduncular** | Contacts CN VII–VIII; supplies flocculus and middle cerebellar peduncle |
| **a4 — cortical** | Distributes to petrosal cerebellar surface |

## Branches

- **Internal auditory (labyrinthine) artery** — to cochlea and vestibule; non-anastomotic end artery (sacrifice = deafness/vertigo)
- **Recurrent perforating arteries** — to lateral pontine tegmentum
- **Subarcuate artery** — variable, into subarcuate fossa of petrous bone
- **Cerebellar surface branches** — petrosal / inferior semilunar lobule

## Clinical Syndromes

### AICA Infarction (Lateral Pontomedullary Syndrome)

A complex brainstem/cerebellar stroke including:

- Ipsilateral facial palsy (CN VII nucleus/fascicle)
- Ipsilateral hearing loss and vertigo (labyrinthine territory / CN VIII)
- Ipsilateral facial sensory loss (spinal trigeminal)
- Ipsilateral Horner syndrome
- Contralateral pain/temperature loss on body (spinothalamic)
- Ipsilateral limb ataxia (middle cerebellar peduncle)

Distinguished from the more caudal Wallenberg (PICA-territory) syndrome by the presence of **hearing loss and CN VII findings**.

### Hemifacial Spasm

AICA (or a PICA-AICA loop) compressing the REZ (root exit zone) of CN VII on the anterolateral pons is the most common neurovascular cause — relieved by retrosigmoid microvascular decompression (MVD) with Teflon or shredded-muscle pledget.

### Vertigo and Sensorineural Hearing Loss

Vascular compression of CN VIII by an AICA loop is implicated in episodic disabling vertigo / sudden SNHL; selective MVD of CN VIII is occasionally performed.

## Anatomic Variants

- **AICA-PICA common trunk** (~20%): one vessel supplies both territories — sacrifice is catastrophic
- **Duplicated AICA** or bilateral asymmetry (one side dominant) is common
- **Loop into IAC (internal auditory canal)**: in a subset of patients, the AICA loop enters the porus acusticus — relevant for vestibular schwannoma and CPA tumor surgery

## Surgical Relevance

### Retrosigmoid Approach

- AICA is the principal obstacle in CPA dissection for **vestibular schwannoma**, **petroclival meningioma**, and **epidermoid cyst**
- The artery, especially its recurrent perforators, must be preserved — injury causes labyrinthine infarction and brainstem deficit
- During tumor dissection, the AICA loop around CN VII–VIII is freed from adhesions before removal

### Microvascular Decompression (MVD)

- For **hemifacial spasm**: AICA or PICA loop at CN VII REZ
- For **glossopharyngeal neuralgia**: PICA > AICA
- Pledget is interposed between vessel and nerve; no vessel sacrifice

### [[far-lateral-approach]]

- AICA and its caudal anastomosis with [[posterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery]] lie at the rostral extent of the far-lateral corridor
- Lower cranial nerve (IX–XI) preservation is aided by respecting AICA perforators to lateral medulla

### Aneurysms

- **AICA aneurysms** are rare (<1% of intracranial); most are distal and associated with trauma, fenestration, or infection
- Present with SAH, facial palsy, hearing loss
- Treated by clipping via retrosigmoid, proximal occlusion, or endovascular parent-vessel sacrifice if distal

## Relationships in the CPA

| Structure | Relation to AICA |
|-----------|------------------|
| CN V (trigeminal) | Superior — usually cleared by superior cerebellar artery |
| CN VI (abducens) | Medial, often above AICA origin |
| CN VII–VIII | **Intimate** — AICA loops around, between, or through the bundle |
| CN IX–XI | Inferior — PICA territory |
| Flocculus | Hangs over AICA and CN VII–VIII — retraction landmark |
| Choroid plexus (luschka) | Emerges from foramen of Luschka adjacent to AICA loop |
