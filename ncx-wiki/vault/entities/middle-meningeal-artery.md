# Middle Meningeal Artery (MMA)

The middle meningeal artery is the principal arterial supply to the cranial [[dura]] above the tentorium. It is relevant to every neurosurgeon for three reasons: it is the classical bleeding source of epidural hematoma, it is a modern endovascular target for chronic subdural hematoma, and its frontal branch course beneath the [[pterion]] constrains burr-hole placement in the [[pterional-approach]].

## Origin and Course

### Extracranial

- Arises from the **first (mandibular) segment of the maxillary artery** in the infratemporal fossa
- Ascends vertically between the sphenomandibular ligament and the lateral pterygoid muscle
- Gives off the accessory meningeal artery before its intracranial passage

### Intracranial Entry

- Enters the middle cranial fossa through the **foramen spinosum** of the greater sphenoid wing
- Immediately pierces the outer dural leaf and runs in a bony groove on the inner table of the temporal squama
- Bifurcates into **frontal (anterior)** and **parietal (posterior)** branches within 1–2 cm of entry

## Branches

| Branch | Distribution |
|--------|--------------|
| **Frontal (anterior) branch** | Runs beneath the [[pterion]] and curves superiorly toward the vertex — anterior supratentorial dura |
| **Parietal (posterior) branch** | Courses posteriorly over the parietal squama — posterior convexity dura |
| **Petrosal branch** | Geniculate ganglion region; anastomoses with facial nerve vasa nervorum |
| **Superior tympanic branch** | To tensor tympani |
| **Meningeal accessory branches** | To cavernous sinus region |
| **Orbital anastomotic branch** | Connects to the ophthalmic artery via the superior orbital fissure (surgically important: a "dangerous anastomosis" during embolization) |

## Surgical Relevance

### Epidural Hematoma

Trauma at or near the [[pterion]] frequently fractures the thin temporal squama and lacerates the MMA — typically the frontal branch. The classic clinical course is transient loss of consciousness, a **lucid interval**, then rapid deterioration as the hematoma strips dura from bone and compresses the [[temporal-lobe]]. Prompt craniotomy with MMA coagulation or bone-wax tamponade of the foramen spinosum is definitive.

### Pterional Craniotomy Technique

- The MMA frontal branch lies in a groove on the inner table beneath the [[pterion]] — stripping dura off bone must be done under direct vision to avoid avulsion
- Bleeding from the stripped MMA in the sphenoid ridge is controlled with bipolar, bone wax, or bipolar at the foramen spinosum itself
- Extensive drilling of the sphenoid ridge (e.g., for a fully mobilized [[pterional-approach]]) may intentionally skeletonize and sacrifice MMA branches

### MMA Embolization

Endovascular MMA embolization with liquid embolic or particles has become a **first-line adjunct** for chronic subdural hematoma — the MMA is the inflow to the neomembrane's fragile capillaries. Contraindications include a prominent orbital anastomosis (risk of retrograde ophthalmic artery embolization → blindness) and petrosal branch dominance of the facial nerve blood supply (risk of peripheral facial palsy).

### Other Pathologies

- **Dural arteriovenous fistula**: MMA is the most common feeder to convexity and transverse-sigmoid DAVFs
- **Meningioma**: convexity, parasagittal, and sphenoid-wing meningiomas are frequently MMA-supplied; preoperative particle embolization reduces intraoperative blood loss
- **MMA pseudoaneurysm**: rare post-traumatic lesion; endovascular coiling is standard

## Anatomic Variants

- **Ophthalmic origin of the MMA** (~1–2%): the entire MMA arises from the ophthalmic artery through the superior orbital fissure — a critical pre-embolization finding
- **Duplicated MMA** or accessory trunks via foramen ovale occur in a minority
- **Petrosquamous sinus persistence** can confound MMA mapping in DAVF cases
