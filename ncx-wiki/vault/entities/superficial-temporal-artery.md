# Superficial Temporal Artery

The superficial temporal artery (STA) is the smaller terminal branch of the [external carotid artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/external-carotid-artery.md) (the other being the [[internal-maxillary-artery|internal maxillary artery]]). It is the primary donor for EC-IC bypass, used in over three-quarters of Lawton's cases. Its accessibility along the [[pterional-approach|pterional craniotomy]] incision makes it nearly universal -- if harvested and ready, the likelihood of performing a bypass increases substantially.

## Anatomy

The STA originates within the [[parotid-gland|parotid gland]] deep to the [facial nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/facial-nerve.md) and ascends over the zygomatic root, anterior to the external auditory canal. It resides in the dense connective tissue layer beneath the skin and above the galea aponeurotica. The STA and [occipital artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/occipital-artery.md) form the ECA's major scalp supply.

### Branching Pattern

At the level of the zygomatic root, the STA gives off a small zygomatico-orbital branch coursing anteriorly toward the orbit, then divides into **frontal** (anterior) and **parietal** (posterior) branches typically 4-5 cm above its origin. This bifurcation is highly variable in location and symmetry. Terminal branches anastomose freely with each other and with contralateral counterparts over the frontal and parietal convexities.

### Morphology

The STA has a characteristic **serpentine morphology**. Anterior branches originate at the anterior-most curve point; posterior branches at the posterior-most. Few branches arise from the lateral wall and none from the medial wall. The superficial temporal vein parallels the STA with larger caliber, thinner walls, and no serpentine course.

## Harvest Technique

Harvesting should take approximately 20 minutes. Keys to a quick harvest:

1. **Incise directly over the parietal branch** -- map the artery preoperatively with a Doppler flow probe
2. **Dissect under the microscope** to visualize branches and tissue planes
3. **Apply upward traction** on the scalp with toothed forceps to separate the dermal layer and subcutaneous fat from the STA
4. **Set bipolar cautery high** to control scalp bleeding without Raney clips

The initial cut-down exposes 8 cm from the zygoma to the superior temporal line -- sufficient for all but the deepest bypasses to [SCA](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/superior-cerebellar-artery.md)/[PCA](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/posterior-cerebral-artery.md). Branches are cauterized and cut 1-2 mm from the trunk. The STA is left in distal continuity to maintain flow until [[microsurgical-anastomosis|anastomosis]].

### Double-Barrel Harvest

When the frontal branch is also needed, the incision is extended anteriorly into the reflected scalp flap, yielding 6-8 cm of additional artery. A linear parietal incision with upward traction exposes 3-5 cm of the frontal branch.

### Bipolar Cutting Technique

An alternative harvest method uses bipolar cautery to seal and then tear branches rather than conventional sharp dissection:

1. Spread the bipolar tips and cauterize the branch 1-2 mm from its origin
2. Squeeze the cauterized branch with the bipolar forceps while turning current off
3. Avulse with a snap of the wrist **toward** the parent STA (not away, to avoid ripping the proximal branch)

This technique dispenses with the protective connective tissue cuff, straightening and elongating the STA to reach deeper targets. Scissors are unnecessary and instrument exchanges are minimized.

## Scalp Anatomy (SCALP Mnemonic)

The scalp has five layers relevant to STA harvest: **S**kin, dense **C**onnective tissue (where the STA resides), galea **A**poneurotica, **L**oose connective tissue, and **P**ericranium. These layers are best appreciated at the vertex; laterally, muscular fascia replaces the periosteum.
