# Saphenous Vein Graft

The saphenous vein graft (SVG) is the highest-flow conduit in the cerebrovascular surgeon's revascularization ladder. Because the great saphenous vein is long (>50 cm), wide (4–6 mm at the groin), and readily accessible, it is the default choice when the recipient territory requires volume flow that exceeds what a pedicled [[superficial-temporal-artery]] or a [[radial-artery-graft]] can deliver — typically full [[mca-aneurysm]] or giant internal-carotid replacement.

## Indications

| Scenario | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| Giant or fusiform ICA/MCA aneurysm requiring parent vessel sacrifice with BTO failure | Replace ~150–200 mL/min of MCA/ICA flow |
| Skull-base tumor resection sacrificing petrous/cavernous ICA | Cervical ICA → supraclinoid ICA or M2 reconstruction |
| Recurrent aneurysm after failed prior bypass or clipping | Revise with high-flow conduit |
| Moyamoya (rare — direct flow too high) | Reserved for adults with severe hemispheric ischemia |
| Traumatic ICA dissection with planned sacrifice | Stage bypass + endovascular occlusion |

A failed balloon test occlusion (BTO) or borderline SPECT/xenon perfusion during BTO is the classical decision point favoring full-flow SVG over medium-flow radial conduits.

## Harvest

- Thigh or calf GSV; long-segment harvest from the groin yields the thickest (~6 mm) caliber at the femoral end.
- Open harvest along the course of the vein, ligating tributaries with clips; endoscopic vein harvest (cardiothoracic technique) reduces wound complications.
- After harvest, the vein is reversed (valves would otherwise obstruct flow) and pressure-distended with heparinized saline under controlled pressure (~200 mmHg) to disrupt spasm, confirm patency, and mark the outflow end.
- Complications: wound dehiscence, saphenous neuropathy (medial calf paresthesia), venous insufficiency in the harvested limb (minor), and lymphedema.

## Anastomosis

- Proximal (inflow) anastomosis: **cervical ICA or ECA**, end-to-side, 6-0 or 7-0 polypropylene — sized to match venous caliber.
- Distal (outflow) anastomosis: **M2/M3 MCA** for supra-MCA replacement, or **supraclinoid ICA** via a pterional/orbitozygomatic corridor (see [[pterional-approach]], [[orbitozygomatic-approach]]).
- Standard [[microsurgical-anastomosis]] with 9-0 nylon; [[temporary-clipping]] with burst-suppression or mild hypothermia as neuroprotection during each suture line.
- Intraoperative Charbel flow probe confirms graft volume (target 70–150 mL/min); ICG angiography confirms patency; Doppler insonation of the graft is routine.

## Comparison with Other Conduits

| Conduit | Flow (mL/min) | Caliber | Length | Harvest morbidity | Best role |
|---------|---------------|---------|--------|-------------------|-----------|
| [[superficial-temporal-artery]] | 15–30 | 1.5 mm | Short, pedicled | Minimal (scalp) | Standard low-flow [[ec-ic-bypass]] |
| [[radial-artery-graft]] | 40–80 | 2.5–3 mm | ~20 cm | Sensory, rare ischemia | Medium-flow replacement |
| Saphenous vein | 100–200 | 4–6 mm | 20–50 cm | Wound, venous insufficiency | High-flow replacement |

SVG is preferred over radial when full arterial-territory flow is required; radial is preferred when a smaller M2/M3 recipient matches its caliber. The [[interposition-graft-bypass]] pattern is the standard SVG configuration; SVG can also participate in [[reimplantation-bypass]] or [[in-situ-bypass]] constructs, though this is uncommon.

## Patency and Outcomes

- Early patency (≤30 days) >90% in high-volume series; graft thrombosis risk is highest in the first week (platelet-mediated anastomotic thrombosis).
- Long-term patency (5–10 years) ≥80%; intimal hyperplasia at anastomotic sites is the dominant late-failure mode (shared with cardiac SVGs).
- Antiplatelet therapy (aspirin ± clopidogrel) post-operatively is standard; full heparinization during construction.
- Stroke risk during construction ranges 3–8% depending on temporary clip duration and collateral reserve; staged SVG-then-occlusion (rather than same-day sacrifice) reduces ischemic risk.
