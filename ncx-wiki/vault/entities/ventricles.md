# Ventricular System

The ventricular system is a continuous set of four CSF-filled cavities within the brain: two **lateral ventricles** (paired C-shaped cavities in each cerebral hemisphere), the midline **third ventricle** between the thalami, and the **fourth ventricle** in the posterior fossa. CSF is produced primarily by the choroid plexus at ~500 mL/day, circulates through the ventricles and subarachnoid space, and is absorbed at the arachnoid granulations and via the glymphatic/lymphatic pathways. Surgical understanding of the ventricular system underlies hydrocephalus, intraventricular tumor, and endoscopic practice.

## Components

| Ventricle | Location | Communication |
|-----------|----------|---------------|
| [[lateral-ventricle]] (×2) | Each hemisphere — frontal horn, body, trigone, occipital and temporal horns | Third ventricle via [[foramen-of-monro]] |
| Third ventricle | Between thalami, above the hypothalamus | Fourth ventricle via cerebral aqueduct (of Sylvius) |
| Fourth ventricle | Between pons/medulla and cerebellum | Subarachnoid space via foramen of Magendie (median) and foramina of Luschka (lateral) |

### Lateral Ventricle Subregions

- **Frontal horn** — anterior to the [[foramen-of-monro]]; bounded medially by the septum pellucidum and laterally by the head of the [[caudate-nucleus]].
- **Body** — between foramen of Monro and the atrium; floor formed by thalamus and thalamostriate vein.
- **Atrium (trigone)** — widest part; houses the glomus of the choroid plexus and abuts the optic radiations laterally.
- **Occipital horn** — into the occipital lobe; often asymmetric.
- **Temporal horn** — sweeps anteriorly into the temporal lobe; contains the hippocampus along its floor and the choroidal fissure along its roof, important in mesial temporal lobe epilepsy and amygdalohippocampectomy.

## Choroid Plexus

- Located along the floor of the lateral ventricle body and the roof of the third and fourth ventricles.
- Supplied by anterior and posterior choroidal arteries; drained into the internal cerebral veins and vein of Galen.
- Responsible for ~70% of CSF production; plexectomy/cauterization is an adjunct in endoscopic third ventriculostomy for certain hydrocephalus phenotypes (infant, post-hemorrhagic).

## CSF Circulation

Choroid plexus → lateral ventricles → [[foramen-of-monro]] → third ventricle → cerebral aqueduct → fourth ventricle → foramina of Magendie/Luschka → subarachnoid space → basal cisterns → cortical convexities → arachnoid granulations (± glymphatic pathway) → venous sinuses.

Obstruction at any level produces [[hydrocephalus]]:

- **Foramen of Monro** — colloid cyst (unilateral or bilateral trapping).
- **Aqueduct of Sylvius** — aqueductal stenosis (congenital, tectal glioma, pineal mass).
- **Fourth ventricle outlets** — Dandy-Walker, posterior fossa tumors, basal meningitis.

## Surgical Access

- **[[transcallosal-approach]]** — interhemispheric corridor through anterior corpus callosum to the body of the [[lateral-ventricle]], [[foramen-of-monro]], and anterior third ventricle; workhorse for colloid cyst and central neurocytoma.
- **Transcortical transventricular** — middle-frontal-gyrus entry to the frontal horn; simpler but risks callosal and subcortical white-matter tracts.
- **Telovelar/transcerebellomedullary** — through the cerebellomedullary fissure into the fourth ventricle without splitting the vermis.
- **Endoscopic third ventriculostomy (ETV)** — perforation of the floor of the third ventricle between the mammillary bodies and infundibulum into the interpeduncular cistern; standard of care for obstructive hydrocephalus in appropriate candidates.
- **External ventricular drain (EVD)** — placed into the frontal horn via Kocher point for monitoring [[intracranial-pressure]] and diverting CSF.

## Clinically Important Pathology

- [[hydrocephalus]] — obstructive vs communicating; classical, [[normal-pressure-hydrocephalus]].
- Colloid cyst of the third ventricle — position-dependent headache, obstructive hydrocephalus, sudden death risk.
- Choroid plexus papilloma/carcinoma — CSF overproduction hydrocephalus in infants.
- Central neurocytoma — young adults, attached to septum pellucidum.
- Ependymoma — fourth ventricle in children, spinal in adults.
- Intraventricular hemorrhage — SAH extension, hypertensive hemorrhage with rupture, AVM, trauma.
