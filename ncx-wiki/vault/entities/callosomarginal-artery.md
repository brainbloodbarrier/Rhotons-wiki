# Callosomarginal Artery

The callosomarginal artery (CmaA) is the second major cortical trunk arising from the distal **anterior cerebral artery** — the companion vessel to the [[pericallosal-artery]], with which it forms the "double-barrel" hallmark of ACA cortical distribution. When present as a discrete trunk (~60% of hemispheres; in the remainder the cortical branches arise directly from the pericallosal artery), the CmaA runs within the **cingulate sulcus** and fans out to supply much of the medial frontal cortex — territory that is at direct risk during any interhemispheric operative corridor.

## Origin and Course

- **Origin**: typically from the A3 segment of the ACA, just distal to the genu of the corpus callosum, where the pericallosal artery turns around the genu and ascends posteriorly.
- **Course**: ascends within the cingulate sulcus, arching over the cingulate gyrus and running parallel to — but superior to — the pericallosal artery.
- **Termination**: beyond the central sulcus into the paracentral lobule, often continuing as the paracentral artery toward the posterior cingulate region.
- **Variants**:
  - Absent in ~40% of hemispheres — cortical branches (frontopolar, anterior/middle/posterior internal frontal, paracentral) arise directly from the pericallosal artery.
  - Duplicated in ~10%.
  - May arise from A2 proximal to the genu (proximal variant), which shifts its course into the anterior interhemispheric corridor earlier.

## Cortical territory

The CmaA is the dominant supply to the **medial surface of the frontal lobe and paracentral lobule** — an eloquent zone that includes the supplementary motor area (SMA) and the medial primary motor/sensory cortex for the contralateral lower limb. Its branches, in approximate anterior-to-posterior order:

| Branch | Territory |
|--------|-----------|
| Anterior internal frontal | Medial superior frontal gyrus |
| Middle internal frontal | Medial middle frontal region, SMA |
| Posterior internal frontal | Posterior SMA, pre-SMA |
| Paracentral | Paracentral lobule (foot/leg motor-sensory cortex) |

## Surgical relevance

### Interhemispheric corridors
During the [[anterior-interhemispheric-approach]] — used to reach [[acoa-aneurysm|ACoA aneurysms]], third-ventricle tumors, and frontal [[frontal-avm|AVMs]] — the surgeon must identify and preserve both the pericallosal and callosomarginal arteries. Their branches typically cross the operative corridor from dorsal to ventral; sacrifice causes medial frontal and paracentral infarction, with a characteristic contralateral lower-extremity monoparesis and/or SMA syndrome.

The [[transcallosal-approach]] to the lateral ventricle passes between the two pericallosal arteries at the body of the corpus callosum (posterior to the CmaA takeoff); during the exposure the CmaA is identified earlier and protected lateral to the falx edge.

### Aneurysms
Callosomarginal-pericallosal-junction aneurysms are rare ACA-distal aneurysms; they behave similarly to [[pericallosal-artery-aneurysm|pericallosal aneurysms]] in surgical corridor and clipping strategy, though the CmaA origin requires careful preservation to avoid medial-frontal infarction.

### AVMs
Medial-frontal [[frontal-avm]] and cingulate AVMs are routinely fed from callosomarginal pial branches in addition to pericallosal feeders. [[avm-feeding-arteries]] detail en-route vs. terminal feeder identification — the callosomarginal trunk is typically en-route (must be preserved distal to the nidus).

## Angiographic identification

- Lateral DSA view: the CmaA is the vessel running **above and parallel** to the pericallosal artery in the cingulate sulcus. The pericallosal hugs the corpus callosum; the CmaA arches higher.
- CTA/MRA: identifiable when present, but smaller cortical branches frequently obscured by partial-volume.
- Surgical cue: the first large ACA branch seen rising out of the cingulate sulcus after the genu is the callosomarginal.
