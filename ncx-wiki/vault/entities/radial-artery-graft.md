# Radial Artery Graft

The radial artery graft (RAG) is a free arterial interposition conduit used in cerebral revascularization when a **medium-flow** bypass is desired. Among the three standard conduits — superficial temporal artery ([[superficial-temporal-artery]]), radial artery, and saphenous vein — the radial artery occupies the middle of the flow spectrum (~40–80 mL/min) and combines arterial wall biology with reasonable length and a caliber (~2.5–3 mm) compatible with the M2/M3 segment of the middle cerebral artery.

## Indications

| Scenario | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| Giant ICA or MCA aneurysm requiring parent-vessel sacrifice | Replace the ~60–100 mL/min MCA flow |
| Complex M2/M3 aneurysm needing [[interposition-graft-bypass]] | STA too small; SVG too much flow |
| Skull-base tumor sacrificing petrous/cavernous ICA | Cervical ICA → MCA IC–IC reconstruction |
| Moyamoya with failed STA–MCA (rare) | Augment flow |
| Failed prior EC–IC bypass | Revision with higher-caliber conduit |

Flow-demand estimation is typically guided by balloon test occlusion with SPECT/xenon CT or quantitative MRA flow; a failed BTO or borderline tolerance favors radial over STA.

## Harvest

- **Nondominant arm** is preferred.
- **Allen test** is mandatory: compress both radial and ulnar arteries, open fist; release ulnar — capillary refill <6 s confirms adequate collateral palmar arch. Modern practice adds **Doppler Allen** or pulse oximetry.
- Pronate the forearm; longitudinal incision along the palpable radial artery from the wrist to the antecubital fossa.
- Skeletonize with venae comitantes in a pedicle; ligate tributaries with clips.
- Reported complications: sensory thumb dysesthesia (superficial radial nerve), hand ischemia (<1%), hematoma, seroma.

## Pharmacologic Handling

- Radial artery is prone to **vasospasm** — harvest requires topical papaverine irrigation and/or hydrostatic distension (Lawton "pressure-distension" technique) to preserve flow.
- Alternatively, pharmacologic pretreatment (intraluminal nicardipine, papaverine bath) reduces spasm.
- Systemic heparinization during clamping is standard.

## Anastomosis

- Both ends anastomosed end-to-side (proximal to cervical ICA, ECA, or STA stump; distal to M2/M3) — classic IC–IC pattern for Lawton giant-MCA bypass.
- Standard [[microsurgical-anastomosis]] technique with interrupted 9-0 or 10-0 nylon; running sutures accepted for long arteriotomies.
- Temporary [[temporary-clipping]] during each anastomosis; burst suppression or mild hypothermia as neuroprotection.
- Intraoperative flow probe (Charbel) confirms graft patency and volume; intraoperative ICG angiography confirms visual patency.

## Comparison with Other Conduits

| Conduit | Flow (mL/min) | Caliber | Length | Harvest morbidity | Best role |
|---------|---------------|---------|--------|-------------------|-----------|
| [[superficial-temporal-artery]] | 15–30 | 1.5 mm | Short, pedicled | Minimal (scalp) | Standard low-flow EC–IC |
| Radial artery | 40–80 | 2.5–3 mm | ~20 cm | Sensory, rare ischemia | Medium-flow replacement |
| Saphenous vein | 100–200 | 4–6 mm | Long | Wound, venous insufficiency | High-flow replacement |

The radial artery thus sits between [[superficial-temporal-artery]] pedicled flow and saphenous vein high-flow conduits, and is chosen when pedicled [[ec-ic-bypass]] is inadequate but a full vein graft would oversupply the territory.

## Patency and Outcomes

- Early patency rates are >90% in high-volume centers.
- Long-term patency is comparable to saphenous vein bypass at 5–10 years in published series.
- Stroke risk during construction is mitigated by short clamp times, temporary bypass, or intraluminal shunts for IC–IC reconstructions.
