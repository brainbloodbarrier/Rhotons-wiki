# Spetzler-Martin Grading System

Published in 1986, the Spetzler-Martin system is the predominant classification for brain [[vascular-malformation|arteriovenous malformations]]. It distills AVM complexity into three variables -- size, eloquence, and venous drainage -- to predict surgical risk. Its simplicity and bedside applicability have made it integral to the language of AVMs.

## Original Grading Scale (Grades I-V)

| Variable | Feature | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Size** | Small (< 3 cm) | 1 |
| | Medium (3-6 cm) | 2 |
| | Large (> 6 cm) | 3 |
| **Eloquence** | Non-eloquent | 0 |
| | Eloquent | 1 |
| **Venous Drainage** | Superficial only | 0 |
| | Deep | 1 |

**Grade = Size + Eloquence + Venous Drainage** (range I to V)

Grade VI designates AVMs too complex for resection (intrinsic [[brainstem]], holohemispheric).

### Eloquent Areas

Defined as [[motor-pathway|sensorimotor cortex]], language areas (Wernicke's and Broca's), [[visual-pathway|visual cortex]], hypothalamus, thalamus, [[internal-capsule]], brainstem, cerebellar peduncles, and deep cerebellar nuclei.

### Venous Drainage

Superficial: drainage into cortical veins and convexity sinuses. Deep: drainage into veins coursing to the [[vein-of-galen|vein of Galen]] (i.e., [[internal-cerebral-vein|ICV]], [[basal-vein-of-rosenthal|BVR]], precentral cerebellar vein). In [[posterior-fossa]] AVMs, only veins draining to the [[straight-sinus]] or [[transverse-sinus]] are superficial. See [[avm-draining-veins]] for the full venous anatomy.

## Clinical Validation

In the original series of 100 consecutive resections:

| Grade | No Deficit | Minor Deficit | Major Deficit |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | High | Rare | 0% |
| II | High | Rare | 0% |
| III | Intermediate | Intermediate | Intermediate |
| IV | Moderate | 20% | 7% |
| V | Low | 19% | 12% |

Three distinct management groups emerged: **low-grade** (I-II) with low morbidity, frequently treated surgically; **high-grade** (IV-V) with high morbidity, frequently observed; and **grade III** requiring individualized decision-making.

## Grade III Modification

Grade III is the most heterogeneous grade, comprising four combinations with markedly different risks. Lawton's analysis of 76 consecutive grade III AVMs:

| Subtype | Size | Eloquence | Deep Drainage | Morbidity | Modified Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1V1E1 | Small | Yes | Yes | 2.9% | **III-** |
| S2V1E0 | Medium | No | Yes | 7.1% | **III** |
| S2V0E1 | Medium | Yes | No | 14.8% | **III+** |
| S3V0E0 | Large | No | No | -- | **III*** (exceedingly rare) |

- **III-**: surgical risk more like grade II; managed surgically
- **III**: average risk; managed surgically
- **III+**: risk more like grade IV; managed conservatively
- **III***: only possible in frontal/temporal poles; insufficient data

## Spetzler-Ponce Three-Tier System

Spetzler himself condensed the five-tier system into three classes (2011):

| Class | SM Grades | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | I + II | Surgical resection with goal of complete removal |
| **B** | III | Multimodality (endovascular + [[radiosurgery]] + surgery) |
| **C** | IV + V | Observation, with exceptions for recurrent hemorrhage, progressive deficits, steal symptoms |

Predictive power was identical to the five-tier system in 1,476 compiled cases. Opponents argue it adds a reclassification step without resolving grade III heterogeneity.

## Lawton-Young Supplementary Grading System

The supplementary system adds variables not in the Spetzler-Martin scale to refine patient selection:

| Variable | Feature | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Age** | < 20 years | 1 |
| | 20-40 years | 2 |
| | > 40 years | 3 |
| **Hemorrhagic presentation** | Ruptured | 0 |
| | Unruptured | 1 |
| **Nidus compactness** | Compact | 0 |
| | Diffuse | 1 |

**Supplementary grade** = Age + Bleeding + Compactness (range 1-5)

In a consecutive series of 300 patients, the supplementary system had higher predictive accuracy than Spetzler-Martin alone and stratified risk more evenly. Favorable outcomes by supplemented grade (SM + supplementary combined):

| Combined Grade | N | Favorable Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 7 | 100% |
| 3 | 21 | 100% |
| 4 | 55 | 91% |
| 5 | 90 | 79% |
| 6 | 70 | 73% |
| 7 | 44 | 45% |
| 8 | 8 | 50% |

**Decision rule**: supplementary grade <=3 or combined grade <=6 suggests acceptably low surgical risk. When SM and supplementary grades agree, the supplementary system confirms the SM recommendation. When they disagree, the supplementary grade may alter management -- 28% of patients with low SM grades had high supplementary grades, with 41% neurologically worse after surgery.

## Factors Not Captured

Several surgical risk determinants are absent from both systems:
- Deep [[perforating-arteries|perforating artery]] supply (lowers favorable outcomes by 15%)
- Prior [[radiosurgery]] or embolization effects on tissue planes
- Specific AVM subtype anatomy (see [[cerebrovascular-avms-overview]])
- [[avm-resection-principles|Dissection difficulty]] from compact vs diffuse nidus morphology
