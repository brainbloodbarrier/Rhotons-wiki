# Glasgow Coma Scale

The Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) is the most widely used grading system for assessing level of consciousness. It has good inter-observer repeatability and is the universal language for describing neurological status in acute care.

## Adult GCS (age ≥ 4 years)

| Points | Best Eye Opening | Best Verbal | Best Motor |
|--------|-----------------|-------------|------------|
| 6 | -- | -- | Obeys commands |
| 5 | -- | Oriented | Localizes pain |
| 4 | Spontaneous | Confused | Withdraws to pain |
| 3 | To speech | Inappropriate words | Flexion (decorticate) |
| 2 | To pain | Incomprehensible sounds | Extension (decerebrate) |
| 1 | None | None | None |

**Total range:** 3 (worst) to 15 (normal)

## Key Cutoffs

- **GCS ≤ 8:** operational definition of coma (90% of patients with GCS ≤8 meet clinical criteria for coma; none with GCS ≥9)
- **GCS 13-15:** mild TBI
- **GCS 9-12:** moderate TBI
- **GCS 3-8:** severe TBI

## Intubated Patients

For intubated patients unable to provide verbal response: award 1 point for verbal score and append "T" notation (e.g., "GCS = 6T").

## Children's Coma Scale (age < 4 years)

Identical to adult GCS except for the verbal component, which is adapted for preverbal children:

| Points | Crying | Interaction |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| 5 | Smiles, oriented to sound | Follows objects, interacts |
| 4 | Consolable | Inappropriate |
| 3 | Inconsistently consolable | Moaning |
| 2 | Inconsolable | Restless |
| 1 | None | None |

Eye opening and motor scores are identical to the adult scale.

## Clinical Significance

- The GCS motor component is the single most predictive element for outcome
- Equal and reactive pupils with metabolic coma vs fixed pupils with structural cause — the light reflex is the most useful sign in distinguishing metabolic from structural coma
- Decorticate posturing implies more rostral lesion (slightly better prognosis) vs decerebrate posturing (more caudal)

## Motor Response Details

**Decorticate (flexion):** abnormal flexion in upper extremities (slow flexion of arm, wrist, fingers with adduction) + extension in lower extremities. Implies disinhibition by removal of corticospinal pathways above the midbrain.

**Decerebrate (extension):** abnormal extension in all extremities. Opisthotonos, arms extended/adducted/hyperpronated, feet plantarflexed. Implies disinhibition of vestibulospinal tract at intercollicular level.
