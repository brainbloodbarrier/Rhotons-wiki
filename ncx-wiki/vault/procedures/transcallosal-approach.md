# Transcallosal Approach

The transcallosal approach is the anterior interhemispheric corridor that incises the anterior body of the corpus callosum to reach the bodies of the [[lateral-ventricle]], the [[foramen-of-monro]], and the third ventricle. It is the default route for colloid cysts of the third ventricle and is a workhorse for midline intraventricular tumors (central neurocytoma, subependymoma, choroid plexus tumors, craniopharyngioma with ventricular extension).

## Indications

| Target | Typical Pathology |
|--------|-------------------|
| Foramen of Monro | Colloid cyst, subependymoma |
| Body of lateral ventricle | Central neurocytoma, astrocytoma, ependymoma |
| Anterior third ventricle (interforniceal) | Craniopharyngioma extension, hypothalamic hamartoma |
| Mid/posterior third ventricle | Select pineal or tectal tumors (transchoroidal variant) |
| Septum pellucidum lesions | Cyst, tumor |

## Positioning and Incision

- **Supine**, head neutral or slightly flexed; vertex elevated; rigid fixation
- Side of approach chosen based on lesion laterality and venous anatomy — usually **right** (non-dominant) unless lesion is predominantly left-sided
- **Bicoronal incision** or curvilinear hairline scalp incision
- Craniotomy: two-thirds anterior and one-third posterior to the coronal suture, crossing the midline by ~1 cm — exposes the edge of the [[superior-sagittal-sinus]] (SSS)

## Craniotomy and Sinus Management

- Burr holes on either side of the SSS; dural tackups to avoid sinus tear
- Preserve the **bridging veins** entering the anterior third of the SSS whenever possible — their sacrifice (especially >2 large veins) risks venous infarction of the [[frontal-lobe]]
- The surgeon works in the window between these veins — craniotomy planning must be image-guided against a venous phase MRV or CTA

## Interhemispheric Dissection

- Dural opening based on the SSS; gentle retraction of the right frontal lobe
- Descend along the falx; identify the cingulate gyri, then split them — a common pitfall is mistaking adherent cingulate gyri for a single midline structure
- **Two [[pericallosal-artery]] (A3) trunks** appear on the superior surface of the corpus callosum, flanked by the [[callosomarginal-artery|callosomarginal]] branches
- Separate the pericallosal pair; the pearly-white corpus callosum lies between them

## Callosotomy and Ventricular Entry

- Midline callosotomy of **1.5–2 cm** anterior to the coronal suture, avoiding >2.5 cm to prevent disconnection syndrome
- Anterior placement preserves posterior commissural fibers and reduces risk of split-brain / alien hand
- Entry into the **right (or left) [[lateral-ventricle]] body** — landmarks:
  - **Choroid plexus** coursing anteriorly to the [[foramen-of-monro]]
  - **Thalamostriate and septal veins** converging at the foramen
  - **[[caudate-nucleus]]** on the lateral wall

## Variants

### Transforaminal

- Lesion reached directly through the [[foramen-of-monro]] — suitable for colloid cyst
- Risks: fornix injury (memory deficit), choroid plexus avulsion

### Transchoroidal (subchoroidal / interforniceal)

- Opens the tela choroidea along the taenia fornicis
- Accesses mid-third ventricle without fornix splitting
- Used for thalamic, tectal, or pineal-region extension

### Interforniceal

- Splits the fornices in the midline — historical route
- High risk of bilateral fornix injury → amnestic syndrome
- Largely supplanted by transchoroidal variant

## Complications

| Complication | Mechanism |
|--------------|-----------|
| Venous infarct | Bridging-vein sacrifice |
| Memory deficit (anterograde) | Fornix injury |
| Disconnection / split-brain | Over-long callosotomy |
| Hemiparesis | Cingulate/frontal retraction injury |
| Hydrocephalus | Blood in ventricles; obstruct foramen of Monro |
| Seizures | Frontal retraction; cortical contusion |
| Pericallosal injury | Inadvertent A3 avulsion during callosotomy |

## Comparison with Alternatives

- **Transcortical transventricular approach** (frontal transfrontal): shorter corridor but traverses white matter; higher risk of cortical seizure focus and longer working distance to midline
- **[[anterior-interhemispheric-approach]] (subfrontal variant)**: for suprachiasmatic / anterior ACoA targets, not for the third ventricle
- **Endoscopic transforaminal**: increasingly replaces transcallosal for pure colloid cyst in favorable anatomy
