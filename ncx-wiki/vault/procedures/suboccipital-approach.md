# Suboccipital Approach

The suboccipital approach is the general family of posterior-fossa craniotomies made below the external occipital protuberance. Its lateral variant — the **retrosigmoid (retromastoid) craniotomy** — is the universal corridor to the [[cerebellopontine-angle]] (CPA) and is one of the most frequently performed posterior-fossa operations. The midline variant exposes the cerebellar hemispheres, vermis, and fourth ventricle; the **[[far-lateral-approach]]** is the most extended lateral variant, reaching the anterolateral foramen magnum.

## Indications

| Variant | Typical Targets |
|---------|-----------------|
| Midline suboccipital | Cerebellar tumors (medulloblastoma, pilocytic astrocytoma, hemangioblastoma), fourth-ventricle tumors, Chiari decompression |
| Retrosigmoid (CPA) | [[vestibular-schwannoma]], meningioma of CPA, epidermoid, [[trigeminal-neuralgia]] microvascular decompression, hemifacial spasm MVD, glossopharyngeal neuralgia MVD, [[anterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery]] aneurysm |
| Far-lateral | [[posterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery]] aneurysm, vertebral aneurysm, foramen magnum meningioma, anterolateral brainstem cavernoma |
| Telovelar / transcerebellomedullary | Fourth ventricle floor lesions without vermis split |

## Positioning

- **Park-bench** (lateral decubitus) is the default for retrosigmoid and far-lateral — surgeon works comfortably, venous pressure low, cerebellum falls away by gravity.
- **Sitting (semi-sitting)** position still used in select centers — excellent CSF drainage and bloodless field, but requires vigilant venous-air-embolism (VAE) monitoring with precordial Doppler and central line ready for aspiration.
- **Prone (Concorde)** for midline suboccipital; head flexed with two-finger breadth between chin and sternum to protect airway and venous drainage.
- Mayfield fixation; shoulder taped down to open the angle between mastoid and shoulder.

## Skin and Muscle Dissection

- Retrosigmoid: curvilinear or linear incision 1–2 cm behind the mastoid, from the superior nuchal line down past the mastoid tip.
- Muscle layers from superficial to deep include trapezius, splenius capitis, longissimus capitis, and the suboccipital triangle (rectus capitis posterior major, superior and inferior obliques) — critical for the far-lateral where the vertebral artery is exposed in the sulcus arteriosus atlantis.
- Preserve the **occipital artery** when a bypass to PICA is planned.

## Craniotomy

- **Asterion** marks the approximate junction of the transverse and sigmoid sinuses and is the key keyhole for retrosigmoid craniotomy.
- Expose dura to the inferior edge of the transverse sinus superiorly and the posterior edge of the sigmoid sinus anteriorly; the **sinus-sinus junction** must be skeletonized (not exposed inadvertently).
- Mastoid air cells encountered during drilling must be obliterated with bone wax at closure to prevent CSF rhinorrhea via the eustachian tube.
- For far-lateral add a C1 hemilaminectomy and optional condylar drilling (up to one-third of the occipital condyle preserves atlanto-occipital stability).

## Intradural Dissection (Retrosigmoid / CPA)

- Open dura in a C-shape based on the sinuses; release CSF from the lateral cerebellomedullary cistern — the cerebellum relaxes, avoiding need for fixed retraction.
- Identify cranial nerves from caudal to rostral: IX–X–XI complex at the jugular foramen, VII–VIII at the internal auditory canal, V at the superior CPA.
- [[anterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery]] loops through the VII–VIII complex; [[posterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery]] runs along the lower nerves and the [[medulla-oblongata]].
- For microvascular decompression, identify the offending vessel and interpose a Teflon pledget between the vessel and the root entry zone.

## Closure and Pitfalls

- Watertight dural closure; seal mastoid air cells; bone flap or cranioplasty; muscle layers in anatomical planes.
- **CSF leak** is the dominant complication — incisional, rhinorrhea (via air cells), otorrhea.
- **Cerebellar retraction injury** and **vein of Dandy injury** (superior petrosal vein) cause venous infarction — sacrifice only when strictly necessary.
- **Facial and cochlear nerve injury** during vestibular schwannoma resection — intraoperative facial EMG and BAER mandatory.
