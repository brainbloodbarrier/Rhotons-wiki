# Anterior Interhemispheric Approach

The anterior interhemispheric approach accesses midline pathology through the interhemispheric fissure. It is the primary corridor for [[pericallosal-artery-aneurysm]] arising from the [[anterior-cerebral-artery]] bifurcation and is used selectively for [[acoa-aneurysm]] with complex or superiorly projecting anatomy. Two positioning variants exist: nose-up for proximal pericallosal aneurysms and lateral-rotation with gravity retraction for distal lesions along the [[corpus-callosum]].

## Indications

Pericallosal artery aneurysms originate from left- or right-sided bifurcations of the [[anterior-cerebral-artery]] but are functionally midline aneurysms located deep to the [[falx-cerebri]]. The interhemispheric fissure need only be accessed from one side, and the craniotomy is made eccentric to that side. The right side is chosen by default to avoid complications in the dominant hemisphere from the craniotomy, venous sacrifice, or retraction.

## Positioning -- Proximal Variant (Nose Up)

The patient is supine with the head in neutral position (nose up) and the neck slightly extended. This orientation places the [[interhemispheric-fissure]] vertically, and the proximal [[anterior-cerebral-artery]] segments are directly below the craniotomy. A bicoronal skin incision begins at the right [[zygomatic-arch]] and ends at the contralateral [[superior-temporal-line]], accommodating the eccentric craniotomy. The scalp is folded to expose supraorbital [[frontal-bone]] from the ipsilateral [[superior-temporal-line]] to the contralateral glabella. Pericranium is harvested during opening to be available for [[frontal-sinus]] coverage during closure.

## Craniotomy

The craniotomy flap has a medial border that crosses the [[superior-sagittal-sinus]] to the contralateral side. This midline exposure allows the [[dura]] to be opened directly to the edge of the [[superior-sagittal-sinus]], providing unobstructed access to the [[interhemispheric-fissure]] without any overhanging bone. The flap extends inferiorly to the [[anterior-cranial-fossa]] floor, laterally to the [[superior-temporal-line]] (leaving the [[temporalis-muscle]] undisturbed), and posteriorly to the [[coronal-suture]].

The [[superior-sagittal-sinus]] is crossed carefully with the craniotome. In younger patients with non-adherent [[dura]], the flap can be raised as a single piece. In elderly patients or those with thin, adherent dura, the flap is raised in two pieces: first a unilateral right frontal flap up to but not across the sinus, then the dura containing the [[superior-sagittal-sinus]] is dissected from the inner table under direct visualization before the second piece crosses the sinus. The inner table of the inferior [[frontal-bone]] in the midline is drilled flat -- analogous to drilling the [[sphenoid-bone]] in the [[pterional-approach]] -- to increase visualization along the [[anterior-cranial-fossa]] floor for proximal control of low-lying aneurysms.

## Dural Opening

The [[dura]] is opened with a semicircular flap based against the [[superior-sagittal-sinus]]. Bridging frontal veins are preserved when they are large and posteriorly located. A vein fusing with the dura before reaching the sinus can be saved by dividing the dural flap on both sides, creating a dural sleeve that preserves the vein. Venous infarction from bridging vein sacrifice is a potentially devastating complication that is difficult to predict; preservation is prioritized whenever possible.

## Gravity Retraction Variant (Distal Pericallosal Aneurysms)

For aneurysms along the A4 or A5 segments lying on the body of the [[corpus-callosum]], a modified position uses gravity to retract the right hemisphere. The patient is supine with bolsters under the left shoulder. The head is turned 90 degrees to the right with the sagittal midline parallel to the floor, then angled 45 degrees upward (lateral neck flexion). This allows the dependent right hemisphere to fall away from the [[falx-cerebri]], opening the [[interhemispheric-fissure]] without a fixed retractor.

A trapdoor incision replaces the bicoronal incision: the anterior limb follows the hairline, the posterior limb sits behind the [[coronal-suture]], and parasagittal limbs cross just past the midline. The craniotomy is two-thirds anterior and one-third posterior to the [[coronal-suture]], crossing the [[superior-sagittal-sinus]]. The dural flap is semicircular, based against the sinus. Gravity pulls the hemisphere down away from the [[falx-cerebri]]; a retractor may be needed only on the inferior free edge of the [[falx-cerebri]] to better expose the [[pericallosal-artery]] and [[corpus-callosum]].

## Key Technical Points

- Right-sided approach is default to protect the dominant hemisphere
- Craniotomy must cross the [[superior-sagittal-sinus]] for true midline exposure; ipsilateral-only craniotomies leave an obstructing bony ledge
- Two-piece flap technique in elderly patients prevents sinus injury from adherent dura
- Bridging vein preservation is non-negotiable when veins are large or posteriorly located
- Gravity retraction eliminates the need for fixed retractors and reduces cortical injury risk for distal aneurysms
