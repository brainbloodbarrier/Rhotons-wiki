# Extracranial-Intracranial Bypass

The extracranial-intracranial (EC-IC) bypass is Lawton's first-generation bypass and the most commonly performed cerebral revascularization procedure. It connects an extracranial scalp artery to an intracranial recipient through an end-to-side anastomosis, delivering low-flow augmentation to ischemic or flow-compromised territories. The [[superficial-temporal-artery]] (STA) and [occipital-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/occipital-artery.md) (OA) serve as the principal donors, each matched to specific vascular territories by their anatomic course and caliber.

## STA-MCA Bypass

The STA-MCA bypass is the workhorse of cerebral revascularization and accounts for the majority of EC-IC bypasses. The [[superficial-temporal-artery]] is harvested from the scalp with a connective tissue cuff to minimize vasospasm, traced from the root of the [[zygomatic-arch]] to its frontal and parietal bifurcation. Donor selection favors the branch with the larger caliber and straighter course to the [pterional-craniotomy](../nsatlas-wiki/vault/approaches/pterional-craniotomy.md) site.

The recipient [middle-cerebral-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/middle-cerebral-artery.md) branch is selected on the cortical surface, typically an M4 cortical artery in the territory requiring revascularization. The arteriotomy is made with a beveled needle and extended with microscissors to match the fish-mouthed STA donor. Two anchoring sutures at heel and toe orient the anastomosis, and a continuous 10-0 nylon suture completes each suture line, typically requiring 12 bites per line. Intraluminal inspection after the first suture line identifies technical errors before completing the second wall.

Indications include [[moyamoya-disease]] (the most common indication, accounting for over half of EC-IC bypasses), deliberate branch occlusion during complex [[aneurysm]] clipping, trapping of giant or thrombotic aneurysms, and hemodynamic insufficiency from atherosclerotic occlusive disease. When two efferent arteries require revascularization after [middle-cerebral-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/middle-cerebral-artery.md) aneurysm trapping, both the frontal and parietal STA branches are incorporated in a double-barrel STA-MCA bypass.

## Flash Fluorescence Technique

Lawton's "flash fluorescence" technique solves the problem of identifying hidden efferent arteries from deep Sylvian or insular aneurysms. The technique uses [[indocyanine-green]] (ICG) videoangiography with three steps: (1) temporary clip occlusion of the aneurysm's afferent artery, (2) ICG injection showing fluorescence in uninvolved cortical arteries only, and (3) clip removal producing a "flash" of fluorescence in the efferent artery's cortical branches. This enables selection of a more superficial and accessible recipient, avoiding deep [[sylvian-fissure]] dissection. The technique identifies recipients for all bypass types, including both EC-IC and IC-IC variants.

## STA-ACA Bypass

Ischemia in the [anterior-cerebral-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/anterior-cerebral-artery.md) territory or ACA aneurysms requiring distal revascularization after parent artery occlusion may call for an STA-ACA bypass to the [[interhemispheric-fissure]]. The diminished STA caliber as it travels to the midline necessitates technical modifications: harvesting the STA without a connective tissue cuff straightens and elongates it, and anastomosing the parietal branch to the end of the frontal branch provides the additional length needed to reach interhemispheric recipients. This effectively converts the EC-IC bypass into a low-flow interpositional configuration.

## OA-PICA Bypass

The OA-PICA bypass is the posterior fossa analogue of the STA-MCA bypass, connecting the [occipital-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/occipital-artery.md) to the [[posterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery]] through a [retromastoid-craniotomy](../nsatlas-wiki/vault/approaches/retromastoid-craniotomy.md) or [[far-lateral-approach]]. OA harvest is considerably more difficult than STA harvest, requiring layer-by-layer dissection through three muscular layers of the suboccipital region: the [[sternocleidomastoid-muscle]] and [[trapezius-muscle]] in the first layer, the [[splenius-capitis]] and [[semispinalis-capitis]] in the second, and the muscles of the suboccipital triangle in the third. The transperiosteal "inside-out" technique offers a faster alternative, elevating the muscles as a single myocutaneous flap and dissecting the OA from the undersurface.

The anastomotic site on the p3 segment of [[posterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery]] is chosen on the caudal loop, away from [lower-cranial-nerves](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/lower-cranial-nerves.md) in the more open midline spaces. The OA can also be extended superiorly to the occipital pole for an OA-P4 PCA bypass, or to the distal [[sylvian-fissure]] for an OA-M4 MCA bypass when the STA is unavailable.

## STA-PCA and STA-SCA Bypasses

The STA has sufficient length to reach recipients in the crural and ambient cisterns for posterior circulation revascularization. The STA-P2A PCA bypass is preferred over the STA-s2 SCA bypass because the pretemporal corridor lateral to the [oculomotor-nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/oculomotor-nerve.md) (the tentorial-oculomotor triangle) provides a comfortable working space with a more substantive recipient artery. Access requires a wide [[sylvian-fissure]] split and lateral temporal lobe retraction through an [[orbitozygomatic-craniotomy]]. The STA-SCA bypass through a subtemporal approach remains an alternative, with the recipient SCA lying just under the tentorial edge.

## Complications and Outcomes

Bypass patency is confirmed intraoperatively with ICG videoangiography and postoperatively with catheter angiography. The completed bypass provides both anterograde flow to the recipient territory and retrograde flow back to the aneurysm, enabling proximal occlusion alone for fusiform aneurysms. When combined with [[aneurysm]] trapping, thrombectomy with the ultrasonic aspirator debulks the lesion to reduce mass effect.
