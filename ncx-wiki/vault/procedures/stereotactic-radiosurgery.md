# Stereotactic Radiosurgery

Stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) delivers a single, high-dose, precisely conformal radiation treatment to an intracranial target, exploiting the union of stereotactic localization (first described by Leksell in 1951) and a steep dose gradient to the surrounding brain. In modern practice SRS complements — but does not replace — microsurgery and endovascular therapy for the common neurosurgical pathologies. In this casebook SRS is frequently invoked as the alternative or adjunct treatment limb in consent discussions.

## Delivery Platforms

| Platform | Technique | Typical dose | Strengths |
|----------|-----------|--------------|-----------|
| **Gamma Knife** (Leksell) | 192 fixed Co-60 sources; frame-based (Leksell G frame) or mask | 12–25 Gy, 50% isodose | Sub-millimeter accuracy, decades of outcome data |
| **Linac-based SRS** (e.g. Varian Edge, Novalis) | Multi-leaf collimator + micro-MLC; frameless mask | 12–25 Gy | Cone or volumetric-arc delivery; body SRS possible on same platform |
| **CyberKnife** | Robotic arm-mounted linac; mask; real-time tracking | 12–25 Gy single, 3–5 fractions for hypofractionated | Frameless; hypofractionation friendly |
| **Proton radiosurgery** | Bragg-peak protons | 12–20 Gy-equivalent | Exquisite distal falloff; pediatric / skull base |

## Target Indications

### AVMs
For AVM nidus ≤3 cm — especially in deep locations (basal ganglia, thalamus, brainstem) where [[cerebrovascular-avms-overview|microsurgical resection]] carries unacceptable eloquence risk — SRS offers obliteration rates of ~70–80% at 3 years (marginal dose 18–22 Gy). During the 2–3 year **latency period** the hemorrhage risk persists; this is the central consent trade-off. Staged SRS (volume-staged or dose-staged) is used for larger nidi. See [[deep-avm]] and [[brainstem-avm]] for the two classic SRS-first indications; larger lesions fall under the [[spetzler-martin-grading]] framework where SRS is increasingly favored for grade IV–V.

### Schwannomas and Meningiomas
Small or residual [[vestibular-schwannoma]] (<3 cm, minimal brainstem compression) is treated with 12–13 Gy marginal dose — hearing preservation rates exceed 60% at 5 years with modern planning. Recurrent or residual [[meningioma]] after subtotal resection is another classic SRS indication, typically 14–16 Gy for WHO grade I and higher doses (18+ Gy) for grade II lesions.

### Metastases
Brain metastases up to ~3 cm are routinely managed with SRS, either primary or adjuvant to resection cavity; dose is inversely proportional to volume (24 Gy for <2 cm, 18 Gy for 2–3 cm). SRS has largely replaced whole-brain radiotherapy for oligometastatic disease (≤4–10 lesions).

### Pituitary Adenomas
For recurrent or residual [[pituitary-adenoma]] — particularly secretory tumors (Cushing, acromegaly) where pharmacologic control is incomplete — SRS at 20–30 Gy can achieve biochemical remission. The optic apparatus must receive <8 Gy, a planning constraint that limits eligibility.

### Functional Targets
- **[[trigeminal-neuralgia]]**: 70–90 Gy to the root entry zone — outcomes comparable to microvascular decompression but with 3–6 month latency to pain relief.
- **Movement disorders** (thalamotomy, pallidotomy): Gamma Knife thalamotomy is a niche alternative to DBS for essential tremor in poor surgical candidates.

## Comparison with Fractionated Radiation

Conventional fractionated radiotherapy delivers ~1.8–2 Gy per session over 5–6 weeks, relying on **the four Rs** (repair, reoxygenation, redistribution, repopulation) for normal-tissue preservation. SRS explicitly *overrides* these Rs — a single high dose yields vascular endothelial damage and mitotic catastrophe within the high-dose volume. The trade-off: SRS requires millimeter targeting and favors well-circumscribed, small-volume lesions; fractionation is superior for infiltrative tumors or large volumes adjacent to eloquent structures.

## Toxicity

- **Radionecrosis** (6–24 months): the signature late complication; risk proportional to volume receiving >12 Gy (V12 metric). Presents as progressive enhancing lesion; managed with corticosteroids ± bevacizumab; surgical resection for refractory cases.
- **Cranial neuropathy**: optic nerve >8 Gy, trigeminal sensory change after SRS for schwannoma or TN.
- **Cerebral edema** after AVM SRS — typically subclinical MRI finding.
- **Delayed cyst formation** — known complication of AVM SRS ~5% at 10 years.

## Role in the NCX casebook

SRS is the reference "non-surgical" arm in most of the casebook's treatment-decision pages. Treatment selection generally follows: if the lesion meets a surgical indication and the [[spetzler-martin-grading]] / Simpson / Koos grade is favorable, microsurgery first; otherwise, SRS or combined modality (embolization + SRS, subtotal resection + SRS boost). The [[cerebrovascular-avms-overview]] and [[avm-resection-principles]] entries carry the per-location micro-vs-SRS decision grids.
