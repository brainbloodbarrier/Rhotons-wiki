# Orbitozygomatic Approach

The orbitozygomatic approach is a skull-base extension of the [[pterional-approach]] that dramatically enhances the operative corridor by removing the orbital rim, orbital roof, lateral orbital wall, and [[zygomatic-arch]]. It is the standard approach for [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm]] and is used selectively for giant or complex [anterior-circulation](../rhoton-wiki/vault/synthesis/anterior-circulation.md) aneurysms and deep bypass procedures.

## Rationale and Indications

When the head is rotated away from the aneurysm and extended, the superior and lateral orbit forms the roof of the operative corridor. Drilling the [[pterion]] raises this roof modestly, but complete removal of orbital walls and depression of the globe with the dural flap opens the corridor far more effectively. [[Zygomatic-arch]] resection increases inferior mobilization of the [[temporalis-muscle]], which opens the [[middle-cranial-fossa]] and facilitates posterolateral retraction of the [[temporal-lobe]].

The widened corridor improves illumination, reduces brain retraction, and enhances instrument maneuverability. The approach provides a sweep of surgical trajectories from supraorbital to transsylvian to pretemporal to subtemporal. Critically, it extends the upward line of sight toward the [basilar-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/basilar-artery.md) apex. In the standard [[pterional-approach]], the orbital rim casts a shadow over high-riding basilar apex aneurysms in the [interpeduncular-cistern](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/interpeduncular-cistern.md) -- the orbitozygomatic extension eliminates this obstruction.

Routine use is for [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm]]. Selective use includes giant [[mca-aneurysm]], complex [[ophthalmic-artery-aneurysm]], and deep bypass procedures that benefit from additional working room. Potential complications include frontalis branch [facial-nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/facial-nerve.md) injury, orbital asymmetry, pulsatile enophthalmos, diplopia from extraocular muscle or nerve injury, and [[frontal-sinus]] or ethmoidal sinus communication with risk of CSF leakage or infection.

## Extracranial Dissection

Patient position and skin incision mirror the [[pterional-approach]]. The key difference is the soft-tissue dissection to expose the orbitozygomatic unit. The [[zygomatic-arch]] and orbital rim are ensheathed by two layers of [[temporalis-muscle]] fascia. In the subfascial technique (preferred for simplicity and speed), both fascial layers are elevated off the [[temporalis-muscle]], then the deep layer is cut along the posterior edge of the lateral orbital rim and the superior edge of the [[zygomatic-arch]], releasing the superficial layer to mobilize over the orbitozygomatic unit. An interfascial alternative separates the two layers, leaving the deep fascia on muscle and mobilizing only the superficial layer.

Both fascial layers are incised from the [[zygomatic-arch]] to the [[superior-temporal-line]] along the skin incision, then anteriorly to the keyhole. The [[temporalis-muscle]] itself is not incised -- the fascial flap elevates cleanly when the muscle remains attached to the skull. A deep fat pad beneath the fascial flap is separated with blunt dissection. The periorbita is stripped from the orbit's interior beginning inferolaterally near the [[inferior-orbital-fissure]], advancing circumferentially along the orbital roof and lateral wall to a depth of approximately 3 cm toward the orbital apex.

## Craniotomy and Osteotomies

The orbitozygomatic unit consists of the orbital rim, orbital roof, lateral orbital wall, and [[zygomatic-arch]]. Removal options include a one-piece technique (better cosmesis, harder osteotomies), a two-piece technique (standard, most common), and a modified orbital-only approach (when zygoma resection adds no meaningful exposure).

The frontotemporal craniotomy resembles the [[pterional-approach]] but extends more medially beyond the [[supraorbital-nerve]] notch. The [[pterion]] does not need separate drilling because it is removed with the orbital unit. Six osteotomies release the orbitozygomatic unit using a reciprocating saw:

1. **Osteotomy 1** -- Zygomatic root: two perpendicular cuts notch the root for secure seating during replacement. A fixation plate is pre-registered.
2. **Osteotomy 2** -- Inferolateral [[zygomatic-arch]] to halfway across the zygomatic bone at the malar eminence.
3. **Osteotomy 3** -- From the [[inferior-orbital-fissure]] through the zygomatic bone to meet osteotomy 2, creating a V-shaped lock.
4. **Osteotomy 4** -- Medial orbital roof, anterior-to-posterior, just lateral to the [[supraorbital-nerve]] notch. [[Dura]] and the globe are protected with Telfa strips.
5. **Osteotomy 5** -- Posterior orbital roof, medial-to-lateral, approximately 2-3 cm posterior to the inner table of the [[frontal-bone]], finishing in the [sphenoid-bone](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/sphenoid-bone.md) ridge.
6. **Osteotomy 6** -- Lateral orbital wall, from [[inferior-orbital-fissure]] connecting to osteotomy 5 at the sphenoid ridge.

After removal, additional bone is resected around the orbital apex -- residual orbital roof, lateral orbital wall, and medial [[lesser-wing-of-sphenoid]] back to the [[superior-orbital-fissure]]. Resection continues to the base of the [anterior-clinoid-process](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/anterior-clinoid-process.md), which is left in place.

## Subtemporal Exposure

After splitting the [[sylvian-fissure]], the [[temporal-lobe]] mobilizes posterolaterally through the pretemporal corridor. The inferior margin of the temporal squamosal bone is drilled flush with the [[middle-cranial-fossa]] floor back to the zygomatic root, removing the bony barrier to smooth retraction. The dural flap, based over the orbit, is tented forward with tacking sutures (often a dozen or more) to depress the globe and maximize the corridor into the [[sylvian-fissure]] and [[carotid-cistern]].

## Key Technical Points

- Subfascial dissection is faster and simpler than the interfascial technique
- The V-shaped zygomatic bone cuts and notched root ensure accurate fragment repositioning
- Extensive dural tacking prevents swollen orbital tissue from bulging into the corridor
- The orbitozygomatic extension eliminates the shadow cast by the orbital rim over high-riding [basilar-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/basilar-artery.md) apex aneurysms
