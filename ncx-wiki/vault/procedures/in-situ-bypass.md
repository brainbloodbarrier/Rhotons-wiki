# In-Situ Bypass

The in-situ bypass is Lawton's third-generation IC-IC bypass and the purest expression of the intracranial-intracranial concept: no scalp artery, no graft, no tunnel, and minimal extra effort to recruit participants. It joins two parallel intracranial arteries through a side-to-side anastomosis, creating a communicating channel governed by deliberate occlusions, pressure gradients, and physiological demand.

Where [[reimplantation-bypass]] requires proximity and mobility, the in-situ bypass requires proximity and **parallelism**. The "one way up" side-to-side anastomosis eliminates the need for mobility, but the intraluminal first suture line is the most technically demanding anastomosis, requiring simultaneous management of four arterial walls with meticulous avoidance of endothelial contact.

## Parallelism: Four Anatomic Sites

Arterial parallelism in the cerebral circulation occurs at only four locations, each corresponding to a specific bypass territory.

### MCA In-Situ Bypass (Sylvian Triangle)

The [middle-cerebral-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/middle-cerebral-artery.md) has the greatest in-situ bypass potential due to its many parallel branches. Three variants exist:

- **M2 MCA-M2 MCA bypass**: Superior and inferior trunks course in parallel through the [[sylvian-fissure]] over the insular cortex, but simultaneous temporary occlusion of two M2 segments risks large-territory ischemia; the [[ec-ic-bypass]] was frequently preferred.
- **ATA-M2 MCA bypass**: The [[anterior-temporal-artery]] originates from M1 proximal to the bifurcation pathology, making it immune to occlusive maneuvers. Its smaller caliber remodels over time with high demand.
- **M3 MCA-M3 MCA bypass**: Performed superficially at the opercular-cortical transition where M3 segments exit the [[sylvian-fissure]] side by side. Flash fluorescence identifies efferent branches of the [[aneurysm]] (recipients) versus unrelated arteries (donors).

The [pterional-craniotomy](../nsatlas-wiki/vault/approaches/pterional-craniotomy.md) must extend posteriorly beyond the angular gyrus for M3 bypasses targeting post-bifurcation insular aneurysms.

### ACA In-Situ Bypass (Falco-Frontal Triangle)

The distal [anterior-cerebral-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/anterior-cerebral-artery.md) has the most anatomically favorable geometry for in-situ bypass. The bilateral A3 segments course in intimate parallel contact all the way around the [corpus-callosum](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/corpus-callosum.md), from rostrum to splenium. The **L A3 ACA-R A3 ACA in-situ bypass** -- the most commonly performed ACA variant (20% of all in-situ bypasses) -- creates a new [anterior-communicating-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/anterior-communicating-artery.md) distal to the native one, capable of cross-filling from left to right.

This enables complex ACoA [[aneurysm]] management: the aneurysm can be occluded, trapped, or proximally clipped as long as one efferent A2 ACA is preserved to cross-fill through the bypass. Access requires a [bifrontal-craniotomy](../nsatlas-wiki/vault/approaches/bifrontal-craniotomy.md) with midline-horizontal head positioning for gravity retraction, opening the falco-frontal triangle without fixed retractors.

Variants include the **L CmaA-R CmaA bypass** between [[callosomarginal-artery]] branches and the **L PcaA-R PcaA bypass** between [[pericallosal-artery]] branches. Superior-inferior PcaA-CmaA bypasses are not feasible with in-situ technique due to their separation across the cingulate gyrus (though [[reimplantation-bypass]] can bridge this gap).

### PCA/SCA In-Situ Bypass (Tentorial-Oculomotor Triangle)

The only parallelism here exists between the P2A [posterior-cerebral-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/posterior-cerebral-artery.md) and s1 [superior-cerebellar-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/superior-cerebellar-artery.md) in the crural cistern, behind the [oculomotor-nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/oculomotor-nerve.md). Critical peduncular and circumflex perforators supplying the [brainstem](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/brainstem.md) must be excluded from temporary clipping. A V4 VA-a3 AICA variant connects the [vertebral-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/vertebral-artery.md) to the [[anterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery]].

### PICA In-Situ Bypass (Vago-Accessory Triangle)

The **L p3 PICA-R p3 PICA bypass** is the single most frequently performed in-situ bypass (44% of all cases, 11 patients). The bilateral [[posterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery]] p3 segments meet behind the [[medulla-oblongata]] in the cisterna magna underneath the cerebellar tonsils, where their ascending limbs course in parallel in the midline toward the roof of the [fourth-ventricle](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/fourth-ventricle.md). This site is remote from the [lower-cranial-nerves](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/lower-cranial-nerves.md) of the vago-accessory triangle.

The early ascending p3 segments inferior to the tonsils are optimal; the vallecula deepens superiorly making late ascending segments more difficult. Perforators along early p3 supply the gracile and cuneate fasciculi and must be excluded from temporary clipping. Access is through a midline suboccipital [retromastoid-craniotomy](../nsatlas-wiki/vault/approaches/retromastoid-craniotomy.md) with tonsillar elevation.

## In-Situ Bypass Technique

The side-to-side anastomosis uses long linear arteriotomies (three times arterial diameter) -- no fish-mouthing. Donor caliber should equal or exceed recipient caliber. Steps:

1. **Arteriotomies** in both parallel arteries
2. **Anchoring stitch** joining the two vessels
3. **Entrance stitch** passing intraluminally through donor wall
4. **Intraluminal suture line** (back wall) sewn continuously
5. **Exit stitch** returning extraluminally
6. **Extraluminal suture line** (front wall) sewn loosely, then tightened and tied

The completed bypass supplies the recipient territory anterograde and retrograde back to the occluded [[aneurysm]]. MCA and ACA parallelism permits flexibility in site selection; PCA/SCA and PICA parallelism limits the anastomosis to prescribed locations.

|---|---|---|
| L p3 PICA-R p3 PICA | 11 | 44% |
| L A3 ACA-R A3 ACA | 5 | 20% |
| M2/M3 MCA variants | 5 | 20% |
| PCA/SCA variants | 2 | 8% |
| CmaA and PcaA variants | 2 | 8% |
