# Posterior Interhemispheric Approach

The posterior interhemispheric approach is the mirror image of the [[anterior-interhemispheric-approach]]: a midline corridor between the occipital lobe and the falx cerebri, descending to the splenium of the corpus callosum, the pineal region, the posterior third ventricle, and the tentorial incisura. When combined with a tentorial incision it is the **occipital transtentorial (Poppen) approach** to the pineal region and superior cerebellum. It is chosen when a supracerebellar infratentorial (Krause) trajectory is unfavorable — for example, large tumors extending supratentorially, lesions above a steep tentorium, or when the vein of Galen complex must be mobilized superiorly.

## Indications

| Target | Typical Pathology |
|--------|-------------------|
| Pineal region (above the vein of Galen) | Pineal parenchymal tumors, germinoma, teratoma, pineoblastoma |
| Splenium of corpus callosum | Glioma, lymphoma, [[cavernous-malformation]] |
| Posterior third ventricle | Tumor extending upward from the pineal region |
| Falcotentorial junction | Falcotentorial [[meningioma]] |
| Tentorial incisura (posterior) | Tentorial meningioma, dural AVF |
| Medial occipital/parieto-occipital AVM | [[parieto-occipital-avm]] near the midline |

## Positioning

- **Three-quarter prone (park-bench-modified)** or **sitting** — both allow the occipital lobe to fall away by gravity, minimizing retraction.
- **Concorde-prone** with the head flexed is an alternative for bilateral access.
- Non-dominant (typically right) side chosen unless lesion laterality dictates otherwise — minimizes risk of language-dominant visual cortex injury.
- Mayfield fixation; head turned to place the approach side dependent; shoulder taped.

## Craniotomy

- Curvilinear or hockey-stick incision crossing the midline.
- Craniotomy is asymmetric: two-thirds on the approach side, one-third across the midline, exposing the edge of the [[superior-sagittal-sinus]] (SSS) and the torcula inferiorly — do **not** expose the torcula unless it must be skeletonized.
- Dural opening U-shaped, based on the SSS and occasionally on the transverse sinus; the **tentorium** is exposed laterally and incised near the straight sinus when transtentorial extension is needed.

## Bridging Veins and Venous Anatomy

- Posterior bridging veins draining into the SSS are **much less numerous** than anteriorly; usually none or one are present in the parieto-occipital region, giving relatively easy access.
- The critical venous structure is the **vein of Galen complex** (internal cerebral veins, basal veins of Rosenthal, precentral cerebellar vein). These drain from the pineal region into the great cerebral vein and must be preserved absolutely — sacrifice causes catastrophic bilateral thalamic and basal ganglia venous infarction.
- The **occipital vein** and **posterior temporal bridging vein** into the transverse sinus are occasionally encountered and must be preserved when possible to protect visual cortex.

## Intradural Dissection

- Gentle retraction of the occipital lobe medially and superiorly — the cuneus and lingual gyri fall toward the tentorium.
- Identify the falcotentorial junction and the straight sinus; incise the tentorium parallel to and ~1 cm lateral to the straight sinus (avoid the sinus and its junction with the transverse).
- For pineal-region tumors, dissect inferiorly through the quadrigeminal cistern; preserve the vein of Galen, internal cerebral veins, and basal veins of Rosenthal.
- The **splenium of the corpus callosum** forms the superior boundary; for deep lesions a small posterior callosotomy preserves anterior commissure and most forniceal fibers.

## Pitfalls

- **Occipital lobe retraction injury** — homonymous hemianopsia is the most common morbidity; minimize with CSF release, gravity retraction, and dynamic retraction.
- **Sinus injury** — SSS, straight sinus, transverse sinus; control with hemostatic agents and avoid suction-cautery directly on sinus wall.
- **Vein of Galen injury** — avoid at all costs; preoperative MRV critical.
- **Cortical venous infarction** — sacrifice of a bridging vein draining eloquent cortex.
- **CSF leak** via mastoid air cells or frontal sinus if the craniotomy strays too lateral or too anterior.

## Comparison with Supracerebellar Infratentorial Approach

| Feature | Posterior interhemispheric (Poppen) | Supracerebellar infratentorial (Krause) |
|---------|-------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Position | Prone / 3/4 prone / sitting | Sitting / prone |
| Tentorium | Incised | Below tentorium |
| Vein of Galen | Approached from above | Approached from below |
| Best for | Tumors extending supratentorially, above vein of Galen | Small pineal tumors below vein of Galen, precentral cerebellar vein preserved |
| Risk | Occipital retraction, visual cortex | Cerebellar retraction, air embolism |
