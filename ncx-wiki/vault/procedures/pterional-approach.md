# Pterional Approach

The pterional craniotomy is the single most versatile approach in cerebrovascular neurosurgery. It provides access to the entire [anterior-circulation](../rhoton-wiki/vault/synthesis/anterior-circulation.md) through the [[sylvian-fissure]], reaching the [internal-carotid-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/internal-carotid-artery.md), [middle-cerebral-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/middle-cerebral-artery.md), and [anterior-cerebral-artery](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/anterior-cerebral-artery.md) territories. The approach is the workhorse for clipping [[pcoa-aneurysm]], [[mca-aneurysm]], [[acoa-aneurysm]], [[ophthalmic-artery-aneurysm]], and select [[basilar-bifurcation-aneurysm]].

## Indications

The pterional approach serves as the default craniotomy for most anterior circulation aneurysms. Its trajectory along the [[sylvian-fissure]] provides a natural corridor to the [[carotid-cistern]], [chiasmatic-cistern](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/chiasmatic-cistern.md), and [lamina-terminalis](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/lamina-terminalis.md). When combined with aggressive drilling of the [sphenoid-bone](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/sphenoid-bone.md), the corridor extends to the [interpeduncular-cistern](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/interpeduncular-cistern.md) for basilar apex pathology. The approach is preferred over the [[orbitozygomatic-approach]] when the aneurysm does not require an upward line of sight or extensive subtemporal mobilization.

## Patient Positioning

The patient is supine with a bolster under the ipsilateral shoulder. The head is rotated 15-20 degrees away from the aneurysm and extended approximately 20 degrees so that the malar eminence becomes the highest point in the field. This extension allows gravity to retract the [[frontal-lobe]] away from the [[anterior-cranial-fossa]] floor. The head is elevated above the heart to improve venous return. Neck lateral flexion is avoided to preserve working space between the shoulder and head.

This position aligns the plane of the [[sylvian-fissure]] vertically, so the [[frontal-lobe]] and [[temporal-lobe]] fall away naturally during fissure splitting -- analogous to pages of a book resting on its binding. A conventional 30-degree rotation leaves the [[temporal-lobe]] overlying the fissure and narrows the operative corridor.

## Skin Incision and Extracranial Dissection

A curvilinear incision begins at the [[zygomatic-arch]] 1 cm anterior to the tragus and arcs to the midline behind the hairline. The [[superficial-temporal-artery]] is preserved posteriorly for potential bypass. The scalp flap is elevated only enough to expose the zygomatic root and the keyhole. Critically, the superficial fat overlying the [[temporalis-muscle]] fascia is not entered because the frontalis branch of the [facial-nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/facial-nerve.md) lies in this tissue plane.

The [[temporalis-muscle]] is incised from the [[zygomatic-arch]] to the [[superior-temporal-line]], then flapped anteriorly. A fascial cuff along the [[superior-temporal-line]] is preserved for closure. In patients with large [[frontal-sinus]] anatomy, a vascularized pericranial flap is harvested during the opening to cover any sinus violation encountered during the craniotomy.

## Craniotomy Technique

A frontotemporal craniotomy is fashioned using a single temporal burr hole. The bone flap follows the [[temporalis-muscle]] incision posteriorly, then curves anteromedially to the [[supraorbital-nerve]] foramen and inferiorly to the [[anterior-cranial-fossa]] floor. Dural preservation is critical along the inferior bone cut where the [[dura]] is thinnest. Dural tears in this region expose [[frontal-lobe]] to injury during subsequent drilling of the [[pterion]].

## Drilling the Pterion

The [[pterion]] lies at the junction of the [[frontal-bone]], [[parietal-bone]], and greater wing of the [sphenoid-bone](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/sphenoid-bone.md), where the coronal suture intersects the sphenoid wing. The drill removes the pterion and [[lesser-wing-of-sphenoid]] medially to the [[superior-orbital-fissure]], creating a flat surface connecting the [[anterior-cranial-fossa]] and [[middle-cranial-fossa]] over the orbit. The [[lesser-wing-of-sphenoid]] is flattened until the lateral edge of the [[superior-orbital-fissure]] is reached -- the dural fold here transmits the [oculomotor-nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/oculomotor-nerve.md), [trochlear-nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/trochlear-nerve.md), [abducens-nerve](../rhoton-wiki/vault/entities/abducens-nerve.md), [[superior-ophthalmic-vein]], and [[ophthalmic-nerve]].

In patients where the sphenoid wing and [anterior-clinoid-process](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/anterior-clinoid-process.md) create a prominent bony hump obscuring the [[carotid-cistern]], further drilling or rongeuring past the [[superior-orbital-fissure]] is performed. The squamosal [temporal-bone](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/temporal-bone.md) is drilled inferiorly to the [[middle-cranial-fossa]] floor, producing a flat surface for dural flap reflection.

## Dural Opening

The [[dura]] is opened with a semicircular incision extending from the [[middle-cranial-fossa]] floor posteroinferiorly to the [[anterior-cranial-fossa]] floor anteroinferiorly. Multiple stay sutures flatten the dural flap. The hinge point is the [anterior-clinoid-process](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/anterior-clinoid-process.md), which requires adequate extradural bony reduction. Thorough pterion resection opens an unobstructed corridor along the dural flap directly into the [[carotid-cistern]].

## Key Technical Points

- Sylvian fissure splitting proceeds without fixed retractors when positioning is correct
- The frontotemporal craniotomy provides adequate access for most anterior circulation aneurysms without orbitozygomatic extension
- Emissary veins at the base of the [anterior-clinoid-process](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/anterior-clinoid-process.md) are controlled with bone wax
- Pericranial flap harvest during opening prevents need for repeat craniotomy if [[frontal-sinus]] is violated
