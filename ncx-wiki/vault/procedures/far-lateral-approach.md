# Far-Lateral Approach

The far-lateral approach (also termed lateral suboccipital, extreme lateral, or ELITE -- extreme lateral inferior transcondylar exposure) provides access to the ventrolateral [[medulla-oblongata]], [[vertebral-artery]], and [[posterior-inferior-cerebellar-artery]] (PICA) at the [[foramen-magnum]]. It is the primary corridor for clipping [[pica-aneurysm]] and for pathology along the intradural [[vertebral-artery]] segment.

## Indications

The approach targets lesions at the [[craniocervical-junction]] requiring a ventrolateral trajectory between the lower [[cranial-nerves]]. The operative corridor looks down the axis of the [[vertebral-artery]] and [[basilar-artery]], working in the angle between the lateral [[medulla-oblongata]] and the inferior [[cerebellum]]. An extended variant combines with the retrosigmoid approach to reach the [[cerebellopontine-angle]].

## Patient Positioning

A modified park-bench (three-quarter prone) position is used with the lesion side upward. The operating table is extended by placing a plastic board under the mattress, pulling both mattress and board 6 inches beyond the table edge. This creates a gap for the dependent arm to hang in a padded sling, dropping the shoulder to enable effective head rotation. This position minimizes [[brachial-plexus]] compression and improves venous return compared with the full prone position.

Three head-positioning maneuvers are performed in sequence: (1) anteroposterior flexion until the chin is one finger-breadth from the sternum; (2) rotation 45 degrees away from the lesion, bringing the nose toward the floor; (3) lateral flexion 30 degrees downward. These maneuvers orient the [[clivus]] perpendicular to the floor, position the ipsilateral [[mastoid-process]] as the highest point in the field, and maximally open the posterior cervical-suboccipital angle. The patient's upward shoulder is taped to keep this angle open.

## Incision and Extracranial Dissection

A hockey-stick incision begins at the cervical midline over the C4 spinous process, extends cephalad to the [[inion]], courses laterally along the [[superior-nuchal-line]] to the [[mastoid-process]], and finishes inferiorly at the mastoid tip. A 1-cm fascial cuff is preserved along the [[superior-nuchal-line]] for closure. The nuchal ligament is identified in the midline and used as the avascular plane for splitting the paraspinous musculature.

The myocutaneous flap is mobilized inferolaterally to expose the [[occipital-bone]] and [[foramen-magnum]]. The muscles are not individually dissected or transected -- mobilization as a single unit eliminates a significant source of postoperative pain. The [[vertebral-artery]] is identified and protected as it courses from the transverse foramen of [[c1-lateral-mass]], through the sulcus arteriosus of the C1 vertebral arch, to its dural entry point. The lateral epidural venous plexus is preserved with blunt dissection to avoid troublesome bleeding.

## Craniotomy

Bone removal comprises three components:

1. **C1 laminotomy** -- The arch of C1 is removed with the drill, making cuts just medial to the sulcus arteriosus and across the contralateral arch. Cuts proceed in a rostral-to-caudal direction to direct any drill lurching away from the [[vertebral-artery]]. Additional atlantal bone can be removed laterally to the transverse foramen under the [[vertebral-artery]] when needed.

2. **Lateral suboccipital craniotomy** -- After stripping dural adhesions around the [[foramen-magnum]] with angled curettes, the craniotomy extends unilaterally from the [[foramen-magnum]] in the midline, up to the muscle cuff at the level of the [[transverse-sinus]], as far laterally as possible, and back to the [[foramen-magnum]]. The rim of the [[foramen-magnum]] is rongeured to extend the opening across the midline and laterally toward the [[occipital-condyle]].

3. **Condylectomy** -- The posteromedial two-thirds of the [[occipital-condyle]] is removed with a 3-mm diamond-tipped drill bit, minimizing injury to adjacent emissary veins. The anterior extent of resection is defined by either the condylar emissary vein or the point where [[dura]] begins to curve anteromedially. Condylar resection rarely extends anterior to the [[hypoglossal-canal]]. The resection enables the dural flap reflected against the condyle to lie completely flat. Bleeding from condylar emissary veins is controlled with bone wax and oxidized cellulose packing.

## Dural Opening

The dural incision curves from the cervical midline across the circular sinus to the lateral edge of the craniotomy. An inferior incision laterally under C1 mobilizes the flap further against the craniotomy margin. Multiple tacking sutures hold the flap under tension against the [[occipital-condyle]]. The arachnoid of the [[cisterna-magna]] is preserved until the microscope is brought in, keeping blood out of the subarachnoid space. The final exposure provides a view down the barrel of the [[vertebral-artery]] with working space in the angle between the lateral [[medulla-oblongata]] and inferior [[cerebellum]].

## Extended Far-Lateral Variants

The far-lateral exposure combines with the extended retrosigmoid approach by removing additional suboccipital bone superiorly to the junction of the [[transverse-sinus]] and [[sigmoid-sinus]]. The [[mastoid-bone]] is drilled through to skeletonize the [[sigmoid-sinus]] down to the [[jugular-bulb]]. A C-shaped dural flap based along the [[sigmoid-sinus]] pulls the sinus forward to open the [[cerebellopontine-angle]]. This addition provides an almost perpendicular vantage point to the standard far-lateral trajectory, which helps clarify operative strategy for large [[vertebral-artery]] aneurysms without directly improving the far-lateral exposure itself.

## Key Technical Points

- Condylectomy is the rate-limiting step -- when you think enough condyle has been drilled, drill more
- Rostral-to-caudal drill direction on C1 protects the [[vertebral-artery]] from inadvertent injury
- Myocutaneous flap mobilization (rather than muscle-by-muscle dissection) reduces postoperative pain
- Park-bench positioning with arm sling prevents [[brachial-plexus]] compression
- The extended retrosigmoid variant adds a perpendicular vantage but does not improve the primary far-lateral corridor
