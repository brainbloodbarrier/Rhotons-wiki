# Superior Orbital Fissure

The superior orbital fissure (SOF) is the elongated gap between the greater and lesser wings of the sphenoid bone that connects the cavernous sinus, posteriorly, with the orbital apex, anteriorly. It transmits the **oculomotor (III), trochlear (IV), ophthalmic (V1), and abducens (VI) nerves**, along with the **superior ophthalmic vein** and sympathetic fibers. Because all of the nerves controlling eye movement and corneal sensation converge through this cleft, injury here produces the clinical constellation known as superior orbital fissure syndrome.

## Anatomy

- **Location:** between the lesser sphenoid wing (superomedial) and the greater sphenoid wing (inferolateral), at the apex of the orbit.
- **Medial border:** body of the sphenoid and optic strut, which separates the SOF from the optic canal.
- **Lateral border:** anterior portion of the greater sphenoid wing, part of the [[middle-cranial-fossa]] floor.
- **Anulus of Zinn** (common tendinous ring) divides the fissure into a superolateral portion and an inferomedial portion for the extraocular muscle origins.

## Contents

| Structure | Region of passage | Function |
|-----------|-------------------|----------|
| CN III (superior and inferior divisions) | Within annulus of Zinn | Extraocular movement (except SO, LR), pupil, levator |
| CN IV (trochlear) | Above annulus, superolateral | Superior oblique |
| CN V1 (ophthalmic) — frontal, lacrimal, nasociliary branches | Above annulus (frontal, lacrimal); within annulus (nasociliary) | Forehead/scalp, lacrimal, corneal sensation |
| CN VI (abducens) | Within annulus of Zinn | Lateral rectus |
| Superior ophthalmic vein | Above annulus | Venous drainage into cavernous sinus |
| Sympathetic fibers | With V1 and CN III | Pupillary dilation, Müller muscle |

## Surgical Relevance

- **[[orbitozygomatic-approach]]** removes the lateral wall and roof of the orbit, the zygomatic arch, and optionally the anterior clinoid — widening the trajectory over the SOF toward the sellar/parasellar region, basilar apex, and interpeduncular cistern.
- **Anterior clinoidectomy** — performed extradurally (Dolenc) or intradurally — unroofs the optic canal medially and the SOF laterally, releasing the falciform ligament and carotico-oculomotor membrane to expose the ophthalmic and clinoidal ICA segments for clipping [[ophthalmic-artery-aneurysm]] and paraclinoid aneurysms.
- **Cavernous sinus surgery** enters the sinus between lateral dural leaflets at the level of the SOF, navigating between CN III, IV, V1, and VI — sparing of these nerves is paramount.
- **[[pterional-approach]]** drills the sphenoid ridge flush to the SOF; over-aggressive drilling risks entering the orbit and injuring V1 branches or the superior ophthalmic vein.

## Pathology

- **Superior orbital fissure syndrome** — ophthalmoplegia of III/IV/VI, V1 sensory loss, proptosis without visual loss; caused by tumor, trauma, or inflammation (Tolosa-Hunt).
- **Orbital apex syndrome** — SOF syndrome plus optic nerve (CN II) involvement (visual loss) — implies extension into the optic canal.
- **Sphenoid wing [[meningioma]]** (medial third) often involves SOF and cavernous sinus — aggressive resection risks cranial neuropathies; many are left as residuals for radiosurgery.
- **Traumatic SOF syndrome** — greater-wing fracture impinging on contents; decompression is controversial.
- **Carotid-cavernous fistula** — engorgement of the superior ophthalmic vein produces proptosis, chemosis, bruit, and elevated intraocular pressure.

## Relationships to Other Foramina

- **Medial (across optic strut):** optic canal — CN II and ophthalmic artery.
- **Inferolateral (greater wing):** foramen rotundum — V2 to pterygopalatine fossa.
- **Posterior to foramen rotundum:** foramen ovale (V3) and foramen spinosum ([[middle-meningeal-artery]]).
- The SOF, optic canal, foramen rotundum, foramen ovale, and foramen spinosum together define the key osseous landmarks of the [[anterior-cranial-fossa]]–[[middle-cranial-fossa]] junction.
