# Middle Cranial Fossa

The middle cranial fossa (MCF) is the paired depression of the skull base that receives the [[temporal-lobe]] on each side, flanking the sella turcica. It is bounded anteriorly by the posterior edge of the lesser sphenoid wing (which separates it from the [[anterior-cranial-fossa]]), posteriorly by the petrous ridge and dorsum sellae, laterally by the squamous temporal bone, and medially by the cavernous sinus and sella. Mastery of its bony and neurovascular landmarks is a prerequisite for pterional, orbitozygomatic, subtemporal, and transpetrosal surgery.

## Bony Boundaries and Landmarks

| Boundary | Structure |
|----------|-----------|
| Anterior | Lesser sphenoid wing, anterior clinoid process, chiasmatic sulcus |
| Posterior | Petrous ridge, dorsum sellae |
| Lateral | Squamous temporal bone, greater sphenoid wing |
| Medial/floor | Sella, cavernous sinus, Meckel cave |
| Floor (anterior-lateral) | Greater wing of sphenoid — pterional craniotomy landmark via [[pterion]] |

Key bony landmarks on the floor — from medial to lateral — include the foramen rotundum (V2), foramen ovale (V3), foramen spinosum (middle meningeal artery entrance), and the arcuate eminence (overlying superior semicircular canal). The **anterior petrous apex** (Kawase triangle) lies between V3, the greater superficial petrosal nerve, and the petrous carotid — a corridor for the anterior petrosectomy.

## Neurovascular Contents

- **[[middle-meningeal-artery]]** — enters via foramen spinosum; grooves the inner table and is the canonical source of epidural hematoma when torn at the [[pterion]].
- **Cavernous sinus** — contains the cavernous ICA, cranial nerves III, IV, V1, V2, and VI; breached in pituitary tumors and in selected aneurysm strategies (ophthalmic segment clipping approached after anterior clinoidectomy).
- **Meckel cave** — dural diverticulum housing the trigeminal ganglion; surgical target for [[trigeminal-neuralgia]] rhizotomy and for trigeminal schwannomas.
- **Greater superficial petrosal nerve (GSPN)** — lies on the petrous floor; sacrificed during anterior petrosectomy, risking dry eye.

## Surgical Corridors

- **[[pterional-approach]]** — exposes the anterolateral MCF after sphenoid wing drilling.
- **[[orbitozygomatic-approach]]** — adds orbital and zygomatic osteotomies to expand trajectory to the cavernous sinus and sellar region.
- **Subtemporal approach** — temporal lobe elevation to reach tentorial incisura, basilar apex, petrous apex; risks vein of Labbé during retraction.
- **Kawase (anterior petrosectomy)** — drills the petrous apex for mid-basilar aneurysms and petroclival meningiomas, preserving cochlea and labyrinth.
- **Transcavernous routes** — for basilar tip aneurysms or parasellar tumors; require dissection of the lateral cavernous wall.

## Common Pathologies

- Sphenoid wing meningioma (medial, middle, lateral thirds)
- Cavernous sinus meningioma and trigeminal schwannoma
- Petroclival [[meningioma]] — straddles the petrous apex into posterior fossa
- [[pituitary-adenoma]] with cavernous extension
- MCF epidermoid cyst, arachnoid cyst, encephalocele through a floor dehiscence

## Pitfalls

- Over-retraction injures the **vein of Labbé**, risking temporal lobe venous infarction.
- Drilling the anterior clinoid without identifying the optic strut risks optic nerve and ICA injury.
- Forcing posterior petrosectomy without facial nerve mapping risks CN VII palsy.
- The floor may be dehiscent over the facial nerve canal or tegmen tympani — CSF leak and hearing loss follow inadvertent entry.
