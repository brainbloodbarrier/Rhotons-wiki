# Cerebellopontine Angle

V-shaped angular cleft formed by the cerebellum folding around the pons and middle cerebellar peduncle. Contains CN IV-XI and the three major cerebellar arteries. The most commonly utilized operative route to the posterior fossa is the retrosigmoid approach directed through the CPA.

## Anatomical Framework

### Cerebellopontine Fissure

- **Shape:** V-shaped with superior and inferior limbs
- **Superior limb:** extends above CN V
- **Inferior limb:** passes below the [cerebellum](../rhoton-wiki/vault/concepts/cerebellum.md) flocculus and CN IX-XI
- **Apex:** located laterally where limbs meet
- **Contents:** CN IV-XI arise in or near this angular space
- Middle cerebellar peduncle fills the interval between the limbs

### Three Neurovascular Complexes (Rhoton)

The posterior fossa structures occur in sets of three with consistent relationships:

| Component | Upper Complex | Middle Complex | Lower Complex |
|-----------|--------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Artery** | SCA | AICA | PICA |
| **Brainstem** | Midbrain | Pons | Medulla |
| **Cerebellar surface** | Tentorial | Petrosal | Suboccipital |
| **Cerebellar peduncle** | Superior | Middle | Inferior |
| **Fissure** | Cerebellomesencephalic | Cerebellopontine | Cerebellomedullary |
| **Cranial nerves** | CN III, IV, V | CN VI, VII, VIII | CN IX, X, XI, XII |

### Upper Neurovascular Complex

- **SCA** courses above and parallel to CN V
- **Trigeminal nerve:** rises from midpons, courses into Meckel's cave
- **Superior petrosal vein (SPV):** drains anterolateral cerebellum; crosses rostrally to superior petrosal sinus
- SPV normally well away from CN V — but in ~20% of brains no dominant SPV exists
- Neurovascular compression here → [[trigeminal-neuralgia]]

### Middle Neurovascular Complex

- **AICA** passes between CN VII and VIII, turns backward along middle cerebellar peduncle
- **Labyrinthine artery:** often arises from AICA loop near the internal acoustic meatus (IAM)
- **CN VII (facial):** anterior-superior in IAM; courses anterior to superior vestibular nerve
- **CN VIII (vestibulocochlear):** posterior in IAM; cleavage plane between superior and inferior vestibular nerves
- **Nervus intermedius:** arises from anterior surface of CN VIII, joins CN VII distally
- Flocculus and choroid plexus protrude from foramen of Luschka behind CN VII/VIII

### Internal Acoustic Meatus — Fundus Organization

| Quadrant | Nerve |
|----------|-------|
| Anterosuperior | **Facial nerve (VII)** |
| Posterosuperior | Superior vestibular nerve |
| Anteroinferior | Cochlear nerve |
| Posteroinferior | Inferior vestibular nerve |

**Landmarks:**
- **Transverse crest (crista falciformis):** separates superior from inferior nerves
- **Vertical crest (Bill's bar):** separates facial from superior vestibular nerve at fundus — critical surgical landmark

### Lower Neurovascular Complex

- **PICA** courses around CN IX, X, XI
- CN IX-XI arise posterior to the olive in a line along the lateral medulla
- Nerves come together as they reach the dura → enter jugular foramen
- Foramen of Luschka opens into CPA below CN VII/VIII junction

## Surgical Approaches

### Retrosigmoid Approach (Primary CPA approach)

- **Position:** Lateral decubitus or park bench
- **Incision:** Vertical, crossing the asterion
- **Burr hole:** Lower edge of asterion (overlies lower half of transverse-sigmoid junction)
- **Exposure:** Petrosal surface of cerebellum elevated to reach CPA
- **Indications:** [[vestibular-schwannoma]], [[trigeminal-neuralgia]], [[meningioma]], epidermoid, microvascular decompression

### Suprameatal Extension

- Extends retrosigmoid approach to access structures above the IAM
- Provides access to the upper neurovascular complex (CN V region)
- Used for trigeminal schwannomas, petroclival lesions

### Other CPA Approaches

- **Translabyrinthine:** Sacrifices hearing; excellent CN VII exposure; no cerebellar retraction
- **Middle fossa:** Extradural; best for small intracanalicular tumors with serviceable hearing
- **Transpetrosal (anterior/posterior):** Combined approach for large/complex lesions

## CPA Pathology

| Lesion | Frequency | Complex |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| [[vestibular-schwannoma]] | Most common CPA tumor (~80%) | Middle |
| [[meningioma]] | Second most common (~10%) | Variable |
| Epidermoid | Third most common (~5%) | Variable |
| [[trigeminal-neuralgia]] | Vascular compression | Upper |
| Hemifacial spasm | Vascular compression CN VII | Middle |
| Glossopharyngeal neuralgia | Vascular compression CN IX/X | Lower |
| Trigeminal schwannoma | | Upper |

## Key Surgical Pearls

- **Three neurovascular complexes** provide a systematic framework for CPA surgery
- **AICA loop** near IAM is the most important arterial relationship during schwannoma surgery — labyrinthine artery must be preserved for hearing
- **Bill's bar** (vertical crest) at IAM fundus — identify to locate CN VII anterior to superior vestibular nerve
- **SPV sacrifice** may be needed for exposure but must assess relationship to CN V first
- **DVA preservation** — associated developmental venous anomalies must be preserved
- **Infraflocular approach** for CN VII decompression in hemifacial spasm — approach junction from below flocculus
